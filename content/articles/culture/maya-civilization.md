The Maya civilisation developed in southern Mexico and Central America, in today's Guatemala, Belize, Honduras and El Salvador, over more than three thousand years. Known for its cities with monumental pyramids and palaces, its fully developed writing system, its calendars, astronomy and mathematics, it reached its height in the Classic period, from about 250 to 900 CE. Millions of Maya people live in the region today.

## Origins

Maya settlements developed from around 2000 BCE, as people grew maize, beans and squash. In the Preclassic period, cities such as El Mirador, in northern Guatemala, built enormous pyramids by around 300 BCE. Lidar surveys, using lasers from aircraft, have revealed that the lowlands were densely settled, with causeways, terraces and reservoirs.

## The Classic period

From around 250 CE, the Maya lowlands were home to dozens of city-states, ruled by divine kings, k'uhul ajaw. Great cities included Tikal, Calakmul, Palenque, Copán, Yaxchilán and Caracol, some with tens of thousands of people. Tikal and Calakmul were great rivals, building networks of allies and vassals. Cities had temple-pyramids, palaces, plazas and ballcourts, where a ritual ballgame was played with a rubber ball. Kings recorded their deeds on carved stone monuments, stelae. At Palenque, the tomb of King K'inich Janaab' Pakal, who ruled from 615 to 683, was discovered inside the Temple of the Inscriptions in 1952, with a jade mask.

## Writing

The Maya developed the most complete writing system in the pre-Columbian Americas, with logograms and syllabic signs, carved on monuments, painted on pottery and written in bark-paper books, codices. Spanish priests burned most codices; only four survive. The script was largely deciphered from the 1950s to the 1990s, by scholars such as Yuri Knorozov, Tatiana Proskouriakoff and Linda Schele, revealing the names and histories of kings.

## Calendars and mathematics

The Maya used a mathematical system based on twenty, including a symbol for zero. They used several calendars: a 260-day ritual calendar, a 365-day solar calendar, which combined to form a 52-year cycle, and the Long Count, which recorded days from a starting date in 3114 BCE. The end of a Long Count cycle in December 2012 led to modern misconceptions about the end of the world. Maya astronomers tracked the movements of Venus and predicted eclipses.

## Religion

The Maya worshipped gods of maize, rain, Chaak, the sun and others. Kings performed bloodletting rituals to communicate with gods and ancestors, and captives were sometimes sacrificed. The Popol Vuh, a book of the K'iche' Maya, written down in the sixteenth century, tells the story of creation and of the Hero Twins.

## The Classic collapse

Between about 800 and 950 CE, many cities of the southern lowlands were abandoned. Causes may include prolonged droughts, shown in climate records, warfare, overpopulation, environmental degradation and political breakdown. In the north of the Yucatán, cities such as Chichén Itzá and later Mayapán flourished.

## Spanish conquest

The Spanish conquered the Maya region over the sixteenth and seventeenth centuries; the last independent Maya kingdom, Nojpetén, fell in 1697. Today, about six million or more Maya people speak about thirty Mayan languages.

## On our maps

Our maps from 1 BCE onward show Mesoamerica, where the Maya lived beside powers such as [Teotihuacan](/place/teotihuacan) and the [Toltec](/place/toltec), and later the [Aztec Empire](/place/aztec-empire).
