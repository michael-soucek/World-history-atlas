The Renaissance, French for "rebirth", was a period of European cultural, artistic, intellectual and scientific flourishing, beginning in Italy in the fourteenth century and spreading across Europe in the fifteenth and sixteenth centuries. Inspired by the rediscovery of classical Greek and Roman learning, it produced great artists, such as Leonardo da Vinci and Michelangelo, and humanist scholars, and marked the transition from the Middle Ages to the modern era.

## Why Italy?

The Renaissance began in Italian cities, especially Florence, which were wealthy from trade, banking and manufacturing. Rich families, such as the Medici of Florence, and popes in Rome, acted as patrons, funding artists and scholars. Italy was full of Roman ruins, reminding people of the classical past. Greek scholars, some fleeing the Ottoman advance on Constantinople, which fell in 1453, brought Greek manuscripts and knowledge to Italy.

## Humanism

Humanism was an intellectual movement that emphasised the study of classical texts, the humanities, grammar, rhetoric, history, poetry and moral philosophy, and the potential and dignity of human beings. Petrarch, in the fourteenth century, collected and studied Latin manuscripts and is often called the father of humanism. Humanists, such as Lorenzo Valla, used critical methods to study texts; Valla proved that the Donation of Constantine was a forgery. Pico della Mirandola's Oration on the Dignity of Man celebrated human potential. Erasmus, in the Netherlands, became the leading humanist of northern Europe.

## Art

Renaissance artists studied nature and the human body and used new techniques, such as linear perspective, developed by Filippo Brunelleschi and described by Leon Battista Alberti, to create the illusion of depth. In Florence, Giotto, in the early fourteenth century, anticipated the Renaissance; Masaccio, Donatello and Botticelli, who painted The Birth of Venus, followed. Brunelleschi built the great dome of Florence Cathedral. In the High Renaissance, Leonardo da Vinci painted The Last Supper and the Mona Lisa; Michelangelo carved the statue of David and painted the ceiling of the Sistine Chapel; and Raphael painted The School of Athens. In Venice, Titian was a master of colour.

## Northern Renaissance

The Renaissance spread to northern Europe, where artists such as Jan van Eyck, who perfected oil painting, Albrecht Dürer and Pieter Bruegel worked, and writers such as Thomas More, author of Utopia, Rabelais, Montaigne, Cervantes and Shakespeare flourished.

## The printing press

Johannes Gutenberg's printing press with movable type, developed in Mainz around 1450, allowed books to be produced cheaply and quickly, spreading knowledge and ideas across Europe. The Gutenberg Bible was printed around 1455.

## Politics and science

Niccolò Machiavelli's The Prince, written in 1513, analysed political power realistically. Renaissance thinkers contributed to the beginnings of the Scientific Revolution: Copernicus proposed that the earth moves around the sun in 1543, and Andreas Vesalius published detailed studies of human anatomy the same year.

## Debate

Historians, following Jacob Burckhardt's influential nineteenth-century work, long saw the Renaissance as a sharp break from the "dark" Middle Ages. Many modern historians emphasise continuity with medieval culture and earlier "renaissances", such as the Carolingian and twelfth-century renaissances, and note that the Renaissance mainly affected elites.
