Mycenaean Greece was the last phase of the Bronze Age in mainland Greece, from about 1600 to 1100 BCE. Named after Mycenae, its most famous site, it was the first advanced civilisation on the Greek mainland, with palace states, writing in an early form of Greek and a warrior aristocracy. It is the world described, in legendary form, by Homer's epics.

## Rise

The Mycenaeans were Greek-speakers who emerged as a wealthy elite around 1600 BCE, as shown by the rich shaft graves at Mycenae, excavated by Heinrich Schliemann in 1876. They contained gold masks, one of which he called the "Mask of Agamemnon", weapons and jewellery. The Mycenaeans were influenced by the Minoan civilisation of Crete, and around 1450 BCE they took control of Crete and its palace at Knossos.

## Palaces

Mycenaean society was organised around palaces, such as those at Mycenae, Pylos, Tiryns and Thebes, ruled by a king, the wanax, with officials and scribes. Mycenae and Tiryns were surrounded by massive walls of huge stones, called "Cyclopean" by later Greeks, who believed only giants could have moved them. The Lion Gate at Mycenae, built around 1250 BCE, has two carved lions above the entrance. Kings were buried in tholos tombs, beehive-shaped stone chambers, such as the "Treasury of Atreus".

## Linear B

The Mycenaeans adapted the Minoan script to write their language, creating Linear B, written on clay tablets. In 1952 the English architect Michael Ventris deciphered Linear B and showed that it was an early form of Greek. The tablets, preserved when palace fires baked them, are administrative records, listing goods, livestock, workers, offerings to gods and land, and show a highly organised economy controlled by the palaces. They include the names of gods worshipped later, such as Zeus, Hera, Poseidon and Dionysus.

## Trade and war

The Mycenaeans traded across the Mediterranean, with Egypt, Cyprus, the Levant, Anatolia and Italy, exporting olive oil, wine and pottery, and importing copper, tin, gold, ivory and glass. Hittite texts mention a land called Ahhiyawa, probably the Mycenaean Greeks, involved in western Anatolia. The Mycenaeans were warriors, using chariots, bronze armour, such as the Dendra panoply, and boar's tusk helmets.

## Troy

Homer's Iliad and Odyssey, composed around the eighth century BCE, tell of the Trojan War, in which a Greek army led by Agamemnon, king of Mycenae, besieged Troy, in Anatolia. Schliemann excavated Troy at Hisarlik, in Turkey, from 1870. Archaeologists debate whether a real war lies behind the legend; Troy was destroyed several times, including around 1180 BCE.

## Collapse

Around 1200 BCE, the Mycenaean palaces were destroyed, as part of the wider Late Bronze Age collapse in the eastern Mediterranean. Possible causes include earthquakes, invasions, internal revolts, drought and the breakdown of trade. Linear B writing disappeared, and Greece entered a "Dark Age" until about 800 BCE, when the Greeks adopted the alphabet. Memories of the Mycenaean age survived in myths and in Homer's epics.
