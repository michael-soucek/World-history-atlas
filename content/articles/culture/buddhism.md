Buddhism is a religion and philosophy founded in India around the fifth century BCE on the teachings of Siddhartha Gautama, the Buddha, "the awakened one". It teaches a path to liberation from suffering through ethical conduct, meditation and wisdom. Spreading across Asia, it developed into several traditions and today has several hundred million followers.

## The Buddha

Siddhartha Gautama was born, according to tradition, a prince of the Shakya clan in Lumbini, in today's Nepal. He left his comfortable life to seek an end to suffering, practised meditation and asceticism and attained enlightenment under the Bodhi tree at Bodh Gaya. He spent the rest of his life teaching in northern India, founding a community of monks and nuns, the sangha, and died at Kushinagar at about eighty.

## Core teachings

The Buddha taught the Four Noble Truths: life involves suffering, dukkha; suffering arises from craving and attachment; suffering can end; and the way to end it is the Noble Eightfold Path of right view, intention, speech, action, livelihood, effort, mindfulness and concentration. He taught that all things are impermanent, that there is no permanent self, anatta, and that actions, karma, influence future lives in a cycle of rebirth, samsara. The goal is nirvana, liberation from the cycle. Buddhists take refuge in the Three Jewels: the Buddha, the Dharma, his teaching, and the Sangha, the community.

## Spread in India

After the Buddha's death, his teachings were passed down orally and organised at councils. The emperor Ashoka, in the third century BCE, supported Buddhism and sent missionaries, including to Sri Lanka. Monasteries, stupas and cave temples were built across India, such as Sanchi and Ajanta. Great monastic universities, such as Nalanda, attracted students from across Asia. Buddhism declined in India from about the seventh century, and after the destruction of monasteries in the twelfth century, it largely disappeared from its homeland, until a revival in the twentieth century, led by B. R. Ambedkar among Dalits.

## Theravada

Theravada, "the teaching of the elders", preserves the Pali Canon, the oldest complete collection of Buddhist scriptures, written down in Sri Lanka in the first century BCE. It emphasises monastic life and the path to becoming an arhat, a liberated person. It is practised in Sri Lanka, Myanmar, Thailand, Laos and Cambodia.

## Mahayana

Mahayana, "the great vehicle", which emerged around the first century BCE, emphasises the ideal of the bodhisattva, who seeks enlightenment for the benefit of all beings, and introduced new scriptures, such as the Lotus Sutra and the Heart Sutra. It spread along the Silk Road to China, from the first century CE, and then to Korea, Japan and Vietnam, where schools such as Pure Land and Chan, known in Japan as Zen, developed.

## Vajrayana

Vajrayana, or tantric Buddhism, developed in India and spread to Tibet from the seventh century, where it became the dominant tradition, led by lamas such as the Dalai Lama, and to Mongolia, Bhutan and the Himalayas.

## Today

Buddhism faced persecution in some periods, such as in China in 845 and during the Cultural Revolution. In the twentieth century, it spread to the West, where meditation and mindfulness became popular. There are perhaps around 500 million Buddhists today.
