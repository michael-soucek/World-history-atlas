Zoroastrianism is one of the world's oldest continuously practised religions, founded in ancient Iran by the prophet Zarathustra, known to the Greeks as Zoroaster. It teaches belief in a supreme wise god, Ahura Mazda, and a cosmic struggle between good and evil. It was the main religion of the Persian empires for over a thousand years, and today has small communities, notably the Parsis of India.

## Zarathustra

Zarathustra's dates are uncertain; scholars place him between about 1500 and 500 BCE, many favouring around 1200 to 1000 BCE, based on the language of his hymns, the Gathas, which is close to the ancient Sanskrit of the Rigveda. He probably lived in eastern Iran or Central Asia. According to tradition, he was a priest who received a revelation from Ahura Mazda, "Wise Lord", at the age of thirty, and preached against older gods and rituals, facing opposition before gaining the support of a king, Vishtaspa.

## Beliefs

Zoroastrianism teaches that Ahura Mazda is the creator of all that is good. Opposing him is Angra Mainyu, or Ahriman, the destructive spirit. The world is a battleground between truth, asha, and falsehood, druj. Humans have free will to choose between good and evil, through good thoughts, good words and good deeds. After death, the soul crosses the Chinvat Bridge, which is wide for the righteous, leading to paradise, and narrow for the wicked, who fall into hell. At the end of time, a saviour, the Saoshyant, will bring the resurrection of the dead, final judgement and the renewal of the world, when evil will be defeated. Scholars debate how far these ideas influenced Judaism, Christianity and Islam.

## Fire

Fire is a symbol of purity and of Ahura Mazda's light, and worship takes place before a sacred fire in fire temples. The most sacred fires, such as the Atash Behram, are kept burning continuously. Zoroastrians traditionally exposed the dead on towers of silence, dakhmas, to be eaten by vultures, to avoid polluting earth and fire.

## Scripture

The Avesta, the sacred texts, includes the Gathas, attributed to Zarathustra, and later hymns and laws, written in Avestan. Much was passed down orally and written down in the Sasanian period, in a script invented for the purpose. Much of the Avesta has been lost.

## The Persian empires

The Achaemenid kings, such as Darius the Great, invoked Ahura Mazda in their inscriptions. Zoroastrianism was the state religion of the Sasanian Empire, from 224 to 651 CE, with a powerful priesthood, led by figures such as Kartir, and great fire temples. The Magi, priests, appear in the Gospel of Matthew.

## After the Arab conquest

After the Arab Muslim conquest of Iran in the seventh century, most Iranians gradually converted to Islam over several centuries. Zoroastrians faced discrimination and pressure. Some migrated to India from the eighth to tenth centuries, landing in Gujarat, where they became the Parsis. Parsis, such as the Tata family, became prominent in business and public life in Mumbai.

## Today

There are perhaps 100,000 to 200,000 Zoroastrians today, in India, Iran and diaspora communities, and numbers are declining. Nowruz, the Iranian new year, has Zoroastrian roots. The name of the car maker Mazda was partly inspired by Ahura Mazda.
