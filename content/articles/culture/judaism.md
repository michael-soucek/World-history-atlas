Judaism is the religion of the Jewish people, one of the oldest monotheistic religions, with a history of over three thousand years. It is based on the covenant between God and the people of Israel, described in the Hebrew Bible, and on a rich tradition of law, interpretation and practice. Christianity and Islam both trace their origins to Abraham, regarded as the father of the Jewish people.

## Beliefs

Judaism teaches belief in one God, who created the world and revealed his will to the people of Israel. The Shema, a central prayer, declares: "Hear, O Israel: the Lord our God, the Lord is one." Jews believe in living according to God's commandments, the mitzvot, which govern ethics, worship, diet, the Sabbath and daily life. Traditional Judaism counts 613 commandments in the Torah. Many Jews await a messiah, a descendant of King David, who will bring an age of peace.

## Scripture

The Hebrew Bible, the Tanakh, consists of the Torah, the five books of Moses, the Prophets, Nevi'im, and the Writings, Ketuvim, including the Psalms. The Torah describes the creation, the patriarchs Abraham, Isaac and Jacob, the slavery of the Israelites in Egypt, the Exodus led by Moses, and the giving of the law at Mount Sinai. The Oral Torah, traditions of interpretation, was written down in the Mishnah, around 200 CE, and discussed in the Talmud, compiled in Babylonia and Palestine between the third and sixth centuries, which remains central to Jewish study.

## Ancient history

The kingdoms of Israel and Judah existed in the first millennium BCE. According to the Bible, King Solomon built the First Temple in Jerusalem, which the Babylonians destroyed in 586 BCE, exiling many Jews to Babylon. After the Persian king Cyrus allowed them to return, the Second Temple was built. In 70 CE, during a revolt, the Romans destroyed the Second Temple. Judaism developed without the Temple, centred on synagogues, prayer, study and rabbis, teachers.

## Diaspora

Jews lived in communities across the Middle East, North Africa and Europe, the diaspora. Ashkenazi Jews lived in central and eastern Europe, speaking Yiddish, and Sephardic Jews in Spain and Portugal, from which they were expelled in 1492 and 1497, many settling in the Ottoman Empire. Mizrahi Jews lived in the Middle East. Jews often faced persecution, expulsions, massacres and restrictions, but also periods of prosperity and cultural achievement, such as the work of the philosopher Maimonides in the twelfth century.

## Practice

Jewish practice includes observing the Sabbath, Shabbat, from Friday evening to Saturday evening; festivals such as Passover, commemorating the Exodus, Yom Kippur, the Day of Atonement, Rosh Hashanah, the new year, and Hanukkah; dietary laws, kashrut; and life-cycle rituals, such as circumcision and bar and bat mitzvah.

## Modern times

In the eighteenth and nineteenth centuries, the Jewish Enlightenment, Haskalah, and emancipation brought changes, leading to movements such as Reform, Conservative and Orthodox Judaism, and Hasidism. Zionism sought a Jewish homeland. During the Holocaust, Nazi Germany murdered six million Jews. The State of Israel was founded in 1948. Today there are about 15 million Jews, mainly in Israel and the United States.
