Hinduism is a diverse family of religious traditions, beliefs and practices that originated in the Indian subcontinent. Often called the world's oldest living religion, with roots going back several thousand years, it has no single founder or single holy book. With over a billion followers, mostly in India and Nepal, it is the world's third-largest religion.

## The name

The word Hindu comes from the Sanskrit Sindhu, the Indus river, used by Persians to refer to the people beyond it. Many Hindus call their religion Sanatana Dharma, "the eternal way".

## Origins

Hinduism developed from many sources. The Vedic religion was brought or developed by Indo-Aryan peoples in north-western India from around 1500 BCE. The Vedas, hymns and rituals in Sanskrit, the oldest being the Rigveda, were passed down orally for centuries. Vedic religion centred on fire sacrifices to gods such as Indra, Agni and Varuna, performed by Brahmin priests. The Upanishads, philosophical texts composed from around 800 BCE, explored the nature of reality, teaching that Brahman, the ultimate reality, is identical with Atman, the inner self. Local traditions and deities also contributed.

## Key beliefs

Most Hindus believe in samsara, the cycle of birth, death and rebirth; karma, that actions have consequences in this and future lives; dharma, duty, ethics and the right way of living; and moksha, liberation from the cycle of rebirth. Paths to liberation include knowledge, jnana, devotion, bhakti, and selfless action, karma yoga. Many Hindus worship a supreme God in many forms. Major traditions focus on Vishnu, who is believed to descend to earth as avatars, such as Rama and Krishna; Shiva, the destroyer and transformer; and the Goddess, Devi, in forms such as Durga and Kali. Ganesha, the elephant-headed god, is widely worshipped.

## Texts

Besides the Vedas and Upanishads, important texts include the epics: the Mahabharata, which includes the Bhagavad Gita, a dialogue between the warrior Arjuna and Krishna about duty and devotion, and the Ramayana, the story of Prince Rama, his wife Sita and the demon king Ravana. The Puranas tell stories of the gods.

## Practice

Hindu practice includes worship, puja, at home and in temples, offerings, prayers and devotional singing, pilgrimages to sacred places, such as Varanasi on the Ganges, festivals, such as Diwali, the festival of lights, and Holi, the festival of colours, and life-cycle rituals. Yoga and meditation have their roots in Hindu traditions.

## Society

Traditional Hindu society was organised into varnas, four classes: Brahmins, priests and scholars; Kshatriyas, warriors and rulers; Vaishyas, merchants and farmers; and Shudras, labourers, as well as many jatis, castes. Those outside the system, now called Dalits, faced discrimination as "untouchables". Untouchability was abolished by India's constitution in 1950, though caste discrimination persists. Reform movements, from the Bhakti saints to modern reformers, have challenged caste hierarchy.

## Spread

Hinduism spread to South-East Asia, where Hindu kingdoms built temples such as Angkor Wat, in Cambodia, and Prambanan, in Java; Bali remains largely Hindu. Through the Indian diaspora, Hinduism is practised worldwide, and teachers such as Swami Vivekananda, who spoke in Chicago in 1893, introduced it to the West.
