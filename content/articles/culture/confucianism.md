Confucianism is a system of thought, ethics and social philosophy based on the teachings of Confucius, a Chinese teacher who lived from 551 to 479 BCE. Emphasising moral cultivation, family loyalty, respect for elders, proper conduct and good government, it shaped the society, politics and culture of China, Korea, Japan and Vietnam for over two thousand years.

## Confucius

Confucius, Kong Fuzi, lived in the state of Lu, during a period of political disorder. He was a teacher who sought to restore social harmony through a return to the values and rituals of the early Zhou dynasty. His sayings and conversations with his disciples were collected in the Analects. He failed to find a ruler to put his ideas into practice but trained many students.

## Core ideas

Central to Confucianism is ren, humaneness, benevolence or love for others. Li, ritual and propriety, governs proper behaviour in all relationships. Xiao, filial piety, the respect and care owed to parents and ancestors, is the root of virtue. Yi, righteousness, means doing what is right. The ideal person is the junzi, the "gentleman" or exemplary person, who cultivates virtue through learning and self-reflection. Confucianism emphasises five relationships: ruler and subject, father and son, husband and wife, elder and younger brother, and friend and friend, each with mutual obligations. Rulers should govern by virtue and moral example rather than force.

## Mencius and Xunzi

After Confucius's death, his followers developed his ideas. Mencius, in the fourth century BCE, taught that human nature is innately good and that rulers who oppress the people lose the Mandate of Heaven and may be overthrown. Xunzi, in the third century BCE, argued that human nature is inclined to selfishness and must be shaped by education and ritual.

## State ideology

The Qin dynasty favoured Legalism and, according to tradition, persecuted Confucian scholars. But under the Han dynasty, Emperor Wu, in the second century BCE, made Confucianism the official ideology and founded an imperial academy to teach the Confucian classics. The Five Classics, including the Book of Songs, the Book of Documents and the Spring and Autumn Annals, became the basis of education.

## Neo-Confucianism

During the Tang and Song dynasties, Confucianism was challenged by Buddhism and Daoism. Neo-Confucian thinkers, such as Zhu Xi, in the twelfth century, incorporated metaphysical ideas and created a comprehensive philosophy. Zhu Xi's commentaries on the Four Books, the Analects, Mencius, the Great Learning and the Doctrine of the Mean, became the basis of the civil-service examinations from 1313 until 1905, through which officials were selected. Wang Yangming, in the sixteenth century, emphasised the unity of knowledge and action.

## East Asia

Confucianism spread to Korea, where the Joseon dynasty, from 1392, made it the state ideology, to Japan, where it shaped Tokugawa society, and to Vietnam.

## Modern times

In the early twentieth century, Chinese reformers blamed Confucianism for China's weakness, and the May Fourth Movement attacked it. The Communist government condemned it, especially during the Cultural Revolution. Since the 1980s, it has been revived in China, and Confucian values remain influential in East Asian societies. Whether Confucianism is a religion, a philosophy or both is debated.
