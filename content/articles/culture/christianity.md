Christianity is a monotheistic religion based on the life and teachings of Jesus of Nazareth, whom Christians believe to be the Son of God and the Messiah, or Christ. It began as a movement within Judaism in the first century CE in the Roman province of Judea and spread around the world, becoming the largest religion, with over two billion followers.

## Beliefs

Most Christians believe in one God in three persons, the Trinity: the Father, the Son and the Holy Spirit. They believe that Jesus was both divine and human, that he died by crucifixion for the sins of humanity and rose from the dead, and that he offers salvation and eternal life to those who believe. The Christian Bible consists of the Old Testament, largely the Hebrew scriptures, and the New Testament, including the four Gospels, the Acts of the Apostles and letters. Central practices include baptism and the Eucharist, or communion.

## The early church

After Jesus's crucifixion, around 30 CE, his followers, led by apostles such as Peter, proclaimed his resurrection. Paul of Tarsus, a Jewish convert, spread Christianity among non-Jews, Gentiles, across the eastern Mediterranean, writing letters that became part of the New Testament. Christian communities grew in cities of the Roman Empire, despite periods of persecution, such as under Nero and Diocletian.

## Empire and councils

In 313 Emperor Constantine, who had converted, granted Christians freedom of worship. In 325 he called the Council of Nicaea, which defined the doctrine of the Trinity in the Nicene Creed. In 380 Theodosius I made Christianity the state religion of the Roman Empire. Church councils debated doctrine; the Council of Chalcedon in 451 defined Christ as having two natures, divine and human, a definition rejected by the Oriental Orthodox churches, such as the Coptic, Ethiopian and Armenian churches.

## East and West

Christianity spread through monasteries and missionaries across Europe, converting peoples such as the Irish, Anglo-Saxons, Franks, Slavs and Scandinavians. The church in the Latin West, led by the bishop of Rome, the pope, and the church in the Greek East, centred on Constantinople, grew apart. In 1054 a schism divided the Roman Catholic Church and the Eastern Orthodox Church. The Crusades were launched in the name of Christianity.

## Reformation

In the sixteenth century, Martin Luther and other reformers challenged the Catholic Church, leading to the Protestant Reformation and the creation of Lutheran, Reformed, Anglican and other churches. The Catholic Church responded with the Counter-Reformation.

## Global spread

From the sixteenth century, European colonisation and missionary activity spread Christianity to the Americas, Africa, Asia and Oceania, often with colonial violence, but also through local evangelists and converts. In the twentieth and twenty-first centuries, Christianity grew rapidly in sub-Saharan Africa, Latin America and parts of Asia, while declining in practice in much of Europe. Pentecostal and charismatic movements spread widely.

## Today

The largest Christian traditions are the Catholic Church, with over a billion members, Protestant churches and Eastern Orthodoxy. Christianity has shaped art, music, literature, law, education and ethics in many societies.
