The Hellenistic period is the era of Mediterranean and Near Eastern history between the death of Alexander the Great in 323 BCE and the Roman conquest of Ptolemaic Egypt in 30 BCE. During this time, Greek language and culture spread across a vast region from Egypt to Afghanistan, blending with local traditions, and kingdoms ruled by Alexander's successors dominated the eastern Mediterranean.

## The successors

Alexander the Great conquered the Persian Empire and died in Babylon in 323 BCE without an adult heir. His generals, the Diadochi, "successors", fought each other for over forty years. Three main dynasties emerged: the Ptolemies in Egypt, founded by Ptolemy I; the Seleucids in Syria, Mesopotamia and Iran, founded by Seleucus I; and the Antigonids in Macedon. Other kingdoms included Pergamon, in Asia Minor, and the Greco-Bactrian kingdom, in today's Afghanistan, whose rulers later expanded into India as the Indo-Greek kings.

## Alexandria

Alexandria, founded by Alexander in Egypt, became the greatest city of the Hellenistic world, with perhaps half a million people. The Ptolemies founded the Museum, a research institution, and the Library of Alexandria, which aimed to collect all the books in the world, perhaps hundreds of thousands of scrolls. The Lighthouse of Alexandria, the Pharos, was one of the Seven Wonders of the Ancient World. The Hebrew Bible was translated into Greek in Alexandria, the Septuagint.

## Science

The Hellenistic period was a golden age of science. Euclid, in Alexandria, wrote the Elements, a textbook of geometry used for over two thousand years. Archimedes, in Syracuse, calculated the value of pi, discovered the principle of buoyancy and designed machines. Eratosthenes calculated the circumference of the earth with remarkable accuracy. Aristarchus of Samos proposed that the earth moves around the sun. Hipparchus founded trigonometry and catalogued stars. Physicians such as Herophilus dissected human bodies. The Antikythera mechanism, found in a shipwreck, is a complex geared device that modelled the movements of the sun, moon and planets.

## Philosophy

New schools of philosophy sought ways to live well in an uncertain world. Stoicism, founded by Zeno of Citium, taught living according to reason and nature and accepting what cannot be changed. Epicureanism, founded by Epicurus, taught that happiness comes from simple pleasures, friendship and freedom from fear. Scepticism and Cynicism also flourished.

## Art

Hellenistic art became more dramatic, emotional and realistic, showing old age, suffering and movement, as in the Winged Victory of Samothrace, the Laocoön group and the Pergamon Altar. The Colossus of Rhodes was another of the Seven Wonders.

## Cultural mixing

Greek became the common language, koine, of the eastern Mediterranean, used in trade and administration; the New Testament was later written in it. Greek culture mixed with Egyptian, Persian, Jewish, Indian and other traditions. In Gandhara, Greek styles influenced Buddhist art. In Judea, resistance to Hellenisation led to the Maccabean revolt in 167 BCE.

## The end

Rome gradually conquered the Hellenistic kingdoms: Macedon in 168 BCE, Greece in 146 BCE, Pergamon in 133 BCE and Seleucid Syria in 63 BCE. When Cleopatra VII died in 30 BCE, the last Hellenistic kingdom became a Roman province. Greek culture continued to dominate the eastern Roman Empire.
