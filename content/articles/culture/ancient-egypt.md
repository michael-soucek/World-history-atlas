Ancient Egypt was a civilisation that flourished along the Nile river in north-eastern Africa for about three thousand years, from the unification of the country around 3100 BCE until its conquest by Rome in 30 BCE. Famous for its pyramids, temples, hieroglyphic writing, mummies and pharaohs, it was one of the world's earliest and longest-lasting civilisations.

## The gift of the Nile

The Greek historian Herodotus called Egypt "the gift of the Nile". Every summer, the river flooded, depositing fertile black silt on the fields. Egyptians called their land Kemet, "black land", in contrast to the red desert. They grew wheat, barley and flax, and built irrigation canals. The river was also the main highway.

## Unification

Around 3100 BCE, Upper Egypt, the valley in the south, and Lower Egypt, the Delta in the north, were united under one king, traditionally named Menes or Narmer. The Narmer Palette shows a king wearing the crowns of both lands. Historians divide Egyptian history into dynasties and periods.

## The Old Kingdom

In the Old Kingdom, from about 2686 to 2181 BCE, the pharaohs, considered divine, built pyramids as tombs. Djoser's step pyramid at Saqqara, designed by the architect Imhotep, was the first. The Great Pyramid of Giza, built for Khufu around 2560 BCE, was the tallest human-made structure for nearly 4,000 years. It was built by organised teams of paid workers, not slaves.

## The Middle and New Kingdoms

After a period of division, the Middle Kingdom, from about 2055 to 1650 BCE, reunified Egypt, and literature flourished. The Hyksos, from western Asia, ruled the north until expelled. In the New Kingdom, from about 1550 to 1070 BCE, Egypt became an empire, extending into the Levant and Nubia. Pharaohs were buried in the Valley of the Kings. Notable rulers include Hatshepsut, Thutmose III, Akhenaten, who promoted worship of a single god, the sun disc Aten, Tutankhamun, whose tomb was found nearly intact in 1922, and Ramesses II.

## Religion

Egyptians worshipped many gods, such as Ra, the sun god, Osiris, god of the dead, Isis, Horus and Anubis. They believed in an afterlife, preparing the dead through mummification and placing goods in tombs. The Book of the Dead contained spells to guide the dead through the underworld, where their heart would be weighed against the feather of Maat, truth and order.

## Writing

Egyptians developed hieroglyphs, a writing system of pictorial signs, around 3200 BCE, and simpler scripts, hieratic and demotic, for everyday use, writing on papyrus. The Rosetta Stone, found in 1799, with the same text in hieroglyphs, demotic and Greek, allowed Jean-François Champollion to decipher hieroglyphs in 1822.

## Later periods

After the New Kingdom, Egypt was ruled at times by Libyans, Nubians, Assyrians and Persians. In 332 BCE Alexander the Great conquered Egypt, and the Ptolemaic dynasty, of Macedonian Greeks, ruled from Alexandria until the death of Cleopatra VII in 30 BCE, when Egypt became a Roman province.

## Legacy

Egyptian achievements in architecture, mathematics, medicine and art influenced Greece and Rome. Egyptology, the study of ancient Egypt, became popular after Napoleon's expedition.
