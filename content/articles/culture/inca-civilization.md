The Inca civilisation was the largest empire in the pre-Columbian Americas. From their capital, Cusco, in the Andes of Peru, the Incas built an empire, called Tawantinsuyu, "the four parts together", which in the early sixteenth century stretched over 4,000 kilometres along western South America, from today's Ecuador to Chile, with perhaps 10 million people. It fell to Spanish conquistadors in the 1530s.

## Origins

According to Inca legend, the first Inca, Manco Capac, and his sister-wife, Mama Ocllo, children of the sun god Inti, emerged from Lake Titicaca, or from a cave at Pacaritambo, and founded Cusco. The Incas were one of many small groups in the Cusco valley from around the thirteenth century, building on the achievements of earlier Andean cultures, such as the Wari and Tiwanaku.

## Expansion

Around 1438, according to tradition, Cusi Yupanqui, who took the name Pachacuti, defeated the Chanka people and began the rapid expansion of the empire. He and his son Topa Inca Yupanqui conquered most of the Andes and the coast, including the Chimú kingdom. Huayna Capac extended the empire into Ecuador. The empire was divided into four quarters, suyus, meeting at Cusco.

## Government

The Sapa Inca, the emperor, was considered a descendant of the sun and ruled absolutely, supported by nobles and officials. The Incas did not use money. Instead, subjects paid taxes through labour, mit'a, working on state farms, building roads and temples, serving in the army or making textiles. The state stored food and goods in storehouses, qollqa, which were distributed in times of need. Conquered peoples were sometimes resettled, mitmaq, to secure control. Quechua was spread as the common language. The Incas kept records using khipus, knotted strings of different colours, recording numbers and perhaps other information.

## Roads and architecture

The Incas built a road network, the Qhapaq Ñan, of about 30,000 to 40,000 kilometres, with suspension bridges, such as the grass-rope bridge at Q'eswachaka, still rebuilt every year. Relay runners, chasquis, carried messages. Inca stonemasons fitted large blocks of stone without mortar so precisely that buildings withstood earthquakes, as at Sacsayhuamán and the Coricancha, the temple of the sun in Cusco. Machu Picchu, a royal estate built around 1450 for Pachacuti, is the most famous Inca site.

## Agriculture

The Incas farmed on mountain slopes using terraces and irrigation, growing maize, potatoes, of which Andean farmers had developed thousands of varieties, quinoa and other crops, and herded llamas and alpacas for wool, meat and transport. They freeze-dried potatoes, chuño, for storage.

## Religion

The Incas worshipped Inti, the sun, Viracocha, the creator, Pachamama, mother earth, and many sacred places and objects, huacas. The mummies of former rulers were preserved and honoured. Children were sometimes sacrificed on mountain tops in the capacocha ceremony.

## Conquest

Around 1527 Huayna Capac died, probably of smallpox, and his sons Huáscar and Atahualpa fought a civil war. In 1532 Francisco Pizarro captured Atahualpa at Cajamarca and executed him in 1533. Resistance continued until 1572. Quechua is still spoken by millions in the Andes.
