The Aztecs were the people who ruled a large empire in central Mexico from the fifteenth century until the Spanish conquest in 1521. Their capital, Tenochtitlan, built on an island in Lake Texcoco, was one of the largest cities in the world. The Aztecs, who called themselves the Mexica, were known for their monumental architecture, sophisticated agriculture, rich culture and religion, which included human sacrifice.

## Origins

According to their traditions, the Mexica migrated from a homeland in the north, called Aztlan, from which the name Aztec comes. Guided by their god Huitzilopochtli, they wandered for generations until they saw an eagle perched on a cactus, eating a snake, on an island in Lake Texcoco, the sign to found their city. They founded Tenochtitlan around 1325. The image is on the Mexican flag.

## The Triple Alliance

At first, the Mexica served as mercenaries for more powerful neighbours. In 1428, under Itzcoatl, they allied with the cities of Texcoco and Tlacopan in the Triple Alliance and defeated the Tepanecs, beginning the empire. Under rulers such as Moctezuma I, Ahuitzotl and Moctezuma II, the empire expanded across central and southern Mexico, collecting tribute from conquered city-states, in goods such as maize, cotton, cacao, feathers, gold and jade, recorded in tribute lists, such as the Codex Mendoza. Conquered peoples kept their own rulers.

## Tenochtitlan

Tenochtitlan had perhaps 200,000 inhabitants or more. It was connected to the mainland by causeways and crossed by canals, with an aqueduct bringing fresh water from Chapultepec. At its centre stood the Templo Mayor, a great pyramid with twin temples to Huitzilopochtli, god of war and the sun, and Tlaloc, god of rain. The market at Tlatelolco, which the Spanish conquistador Bernal Díaz described with amazement, attracted tens of thousands of people.

## Agriculture

The Aztecs grew maize, beans, squash, chilli peppers, tomatoes, amaranth and cacao. Around the lake, they built chinampas, artificial fields built from mud and vegetation in shallow water, which produced several crops a year. Some survive at Xochimilco.

## Society

Aztec society was hierarchical, with the ruler, the huey tlatoani, nobles, priests, warriors, merchants, called pochteca, craftsmen and commoners. Boys and girls attended schools. Warriors gained status by capturing enemies in battle; elite warriors joined orders such as the eagle and jaguar warriors.

## Religion

The Aztecs worshipped many gods, including Quetzalcoatl, the feathered serpent, and Tezcatlipoca. They believed that the gods had sacrificed themselves to create the sun and that human blood was needed to keep the sun moving and the world alive. Priests sacrificed captives, often by removing the heart, at the Templo Mayor and other temples. The number of victims is debated. The Aztecs used two calendars, a 365-day solar calendar and a 260-day ritual calendar. The Sun Stone is a famous carved monument.

## The Spanish conquest

In 1519 Hernán Cortés arrived with a small Spanish force and allied with Indigenous enemies of the Aztecs, especially Tlaxcala. After the death of Moctezuma II and a smallpox epidemic, Tenochtitlan fell in 1521 after a long siege. Mexico City was built on its ruins. Nahuatl, the Aztec language, is still spoken by over a million people today.
