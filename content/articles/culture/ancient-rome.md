Ancient Rome was the civilisation that grew from a small settlement on the Tiber river in Italy, traditionally founded in 753 BCE, into a vast empire that ruled the Mediterranean world and much of Europe and the Middle East. It passed through phases as a kingdom, a republic and an empire, and its law, language, engineering and institutions shaped Western civilisation.

## Origins and kings

According to legend, Rome was founded by Romulus, who, with his twin Remus, had been raised by a she-wolf. Archaeology shows villages on the hills by the Tiber from the tenth century BCE. Rome was ruled by kings, the last of whom, Tarquin the Proud, was expelled, according to tradition, in 509 BCE, when Rome became a republic.

## The Republic

The Roman Republic was governed by two consuls, elected annually, the Senate, made up of aristocrats, and popular assemblies. The plebeians, ordinary citizens, struggled for rights against the patrician aristocracy, gaining their own officials, the tribunes, and written laws, the Twelve Tables, around 450 BCE. Rome conquered Italy by 264 BCE, then defeated Carthage in the Punic Wars, overcoming Hannibal, and conquered Greece, Spain and other lands. Its army of citizen legionaries was highly disciplined.

## Crisis and civil war

Conquest brought wealth and slaves, but also inequality and tension. The Gracchi brothers, tribunes who proposed land reform, were killed in 133 and 121 BCE. Ambitious generals, such as Marius, Sulla, Pompey and Julius Caesar, used their armies in civil wars. Caesar conquered Gaul, defeated Pompey and became dictator, but was assassinated in 44 BCE.

## The Empire

Caesar's adopted heir, Octavian, defeated Mark Antony and Cleopatra and in 27 BCE became Augustus, the first emperor. The Pax Romana, two centuries of relative peace, followed. Under emperors such as Trajan, the empire reached its greatest extent in 117 CE, from Britain to Mesopotamia, with perhaps 60 million people. Hadrian built a wall across northern Britain.

## Life and culture

Rome itself grew to about a million people. The Romans built roads, aqueducts, bridges, baths, amphitheatres, such as the Colosseum, completed in 80 CE, and temples, such as the Pantheon, using concrete. Latin literature flourished with Cicero, Virgil, Horace, Ovid, Livy and Tacitus. Roman law, developed over centuries, became the basis of many legal systems. Slavery was widespread.

## Christianity

Christianity, which began in the Roman province of Judea, spread despite persecution. Emperor Constantine converted and ended persecution in 313, and under Theodosius I, Christianity became the state religion in 380.

## Crisis and fall

In the third century, the empire suffered civil wars, invasions and economic crisis. Diocletian and Constantine restored order, and Constantine founded Constantinople. The empire was divided into East and West in 395. In the fifth century, Germanic peoples settled in and took over the western provinces; Rome was sacked in 410 and 455, and the last western emperor was deposed in 476. The Eastern Roman, or Byzantine, Empire survived until 1453.

## Legacy

Latin evolved into the Romance languages, Italian, French, Spanish, Portuguese and Romanian, and influenced English. The Catholic Church preserved Latin and Roman institutions. Roman architecture, law, government and calendar have influenced the world.
