The Viking Age was the period from about 793 to 1066 CE when Scandinavian seafarers, the Vikings, raided, traded, explored and settled across Europe and beyond, from North America to the Caspian Sea. The age began with the raid on the monastery of Lindisfarne and is often said to end with the Norwegian defeat at Stamford Bridge in 1066.

## Who were the Vikings?

The Vikings came from what is now Denmark, Norway and Sweden. The word víking probably referred to sea-raiding expeditions, and those who took part. Most Scandinavians were farmers, fishers and craftspeople, and only some went raiding. They spoke Old Norse and wrote in runes. Society was divided into jarls, nobles, karls, free farmers, and thralls, slaves. Assemblies, things, made laws and settled disputes.

## Ships

The Vikings' success depended on their ships: longships, fast, light, shallow-draft vessels with oars and a square sail, which could cross seas and sail up rivers. Ships found in burials, such as the Oseberg and Gokstad ships in Norway, show their skill. They also built broader cargo ships, knarrs, for trade and settlement.

## Raids

On 8 June 793, Vikings attacked the monastery of Lindisfarne, off the coast of Northumbria, shocking Christian Europe. Monasteries, rich and undefended, were frequent targets. Raids spread to Ireland, Scotland, England, France and Spain. Vikings besieged Paris in 845 and 885–886.

## Settlement

The Vikings also settled. In England, the "Great Heathen Army" arrived in 865, and Danes conquered much of eastern England, the Danelaw, until King Alfred the Great of Wessex resisted them. In 1016 the Danish king Cnut became king of England, ruling a North Sea empire. In Ireland, Vikings founded Dublin, Waterford and Limerick. In 911 the French king granted land to the Viking leader Rollo, creating Normandy. Norse settlers colonised the Faroe Islands, Shetland, Orkney and Iceland, from around 870, where they founded the Althing, one of the oldest parliaments, in 930.

## Greenland and America

Erik the Red founded settlements in Greenland around 985. Around 1000, his son Leif Erikson reached North America, which the sagas call Vinland. Excavations at L'Anse aux Meadows, in Newfoundland, confirmed a Norse site, and tree-ring evidence dates it to 1021.

## The East

Swedish Vikings, called Varangians or Rus', travelled along the rivers of eastern Europe, trading furs, slaves and amber for silver from the Islamic world. They helped found the state of Kyivan Rus', and the Varangian Guard served the Byzantine emperors in Constantinople. Hoards of Arab silver coins have been found in Scandinavia.

## Religion

The Vikings worshipped Norse gods, such as Odin, Thor and Freyja, and believed in Valhalla, the hall of slain warriors. Christianity spread from the tenth century; Denmark, Norway and Iceland converted around 1000, Sweden later.

## End

As Scandinavian kingdoms became Christian and centralised, raiding declined. In 1066 Harald Hardrada of Norway was killed at Stamford Bridge, in England. The Vikings left a legacy in place names, language and culture, and in the Icelandic sagas, written in the thirteenth century.
