East Asia's history has turned on China, the largest and longest-lived state in the region, and on the neighbours that borrowed from it, resisted it and sometimes conquered it. Chinese writing, Confucian ideas of government and Buddhism, which arrived from India, created a shared cultural world across China, Korea, Japan and Vietnam, while the peoples of the northern steppe, Tibet and Manchuria repeatedly reshaped the map from outside it.

## Dynasties of early China

Millet farming in the Yellow River basin and rice farming along the Yangtze were under way by about 7000 BCE. The Shang dynasty, which ruled the north China plain from roughly 1600 BCE until 1046 BCE, left the earliest Chinese writing on oracle bones. The [Zhou dynasty](/place/zhou-dynasty) that overthrew it justified its rule with the idea of a Mandate of Heaven, but its authority dissolved into rival states, and the Warring States period ended only when the king of Qin conquered all his rivals in 221 BCE and took the title of First Emperor.

Qin standardised script, coinage and weights but fell within fifteen years. The [Han dynasty](/place/han-dynasty), from 206 BCE to 220 CE, gave China its enduring model of a centralised bureaucratic empire, pushed into Central Asia against the Xiongnu and opened the overland routes later called the Silk Road. After almost four centuries of division the Sui reunified China in 589, and the [Tang dynasty](/place/tang-dynasty) (618 to 907) ruled a cosmopolitan empire whose capital Chang'an, modern Xi'an, drew merchants, monks and envoys from across Asia. The Song (960 to 1279) presided over a commercial revolution, printing and the use of gunpowder, while sharing the north with the Liao, Western Xia and Jin states.

## Korea, Japan and the steppe

In Korea, the kingdom of Silla united most of the peninsula in the 660s with Tang help, then drove the Tang out. The Goryeo dynasty, from which the name Korea comes, followed in 918, and the Joseon dynasty ruled from 1392 to 1897, when its last king proclaimed the short-lived Korean Empire. Under Sejong in the 1440s Joseon created the hangul alphabet. In Japan, the Yamato court built a state modelled partly on Tang China, with capitals at Nara from 710 and Heian, modern Kyoto, from 794. From 1185 real power passed to military governments, the shogunates, beginning with the Kamakura shogunate.

The Mongols, united under Genghis Khan in 1206, conquered northern China and Korea, and in 1279 Kublai Khan completed the conquest of the Song, ruling all of China as the Yuan dynasty. Two Mongol invasions of Japan, in 1274 and 1281, failed. The Ming dynasty restored native rule in 1368, but in 1644 the Manchus took Beijing and founded the [Qing dynasty](/place/qing-dynasty), which by the 1750s had greatly enlarged the empire by adding Mongolia, Xinjiang and Tibet. Japan, reunified after a century of civil war, was governed by the [Tokugawa shogunate](/place/tokugawa-shogunate) from 1603 to 1868 under tight restrictions on foreign contact.

## Empire, war and revival

In the nineteenth century Western powers forced the region open. Britain defeated China in the [First Opium War](/event/first-opium-war) of 1839 to 1842, and the Taiping Rebellion that followed killed millions. Japan responded differently: the [Meiji Restoration](/event/meiji-restoration) of 1868 began a rapid modernisation that turned it into an imperial power, which took Taiwan in 1895 and annexed Korea in 1910. The Qing fell in 1912 and China entered decades of warlord rule, Japanese invasion from 1937 and civil war.

Japan's defeat in 1945 ended its empire. In 1949 the Communist Party founded the People's Republic of China in Beijing, while the government of the Republic of China withdrew to Taiwan; the status of Taiwan remains disputed. Korea was divided at the 38th parallel, and the [Korean War](/event/korean-war) of 1950 to 1953 ended in an armistice without a peace treaty, leaving North and South Korea separate states. Japan, then South Korea and Taiwan, and from the reforms of 1978 mainland China, went on to rapid economic growth, and by the 2010s China had the second-largest economy in the world.
