The Americas developed for thousands of years in near-complete isolation from Africa and Eurasia. Their peoples domesticated their own crops, built their own cities and states and invented writing in Mesoamerica independently. That separation ended abruptly in 1492, and the collision that followed, with conquest, epidemic disease and the forced migration of millions of enslaved Africans, created the societies of the modern hemisphere and the independent nations that broke away from European empires after 1776.

## Before 1492

People entered the Americas from north-east Asia during or after the last Ice Age; the site of Monte Verde in southern Chile shows that humans had reached the far south by about 14,500 years ago. Maize was domesticated from a wild grass in southern Mexico around 9,000 years ago, and potatoes, beans, squash, cotton and many other plants were developed independently in the Andes and elsewhere. In coastal Peru, the Caral culture built monumental platforms around 3000 to 1800 BCE, among the earliest in the hemisphere.

In Mesoamerica the [Olmecs](/place/olmecs) of the Gulf Coast, from about 1200 to 400 BCE, carved colossal stone heads and set patterns of ritual and kingship later followed across the region. The great city of Teotihuacan dominated central Mexico in the first half of the first millennium CE, while the city-states of the [Maya civilisation](/culture/maya-civilization) reached their height between about 250 and 900 CE, with a full writing system, a sophisticated calendar and large populations in the lowland forests. The Toltecs and others followed, and in 1325 the Mexica founded Tenochtitlan, capital of the [Aztec Empire](/place/aztec-empire), which by 1500 drew tribute from much of central Mexico.

In the Andes a succession of cultures, including the Moche of the north coast and the highland states of Tiwanaku and Wari, preceded the [Inca Empire](/place/inca-empire). Under Pachacuti, from 1438, the Inca expanded from Cusco into a state that stretched more than 4,000 kilometres along the mountains and coast, absorbing the coastal kingdom of Chimor and linking its provinces with a great road network. In North America, farming towns of the Ancestral Puebloans flourished in the south-west, the Mississippian city of Cahokia peaked between about 1050 and 1350, and the Haudenosaunee formed a confederacy of nations in the north-east.

## Conquest and colonisation

Norse settlers reached Newfoundland around 1000 but did not stay. Christopher Columbus's landing in the Caribbean in 1492 began permanent contact. Spanish conquistadors overthrew the Aztecs, whose capital fell in 1521, and the Inca, whose ruler Atahualpa was captured in 1532. Smallpox and other Old World diseases killed a large majority of the Indigenous population in the following century. Spain ruled most of the mainland through viceroyalties such as New Spain and Peru, Portugal colonised Brazil, and France, England and the Netherlands settled the Caribbean and North America from the early 1600s. The [Columbian exchange](/event/columbian-exchange) of crops, animals and diseases transformed diets and ecosystems on both sides of the Atlantic, and plantation economies in Brazil, the Caribbean and the southern colonies depended on enslaved Africans.

## Independence and modern nations

The thirteen British colonies declared independence as the United States in 1776. The [Haitian Revolution](/event/haitian-revolution) created the first independent state in Latin America in 1804, led by formerly enslaved people. The [Spanish American wars of independence](/event/spanish-american-wars-of-independence), led by figures such as Simón Bolívar and José de San Martín, freed most of the Spanish mainland by 1825, and Brazil separated from Portugal in 1822 as an empire. The United States expanded westward, gaining roughly half of Mexico's former territory through the annexation of Texas and the war of 1846 to 1848, and fought a civil war from 1861 to 1865 that ended slavery. Canada became a self-governing confederation in 1867.

The twentieth century brought the Mexican Revolution from 1910, the opening of the Panama Canal in 1914, the rise of the United States to global power, the Cuban Revolution of 1959, and a wave of military dictatorships in South America in the 1960s and 1970s that gave way to elected governments by the early 1990s. Today the hemisphere has 35 independent countries and more than a billion people.
