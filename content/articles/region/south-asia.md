South Asia, the subcontinent closed off by the Himalayas and the Hindu Kush to the north and by ocean on its other sides, has been home to a large share of humanity for thousands of years. Its history alternates between periods when one power united most of it and long stretches when many regional kingdoms coexisted. Hinduism, Buddhism, Jainism and Sikhism all began here, and Islam, arriving through trade and conquest, became the second great religious tradition of the region.

## From the Indus cities to the Guptas

Farming villages such as Mehrgarh, in present-day Pakistan, existed by about 7000 BCE. Around 2600 BCE the [Indus Valley Civilisation](/place/indus-valley-civilisation) built planned cities such as Harappa and Mohenjo-daro, with brick drainage, standard weights and a script that has never been deciphered. Its cities declined after about 1900 BCE. During the following Vedic period Indo-Aryan languages and the ritual texts of the Vedas spread across the north, and by the sixth century BCE a series of kingdoms and republics had formed in the Ganges plain, the most powerful of them Magadha. The Buddha and Mahavira, founder figures of Buddhism and Jainism, taught there in roughly the fifth century BCE.

Chandragupta Maurya seized Magadha around 321 BCE and built the [Maurya Empire](/place/maurya-empire), the first state to rule most of the subcontinent. His grandson [Ashoka](/person/ashoka), horrified by his bloody conquest of Kalinga, promoted Buddhist ethics through edicts carved on rocks and pillars. After the Maurya, the north-west passed to Indo-Greek, Saka and Kushan rulers, while the Satavahanas held the Deccan. The [Gupta Empire](/place/gupta-empire), from about 320 to 550 CE, is remembered for Sanskrit literature, temple building and mathematics: the astronomer Aryabhata worked in this period, and the place-value decimal system with a symbol for zero took shape in India in these centuries.

## Regional kingdoms and the sultans

After the Guptas, power was divided. The Pala kings of Bengal patronised the Buddhist university at Nalanda, and in the far south the Chola dynasty built a maritime state that in the early eleventh century sent naval expeditions against Srivijaya in Southeast Asia. Arab forces had taken Sindh in 711, and from about 1000 Mahmud of Ghazni raided northern India repeatedly. In 1206 the [Delhi Sultanate](/place/delhi-sultanate) was founded, and under a succession of Turkic and Afghan dynasties it came to rule much of the subcontinent. In the south, the Hindu Vijayanagara Empire, founded in 1336, was its great rival until its defeat at Talikota in 1565.

Babur, a Timurid prince from Central Asia, defeated the last Delhi sultan at Panipat in 1526 and founded the [Mughal Empire](/place/mughal-empire). Under Akbar (1556 to 1605) and his successors it became one of the richest states in the world, building the Taj Mahal and ruling, at its height under Aurangzeb, nearly the whole subcontinent. After Aurangzeb's death in 1707 the Marathas, the Sikhs under Ranjit Singh and regional governors broke away.

## Company rule, the Raj and partition

European trading companies had established coastal posts since the Portuguese reached Calicut in 1498. The British East India Company's victory at Plassey in 1757 gave it control of Bengal, and over the next century it conquered or made dependent most of the subcontinent. After the great rebellion of 1857 the British Crown took direct control, creating the [British Raj](/place/british-raj). The Indian National Congress, founded in 1885, and later the mass campaigns led by Mahatma Gandhi, pressed for self-rule.

Independence in 1947 came with the [partition of India](/event/partition-of-india) into India and Pakistan, accompanied by mass violence and the displacement of perhaps 15 million people. Sri Lanka and Burma became independent in 1948, and East Pakistan broke away as Bangladesh in 1971. The former princely state of Kashmir remains divided and claimed by both India and Pakistan. Nepal and Bhutan, never colonised, became republics or constitutional monarchies in the twenty-first century, and Afghanistan has passed through monarchy, Soviet intervention, civil war and, since 2021, Taliban rule. In 2023 India overtook China as the world's most populous country.
