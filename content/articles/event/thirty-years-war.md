The Thirty Years' War was a series of conflicts fought mainly in the Holy Roman Empire from 1618 to 1648. Beginning as a religious war between Protestant and Catholic states, it became a wider struggle for power involving most of the major European powers. It was one of the most destructive wars in European history, killing an estimated 4.5 to 8 million people, and ended with the Peace of Westphalia.

## Background

The Peace of Augsburg of 1555 had allowed each prince of the Holy Roman Empire to choose Lutheranism or Catholicism for his territory, but excluded Calvinism, and tensions grew. Protestant states formed the Protestant Union in 1608, and Catholic states the Catholic League in 1609. The Habsburg emperors, Catholic, sought to strengthen their authority.

## The Defenestration of Prague

In 1617 Ferdinand of Styria, a devout Catholic, became king of Bohemia, where many nobles were Protestant. When he restricted Protestant rights, on 23 May 1618 Protestant nobles threw two of his officials and a secretary out of a window of Prague Castle, the Defenestration of Prague; they survived, landing on a dung heap according to one account. The Bohemian estates rebelled and in 1619 elected the Calvinist Frederick V of the Palatinate as king. Ferdinand became Emperor Ferdinand II. On 8 November 1620, the imperial and Catholic League army defeated the Bohemians at White Mountain, near Prague. Frederick, the "Winter King", fled. Bohemia was re-Catholicised, and many Protestants left.

## Danish and Swedish phases

King Christian IV of Denmark intervened for the Protestants in 1625 but was defeated by the imperial general Albrecht von Wallenstein, a Bohemian noble who raised a huge army financed by levies on occupied territories, and the Catholic League's general Tilly. In 1629 the emperor's Edict of Restitution ordered the return of church lands taken by Protestants since 1552. In 1630 King Gustavus Adolphus of Sweden landed in Germany with a modern, disciplined army. In 1631 Tilly's army sacked Magdeburg, killing about 20,000 people. Gustavus defeated Tilly at Breitenfeld in 1631 and advanced into southern Germany, but was killed at Lützen in 1632. Wallenstein, suspected of treason, was assassinated on the emperor's orders in 1634. The Swedes were defeated at Nördlingen in 1634, and in 1635 the Peace of Prague reconciled most German Protestant states with the emperor.

## France joins

Catholic France, under Cardinal Richelieu, had funded Sweden and the Protestants against the Habsburgs, its rivals. In 1635 France declared war on Spain and entered the conflict directly, making it a struggle between France and the Habsburgs. The French won at Rocroi in 1643.

## Devastation

Armies, often unpaid, lived off the land, looting, burning and killing. Famine and plague followed. Some regions of Germany lost a third or more of their population. The war was depicted in the novel Simplicius Simplicissimus and in Jacques Callot's etchings The Miseries of War.

## The Peace of Westphalia

Peace negotiations at Münster and Osnabrück produced the Peace of Westphalia in 1648. It recognised Calvinism, gave German princes greater autonomy, recognised the independence of the Dutch Republic and Switzerland, and gave territories to Sweden and France. It is often seen as establishing the principle of state sovereignty in international relations, though historians debate this.
