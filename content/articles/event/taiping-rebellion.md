The Taiping Rebellion was a massive civil war in China from 1850 to 1864, between the Qing dynasty and the Taiping Heavenly Kingdom, a movement led by Hong Xiuquan, who believed he was the younger brother of Jesus Christ. One of the deadliest conflicts in history, it killed an estimated 20 to 30 million people and seriously weakened the Qing dynasty.

## Hong Xiuquan

Hong Xiuquan was born in 1814 in Guangdong province, into a Hakka farming family. He repeatedly failed the imperial civil-service examinations. After a failure in 1837, he fell ill and had visions of a heavenly father and elder brother. Years later, reading Christian pamphlets by a Chinese convert, he concluded that the father was God and the brother Jesus, and that he himself was the younger son of God, sent to rid China of demons, including the Manchu rulers of the Qing dynasty. He briefly studied with an American Baptist missionary, Issachar Roberts.

## The God Worshipping Society

Hong and his followers founded the God Worshipping Society in Guangxi province, attracting poor peasants, miners, Hakka people and others suffering from poverty, famine and ethnic conflict. In January 1851, after clashes with Qing forces, Hong proclaimed the Taiping Tianguo, the Heavenly Kingdom of Great Peace, with himself as Heavenly King.

## Nanjing

The Taiping army, highly disciplined and motivated, marched north through Hunan and Hubei, growing to hundreds of thousands, and in March 1853 took Nanjing, which became their capital, Tianjing, "Heavenly Capital". A northern expedition to take Beijing failed.

## The Heavenly Kingdom

The Taiping promoted a radical programme: strict separation of men and women, bans on opium, alcohol, tobacco, gambling, foot-binding and prostitution, common ownership of property and land redistribution, though these were not consistently applied. Women served as soldiers and officials. They destroyed Buddhist and Confucian temples and idols. Their leaders, however, lived in luxury. In 1856 a power struggle among the leaders led to the murder of the East King, Yang Xiuqing, who claimed to speak for God, and thousands of his followers. Hong withdrew from government. His cousin, Hong Rengan, proposed modernising reforms.

## The Qing response

The Qing regular armies, the Banners and Green Standard Army, proved ineffective. Officials such as Zeng Guofan, a Confucian scholar from Hunan, raised regional armies, such as the Xiang Army, funded by local taxes. Li Hongzhang raised the Huai Army. After the Second Opium War, Western powers, which had initially been neutral, supported the Qing, and the Ever-Victorious Army, a force of Chinese soldiers led by Western officers, such as the American Frederick Townsend Ward and later the British Charles Gordon, fought the Taiping near Shanghai.

## The fall of Nanjing

Qing forces besieged Nanjing. Hong Xiuquan died in June 1864, probably from illness. In July 1864, Zeng Guofan's army took Nanjing and massacred much of the population. Remaining Taiping forces fought on for several years.

## Consequences

The war devastated the rich Yangtze region, killing tens of millions through fighting, famine and disease. Other rebellions, such as the Nian in the north and Muslim rebellions in the north-west and Yunnan, broke out at the same time. The rise of regional armies and their leaders weakened central authority, and the dynasty was further weakened. Sun Yat-sen and Mao Zedong both admired the Taiping as revolutionaries.
