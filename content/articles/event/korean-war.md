The Korean War was fought from 1950 to 1953 between North Korea, supported by China and the Soviet Union, and South Korea, supported by a United Nations force led by the United States. It began when North Korea invaded the South, and ended in an armistice that left Korea divided, with no peace treaty. It was one of the first major conflicts of the Cold War.

## A divided Korea

Korea had been a Japanese colony from 1910 to 1945. When Japan surrendered in August 1945, the Soviet Union and the United States divided Korea at the 38th parallel, with Soviet forces in the north and American forces in the south. Plans for reunification failed, and in 1948 two states were formed: the Republic of Korea in the south, under Syngman Rhee, and the Democratic People's Republic of Korea in the north, under Kim Il Sung. Both claimed the whole peninsula, and border clashes were frequent.

## The invasion

With Stalin's approval and Mao Zedong's support, North Korea invaded the South on 25 June 1950. Its army, equipped with Soviet tanks, quickly took Seoul and pushed the South Korean and American forces back to a small area around the port of Busan, the Pusan Perimeter. The United Nations Security Council, which the Soviet Union was boycotting, called on member states to help South Korea. Sixteen countries sent troops, though the United States provided most.

## Inchon

On 15 September 1950, General Douglas MacArthur, the UN commander, launched a daring amphibious landing at Inchon, near Seoul, behind North Korean lines. The North Korean army collapsed. UN forces retook Seoul and crossed the 38th parallel, advancing towards the Yalu river, the border with China, intending to unify Korea.

## China intervenes

China, warning that it would not tolerate American troops on its border, sent hundreds of thousands of soldiers, the Chinese People's Volunteers, across the Yalu in October 1950. In November and December, they drove UN forces back in brutal winter conditions, as at the Chosin Reservoir. Chinese and North Korean forces retook Seoul in January 1951. UN forces counter-attacked and retook Seoul in March 1951. In April President Harry Truman dismissed MacArthur, who had publicly called for attacking China, possibly with nuclear weapons.

## Stalemate

From mid-1951, the front stabilised near the 38th parallel, and the war became one of trenches and costly battles for hills, such as Heartbreak Ridge and Pork Chop Hill, while armistice negotiations dragged on for two years, mainly over the repatriation of prisoners of war, many of whom did not want to return to communist countries. American bombing devastated North Korea, destroying most of its cities and dams.

## Armistice

The armistice was signed at Panmunjom on 27 July 1953, after Stalin's death. It created a Demilitarised Zone, about four kilometres wide, along the front line. South Korea did not sign it. No peace treaty has ever been signed.

## Cost

About three million people died, a large proportion of them civilians, including about 1.5 million or more in North Korea, about 1 million in the South, around 180,000 or more Chinese soldiers and about 36,000 Americans. Millions of Koreans were displaced, and families were separated. Both sides committed atrocities, such as massacres of suspected political opponents. Korea remains divided, and the war strengthened Cold War divisions in Asia.
