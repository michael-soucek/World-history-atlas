The Spanish conquest of the Inca Empire was the campaign, beginning in 1532, in which a small force of Spanish conquistadors led by Francisco Pizarro captured the Inca ruler Atahualpa and took control of the largest empire in the pre-Columbian Americas. Resistance continued for decades, until the fall of the last Inca stronghold, Vilcabamba, in 1572.

## The Inca Empire

The Inca Empire, Tawantinsuyu, stretched along the Andes from today's Ecuador to Chile, with perhaps ten million people, connected by roads and governed from Cusco. Around 1527 the Inca ruler Huayna Capac died, probably of smallpox, which had spread from the Spanish colonies ahead of the Spaniards themselves, along with his heir. His sons Huáscar, based in Cusco, and Atahualpa, based in Quito, fought a civil war. In 1532 Atahualpa's generals defeated and captured Huáscar.

## Pizarro

Francisco Pizarro, an illiterate soldier from Extremadura, in Spain, had taken part in the conquest of Panama. With his partner Diego de Almagro, he made two expeditions down the Pacific coast in the 1520s and learned of the rich Inca Empire. In 1529 he obtained permission from Charles V to conquer Peru. In 1532 he set out with about 168 men, including 62 horsemen, and his brothers.

## Cajamarca

In November 1532, Pizarro reached the town of Cajamarca, where Atahualpa was camped with a large army after his victory. On 16 November, Atahualpa entered the town square with thousands of unarmed attendants. A Spanish friar, Vicente de Valverde, presented him with a Bible and demanded that he accept Christianity and submit to the Spanish king; when Atahualpa threw the book down, the Spaniards, hidden in buildings around the square, attacked with cannon, firearms and cavalry, killing thousands and capturing Atahualpa.

## The ransom

Atahualpa offered a ransom for his release: to fill a room once with gold and twice with silver. Over the following months, gold and silver objects were brought from across the empire and melted down. From captivity, Atahualpa ordered Huáscar's execution. Despite the ransom, the Spaniards tried Atahualpa on charges including plotting against them, and executed him by garrotte on 26 July 1533, an act criticised even in Spain.

## Cusco

Pizarro installed a puppet Inca, Túpac Huallpa, and then Manco Inca, a son of Huayna Capac, and took Cusco in November 1533, looting its temples and palaces. Indigenous allies, such as peoples who had resented Inca rule and supporters of Huáscar, helped the Spaniards. In 1535 Pizarro founded Lima on the coast.

## Resistance

Mistreated by the Spaniards, Manco Inca escaped and in 1536 led a great rebellion, besieging Cusco for months with a large army and attacking Lima. The rebellion failed, and Manco withdrew to Vilcabamba, in the remote forested mountains, where a neo-Inca state survived. Manco was killed in 1544 by Spaniards he had sheltered.

## Civil wars

The conquistadors fought each other. Almagro was executed by the Pizarros in 1538, and his supporters assassinated Francisco Pizarro in Lima in 1541. Spain created the Viceroyalty of Peru in 1542, and Gonzalo Pizarro's rebellion was crushed in 1548.

## The end

In 1572 the viceroy Francisco de Toledo sent an expedition that took Vilcabamba and captured the last Inca ruler, Túpac Amaru, who was executed in Cusco. Epidemics, war, forced labour and social disruption caused a catastrophic decline of the Andean population.
