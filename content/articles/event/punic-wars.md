The Punic Wars were three wars fought between Rome and Carthage from 264 to 146 BCE. Through them, Rome became the dominant power of the western Mediterranean, and Carthage was destroyed. The name comes from the Latin Punicus, meaning Phoenician, as Carthage had been founded by Phoenicians.

## Carthage

Carthage, in today's Tunisia, was founded by Phoenicians from Tyre, according to tradition in 814 BCE. It became the centre of a trading empire with territories in North Africa, Sicily, Sardinia and Spain, with a powerful navy. Rome, by the 260s BCE, controlled most of the Italian peninsula.

## The First Punic War

The First Punic War, from 264 to 241 BCE, was fought mainly over Sicily. It began when both powers intervened in a conflict at Messana. Rome, which had little experience at sea, built fleets, using a boarding bridge, the corvus, to turn sea battles into infantry fights, and won a naval victory at Mylae in 260 BCE. A Roman invasion of Africa, led by Regulus, was defeated in 255 BCE. Both sides lost hundreds of ships in battles and storms. The Carthaginian general Hamilcar Barca held out in Sicily. Rome won a final naval victory at the Aegates Islands in 241 BCE. Carthage gave up Sicily, which became Rome's first province, and paid an indemnity. Rome later seized Sardinia and Corsica.

## The Second Punic War

Carthage, led by the Barca family, built a new empire in Spain. In 218 BCE Hamilcar's son Hannibal marched from Spain across the Pyrenees and the Alps into Italy, with an army and elephants. He defeated the Romans at the Trebia, Lake Trasimene and, in 216 BCE, Cannae, where he encircled and destroyed a Roman army of over 50,000 men. Many southern Italian cities joined him, but Rome refused to surrender and most of its allies remained loyal. Under Fabius Maximus, the Romans avoided battle and wore Hannibal down. Hannibal remained in Italy for fifteen years. Meanwhile, Publius Cornelius Scipio conquered Carthaginian Spain and in 204 BCE invaded Africa. Hannibal was recalled and defeated by Scipio at Zama in 202 BCE. Carthage gave up Spain and its fleet, paid a large indemnity and could not wage war without Rome's permission.

## The Third Punic War

Carthage recovered economically, which alarmed some Romans. The senator Cato the Elder ended his speeches with the words "Carthage must be destroyed". When Carthage fought Numidia, Rome's ally, in self-defence in 150 BCE, Rome declared war. Rome demanded that the Carthaginians abandon their city and move inland; they refused. After a three-year siege, Scipio Aemilianus took Carthage in 146 BCE. The city was destroyed, and its surviving inhabitants, about 50,000, were enslaved. The territory became the Roman province of Africa. The story that the Romans sowed the land with salt is a later invention.

## Consequences

The Punic Wars made Rome a Mediterranean empire, with provinces in Sicily, Spain and Africa. In the same year, 146 BCE, Rome also destroyed Corinth in Greece. The wealth and slaves from conquest transformed Roman society, contributing to the tensions that led to the end of the Republic. Julius Caesar later founded a Roman colony at Carthage, which became one of the largest cities of the empire.
