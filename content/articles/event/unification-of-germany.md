The unification of Germany was the process by which the German states, which had been divided into dozens of separate states, were united into the German Empire on 18 January 1871, under the leadership of Prussia. It was achieved largely by the Prussian minister-president Otto von Bismarck through three wars, and it created a new great power in the centre of Europe.

## A divided land

For centuries, the German lands were part of the Holy Roman Empire, a loose collection of hundreds of states. Napoleon dissolved the empire in 1806. The Congress of Vienna in 1815 created the German Confederation, a loose association of 39 states, dominated by Austria and Prussia. The Zollverein, a customs union led by Prussia, founded in 1834, excluded Austria and economically united most of the German states. Nationalism grew, promoted by writers, students and gymnastic and singing societies.

## 1848

In the revolutions of 1848, liberals and nationalists convened the Frankfurt Parliament, an elected assembly that drafted a constitution for a united Germany and offered the crown to King Frederick William IV of Prussia in 1849. He refused a "crown from the gutter", and the revolutions were suppressed.

## Bismarck

In 1862 King Wilhelm I appointed Otto von Bismarck as minister-president of Prussia. Bismarck declared that the great questions of the day would be decided by "iron and blood". He strengthened the army, under Helmuth von Moltke and Albrecht von Roon.

## Three wars

In 1864 Prussia and Austria defeated Denmark over the duchies of Schleswig and Holstein. In 1866 Bismarck provoked war with Austria; in the Seven Weeks' War, Prussia, using railways and the breech-loading needle gun, defeated Austria at Königgrätz. Austria was excluded from Germany. Prussia annexed Hanover, Hesse-Kassel, Nassau and Frankfurt and formed the North German Confederation. In 1870 a dispute over a Hohenzollern candidate for the Spanish throne led Bismarck to edit a telegram from the king at Ems to make it appear insulting to France, which declared war. The southern German states, Bavaria, Württemberg and Baden, joined Prussia. The Germans defeated the French at Sedan on 2 September 1870, capturing Emperor Napoleon III, and besieged Paris.

## The empire

On 18 January 1871, in the Hall of Mirrors at Versailles, Wilhelm I was proclaimed German emperor. Bismarck became chancellor. The new empire was a federation of 25 states, dominated by Prussia, with a parliament, the Reichstag, elected by universal male suffrage, but with power concentrated in the emperor and chancellor. Germany annexed Alsace-Lorraine from France, which caused lasting French resentment.

## Kleindeutschland

The unification created a "small Germany", Kleindeutschland, excluding Austria, rather than a "greater Germany" including it. Germany became the leading industrial power of continental Europe. Its rise changed the European balance of power, contributing to tensions that led to the First World War. Germany was later divided after the Second World War and reunified in 1990.
