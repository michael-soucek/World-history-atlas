The Napoleonic Wars were a series of major conflicts, from 1803 to 1815, between the French Empire of Napoleon Bonaparte and its allies and shifting coalitions of European powers, led by Britain. Napoleon's armies dominated much of the continent, but defeats in Spain and Russia led to his downfall. The wars spread the ideas and reforms of the French Revolution and reshaped the map of Europe.

## Background

The French Revolutionary Wars had begun in 1792. Napoleon, who seized power in 1799, made peace with Austria and Britain by 1802. But the Peace of Amiens with Britain broke down in May 1803, over French expansion and British refusal to leave Malta. Napoleon prepared to invade Britain, gathering an army at Boulogne, and in 1804 crowned himself emperor.

## The Third Coalition

In 1805 Britain, Austria, Russia and Sweden formed the Third Coalition. On 21 October 1805, the British fleet under Admiral Horatio Nelson destroyed the French and Spanish fleets at Trafalgar, off Spain, securing British naval supremacy for a century; Nelson was killed. But on land, Napoleon marched his Grande Armée from the Channel to central Europe, captured an Austrian army at Ulm and, on 2 December 1805, crushed the Austrians and Russians at Austerlitz, his greatest victory. Austria made peace. In 1806 Napoleon dissolved the Holy Roman Empire and created the Confederation of the Rhine.

## Prussia and Russia

In 1806 Prussia went to war and was crushed at Jena and Auerstedt. Napoleon entered Berlin. After a bloody draw with the Russians at Eylau, he defeated them at Friedland in 1807. At Tilsit, on a raft in the Niemen river, Napoleon and Tsar Alexander I made peace. Napoleon created the Duchy of Warsaw. He imposed the Continental System, forbidding trade with Britain, to ruin its economy.

## The Peninsular War

In 1807–1808 Napoleon invaded Portugal and placed his brother Joseph on the throne of Spain. The Spanish rose against the French, beginning a long and brutal war, in which guerrillas harassed French forces, and a British army under Arthur Wellesley, later the Duke of Wellington, fought in Portugal and Spain. The "Spanish ulcer" tied down hundreds of thousands of French troops. Goya's paintings depict its horrors.

## Austria again

Austria went to war in 1809 and was defeated at Wagram. Napoleon married the Austrian archduchess Marie Louise in 1810. His empire reached its greatest extent, with his relatives ruling Spain, Naples, Holland and Westphalia.

## Russia

Russia withdrew from the Continental System. In June 1812, Napoleon invaded Russia with the largest army ever assembled in Europe, over 600,000 men. The Russians retreated, burning crops. After the bloody Battle of Borodino, Napoleon took Moscow, but the city was set on fire, and Alexander refused to negotiate. The retreat in winter, harassed by Cossacks, destroyed the Grande Armée; perhaps fewer than 100,000 returned.

## The fall of Napoleon

Prussia, Austria, Russia, Sweden and Britain formed the Sixth Coalition and defeated Napoleon at Leipzig in October 1813, the "Battle of the Nations". The allies entered Paris in March 1814, and Napoleon abdicated and was exiled to Elba. He escaped in March 1815, returned to power for the Hundred Days, and was defeated at Waterloo on 18 June 1815 by Wellington and the Prussian Blücher. He was exiled to Saint Helena.

## Legacy

The wars killed several million soldiers and civilians. The Congress of Vienna redrew Europe's map. Napoleon's legal code and reforms spread across Europe, and the wars stimulated nationalism in Germany, Italy, Spain and elsewhere. Spain's weakness contributed to the independence of its American colonies.
