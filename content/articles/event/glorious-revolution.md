The Glorious Revolution was the overthrow of King James II of England, Scotland and Ireland in 1688 by a union of English Parliamentarians with the Dutch prince William of Orange, who invaded England and became King William III, ruling jointly with his wife, Mary II, James's daughter. It established the supremacy of Parliament, and the Bill of Rights of 1689 became a foundation of British constitutional government.

## James II

James II became king in 1685, succeeding his brother Charles II. He was a Catholic in a largely Protestant country, where many feared Catholicism, associated with absolute monarchy, as in Louis XIV's France. James crushed a rebellion led by the Duke of Monmouth in 1685, and many rebels were executed in the "Bloody Assizes". He appointed Catholics to positions in the army, government and universities, using his power to dispense with laws that excluded them, and kept a large standing army. In 1687 he issued a Declaration of Indulgence, granting religious freedom to Catholics and Protestant dissenters. When seven bishops petitioned against it, he had them tried for seditious libel; they were acquitted in June 1688, to public celebration.

## The heir

James's heirs had been his Protestant daughters, Mary, married to William of Orange, the ruler of the Dutch Republic, and Anne. But on 10 June 1688, James's second wife, the Catholic Mary of Modena, gave birth to a son, James Francis Edward Stuart, raising the prospect of a Catholic dynasty. Rumours spread that the baby had been smuggled into the queen's bed in a warming pan.

## The invitation

On 30 June 1688, seven prominent English politicians, the "Immortal Seven", invited William to come to England with an army. William, who was at war with Louis XIV, wanted to bring England's resources into the struggle against France.

## The invasion

On 5 November 1688, William landed at Torbay, in Devon, with a large army of about 20,000 men, carried on hundreds of ships. James's army, though larger, suffered desertions, including that of his general John Churchill, later Duke of Marlborough, and his daughter Anne. James lost his nerve, sent his wife and son to France and tried to flee in December, throwing the Great Seal into the Thames. He was caught but allowed to escape to France.

## The settlement

A Convention Parliament declared that James had abdicated by fleeing, and offered the crown jointly to William and Mary in February 1689. They accepted the Declaration of Rights, enacted as the Bill of Rights in December 1689, which declared that the king could not suspend laws, levy taxes or keep a standing army in peacetime without Parliament's consent, required free elections and freedom of speech in Parliament, and barred Catholics from the throne. The Toleration Act of 1689 gave freedom of worship to Protestant dissenters, but not Catholics.

## Scotland and Ireland

The revolution was not bloodless outside England. In Scotland, Jacobites, supporters of James, rose and won at Killiecrankie in 1689 but were defeated. In Ireland, James landed with French support, and the Catholic majority supported him, but William defeated him at the Battle of the Boyne on 1 July 1690. Protestant ascendancy and penal laws against Catholics followed in Ireland, a legacy still commemorated and contested.

## Legacy

Whig historians presented the revolution as the triumph of liberty and parliamentary government, an interpretation now seen as too simple. The revolution also brought England into wars against France, and the Bank of England was founded in 1694.
