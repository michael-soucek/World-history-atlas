The First Opium War was fought between Britain and Qing China from 1839 to 1842. It began when China tried to stop the illegal British trade in opium and ended with China's defeat, the Treaty of Nanking, the opening of Chinese ports and the cession of Hong Kong. In China it is seen as the beginning of a "century of humiliation".

## The trade

In the eighteenth century, British demand for Chinese tea, silk and porcelain was huge, but China wanted few British goods, so Britain paid in silver. China restricted foreign trade to the port of Canton, Guangzhou, through licensed merchants, the Cohong, under the Canton System. To reverse the flow of silver, the British East India Company grew opium in India, especially Bengal, and sold it to private merchants, who smuggled it into China. Opium imports rose rapidly, from about 4,000 chests a year around 1800 to about 40,000 by 1838. Addiction spread, and silver flowed out of China.

## Lin Zexu

The Daoguang emperor banned opium and sent the official Lin Zexu to Canton in 1839 to stop the trade. Lin demanded that foreign merchants surrender their opium, blockaded them in their factories and, in June 1839, destroyed over 20,000 chests, about 1,200 tonnes, mixing it with lime and salt and washing it into the sea. He wrote a letter to Queen Victoria, asking how Britain would feel if another country sold opium in England.

## War

Tensions grew after the killing of a Chinese villager by British sailors. In 1840 the British government, pressed by merchants such as William Jardine, sent a naval expedition. The Royal Navy's steam-powered warships, such as the Nemesis, and modern guns overwhelmed Chinese forces. In Britain, the young William Gladstone denounced the war in Parliament as unjust. The British occupied Chusan, blockaded ports and took forts near Canton. Lin was dismissed. Negotiations failed, and in 1841–1842 the British took Amoy, Ningbo, Shanghai and Zhenjiang, cutting the Grand Canal, and threatened Nanjing.

## The Treaty of Nanking

On 29 August 1842 China signed the Treaty of Nanking, the first of the "unequal treaties". China ceded Hong Kong Island to Britain, opened five ports, Canton, Amoy, Fuzhou, Ningbo and Shanghai, to British trade and residence, abolished the Cohong monopoly and paid an indemnity of 21 million silver dollars. A supplementary treaty in 1843 gave British subjects extraterritoriality, exemption from Chinese law, and most-favoured-nation status. The United States and France obtained similar treaties in 1844. Opium was not mentioned; the trade continued.

## Consequences

The war exposed the military weakness of the Qing dynasty and opened China to Western influence and pressure. Shanghai grew into a major international port. The Second Opium War, from 1856 to 1860, in which British and French forces took Beijing and burned the Summer Palace, forced further concessions, including the legalisation of opium. The humiliations contributed to rebellions, such as the Taiping, and to the decline of the Qing dynasty. Hong Kong remained British until 1997.
