Apollo 11 was the first crewed mission to land on the moon. On 20 July 1969, the American astronauts Neil Armstrong and Buzz Aldrin landed in the lunar module Eagle, and Armstrong became the first person to step onto the moon's surface, while Michael Collins orbited above in the command module Columbia. They returned safely to earth on 24 July.

## The space race

The Soviet Union launched Sputnik, the first artificial satellite, in 1957, and Yuri Gagarin became the first human in space in April 1961, alarming the United States during the Cold War. In May 1961 President John F. Kennedy set the goal of "landing a man on the moon and returning him safely to the earth" before the end of the decade. NASA's Project Mercury and Project Gemini developed the techniques needed, such as spacewalks and docking.

## The Apollo programme

The Apollo programme employed about 400,000 people and cost about 25 billion dollars at the time. In January 1967 a fire during a test killed the three astronauts of Apollo 1. Apollo 8 orbited the moon in December 1968, taking the famous Earthrise photograph. Apollo 9 and 10 tested the lunar module.

## The crew

Neil Armstrong, the commander, was a former navy pilot and test pilot who had flown the X-15 rocket plane and Gemini 8. Edwin "Buzz" Aldrin, the lunar module pilot, was an air force pilot with a doctorate in astronautics who had made a record spacewalk on Gemini 12. Michael Collins, the command module pilot, had flown on Gemini 10.

## Launch

On 16 July 1969, Apollo 11 was launched from Kennedy Space Center in Florida on a Saturn V rocket, the most powerful rocket ever flown at the time, watched by about a million people nearby and a huge television audience. After three days, the spacecraft entered lunar orbit.

## The landing

On 20 July, Armstrong and Aldrin entered Eagle, separated from Columbia and descended. During the descent, computer alarms went off, overloaded with data, but mission control decided to continue. Armstrong saw that the computer was guiding them towards a field of boulders and took manual control, landing with only about 25 seconds of fuel left before an abort. He radioed: "Houston, Tranquility Base here. The Eagle has landed." They had landed in the Sea of Tranquility.

## On the moon

About six and a half hours later, Armstrong climbed down the ladder and stepped onto the surface, saying "That's one small step for man, one giant leap for mankind" (he said he meant "for a man"). About 600 million people watched on television. Aldrin joined him. They spent about two and a half hours outside, planting an American flag, setting up experiments, including a seismometer and a laser reflector still used today, collecting 21.5 kilograms of rock and soil, and taking photographs. They left a plaque reading "We came in peace for all mankind" and a patch commemorating the Apollo 1 crew.

## Return

Eagle lifted off and docked with Columbia, and the astronauts returned to earth, splashing down in the Pacific on 24 July 1969. They were quarantined for three weeks in case of lunar germs. They received a ticker-tape parade and went on a world tour.

## Later missions

Six more Apollo missions went to the moon, and five landed, the last, Apollo 17, in December 1972. Apollo 13 was aborted after an explosion, but its crew returned safely. In all, twelve astronauts walked on the moon.
