The Age of Discovery, or Age of Exploration, is the period from the early fifteenth century to the seventeenth century when European sailors explored the oceans, reaching Africa, Asia and the Americas by sea. It connected the continents as never before, beginning global trade and European colonial empires, with enormous consequences, many of them devastating, for the peoples encountered.

## Motives

Europeans sought direct access to the spices, silk and other goods of Asia, which reached Europe through Muslim and Venetian intermediaries at high prices, and to the gold of West Africa. They were also motivated by the desire to spread Christianity, to find the legendary Christian king Prester John, to fight Islam, and by curiosity and the search for glory. New ships, such as the caravel, which could sail against the wind, and navigational instruments, such as the magnetic compass, the astrolabe and the quadrant, together with improved maps, made long voyages possible.

## Portugal and Africa

Portugal led the way. Prince Henry, known as Henry the Navigator, sponsored voyages down the coast of Africa. Portuguese sailors reached Madeira and the Azores, and in 1434 Gil Eanes passed Cape Bojador. The Portuguese traded gold and began the Atlantic slave trade in the 1440s. In 1488 Bartolomeu Dias rounded the Cape of Good Hope, and in 1497–1499 Vasco da Gama sailed around Africa to Calicut, in India, opening a sea route to Asia. Portugal built a trading empire of fortified ports, from Goa, taken in 1510, to Malacca and Macau.

## Columbus and the Americas

Christopher Columbus, a Genoese navigator sponsored by Spain, sailed west in 1492, hoping to reach Asia, and reached the Bahamas and the Caribbean. He made four voyages, believing until his death that he had reached Asia. Others, such as Amerigo Vespucci, whose name was given to the continents, recognised a "New World". In 1494 the Treaty of Tordesillas divided the newly found lands between Spain and Portugal. In 1500 Pedro Álvares Cabral reached Brazil.

## Around the world

In 1513 Vasco Núñez de Balboa crossed Panama and saw the Pacific. In 1519–1522 Ferdinand Magellan's expedition, sponsored by Spain, sailed around South America and across the Pacific; Magellan was killed in the Philippines, and Juan Sebastián Elcano completed the first circumnavigation of the globe with one ship.

## Conquest

Spanish conquistadors conquered the Aztec Empire, under Hernán Cortés, in 1519–1521, and the Inca Empire, under Francisco Pizarro, in the 1530s. Silver from Mexico and Peru flowed to Europe and Asia.

## The north

England, France and the Netherlands joined the exploration. John Cabot reached Newfoundland in 1497, Jacques Cartier explored the St Lawrence in the 1530s, and Henry Hudson and others searched for a northwest passage. The Dutch reached Australia in 1606.

## Consequences

The voyages led to the Columbian Exchange, the transfer of plants, animals and diseases between hemispheres. Smallpox and other diseases killed a large proportion of the Indigenous peoples of the Americas. The Atlantic slave trade forcibly transported about 12.5 million Africans to the Americas. Global trade networks emerged, and European powers built colonial empires that lasted for centuries. Historians today often prefer terms such as "encounter" or "contact", recognising that the lands "discovered" were already inhabited. Earlier voyages, such as those of the Norse to North America around 1000 and the Chinese treasure fleets of Zheng He in the Indian Ocean in 1405–1433, had not led to lasting connections of this kind.
