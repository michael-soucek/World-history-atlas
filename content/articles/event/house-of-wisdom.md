The House of Wisdom, Bayt al-Hikma, was a major intellectual centre in Baghdad during the Abbasid Caliphate, in the ninth century. Associated with the caliphs Harun al-Rashid and especially al-Ma'mun, it was a library and a centre for translating Greek, Persian and Indian works into Arabic, and its scholars contributed to mathematics, astronomy, medicine and philosophy during the Islamic Golden Age.

## What it was

The exact nature of the House of Wisdom is debated. It probably began as a palace library under Harun al-Rashid, who ruled from 786 to 809, or even earlier, under al-Mansur, modelled on the libraries of the Sasanian Persian kings. Under al-Ma'mun, who ruled from 813 to 833, it became more important, associated with scholars, translators and astronomical observations. Some historians caution that its role has been exaggerated in modern accounts, and that much of the translation and research of the period took place outside it, sponsored by wealthy patrons.

## The translation movement

From the late eighth to the tenth century, a huge translation movement in Baghdad turned many works of Greek philosophy, science and medicine, including Aristotle, Plato, Euclid, Ptolemy, Galen and Hippocrates, into Arabic, often through Syriac. Persian and Indian works on astronomy, mathematics and literature were also translated. The availability of paper, made in Baghdad from the late eighth century, helped spread books. Al-Ma'mun reportedly sent envoys to the Byzantine Empire to obtain Greek manuscripts.

## Hunayn ibn Ishaq

The most famous translator was Hunayn ibn Ishaq, a Nestorian Christian physician, who lived from 809 to 873. With his son and nephew, he translated many works of Galen and Hippocrates and others, carefully comparing manuscripts. He also wrote original works on ophthalmology. Many translators were Christians, Jews and Sabians, as well as Muslims.

## Mathematics

The Persian mathematician Muhammad ibn Musa al-Khwarizmi worked in Baghdad under al-Ma'mun. His book on calculation by completion and balancing, al-jabr, gave its name to algebra, and his name gave us the word "algorithm". His work on Indian numerals helped spread the Hindu-Arabic numeral system, including zero, to the Islamic world and later Europe. The Banu Musa brothers wrote on mechanics, describing ingenious devices.

## Astronomy

Al-Ma'mun sponsored observatories in Baghdad and Damascus, where astronomers made observations to correct Ptolemy's tables. His astronomers measured a degree of latitude in the Syrian desert to calculate the earth's circumference, obtaining a result close to the true value.

## Philosophy

Al-Kindi, called "the philosopher of the Arabs", who worked in Baghdad in the ninth century, sought to reconcile Greek philosophy with Islam and wrote on mathematics, music, optics and cryptography, including the first known description of frequency analysis to break codes.

## The end

The translation movement declined in the tenth century, by which time most important Greek texts had been translated, and scholarship spread to other centres, such as Cairo, Córdoba and Bukhara. According to accounts, when the Mongols sacked Baghdad in 1258, they threw so many books into the Tigris that the river ran black with ink, though the House of Wisdom as an institution may no longer have existed by then.

## Legacy

The works translated and developed in Baghdad were later translated into Latin, especially in twelfth-century Spain and Sicily, contributing to the revival of learning in Europe.
