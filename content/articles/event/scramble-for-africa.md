The Scramble for Africa was the rapid invasion, occupation and colonisation of most of Africa by European powers between about 1880 and 1914. In 1870 Europeans controlled about 10 percent of Africa, mostly near the coast; by 1914 they controlled about 90 percent, with only Ethiopia and Liberia remaining independent.

## Causes

Several factors drove the Scramble. European industrial economies sought raw materials, such as rubber, palm oil, cotton, copper and gold, and markets. Rivalry between European powers, especially after the unification of Germany and Italy, made colonies a matter of national prestige. Explorers, such as David Livingstone and Henry Morton Stanley, and missionaries publicised Africa's interior, and Europeans justified colonisation with claims of a "civilising mission", ending the slave trade and racist ideas. Medical advances, such as quinine against malaria, steamships, railways and weapons, such as the Maxim machine gun, gave Europeans the means to conquer. Hilaire Belloc wrote: "Whatever happens, we have got the Maxim gun, and they have not."

## Triggers

In 1881 France occupied Tunisia, and in 1882 Britain occupied Egypt, to control the Suez Canal. King Leopold II of Belgium, through a private association, employed Stanley to acquire territory in the Congo basin. France, Portugal and Germany staked rival claims.

## The Berlin Conference

In 1884–1885, the German chancellor Otto von Bismarck hosted the Berlin Conference, attended by European powers and the United States, but no Africans. It set rules for claiming African territory, requiring "effective occupation", and recognised Leopold's Congo Free State as his personal possession. In the following years, Europeans signed treaties with African rulers, often misleadingly, and drew borders on maps, often with little knowledge of the land and its peoples.

## Conquest

Africans resisted. The Zulu defeated the British at Isandlwana in 1879 before being conquered. Samori Touré fought the French in West Africa for nearly two decades until 1898. The Asante fought the British several times. In 1896 the Ethiopians under Emperor Menelik II defeated the Italians at Adwa, preserving Ethiopia's independence. In Sudan, the Mahdist state was destroyed by the British at Omdurman in 1898, where machine guns killed about 11,000 Sudanese. In German South West Africa, today's Namibia, the German army's campaign against the Herero and Nama, from 1904 to 1908, killed tens of thousands, in what is considered the first genocide of the twentieth century. The Maji Maji rising in German East Africa, from 1905 to 1907, was crushed with famine tactics.

## The Congo Free State

In Leopold's Congo Free State, forced labour to collect rubber, enforced with hostage-taking, mutilation and killing, caused millions of deaths. International campaigns, led by Edmund Morel and Roger Casement, forced Belgium to take over the colony from Leopold in 1908.

## Rivalries

European rivalries nearly led to war, as at Fashoda in Sudan in 1898 between Britain and France, and in the Moroccan crises of 1905 and 1911 between France and Germany. The British dreamed of a Cape-to-Cairo railway; the Boer War, from 1899 to 1902, gave Britain control of South Africa.

## Legacy

Colonial borders, economies built around exporting raw materials, and administrative systems shaped the African states that became independent mainly in the 1950s to 1970s.
