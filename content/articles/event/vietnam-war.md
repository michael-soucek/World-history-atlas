The Vietnam War was fought from 1955 to 1975 between communist North Vietnam, supported by the Soviet Union and China, and South Vietnam, supported by the United States and its allies. It ended with the fall of Saigon and the reunification of Vietnam under communist rule. It killed millions of people and deeply divided American society.

## Background

Vietnam was part of French Indochina from the late nineteenth century. During the Second World War, Japan occupied it, and the Viet Minh, a communist-led independence movement under Ho Chi Minh, fought the Japanese. In September 1945 Ho declared Vietnam's independence. France tried to restore its rule, and the First Indochina War followed, ending with the French defeat at Điện Biên Phủ in 1954. The Geneva Accords divided Vietnam temporarily at the 17th parallel, with the communist North and the anti-communist South, and called for national elections in 1956. The South's leader, Ngô Đình Diệm, supported by the United States, refused to hold them.

## Insurgency

From the late 1950s, communist guerrillas in the South, called the Viet Cong by their enemies, began an insurgency, supported by the North through the Ho Chi Minh Trail, a network of routes through Laos and Cambodia. The United States, fearing that the fall of South Vietnam would cause other countries to fall to communism, the "domino theory", sent military advisers. Diệm's government was unpopular, especially among Buddhists, and in 1963 he was overthrown and killed in a coup.

## American escalation

In August 1964, after reported attacks on American destroyers in the Gulf of Tonkin, Congress passed a resolution allowing President Lyndon Johnson to use military force. In 1965 the United States began bombing North Vietnam, in Operation Rolling Thunder, and sent combat troops. By 1968 there were over 500,000 American troops in Vietnam. The war was fought in jungles, villages and rice fields, with "search and destroy" missions, helicopters, napalm and defoliants, such as Agent Orange, which caused lasting health effects.

## Tet

On 30 January 1968, during the Vietnamese New Year, Tết, communist forces launched surprise attacks on cities across South Vietnam, even attacking the US embassy in Saigon. The offensive was a military defeat for the communists, but it shocked American public opinion, which had been told the war was being won. In March 1968, American soldiers massacred hundreds of civilians at Mỹ Lai. Protests against the war spread in the United States and around the world.

## Withdrawal

President Richard Nixon pursued "Vietnamization", gradually withdrawing American troops while building up the South Vietnamese army, but expanded the war into Cambodia and Laos and bombed the North heavily. The Paris Peace Accords of January 1973 led to the withdrawal of American forces. Fighting between North and South continued.

## The fall of Saigon

In 1975 North Vietnam launched a final offensive. South Vietnamese forces collapsed, and on 30 April 1975 North Vietnamese tanks entered Saigon, as the last Americans were evacuated by helicopter. Vietnam was reunified in 1976, and Saigon was renamed Ho Chi Minh City.

## Cost

Estimates of the dead vary widely; perhaps between 1.3 and 3 million Vietnamese, soldiers and civilians, died, along with about 58,000 Americans and tens of thousands of other allied soldiers. Many Cambodians and Laotians died in related conflicts, and Cambodia fell to the Khmer Rouge. After the war, hundreds of thousands of Vietnamese fled by sea, the "boat people".
