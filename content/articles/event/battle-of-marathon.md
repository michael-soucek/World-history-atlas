The Battle of Marathon, fought in 490 BCE, was a victory of the Athenians and their Plataean allies over a Persian invasion force sent by King Darius I. Fought on the plain of Marathon, about 40 kilometres north-east of Athens, it was the first major Greek victory over the Persians and became a symbol of Athenian courage and democracy.

## The Ionian Revolt

In 499 BCE the Greek cities of Ionia, on the coast of Asia Minor, rebelled against Persian rule. Athens and Eretria sent ships to help, and the rebels burned Sardis, a Persian provincial capital. The Persians crushed the revolt by 494 BCE, sacking Miletus. According to Herodotus, our main source, Darius ordered a servant to remind him three times at dinner, "Master, remember the Athenians."

## The Persian expedition

In 490 BCE Darius sent a fleet across the Aegean under the commanders Datis and Artaphernes, with perhaps 20,000 to 25,000 soldiers, including cavalry. They conquered the Cycladic islands and destroyed Eretria, on Euboea, deporting its people. They then landed at the bay of Marathon, advised by Hippias, the former tyrant of Athens, who hoped to be restored.

## The Athenian response

The Athenians sent an army of about 10,000 hoplites, heavily armed citizen infantry, joined by about 1,000 from Plataea. They also sent a runner, Pheidippides, to Sparta, covering about 240 kilometres in two days, to ask for help. The Spartans agreed but said they could not march until the full moon, because of a religious festival. The Athenian army was led by ten generals, including Miltiades, who had experience fighting the Persians, and the polemarch Callimachus. For several days the two armies faced each other.

## The battle

According to Herodotus, Miltiades persuaded Callimachus to fight. The Athenians stretched their line to match the length of the Persian line, thinning the centre and strengthening the wings. They advanced, running the last stretch to reduce exposure to Persian arrows. The Persian centre, with the best troops, pushed back the Athenian centre, but the Athenian wings defeated their opponents and turned inwards, enveloping the Persians, who fled to their ships. Herodotus records that 6,400 Persians and 192 Athenians died, including Callimachus. The Athenian dead were buried in a mound, the soros, which still stands on the battlefield. The Persian cavalry, perhaps re-embarking, seems to have played no part, which may explain the timing of the attack.

## The race to Athens

The Persian fleet sailed around Cape Sounion to attack Athens, but the Athenian army marched back quickly and arrived first, and the Persians sailed home. The Spartans arrived after the battle.

## The marathon

According to a later legend, recorded by Plutarch and Lucian centuries afterwards, a messenger ran from Marathon to Athens to announce the victory, crying "We have won!" before collapsing and dying. This story inspired the marathon race, introduced at the first modern Olympic Games in Athens in 1896, now run over 42.195 kilometres.

## Significance

Marathon showed that the Persians could be beaten and gave confidence to Athens's young democracy. The playwright Aeschylus, who fought there, mentioned only his participation in the battle in his epitaph, not his plays. Darius's son Xerxes launched a much larger invasion in 480 BCE.
