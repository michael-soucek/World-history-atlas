The First World War was a global conflict fought from 1914 to 1918, mainly in Europe, between the Allied Powers, including France, Britain, Russia, Italy and, from 1917, the United States, and the Central Powers, Germany, Austria-Hungary, the Ottoman Empire and Bulgaria. About 9 to 10 million soldiers and millions of civilians died. The war destroyed four empires and reshaped the world.

## Causes

Rivalries among the great powers, an arms race, especially the naval race between Britain and Germany, nationalism, imperial competition and a system of alliances divided Europe into two blocs: the Triple Alliance of Germany, Austria-Hungary and Italy, and the Triple Entente of France, Russia and Britain. The Balkans, where Slavic nationalism challenged Austria-Hungary, were a source of tension.

## Sarajevo

On 28 June 1914, Gavrilo Princip, a Bosnian Serb nationalist, assassinated Archduke Franz Ferdinand, heir to the Austro-Hungarian throne, in Sarajevo. Austria-Hungary, with German support, issued an ultimatum to Serbia and declared war on 28 July. Russia mobilised to support Serbia, Germany declared war on Russia and France, and invaded Belgium to attack France under the Schlieffen Plan, bringing Britain into the war on 4 August.

## The Western Front

The German advance was stopped at the Marne in September 1914. Both sides dug trenches, which soon stretched from the North Sea to Switzerland. Machine guns, artillery and barbed wire made attacks extremely costly. At Verdun in 1916, the French and Germans suffered about 700,000 casualties in ten months; on the first day of the Battle of the Somme, 1 July 1916, the British suffered about 57,000 casualties. Poison gas was used from 1915, and tanks from 1916.

## Other fronts

On the Eastern Front, Germany defeated Russia at Tannenberg in 1914, and the front moved more than in the west. The Ottoman Empire joined the Central Powers in 1914. The Allied attack at Gallipoli in 1915 failed. The British fought the Ottomans in Mesopotamia and Palestine, supported by an Arab revolt. Italy joined the Allies in 1915, fighting Austria-Hungary in the Alps. The war spread to Africa and the Pacific, and colonial troops fought in Europe. During the war, the Ottoman government carried out the Armenian genocide, killing about a million Armenians.

## The war at sea

The British navy blockaded Germany, causing food shortages. Germany used submarines, U-boats, to attack shipping; the sinking of the liner Lusitania in 1915 killed nearly 1,200 people. Germany's resumption of unrestricted submarine warfare in 1917, and the Zimmermann Telegram, proposing an alliance with Mexico, brought the United States into the war in April 1917.

## The end

The Russian Revolution of 1917 led Russia to make peace at Brest-Litovsk in March 1918. Germany launched a great offensive in the west in spring 1918, but it failed. The Allies, reinforced by American troops, counter-attacked from August 1918. Germany's allies collapsed, revolution broke out in Germany, the Kaiser abdicated, and an armistice was signed on 11 November 1918.

## Consequences

The Treaty of Versailles of 1919 imposed harsh terms on Germany. The German, Austro-Hungarian, Russian and Ottoman empires collapsed, and new states, such as Poland, Czechoslovakia and Yugoslavia, emerged. The League of Nations was founded. The influenza pandemic of 1918–1919 killed tens of millions more. The war's legacy contributed to the Second World War.
