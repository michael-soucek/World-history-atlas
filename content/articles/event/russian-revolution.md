The Russian Revolution was a period of political and social revolution in Russia in 1917, which overthrew the tsarist autocracy and brought the Bolsheviks, led by Vladimir Lenin, to power. It led to a civil war and the creation of the Soviet Union, the world's first communist state, which lasted until 1991.

## Background

Russia in the early twentieth century was an autocracy ruled by Tsar Nicholas II. Most of the population were peasants, and rapid industrialisation had created a working class in cities living in harsh conditions. The revolution of 1905, triggered by Bloody Sunday, when troops fired on peaceful protesters in St Petersburg, forced the tsar to create a parliament, the Duma, but he kept most power. Revolutionary parties, such as the Socialist Revolutionaries and the Marxist Social Democrats, split into Bolsheviks and Mensheviks, were active.

## War

The First World War was a disaster for Russia. Millions of soldiers were killed, wounded or captured. Nicholas took personal command of the army in 1915, leaving the government to the Empress Alexandra, who was influenced by the mystic Grigori Rasputin, murdered by nobles in December 1916. Food and fuel shortages, inflation and military defeats caused growing discontent.

## The February Revolution

In February 1917, by the old Russian calendar, March by the Western one, strikes and bread protests in Petrograd, beginning on International Women's Day, grew into mass demonstrations. Soldiers refused to fire on the crowds and joined them. On 2 March 1917, Nicholas II abdicated, ending over three hundred years of Romanov rule. A Provisional Government, of Duma liberals and later moderate socialists, took power, while the Petrograd Soviet, a council of workers' and soldiers' deputies, held real influence: "dual power".

## The Provisional Government

The Provisional Government granted civil liberties but continued the war and postponed land reform and elections. Lenin returned from exile in April, with German help, and called for "peace, land and bread" and "all power to the soviets". After failed protests in July, the Bolsheviks were suppressed. In August, General Lavr Kornilov's attempted march on Petrograd was defeated with the help of armed workers, including the Bolsheviks, whose support grew. Alexander Kerensky led the government.

## The October Revolution

On 25 October 1917, old style, 7 November new style, the Bolsheviks, organised by Leon Trotsky through the Petrograd Soviet's Military Revolutionary Committee, seized key points in Petrograd and stormed the Winter Palace, arresting the government's ministers. The Congress of Soviets approved a new government led by Lenin, which issued decrees on peace and land. Elections to the Constituent Assembly in November gave the Socialist Revolutionaries a majority; the Bolsheviks dissolved it in January 1918.

## Civil war

In March 1918 the Bolsheviks signed the Treaty of Brest-Litovsk with Germany, giving up huge territories. A civil war followed, from 1918 to 1921, between the Bolsheviks' Red Army, organised by Trotsky, and the Whites, a varied coalition of anti-Bolshevik forces, supported by foreign interventions. Peasant armies, nationalist movements and others also fought. Both sides committed atrocities; the Bolsheviks' secret police, the Cheka, carried out the Red Terror. In July 1918, the tsar and his family were executed at Yekaterinburg. Millions died from fighting, terror, disease and famine.

## The Soviet Union

The Bolsheviks won by 1921, and the Union of Soviet Socialist Republics was formed in December 1922. The revolution inspired communist movements worldwide and shaped twentieth-century history; its interpretation remains contested.
