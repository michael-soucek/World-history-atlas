The Peloponnesian War was fought from 431 to 404 BCE between Athens and its empire, and the Peloponnesian League led by Sparta. It ended with the defeat of Athens and the end of its golden age. Its history was written by the Athenian general Thucydides, whose account is considered one of the first works of scientific history.

## Causes

After the Persian Wars, Athens led the Delian League, an alliance of Greek states, which became an Athenian empire, collecting tribute and controlling the Aegean with its navy. Sparta, a land power with the strongest army in Greece, led the Peloponnesian League. Conflicts between Athens and Sparta's ally Corinth, over Corcyra and Potidaea, and an Athenian trade embargo on Megara, led to war. Thucydides wrote that the real cause was "the growth of the power of Athens, and the alarm which this inspired in Sparta", a phrase that has been used to describe great power rivalry, the "Thucydides trap".

## The Archidamian War

Pericles, the Athenian leader, planned to avoid land battles, bring the population of Attica inside the city's Long Walls, which connected Athens to its port at Piraeus, and use the navy to raid the Peloponnese. The Spartans, under King Archidamus, invaded Attica each year, destroying crops. In 430 BCE a plague broke out in crowded Athens, killing perhaps a quarter of the population, including Pericles in 429 BCE. In 425 BCE the Athenians captured 120 Spartan citizens at Sphacteria, near Pylos, a shock to Sparta. The Spartan general Brasidas took Amphipolis, in northern Greece, in 424 BCE; Thucydides, an Athenian general, failed to save it and was exiled, which gave him time to write his history. In 421 BCE, after Brasidas and the Athenian leader Cleon were killed, the Peace of Nicias was signed.

## The Melian dialogue

In 416 BCE Athens demanded that the neutral island of Melos submit. Thucydides presents a dialogue in which the Athenians argue that "the strong do what they can and the weak suffer what they must". Melos refused, and Athens killed its men and enslaved its women and children.

## The Sicilian Expedition

In 415 BCE, persuaded by the ambitious Alcibiades, Athens sent a huge expedition to conquer Syracuse, in Sicily. Alcibiades was recalled to face charges of sacrilege and defected to Sparta. The expedition ended in disaster in 413 BCE: the Athenian fleet and army were destroyed, and thousands of prisoners died in the stone quarries of Syracuse.

## The Ionian War

Sparta occupied Decelea, in Attica, cutting Athens off from its silver mines, and with Persian money built a fleet. Many Athenian allies revolted. In 411 BCE an oligarchic coup briefly overthrew democracy in Athens. Athens won naval victories, but in 405 BCE the Spartan admiral Lysander destroyed the Athenian fleet at Aegospotami, in the Hellespont. Besieged and starving, Athens surrendered in 404 BCE. Its Long Walls were torn down, its empire dissolved, and a pro-Spartan oligarchy, the Thirty Tyrants, ruled until democracy was restored in 403 BCE.

## Consequences

The war weakened Greece. Sparta's dominance was challenged by Thebes and others, and Persia regained influence. In the fourth century, Macedon under Philip II conquered Greece. Thucydides's history, though unfinished, breaking off in 411 BCE, remains a classic study of war and power.
