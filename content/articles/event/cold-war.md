The Cold War was the period of geopolitical rivalry between the United States and the Soviet Union and their allies, from the end of the Second World War in 1945 to the dissolution of the Soviet Union in 1991. It was called "cold" because the two superpowers never fought each other directly, but it involved an arms race, proxy wars, propaganda and competition in every field, including space.

## Origins

The United States and the Soviet Union were allies against Nazi Germany, but deeply distrustful, with opposing systems: capitalist democracy and communist one-party rule. After 1945, the Soviet Union established communist governments in the countries of eastern Europe its armies had occupied. In 1946 Winston Churchill warned that an "iron curtain" had descended across Europe. In 1947 the United States adopted the Truman Doctrine, promising to support countries resisting communism, and the policy of containment. The Marshall Plan gave aid to rebuild western Europe.

## A divided Europe

Germany was divided into occupation zones. In 1948–1949, the Soviets blockaded West Berlin, and the Western allies supplied the city by air, the Berlin Airlift. In 1949 Germany was divided into the Federal Republic in the west and the German Democratic Republic in the east, and the Western allies founded NATO. The Soviet bloc formed the Warsaw Pact in 1955. In 1961 East Germany built the Berlin Wall.

## The arms race

The Soviet Union tested an atomic bomb in 1949, ending the American monopoly. Both sides developed hydrogen bombs, intercontinental ballistic missiles and nuclear submarines, building tens of thousands of nuclear weapons. The doctrine of mutually assured destruction held that neither side could launch a nuclear attack without being destroyed itself. In October 1962, the Cuban Missile Crisis, over Soviet missiles in Cuba, brought the world closest to nuclear war.

## Proxy wars

The superpowers fought through proxies. In the Korean War, from 1950 to 1953, American-led UN forces fought North Korea and China. In the Vietnam War, the United States fought North Vietnam and the Viet Cong until 1973. The Soviet Union fought in Afghanistan from 1979 to 1989 against mujahideen supported by the United States. Both sides supported governments and rebels in Africa, Latin America and Asia, and the CIA and KGB conducted covert operations and coups, such as in Iran in 1953 and Guatemala in 1954.

## Competition

The superpowers competed in science, sport, culture and space. The Soviet Union launched Sputnik in 1957 and the first human, Yuri Gagarin, in 1961, and the United States landed on the moon in 1969. Many newly independent countries joined the Non-Aligned Movement. China, communist from 1949, split with the Soviet Union around 1960 and moved closer to the United States in the 1970s.

## Détente and renewed tension

In the 1970s, the superpowers pursued détente, a relaxation of tensions, signing arms control treaties and the Helsinki Accords of 1975. Tensions rose again after the Soviet invasion of Afghanistan, under US President Ronald Reagan.

## The end

Mikhail Gorbachev, who became Soviet leader in 1985, introduced reforms, signed arms reduction treaties and withdrew from Afghanistan. In 1989 communist regimes in eastern Europe fell, and the Berlin Wall was opened on 9 November. Germany reunified in 1990, and the Soviet Union was dissolved in December 1991.
