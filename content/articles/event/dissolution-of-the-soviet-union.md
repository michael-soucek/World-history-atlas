The dissolution of the Soviet Union was the process by which the USSR broke apart into fifteen independent countries, ending formally on 26 December 1991. It ended the Cold War and transformed the map of Europe and Asia.

## Gorbachev's reforms

By the 1980s the Soviet economy was stagnating, burdened by military spending, the war in Afghanistan and the falling price of oil. Mikhail Gorbachev, who became general secretary of the Communist Party in 1985, launched reforms: perestroika, restructuring of the economy, and glasnost, openness, allowing more freedom of speech and criticism. The Chernobyl nuclear disaster in April 1986 and the government's initial secrecy undermined trust. In 1989 the first partly free elections were held to a new parliament, the Congress of People's Deputies.

## Eastern Europe

Gorbachev abandoned the Brezhnev Doctrine, which had justified Soviet intervention in eastern Europe. In 1989 communist regimes fell in Poland, Hungary, East Germany, Czechoslovakia, Bulgaria and Romania. The Berlin Wall was opened on 9 November 1989, and Germany reunited in October 1990.

## The republics

The Soviet Union was a federation of fifteen republics, dominated by Russia. Glasnost allowed national movements to emerge. In the Baltic republics, Estonia, Latvia and Lithuania, which had been annexed in 1940, popular fronts demanded independence; in August 1989, about two million people formed a human chain, the Baltic Way, across the three republics. Lithuania declared independence in March 1990. Conflicts broke out between Armenia and Azerbaijan over Nagorno-Karabakh, and in Georgia. In January 1991, Soviet troops killed protesters in Vilnius and Riga. Republics declared sovereignty. In June 1991 Boris Yeltsin was elected president of the Russian republic, becoming a powerful rival to Gorbachev. Gorbachev negotiated a new union treaty, giving more power to the republics.

## The August coup

On 19 August 1991, the day before the union treaty was due to be signed, a group of hardline Communist officials, including the vice-president, the head of the KGB and the defence minister, staged a coup. They placed Gorbachev under house arrest at his holiday home in Crimea and sent tanks into Moscow. Yeltsin climbed onto a tank outside the Russian parliament building, the White House, and called for resistance. Thousands of Muscovites defended the building, and three were killed. The army refused to attack, and the coup collapsed on 21 August.

## The end

The coup destroyed the authority of the Communist Party and of Gorbachev. Yeltsin banned the party's activities in Russia. Republics declared independence, including Ukraine, on 24 August, confirmed by a referendum in December, with over 90 percent in favour. The Baltic states' independence was recognised in September. On 8 December 1991, the leaders of Russia, Ukraine and Belarus signed the Belovezha Accords, declaring that the Soviet Union had ceased to exist and creating the Commonwealth of Independent States. On 25 December Gorbachev resigned, and the Soviet flag was lowered from the Kremlin. The Soviet Union was formally dissolved the next day.

## Aftermath

Russia took over the Soviet Union's seat on the UN Security Council and its nuclear weapons. The transition to market economies in the 1990s brought economic collapse and hardship. Conflicts broke out in several regions, such as Chechnya, Transnistria and the Caucasus. Russia's war against Ukraine since 2014, and its full-scale invasion in 2022, are partly rooted in the disputes that followed.
