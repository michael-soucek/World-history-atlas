The Sengoku period, "Warring States period", was an era of nearly constant civil war in Japan, from the Ōnin War, which began in 1467, to the unification of the country around 1590, or the establishment of the Tokugawa shogunate in 1603. As the central authority of the Ashikaga shoguns collapsed, regional warlords, daimyo, fought for land and power, until three great leaders unified Japan.

## The collapse of central authority

Japan was ruled in name by the emperor in Kyoto and in practice by the Ashikaga shoguns, military rulers. In 1467 a succession dispute in the shogunate and among powerful families led to the Ōnin War, which lasted until 1477 and devastated Kyoto. Afterwards, the shoguns had little real power, and provincial lords, many of whom had risen by overthrowing their superiors, ruled their own domains independently.

## The daimyo

The daimyo built castles, raised armies of samurai and foot soldiers, ashigaru, and issued their own laws. They encouraged agriculture, trade and mining to fund their wars. Famous daimyo included Takeda Shingen, of Kai, and Uesugi Kenshin, of Echigo, whose rivalry led to five battles at Kawanakajima, and the Hōjō of Odawara. Buddhist temples, such as the Enryaku-ji on Mount Hiei, and religious leagues, such as the Ikkō-ikki, also had armies. Peasants and townspeople sometimes formed self-governing leagues.

## Firearms and Christianity

In 1543 Portuguese traders shipwrecked on Tanegashima, an island in the south, introduced firearms, which Japanese smiths soon copied and produced in large numbers. In 1549 the Jesuit missionary Francis Xavier arrived, and Christianity spread, especially in Kyushu, where some daimyo converted, partly for trade with the Portuguese.

## Oda Nobunaga

Oda Nobunaga, a daimyo of Owari, began the unification. In 1560 he defeated the much larger army of Imagawa Yoshimoto at Okehazama. In 1568 he entered Kyoto and installed a shogun, Ashikaga Yoshiaki, whom he later expelled, ending the Ashikaga shogunate in 1573. He destroyed the Enryaku-ji in 1571, killing thousands, and fought the Ikkō-ikki. In 1575 at Nagashino, with Tokugawa Ieyasu, he defeated the Takeda cavalry using massed volleys of firearms. He built Azuchi Castle. In 1582, having conquered much of central Japan, he was betrayed by his general Akechi Mitsuhide and killed himself at the Honnō-ji temple in Kyoto.

## Toyotomi Hideyoshi

Hideyoshi, a general of humble origins who had risen in Nobunaga's service, defeated Mitsuhide and took over Nobunaga's position. He conquered Shikoku, Kyushu and, in 1590, the Hōjō, completing the unification of Japan. He conducted a land survey, ordered the "sword hunt" of 1588 to disarm peasants, and froze social classes. He invaded Korea in 1592 and 1597, intending to conquer China, but was resisted by the Koreans, especially the admiral Yi Sun-sin, and the Ming. He died in 1598.

## Tokugawa Ieyasu

After Hideyoshi's death, Tokugawa Ieyasu defeated his rivals at Sekigahara in 1600 and became shogun in 1603, establishing the Tokugawa shogunate, which brought over 250 years of peace. A saying sums up the three unifiers: Nobunaga mixed the rice cake, Hideyoshi kneaded it, and Ieyasu ate it.
