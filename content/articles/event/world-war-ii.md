The Second World War was a global conflict from 1939 to 1945 between the Allies, led by Britain, the Soviet Union, the United States and China, and the Axis powers, Germany, Italy and Japan. It was the deadliest conflict in history, killing an estimated 70 to 85 million people, including the six million Jews murdered in the Holocaust, and it transformed the world.

## Origins

The peace settlement after the First World War, the Great Depression and the rise of aggressive dictatorships led to war. Japan invaded Manchuria in 1931 and launched a full-scale war against China in 1937. Italy, under Mussolini, conquered Ethiopia in 1935–1936. In Germany, Adolf Hitler came to power in 1933, rearmed, annexed Austria in 1938 and took Czechoslovakia in 1938–1939. Britain and France followed a policy of appeasement. In August 1939, Germany and the Soviet Union signed a non-aggression pact.

## War in Europe

On 1 September 1939, Germany invaded Poland, and Britain and France declared war. The Soviet Union invaded eastern Poland. In 1940 Germany conquered Denmark, Norway, the Netherlands, Belgium and France with rapid "Blitzkrieg" tactics. British troops were evacuated from Dunkirk. Britain, led by Winston Churchill, fought on alone and won the Battle of Britain in the air. Italy joined Germany and fought in North Africa and the Balkans.

## The Eastern Front

On 22 June 1941, Germany invaded the Soviet Union, with over three million troops. The Germans reached the outskirts of Moscow and besieged Leningrad, where about a million civilians died. The Soviet victory at Stalingrad, in 1942–1943, was a turning point, followed by victory at Kursk in 1943. The war in the east was fought with extreme brutality, and the Soviet Union lost about 27 million people.

## The Pacific

On 7 December 1941, Japan attacked the American naval base at Pearl Harbor, and the United States entered the war. Japan conquered Southeast Asia and the Pacific islands, including the Philippines, Malaya, Singapore and Indonesia. The American naval victory at Midway in June 1942 turned the tide. The Allies advanced island by island in the Pacific, in battles such as Guadalcanal, Iwo Jima and Okinawa.

## Victory in Europe

The Allies won in North Africa in 1943 and invaded Italy, which surrendered in September 1943. On 6 June 1944, D-Day, the Allies landed in Normandy and liberated France. Soviet forces advanced from the east. Hitler killed himself on 30 April 1945, as Soviet forces took Berlin, and Germany surrendered on 8 May 1945.

## The atomic bomb

The United States dropped atomic bombs on Hiroshima on 6 August 1945 and Nagasaki on 9 August, killing over 100,000 people immediately, and more later. The Soviet Union declared war on Japan on 8 August. Japan surrendered on 15 August, signing the surrender on 2 September 1945.

## Consequences

The war killed vast numbers of civilians through bombing, massacres, famine and genocide. The Holocaust and other atrocities led to the Nuremberg and Tokyo trials. The United Nations was founded in 1945. The United States and the Soviet Union emerged as superpowers, and the Cold War began. Europe was divided, Germany occupied and split, and decolonisation accelerated in Asia and Africa.
