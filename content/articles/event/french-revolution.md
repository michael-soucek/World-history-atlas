The French Revolution was a period of political and social upheaval in France from 1789 to 1799. It overthrew the absolute monarchy, abolished feudal privileges, proclaimed the rights of man and the citizen, established a republic and executed the king, and passed through a period of terror before ending with the rise of Napoleon Bonaparte. Its ideas of liberty, equality and national sovereignty shaped modern politics.

## The crisis

In the late eighteenth century, France was in financial crisis, after costly wars, including support for the American Revolution. The clergy and nobility, the First and Second Estates, were largely exempt from taxes, which fell on the Third Estate, the rest of the population. Enlightenment ideas criticised absolute monarchy and privilege. Poor harvests in 1788 drove up bread prices. King Louis XVI called the Estates-General, an assembly that had not met since 1614, to approve new taxes.

## 1789

The Estates-General met at Versailles in May 1789. The Third Estate demanded voting by head rather than by estate, and on 17 June declared itself the National Assembly. Locked out of their hall, its members swore the Tennis Court Oath not to separate until France had a constitution. On 14 July 1789, Parisians stormed the Bastille, a royal fortress and prison, to seize weapons. In the countryside, peasants attacked manors in the "Great Fear". On 4 August, the Assembly abolished feudal privileges, and on 26 August adopted the Declaration of the Rights of Man and of the Citizen, which proclaimed that men are born free and equal in rights. In October, women marched to Versailles and brought the royal family to Paris.

## Constitutional monarchy

The Assembly reorganised France into departments, confiscated church lands and placed the clergy under state control. In June 1791 the king tried to flee and was caught at Varennes, destroying trust. A constitution created a limited monarchy. In April 1792 France declared war on Austria, which, with Prussia, threatened to restore the king's power. On 10 August 1792, a Paris crowd stormed the Tuileries Palace, and the monarchy was suspended. In September, mobs massacred over a thousand prisoners.

## The Republic and the Terror

The National Convention, elected by male suffrage, proclaimed the Republic on 21 September 1792. Louis XVI was tried and guillotined on 21 January 1793, and Queen Marie Antoinette in October. Facing foreign invasion and civil war, especially in the Vendée, where tens of thousands were killed, the Convention created the Committee of Public Safety, dominated by Maximilien Robespierre. During the Reign of Terror, from 1793 to 1794, about 17,000 people were officially executed and many more died in prison or without trial. The government introduced price controls, conscription and a new calendar, and attempted to de-Christianise France. Slavery was abolished in the colonies in 1794. On 27 July 1794, 9 Thermidor, Robespierre was overthrown and executed.

## The Directory

A new constitution in 1795 created the Directory, a government of five directors, which was weak and corrupt and relied on the army. French armies, under generals such as Napoleon Bonaparte, won victories abroad. On 9 November 1799, 18 Brumaire, Napoleon seized power in a coup.

## Legacy

The Revolution spread ideas of popular sovereignty, nationalism, secularism and human rights, and the terms "left" and "right", from the seating in the Assembly. Historians continue to debate its causes, character and consequences.
