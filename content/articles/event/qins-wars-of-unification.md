The Qin wars of unification were a series of military campaigns, from 230 to 221 BCE, in which the state of Qin conquered the six other major states of the Warring States period: Han, Zhao, Wei, Chu, Yan and Qi. The victory created the first unified Chinese empire, under the Qin dynasty, whose king became the first emperor, Qin Shi Huang.

## The Warring States

After the Zhou dynasty's kings lost power, China was divided into rival states, which, during the Warring States period, from about 475 to 221 BCE, fought constant wars with large infantry armies, crossbows and iron weapons. Seven major states remained. Qin, in the west, in the Wei river valley, protected by mountains, was considered semi-barbarian by the others, but became the strongest.

## Qin's reforms

In the fourth century BCE, the minister Shang Yang reformed Qin along Legalist lines: strict laws, collective responsibility, ranks earned through military service, rewards for farming and the abolition of hereditary privileges of the aristocracy. Qin built irrigation works, such as the Dujiangyan in Sichuan, conquered in 316 BCE, and the Zhengguo canal, making it rich. In 260 BCE, at Changping, Qin defeated Zhao in one of the largest battles of the ancient world; according to the historian Sima Qian, the Qin general Bai Qi had hundreds of thousands of surrendered Zhao soldiers buried alive, a figure historians consider exaggerated. In 256 BCE Qin ended the Zhou dynasty.

## King Zheng

Ying Zheng became king of Qin in 246 BCE, at thirteen. With advisers such as the Legalist Li Si and generals such as Wang Jian and his son Wang Ben, he launched the final conquest. Qin used diplomacy, bribery and spies to divide its enemies, as well as war.

## The conquests

Han, the weakest state, fell first, in 230 BCE. Zhao, weakened by famine and the execution of its general Li Mu, after Qin spies spread rumours of treason, fell in 228 BCE. In 227 BCE the crown prince of Yan, Dan, sent the assassin Jing Ke to kill King Zheng, disguised as an envoy bearing a map, with a dagger hidden inside; the attempt failed, and Qin attacked Yan. Wei fell in 225 BCE, after Qin flooded its capital, Daliang, by diverting the Yellow River. Chu, the largest state, defeated a Qin army of 200,000 men, but in 223 BCE Wang Jian, with an army of 600,000, conquered it. Yan fell in 222 BCE, and Qi, the last, surrendered without fighting in 221 BCE.

## The first empire

In 221 BCE King Zheng took the title Qin Shi Huangdi, "first emperor of Qin". He abolished the old states and divided the empire into commanderies and counties ruled by officials, standardised writing, coinage, weights and measures, and axle widths, built roads, canals and walls, and confiscated weapons. The name China probably comes from Qin.

## Legacy

The Qin dynasty collapsed in 206 BCE, soon after the first emperor's death, but the Han dynasty, which followed, kept the unified state. The ideal of a unified China ruled by one emperor endured for over two thousand years.
