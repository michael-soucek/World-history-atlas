The Fall of Tenochtitlan, the capital of the Aztec Empire, came on 13 August 1521, after a siege of nearly three months by Spanish conquistadors led by Hernán Cortés and their tens of thousands of Indigenous allies, particularly the Tlaxcalans. The fall marked the end of the Aztec Empire and the beginning of Spanish rule in Mexico.

## The city

Tenochtitlan, founded by the Mexica around 1325 on an island in Lake Texcoco, was one of the largest cities in the world, with perhaps 200,000 people. It was connected to the shore by causeways, crossed by canals, and dominated by the Templo Mayor, a great double pyramid temple. The Mexica, with their allies Texcoco and Tlacopan, ruled an empire that collected tribute from much of central Mexico. Many subject peoples resented Aztec rule and the demand for captives for human sacrifice.

## Cortés and Moctezuma

In 1519 Cortés landed on the Gulf coast with about 500 Spaniards and allied with the Totonacs and, after fighting, the Tlaxcalans, enemies of the Aztecs. The Spaniards were welcomed into Tenochtitlan by the ruler Moctezuma II in November 1519, and soon took him hostage. In May 1520, while Cortés was away, his lieutenant Pedro de Alvarado massacred Aztec nobles during a festival. The city rose, and Moctezuma was killed. On 30 June 1520, La Noche Triste, the Spaniards fled the city along a causeway, losing hundreds of men and much of their gold.

## Smallpox

The Spaniards retreated to Tlaxcala and regrouped. Meanwhile, smallpox, brought by a member of a Spanish expedition, spread through central Mexico in 1520, killing a large part of the population of Tenochtitlan, including the new ruler, Cuitláhuac. He was succeeded by the young Cuauhtémoc.

## The siege

Cortés gathered tens of thousands of Indigenous allies, mostly Tlaxcalans, as well as Spanish reinforcements. He had thirteen brigantines, small sailing ships, built in Tlaxcala and carried in pieces to the lake, to control the water. In May 1521 the Spaniards and their allies besieged Tenochtitlan, cutting off the causeways and the aqueduct from Chapultepec that brought fresh water. The defenders fought fiercely, capturing and sacrificing Spanish prisoners. The attackers advanced street by street, destroying buildings to fill in canals. Starvation, thirst and disease killed many inhabitants.

## The fall

On 13 August 1521, Cuauhtémoc was captured as he tried to escape by canoe. The city surrendered. Much of it was in ruins, and the surviving inhabitants left. The total number of deaths during the siege is unknown, perhaps over 100,000. Cuauhtémoc was tortured to reveal the location of gold and was executed in 1525.

## Aftermath

Cortés built Mexico City on the ruins of Tenochtitlan, using its stones; the cathedral stands near the site of the Templo Mayor, whose ruins were excavated from 1978. Mexico became the centre of New Spain. The Tlaxcalans received privileges for their alliance. Indigenous accounts of the conquest, such as those collected by the friar Bernardino de Sahagún in the Florentine Codex, record the Mexica perspective. Historians now emphasise that the conquest was largely achieved by Indigenous armies and epidemics, as well as by the Spaniards.
