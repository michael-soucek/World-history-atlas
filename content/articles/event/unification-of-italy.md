The unification of Italy, the Risorgimento, "resurgence", was the political and social movement that united the states of the Italian peninsula into the Kingdom of Italy in the nineteenth century. The kingdom was proclaimed in 1861, Venice was added in 1866, and Rome in 1870, which became the capital.

## A divided peninsula

After the fall of the Western Roman Empire, Italy was divided among many states. After Napoleon's defeat, the Congress of Vienna in 1815 restored the old rulers. Italy was divided into the Kingdom of Sardinia, ruled by the House of Savoy from Turin, which included Piedmont and Sardinia; Lombardy-Venetia, ruled by Austria; the Papal States in central Italy, ruled by the pope; the Kingdom of the Two Sicilies in the south; and smaller duchies, such as Tuscany, Parma and Modena, under Austrian influence.

## Nationalism

Secret societies, such as the Carbonari, organised revolts in the 1820s and 1830s, which were crushed. Giuseppe Mazzini founded Young Italy in 1831, promoting a united Italian republic. In the revolutions of 1848, revolts broke out across Italy, Sardinia's King Charles Albert declared war on Austria but was defeated, and a Roman Republic, led by Mazzini and defended by Giuseppe Garibaldi, was crushed by French troops in 1849.

## Cavour

Camillo Benso, Count of Cavour, prime minister of Sardinia from 1852, modernised its economy and army and sought foreign allies. He sent troops to the Crimean War to gain the support of Britain and France. In 1858 he made a secret agreement with Napoleon III of France. In 1859 France and Sardinia defeated Austria at Magenta and Solferino, and Sardinia gained Lombardy. In 1860 the central Italian states, Tuscany, Parma, Modena and part of the Papal States, voted to join Sardinia, which gave Nice and Savoy to France.

## Garibaldi

In May 1860, Garibaldi, a revolutionary and soldier, sailed from Genoa with about a thousand volunteers, the Redshirts, to Sicily, where a revolt had broken out. He conquered Sicily and crossed to the mainland, taking Naples in September. Cavour, fearing that Garibaldi would attack Rome and provoke France, sent the Sardinian army through the Papal States to the south. Garibaldi met King Victor Emmanuel II at Teano and handed over his conquests. Plebiscites approved the annexation.

## The Kingdom of Italy

On 17 March 1861, the Kingdom of Italy was proclaimed, with Victor Emmanuel II as king. Cavour died in June 1861. Rome and Venice remained outside. In 1866 Italy allied with Prussia against Austria and gained Venetia. In 1870, when French troops protecting Rome were withdrawn during the Franco-Prussian War, Italian troops entered Rome on 20 September 1870, and Rome became the capital in 1871. Pope Pius IX refused to recognise the kingdom and considered himself a prisoner in the Vatican; the dispute was settled by the Lateran Treaty of 1929.

## After unification

Italy faced many problems: regional differences, especially between the industrialising north and the poor, rural south, where brigandage was suppressed by the army, a small electorate and mass emigration. The statesman Massimo d'Azeglio reportedly said, "We have made Italy; now we must make Italians." Trento and Trieste were added after the First World War.
