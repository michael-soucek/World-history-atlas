The American Revolution was the political and military struggle in which thirteen British colonies in North America won their independence and founded the United States. Beginning with protests against British taxation in the 1760s, it led to the Declaration of Independence in 1776, a war from 1775 to 1783, and the creation of a republic based on principles of popular sovereignty and individual rights.

## Taxation without representation

After the Seven Years' War, which ended in 1763 with Britain taking Canada from France, Britain sought to raise money from the colonies to pay its war debts and the cost of defending them. Parliament passed the Sugar Act in 1764 and the Stamp Act in 1765, which taxed printed materials. Colonists protested that they had no representatives in Parliament, "no taxation without representation", boycotted British goods and organised groups such as the Sons of Liberty. The Stamp Act was repealed, but new taxes, the Townshend Acts, followed. In 1770 British soldiers killed five people in the Boston Massacre.

## The Boston Tea Party

In 1773 colonists dressed as Mohawks dumped chests of tea from the East India Company into Boston harbour, protesting against the Tea Act. Parliament responded with the Coercive Acts, called the Intolerable Acts by colonists, closing Boston's port and restricting self-government in Massachusetts. In 1774 delegates from twelve colonies met in Philadelphia as the First Continental Congress.

## War

On 19 April 1775, British troops marching to seize weapons at Concord, Massachusetts, clashed with militia at Lexington and Concord, starting the war. The Second Continental Congress created the Continental Army, with George Washington as commander. In June 1775, the Battle of Bunker Hill showed that colonial militia could inflict heavy losses on British regulars.

## Independence

In January 1776, Thomas Paine's pamphlet Common Sense argued for independence. On 4 July 1776, Congress adopted the Declaration of Independence, drafted by Thomas Jefferson, which declared that "all men are created equal" and endowed with rights to "life, liberty and the pursuit of happiness", and listed grievances against King George III. The declaration's ideals contrasted with the slavery practised in all thirteen colonies.

## The course of the war

The British took New York in 1776 and Philadelphia in 1777. Washington's victories at Trenton and Princeton in the winter of 1776–1777 restored morale. In October 1777, a British army under John Burgoyne surrendered at Saratoga, which persuaded France to ally with the Americans in 1778; Spain and the Dutch Republic later joined against Britain. The Continental Army survived a harsh winter at Valley Forge. The British shifted to the south, taking Savannah and Charleston. In 1781 an American and French army, with a French fleet, trapped Lord Cornwallis's army at Yorktown, Virginia, which surrendered on 19 October 1781.

## Peace

The Treaty of Paris of 1783 recognised the independence of the United States, with territory stretching to the Mississippi. About 60,000 to 80,000 Loyalists, colonists who remained loyal to Britain, left, many to Canada. Thousands of enslaved people who had escaped to the British were evacuated. Many Native American nations, which had mostly sided with Britain, lost land.

## A new nation

The states first united under the Articles of Confederation, but in 1787 the Constitutional Convention drafted the United States Constitution, which created a federal government with separation of powers. The Bill of Rights was added in 1791. The revolution inspired others, including the French Revolution.
