The An Lushan Rebellion was a devastating revolt against the Tang dynasty of China, lasting from 755 to 763. Led by the general An Lushan and continued by his successors, it nearly destroyed the dynasty, caused enormous loss of life and permanently weakened central authority, marking a turning point in Chinese history.

## The Tang at its height

In the first half of the eighth century, under the Xuanzong emperor, the Tang dynasty was at the height of its power and cultural brilliance, the age of the poets Li Bai and Du Fu. Its capital, Chang'an, was perhaps the largest city in the world. To defend its long frontiers, the Tang relied on large professional armies under military governors, jiedushi, who controlled frontier regions.

## An Lushan

An Lushan was a general of Sogdian and Turkic origin, born around 703, who rose in the army of the north-east. He won the favour of the emperor and of his beloved consort, Yang Guifei, who adopted him as her son in a playful ceremony. By 751 he was military governor of three regions, commanding nearly 200,000 troops. He came into conflict with Yang Guozhong, Yang Guifei's cousin, who became chief minister and accused An of plotting rebellion.

## The revolt

In December 755 An Lushan rebelled, marching south from his base near today's Beijing with a large army. He took Luoyang, the eastern capital, and in 756 proclaimed himself emperor of a new Yan dynasty. In July 756 his forces broke through the pass of Tongguan and threatened Chang'an. The emperor fled west to Sichuan. At Mawei, the imperial guards mutinied, killed Yang Guozhong and demanded the death of Yang Guifei, whom they blamed for the disaster; the emperor allowed her to be strangled. The story of their love became a famous theme in Chinese literature, as in Bai Juyi's poem "Song of Everlasting Sorrow". Xuanzong abdicated in favour of his son, Suzong.

## The war

In 757 An Lushan, increasingly blind and violent, was murdered by his son, An Qingxu, who was later killed by An's general Shi Siming, who continued the rebellion until he too was killed by his son, Shi Chaoyi. Tang forces, helped by Uyghur cavalry and troops from Central Asia, retook Chang'an and Luoyang in 757; the Uyghurs plundered Luoyang. The rebellion ended in 763 when Shi Chaoyi killed himself and his generals surrendered.

## Cost

The war devastated northern China. Census figures fell from about 53 million in 754 to about 17 million in 764, though this partly reflects the breakdown of registration rather than deaths alone; nonetheless, losses were enormous. The poet Du Fu, who lived through the war, described its suffering in his poems.

## Consequences

The Tang dynasty survived for another 150 years, but its power was greatly reduced. Military governors in the north-east became virtually independent and hereditary. The Tang lost control of Central Asia, and the Tibetan Empire took Chang'an briefly in 763 and occupied the Hexi corridor. The government came to depend on new taxes, such as a salt monopoly, and the economic centre of China shifted south to the Yangtze region. The rebellion also contributed to a reaction against foreigners and foreign religions.
