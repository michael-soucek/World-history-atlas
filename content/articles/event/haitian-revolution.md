The Haitian Revolution, from 1791 to 1804, was a successful uprising of enslaved people in the French colony of Saint-Domingue against slavery and colonial rule. It led to the abolition of slavery in the colony and the founding of Haiti, the first independent nation in Latin America and the Caribbean, the second republic in the Americas, and the only state in history founded by a successful slave revolt.

## Saint-Domingue

Saint-Domingue, the western part of the island of Hispaniola, was France's richest colony and perhaps the most profitable colony in the world, producing about 40 percent of Europe's sugar and over half its coffee. About 500,000 enslaved Africans, most born in Africa, worked on its plantations under brutal conditions, with high death rates. There were about 40,000 white colonists and about 30,000 free people of colour, many of mixed race, some of them wealthy planters, who faced discrimination.

## The uprising

The French Revolution of 1789 brought debates over rights. Free people of colour demanded equality, and in 1790 Vincent Ogé led a revolt, crushed brutally. On the night of 22 August 1791, after a Vodou ceremony at Bois Caïman, according to tradition led by the priest Dutty Boukman, enslaved people in the northern plain rose up, burning plantations and killing colonists. Within weeks, tens of thousands had joined the revolt.

## Abolition

War spread, involving enslaved rebels, free people of colour, colonists, French republican commissioners, and Spanish and British forces, who invaded the colony. In 1793 the French commissioner Léger-Félicité Sonthonax, seeking support, proclaimed the abolition of slavery in the north, and in February 1794 the National Convention in Paris abolished slavery in all French colonies.

## Toussaint Louverture

Toussaint Louverture, a formerly enslaved coachman who had become a leader of the revolt, at first fought for Spain, then switched to France in 1794 after abolition. He defeated the Spanish and the British, who withdrew in 1798, and his rival André Rigaud. By 1801 he controlled the whole island and issued a constitution making himself governor for life.

## Napoleon's expedition

Napoleon, intending to restore French control and slavery, which he restored in Guadeloupe and Martinique in 1802, sent an expedition of about 30,000 troops under his brother-in-law, Charles Leclerc, in 1802. Toussaint was captured by trickery and deported to France, where he died in prison in 1803. When it became clear that the French intended to restore slavery, Black and mixed-race leaders, Jean-Jacques Dessalines, Henri Christophe and Alexandre Pétion, resumed the war. Yellow fever killed tens of thousands of French soldiers, including Leclerc. On 18 November 1803, Dessalines's forces defeated the French at Vertières.

## Independence

On 1 January 1804, Dessalines declared independence, naming the country Haiti, the Taíno name of the island. In 1804 he ordered the killing of most of the remaining white French inhabitants. The revolution cost perhaps 200,000 lives.

## Legacy

The revolution terrified slaveholders across the Americas and inspired enslaved people and abolitionists. Haiti was isolated: the United States did not recognise it until 1862, and in 1825 France forced Haiti to pay a huge indemnity, 150 million francs, later reduced, to compensate former slaveholders, in exchange for recognition, a debt that burdened Haiti's economy for over a century. Haiti supported Simón Bolívar's independence struggle.
