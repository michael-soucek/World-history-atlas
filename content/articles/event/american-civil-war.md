The American Civil War was fought from 1861 to 1865 between the United States, the Union, and the Confederate States of America, formed by southern states that seceded to preserve slavery. The deadliest war in American history, it ended with the Union's victory, the preservation of the United States and the abolition of slavery.

## Causes

The main cause of the war was slavery. By 1860 about four million enslaved people lived in the southern states, whose economy depended on cotton grown by enslaved labour. Northern states had abolished slavery. Conflict grew over whether slavery would spread into the western territories. The Compromise of 1850, the Kansas-Nebraska Act of 1854, which led to violence in "Bleeding Kansas", and the Supreme Court's Dred Scott decision of 1857, which ruled that Black people could not be citizens, deepened divisions. In 1859 the abolitionist John Brown raided the federal arsenal at Harpers Ferry. The election of Abraham Lincoln, of the anti-slavery Republican Party, as president in November 1860 led South Carolina to secede in December, followed by six more states. They formed the Confederacy, with Jefferson Davis as president.

## Fort Sumter

On 12 April 1861, Confederate forces bombarded Fort Sumter, a federal fort in Charleston harbour, which surrendered. Lincoln called for volunteers, and four more states, including Virginia, joined the Confederacy. Richmond became its capital. The Union had far more people, industry and railways, but the Confederacy had skilled generals, such as Robert E. Lee and Thomas "Stonewall" Jackson, and fought on its own territory.

## The war in the east

The Confederates won the first major battle at Bull Run in July 1861. In 1862 Lee repelled a Union army near Richmond. Lee invaded Maryland, but was stopped at Antietam on 17 September 1862, the bloodiest single day in American history. Lee won at Fredericksburg and Chancellorsville, then invaded Pennsylvania, but was defeated at Gettysburg on 1–3 July 1863, the largest battle of the war.

## The war in the west

In the west, Union forces under Ulysses S. Grant took forts in Tennessee, won at Shiloh in 1862 and, after a long siege, took Vicksburg on 4 July 1863, giving the Union control of the Mississippi and splitting the Confederacy. The Union navy blockaded southern ports.

## Emancipation

On 1 January 1863, Lincoln's Emancipation Proclamation declared free the enslaved people in Confederate-held areas. About 180,000 Black men served in the Union army and navy. Many enslaved people escaped to Union lines.

## The end

Grant took command of all Union armies in 1864 and fought Lee in a series of bloody battles in Virginia, besieging Petersburg. General William T. Sherman took Atlanta and marched to the sea, destroying property across Georgia. Lee abandoned Richmond and surrendered at Appomattox Court House on 9 April 1865. Five days later, Lincoln was assassinated.

## Aftermath

About 620,000 to 750,000 soldiers died, more than in any other American war. The Thirteenth Amendment of 1865 abolished slavery, the Fourteenth granted citizenship and equal protection, and the Fifteenth prohibited denial of the vote based on race. During Reconstruction, Black Americans gained political rights, but after 1877 southern states imposed segregation and disenfranchisement, the Jim Crow system, which lasted until the civil rights movement of the 1950s and 1960s.
