The Chinese Civil War was fought between the Nationalist government of the Kuomintang and the Chinese Communist Party, intermittently from 1927 to 1949. It ended with the Communists' victory, the founding of the People's Republic of China on the mainland and the withdrawal of the Nationalist government to Taiwan, a division that persists.

## The first phase

The Kuomintang, founded by Sun Yat-sen, and the Communist Party, founded in 1921, allied in the 1920s, with Soviet support, to unify China, divided among warlords. During the Northern Expedition, in April 1927, Chiang Kai-shek, the Nationalist leader, turned on the Communists, massacring thousands in Shanghai and other cities. The Communists launched uprisings, which failed, and retreated to the countryside. Mao Zedong and Zhu De built a rural base in Jiangxi, the Chinese Soviet Republic. Chiang launched a series of "encirclement campaigns" against them.

## The Long March

In 1934, the fifth campaign forced the Communists to break out. Their Long March, a retreat of about 10,000 kilometres through mountains, rivers and marshes, ended in Yan'an, in Shaanxi, in 1935. Most of the marchers died or deserted. During the march, Mao became the party's leader.

## United against Japan

Japan seized Manchuria in 1931. In December 1936, in the Xi'an Incident, the Nationalist general Zhang Xueliang kidnapped Chiang and forced him to agree to a united front with the Communists against Japan. When Japan launched a full-scale invasion in 1937, the two sides fought nominally together, but distrust remained, and in 1941 Nationalist forces attacked the Communist New Fourth Army. The Nationalists bore the brunt of the conventional war and suffered heavy losses, while the Communists expanded their bases behind Japanese lines through guerrilla warfare and grew from about 40,000 to over a million troops.

## Civil war resumes

After Japan surrendered in August 1945, both sides raced to take territory. The Soviet Union, which had occupied Manchuria, handed captured Japanese weapons to the Communists. The United States, which supported the Nationalists, sent General George Marshall to mediate, but negotiations failed, and full-scale war resumed in 1946. The Nationalists initially had more troops and American equipment and took Yan'an in 1947.

## Communist victory

The Nationalist government suffered from corruption, hyperinflation, which destroyed savings, and low morale. The Communists won support among peasants with land reform, redistributing land from landlords, often violently. Under generals such as Lin Biao, the Communists won three decisive campaigns in 1948–1949: Liaoshen, which took Manchuria; Huaihai, in eastern China, in which hundreds of thousands of Nationalist troops were killed, captured or defected; and Pingjin, which took Beijing and Tianjin. In April 1949 Communist forces crossed the Yangtze and took Nanjing, the Nationalist capital, and Shanghai.

## Two Chinas

On 1 October 1949, Mao proclaimed the People's Republic of China in Beijing. Chiang Kai-shek's government retreated to Taiwan in December 1949, with about two million soldiers and civilians, taking China's gold reserves and art treasures, now in the National Palace Museum in Taipei. No peace treaty was signed. Both governments claimed to represent all of China. The United States protected Taiwan after the outbreak of the Korean War. In 1971 the People's Republic took China's seat at the United Nations. The status of Taiwan remains unresolved.
