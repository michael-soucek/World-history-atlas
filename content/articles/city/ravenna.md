Ravenna lies in the Emilia-Romagna region of north-eastern Italy, an inland city joined to the Adriatic by a canal. For most of the fifth century it was the seat of the Western Roman emperors; it then became the capital of the Ostrogothic kingdom and of Byzantine Italy. The churches and mosaics of those centuries make it one of the richest places in Europe for late Roman and early Byzantine art.

## Lagoon town and imperial capital

Both the origin of the name and the date of the first town are uncertain. Archaeology shows the Umbri living here by the fifth century BCE at the latest, and the Celtic Senones settled the countryside to the south. The early town was built on piles across small islands in a marshy lagoon. Rome admitted it as a federated town in 89 BCE. Julius Caesar gathered his army at Ravenna in 49 BCE before crossing the Rubicon, and after 31 BCE Octavian founded the naval base of Classis nearby, an important station of the imperial fleet. Early in the second century Trajan gave the city an aqueduct about 70 kilometres long.

In 402 the emperor Honorius moved his court here, and Ravenna served as the effective capital of the [Western Roman Empire](/place/western-roman-empire) for most of the century, when it may have had some 50,000 inhabitants. Galla Placidia, daughter of Theodosius I, later returned to the city with her son, the emperor Valentinian III, and under them the court favoured Christianity. In 476 the general Odoacer deposed Romulus Augustulus, the last western emperor, at Ravenna and ruled Italy as king.

## Goths and Byzantines

In 489, at the urging of the eastern emperor Zeno, the Ostrogothic king [Theodoric](/person/theodoric-the-great) marched on Italy to overthrow Odoacer. After several years of war Theodoric besieged Odoacer in Ravenna; a treaty in February 493 made the two joint rulers, but within weeks of entering the city Theodoric killed Odoacer at a banquet. Ravenna now served as the Gothic king's capital, and Theodoric was buried there in a stone mausoleum still counted among the city's finest monuments.

After Theodoric's death in 526 the Gothic monarchy weakened. [Justinian I](/person/justinian-i) sent his general Belisarius into Italy in 535, and Belisarius took Ravenna in 540. From then on the city housed the exarch, the emperor's governor in Italy, whose territory took the name of the Exarchate of Ravenna. The archbishop of Ravenna ranked second in Italy only to the pope.

## Lombards, popes and lords

The Lombard king Liutprand occupied Ravenna in 712 but had to hand it back. In 751 the [Byzantine Empire](/place/byzantine-empire) finally surrendered the exarchate to the Lombard king Aistulf. Pope Stephen II turned to Pepin, king of the Franks, whose campaigns forced Aistulf in 756 to give up his conquests; Pepin then granted the former exarchate to the papacy, the gift that laid the legal foundation of the [Papal States](/place/papal-states). Papal authority stayed distant for a long time, and in the eleventh and twelfth centuries emperors and popes contested the city.

In the thirteenth century Ravenna was caught up in the struggle of Guelphs and Ghibellines. The Traversari family ruled from 1218 until the emperor Frederick II besieged the city and expelled them in 1240; Pope Innocent IV recovered it in 1248. From 1275 the Da Polenta family governed as papal vassals, and under them the exiled Florentine poet [Dante](/person/dante-alighieri) spent most of his last years in the city, where his funeral took place in 1321 and where he is still buried. The [Republic of Venice](/place/republic-of-venice) removed the last Da Polenta lord in 1441 and held Ravenna until 1509, when the War of the League of Cambrai brought it back under papal rule. In 1512 French troops besieged the city, defeated the relieving army in the Battle of Ravenna and then sacked it.

## Modern Ravenna

Apart from a Venetian occupation in 1527–29, Ravenna stayed papal until 1796, when it was absorbed into French client republics and then Napoleon's Kingdom of Italy. Returned to the pope in 1814, it was occupied by Piedmontese troops in 1859 and joined the unified Kingdom of Italy in 1861.

In the Second World War 52 Allied air raids badly damaged the city; bombs aimed at the railway destroyed the church of San Giovanni Evangelista in August 1944. Canadian and British troops entered Ravenna on 5 November 1944. Today it is a provincial capital of about 156,000 people, and eight of its early Christian and Byzantine buildings together form a UNESCO World Heritage Site.
