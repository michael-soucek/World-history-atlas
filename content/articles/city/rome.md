Rome grew up on a group of hills beside the Tiber, about 25 kilometres from the sea, at the lowest point where the river could easily be crossed and close to an island that made bridging it simpler. The crossing linked the Etruscan cities to the north with the Latin and Greek lands to the south, and the hills offered defence and a dry place to live above marshy valleys. From this site Rome built an empire that ruled the whole Mediterranean, and after that empire fell it became the centre of the Catholic Church. Those two roles, imperial capital and papal city, define its history.

## Kings, republic and empire

Huts stood on the Palatine Hill by around 1000 BCE, and later Romans dated their city's founding by Romulus to 753 BCE. Under kings, some of them Etruscan, the villages merged into a city; the drainage of the valley that became the Forum and the building of the first temples date from this time. Tradition holds that the last king was expelled in 509 BCE and the [Roman Republic](/place/roman-republic) founded. Gallic warriors sacked the city around 390 BCE, after which a stone wall was built, but over the next three centuries Rome conquered Italy, defeated Carthage in the Punic Wars and took over the Greek east.

Civil wars ended the republic, and after [Augustus](/person/augustus) became the first emperor in 27 BCE, Rome was the capital of the [Roman Empire](/place/roman-empire) and probably the largest city in the world, with perhaps a million inhabitants fed by grain shipped from Egypt and North Africa. After the great fire of 64 CE emperors added the Colosseum, opened in 80 CE, vast baths and new forums. In the 270s, as invasions threatened, Aurelian surrounded the city with new walls.

## Decline and the papal city

Rome lost political weight as emperors ruled from frontier capitals, and Constantine I, who favoured Christianity and built the first basilica of St Peter, founded Constantinople as a new capital in 330. The Visigoths sacked Rome in 410 and the Vandals in 455, and in 476 the last western emperor was deposed in Ravenna. The Gothic wars of the sixth century, in which the Byzantine Empire recovered the city from the Ostrogoths, left it ruined, and its population fell to a few tens of thousands.

The bishops of Rome, the popes, filled the vacuum. In 754 they allied with the Frankish kings, and on Christmas Day 800 Pope Leo III crowned [Charlemagne](/person/charlemagne) emperor in St Peter's. Rome became the capital of the [Papal States](/place/papal-states), a territory across central Italy, though popes often struggled with Roman noble families, the city commune and German emperors. When the papacy moved to Avignon from 1309 to 1377, the city decayed further. After the popes returned, Renaissance and Baroque pontiffs rebuilt it: the new St Peter's Basilica, begun in 1506, Michelangelo's Sistine Chapel ceiling and the squares and fountains of Bernini all date from this era. Imperial troops sacked the city brutally in 1527.

## Capital of Italy

The French Revolutionary armies seized Rome in 1798, and a short-lived Roman Republic of 1849 was crushed by French troops, who then protected the pope until 1870. On 20 September 1870, as part of the [unification of Italy](/event/unification-of-italy), Italian forces breached the walls, and Rome became the capital of the Kingdom of Italy in 1871. [Benito Mussolini](/person/benito-mussolini)'s Fascists seized power after the March on Rome in 1922 and cut broad roads through ancient sites. The Lateran Treaty of 1929 created the independent state of [Vatican City](/country/vatican-city) within Rome. German forces occupied the city in September 1943 until the Allies entered it on 4 June 1944. Rome today has about 2.8 million inhabitants and is the capital of the Italian Republic, the seat of the papacy and one of the world's great repositories of art and architecture.
