Naples curves around a broad bay on the west coast of southern Italy, between the volcanic fields of the Campi Flegrei and the cone of Vesuvius. Its harbour, the fertile volcanic soil of the Campanian plain behind it and its mild climate have drawn settlers for nearly three thousand years. Founded by Greeks, it was for more than five centuries the capital of a kingdom covering the whole of southern Italy and, in the seventeenth and eighteenth centuries, one of the largest cities in Europe. Its history combines royal splendour, foreign rule, popular revolt and recurring disasters from plague, war and the volcano.

## Greek city to royal capital

Greek colonists from Cumae, the oldest Greek settlement on the Italian mainland, founded a small town called Parthenope on the hill of Pizzofalcone in the late eighth or seventh century BCE. Around the late sixth or early fifth century BCE they built a larger planned town nearby, Neapolis, "new city", whose grid of streets still forms the historic centre (see [Ancient Greece](/culture/ancient-greece)). After a siege in 326 BCE Neapolis became an ally of the [Roman Republic](/place/roman-republic), but it kept its Greek language and customs for centuries and was a favourite retreat for wealthy Romans and writers such as Virgil. The eruption of Vesuvius in 79 CE buried nearby Pompeii and Herculaneum, though not Naples itself.

After the fall of the western empire the city passed to the Ostrogoths and then, in the sixth century, to the [Byzantine Empire](/place/byzantine-empire). From the seventh century the Duchy of Naples was in practice self-governing, holding off the Lombards and Arab raiders, until it submitted to the Norman king Roger II of Sicily in 1137. The emperor Frederick II founded its university in 1224. When Charles of Anjou seized the southern kingdom in 1266, he made Naples his capital, and after the revolt of the Sicilian Vespers in 1282 cut off Sicily, the mainland realm became known as the Kingdom of Naples. Alfonso V of Aragon conquered it in 1442 and made it the centre of a brilliant Renaissance court.

## Viceroys, Bourbons and Italy

From the early 1500s Naples was ruled by viceroys of the [Spanish Empire](/place/spanish-empire). It grew into one of the most populous cities in Europe, crowded with nobles, clergy and the urban poor. In 1647 a fisherman named Masaniello led a popular revolt against new taxes, and in 1656 a plague epidemic killed roughly half the population. Austrian rule followed in 1707, until in 1734 Charles of Bourbon made Naples the capital of an independent kingdom again. The Bourbons built the San Carlo opera house, the vast palace at Caserta and the excavations at Pompeii and Herculaneum.

The French-backed Parthenopean Republic of 1799 was crushed within months, and its leaders were executed. Under [Napoleon](/person/napoleon) the kingdom was ruled by his brother Joseph and then by Joachim Murat, before the Bourbons returned and formed the Kingdom of the Two Sicilies in 1816. In September 1860 [Giuseppe Garibaldi](/person/giuseppe-garibaldi) entered Naples, and a plebiscite joined the south to the new Kingdom of Italy during the [unification of Italy](/event/unification-of-italy). Losing its status as a capital, Naples became a centre of mass emigration, and a cholera epidemic in 1884 led to the demolition and rebuilding of its worst slums.

During the [Second World War](/event/world-war-ii) Naples was heavily bombed, and in September 1943 its people rose against the German occupiers in the Four Days of Naples before Allied troops arrived. Vesuvius last erupted in 1944. The historic centre was added to the UNESCO World Heritage List in 1995. Naples has about 900,000 inhabitants and some 3 million in its metropolitan area, making it the largest city of southern Italy.
