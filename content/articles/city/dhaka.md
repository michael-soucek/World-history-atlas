Dhaka stands on the north bank of the Buriganga, a channel of the vast river system where the Ganges, Brahmaputra and Meghna meet before entering the Bay of Bengal. Rivers made it a trading town, and politics made it a capital: Dhaka rose to importance as the seat of Mughal Bengal in the 17th century, and three centuries later it became the capital of an independent Bangladesh.

## From river town to Mughal capital

The delta was ruled for centuries by Bengali dynasties such as the Buddhist [Pala Empire](/place/pala-empire) and the Hindu Senas, and settlements existed around the site long before it was a city of note. After the Turkic conquest of Bengal in the early 13th century the area passed under governors linked to the [Delhi Sultanate](/place/delhi-sultanate) and then to the independent Bengal Sultanate. By the late 16th century eastern Bengal was held by a group of local chiefs, the Baro-Bhuiyans, who resisted Mughal expansion.

The Mughal general Islam Khan Chishti subdued them and in 1610 made Dhaka the provincial capital of Bengal, naming it Jahangirnagar in honour of the emperor Jahangir. Under the [Mughal Empire](/place/mughal-empire) the city grew rich on river trade and on its fine muslin cloth, famous across Asia and Europe. Governors built the Bara Katra caravanserai and began the Lalbagh Fort in 1678. Dhaka's political role then faded when the capital of Bengal moved to Murshidabad in the early 18th century.

## Colonial decline and two partitions

After the Battle of Plassey in 1757 the British East India Company took control of Bengal, and Calcutta became the centre of power. Dhaka shrank as machine-made British textiles destroyed the muslin industry. It revived in the 19th century as an administrative and educational town, and in 1905 the first partition of Bengal made it capital of the new province of Eastern Bengal and Assam. The partition was reversed in 1911, but the University of Dhaka, opened in 1921, made the city the intellectual centre of Muslim Bengal.

The [partition of India](/event/partition-of-india) in 1947 made Dhaka the capital of East Bengal, later East Pakistan, separated from the rest of [Pakistan](/country/pakistan) by about 1,600 kilometres of India. Tension grew over language and power. On 21 February 1952 police fired on students demonstrating for Bengali to be recognised as a state language, a date now marked worldwide as International Mother Language Day. After the Awami League won the 1970 election and was denied office, the Pakistani army launched a crackdown in Dhaka on the night of 25 March 1971. Nine months of war followed, ending when Pakistani forces surrendered in the city on 16 December 1971.

## Capital of Bangladesh

As capital of [Bangladesh](/country/bangladesh), Dhaka grew at extraordinary speed, drawing migrants from the countryside to its garment factories, which became the backbone of the export economy. The 2013 collapse of the Rana Plaza factory building nearby, which killed more than 1,100 workers, drew global attention to working conditions. In August 2024 student-led protests in the city brought down the government of Sheikh Hasina. Greater Dhaka now holds more than 20 million people, making it one of the most densely populated urban areas on earth.
