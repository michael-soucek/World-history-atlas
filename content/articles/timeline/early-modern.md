At the start of this age every large state on the map is a land power whose territory is contiguous. Europe is a crowded patchwork, the Islamic world is divided among the Mamluks, the rising Ottomans and Timur's successors, and Ming China is the largest single block in the east. The Americas show no European presence at all. By 1800 the map has been turned inside out: small kingdoms on the Atlantic edge of Europe claim territory on every inhabited continent, and colours that belong to Madrid, Lisbon, London and Paris appear thousands of kilometres from home.

The snapshots for 1492, 1500 and 1530 capture the hinge. The [Inca Empire](/place/inca-empire) and the Aztec realm are near their greatest size around 1500, and within a generation both have vanished into the [Spanish Empire](/place/spanish-empire). In the same decades the Ottomans absorb Syria, Egypt and, after 1526, much of Hungary, the Safavids unite Iran, and Babur founds the Mughal state in northern India.

From 1600 to 1800 watch three kinds of growth. The first is the slow spread of European coastal colonies inland, from footholds in Virginia, Quebec, Brazil, the Cape and Java. The second is the steady advance of Russia, whose colour stretches across Siberia to the Pacific by the middle of the seventeenth century and later into Alaska. The third is the expansion of the [Qing dynasty](/place/qing-dynasty), which by 1800 includes Mongolia, Xinjiang and Tibet.

Treat the colonial outlines with care. On paper, Spain, Portugal, Britain and France claimed enormous interiors, but in practice most of North and South America away from the coasts and mining districts remained in the hands of Indigenous nations such as the Haudenosaunee and the [Comanche](/place/comanche), who controlled much of the southern Plains in the eighteenth century. In Africa, European rule before 1800 was confined almost entirely to coastal forts, the Cape Colony and Portuguese footholds in Angola and Mozambique, while inland states such as Asante and Oyo held power.

In Europe, by contrast, borders become firmer. After the Peace of Westphalia in 1648, rulers increasingly recognised one another as sovereign over clearly defined territory, and peace treaties such as Utrecht (1713) moved provinces between dynasties like pieces on a board. The Holy Roman Empire remained a mosaic of hundreds of small states. The last snapshots show the first new republic born from a colonial empire: the [United States](/place/united-states), recognised by Britain in 1783, while British power in India is expanding as Mughal authority shrinks to a small area around Delhi.
