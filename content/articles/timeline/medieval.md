In 500 the political map is a landscape of fragments. Rome survives only in the east; the western provinces are shared among Germanic kings, China is split between northern and southern dynasties, and the Gupta realm in India is breaking up. Only Sasanian Persia remains a large, stable block. By 1400 the map is far fuller: states cover much more of Eurasia and Africa, many of the kingdoms that became modern European nations have taken recognisable shape, and new empires are about to form in the Americas.

The most dramatic change comes early. Between the snapshots for 600 and 800, a single new colour floods outward from Arabia to cover the Middle East, North Africa, Spain, Iran and the edge of India, as the caliphates replace the Sasanians entirely and strip Byzantium of its southern provinces. Watch how that unity then dissolves: by 1000 the [Abbasid Caliphate](/place/abbasid-caliphate) is surrounded by independent dynasties such as the Fatimids in Egypt and the Samanids and Ghaznavids in the east, and in the eleventh century the [Seljuk Empire](/place/seljuk-empire) sweeps in from Central Asia.

In the east, China reunifies under the Sui and Tang, while a [Tibetan Empire](/place/tibetan-empire) briefly becomes a great power on the plateau. After 907 China fragments again; in the snapshot for 1000 the Song share the country with the Liao, and later with the Western Xia and Jin. In western Europe the [Carolingian Empire](/place/carolingian-empire) unites a broad territory around 800 and then divides, leaving France, the Holy Roman Empire and Italy on separate paths. Remember that European borders of this period are especially approximate: a king's map outline often hides dozens of semi-independent lordships.

The single biggest swing of colour on the medieval maps belongs to the Mongols. The 1279 snapshot shows their conquests at close to their greatest extent, from Korea and China to eastern Europe and Anatolia, by then divided among four khanates. By 1400 the Yuan has given way to the Ming, the [Golden Horde](/place/golden-horde) is weakening, and Timur's realm sprawls across Iran and Central Asia.

Away from these headline empires, look for steady growth at the margins. Kievan Rus', Poland, Hungary and the Scandinavian kingdoms appear in the north and east of Europe around 1000. In Africa, Ghana, then Mali, occupies the western Sahel, and Ethiopia and the Swahili coast become visible in the east. Angkor, Pagan and Srivijaya mark Southeast Asia. In the Americas, outlines appear for the Toltecs, Chimor and, by 1400, Mixtec and Zapotec states, while the Pacific islands carry the names of their Polynesian settlers.
