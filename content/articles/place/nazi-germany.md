Nazi Germany was the German state under Adolf Hitler and the Nazi Party, from 1933 to 1945. In twelve years it turned Germany into a totalitarian dictatorship, started the Second World War in Europe and carried out the Holocaust, the murder of six million Jews.

## The Weimar Republic's collapse

The Nazi Party, the National Socialist German Workers' Party, emerged in Munich after the First World War, combining extreme nationalism, antisemitism and hostility to democracy and communism. After the failed Beer Hall Putsch of 1923, Hitler pursued power through elections. The Great Depression, which brought six million unemployed by 1932, made the Nazis the largest party. Conservative politicians around President Hindenburg, believing they could control Hitler, persuaded the president to appoint him chancellor on 30 January 1933.

## Building the dictatorship

After the Reichstag fire in February 1933, an emergency decree suspended civil liberties, and the Enabling Act of March 1933 allowed Hitler's government to make laws without parliament. Trade unions were dissolved, other parties banned, and the states brought under central control. In the "Night of the Long Knives" in June–July 1934, Hitler had the leadership of the SA, the Nazi stormtroopers, including Ernst Röhm, and other opponents killed. The SS, led by Heinrich Himmler, and the Gestapo, the secret police, ran a growing system of concentration camps.

## Propaganda and society

Joseph Goebbels's Ministry of Propaganda controlled the press, radio, film and culture. Books by Jewish and opposition authors were burned in 1933. Young people were organised in the Hitler Youth and League of German Girls. The regime promoted the idea of a "people's community" based on race. Public works, rearmament and the suppression of unions reduced unemployment. Leni Riefenstahl's films glorified the regime, and the 1936 Berlin Olympics were used for propaganda.

## Racial persecution

The regime defined Jews as a race and excluded them from society. Jewish businesses were boycotted, Jewish professionals barred, and the Nuremberg Laws of 1935 stripped Jews of citizenship. After Kristallnacht in November 1938, Jews were forced out of the economy, and many emigrated. The regime sterilised about 400,000 people considered hereditarily ill and, from 1939, murdered disabled people in the "T4" programme. Roma, gay men, Jehovah's Witnesses and others were persecuted.

## Aggression

Hitler withdrew from the League of Nations, reintroduced conscription in 1935, remilitarised the Rhineland in 1936, annexed Austria in the Anschluss of March 1938 and, after the Munich Agreement of September 1938, took the Sudetenland from Czechoslovakia. In March 1939 Germany occupied the rest of the Czech lands. After the Nazi-Soviet Pact of August 1939, Germany invaded Poland on 1 September.

## War and genocide

Germany conquered most of Europe by 1941 and invaded the Soviet Union in June 1941, beginning a war of annihilation in the east, in which millions of Soviet prisoners of war and civilians were killed. The Holocaust murdered about six million Jews. Occupied countries were plundered, and millions of forced labourers were brought to Germany.

## Resistance and defeat

Some Germans resisted: the White Rose student group in Munich, executed in 1943, and the officers' plot of 20 July 1944, when Claus von Stauffenberg tried to kill Hitler with a bomb. After defeats at Stalingrad and in Normandy, Germany was invaded. Hitler killed himself on 30 April 1945, and Germany surrendered on 8 May. Nazi leaders were tried at Nuremberg in 1945–1946.

## On our maps

Our maps show Nazi Germany on the 1938 map, at about 560,000 square kilometres, after the annexation of Austria. About 62 percent of that area is in today's Germany, with Austria and Poland making up most of the rest, since Germany's eastern provinces then reached into what is now Poland and Russia's Kaliningrad region.
