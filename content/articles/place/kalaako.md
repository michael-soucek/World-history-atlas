Kalaako, also written Kalarko, is the recorded name of an Aboriginal Australian people from Western Australia's Goldfields-Esperance region, whose Country includes the area around Norseman and Salmon Gums and reaches towards Coolgardie. Most of what is written about them comes from the anthropologist Norman Tindale's catalogue of 1974, which tried to map Aboriginal groups as they were at the time of European contact.

## Country

Tindale gave the Kalaako an area of about 24,000 square miles, roughly 62,400 square kilometres. In his description their lands ran from Grass Patch and Scaddan in the south to beyond Widgiemooltha in the north, taking in Mount Monger, Golden Ridge and Burbanks. To the east they reached Karkanja, a red ochre deposit about 15 miles west of Fraser Range; to the west, the Bremer Range, the Johnston Lakes, Mount Holland, Barker Lake and Koongornin. Tindale noted a boundary camp about three miles south of Coolgardie.

## Neighbours and names

The groups recorded around the Kalaako include the Maduwongga to the north, the Tjeraridjal to the north-east, the Ngadjunmaia, the Njunga to the south, the [Wudjari](/place/wudjari), the Njakinjaki and the [Kalamaia](/place/kalamaia). The Wudjari applied the name Malba to them and to other groups immediately to the Wudjari's north; according to Tindale it meant the circumcised and subincised ones.

## Language

Their language, known as Galaagu or Kallaargu and also as Malpa, belongs to the Pama-Nyungan family. In 2011 the linguist Claire Bowern classed it as the closest relative of the Nyungar languages. Linguists also list a Kalarko-Mirniny language, linking it with the speech of the [Mirning](/place/mirning).

## On our maps

Our maps label this territory Kalaako/Malpa and draw it in 1600, 1650, 1700, 1715, 1783, 1800 and 1815; the outline is approximate, and the map years are not dates in the people's history. The region today lies in [Australia](/country/australia).
