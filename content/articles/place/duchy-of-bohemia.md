The Duchy of Bohemia was the early medieval Czech state, ruled from Prague by the Přemyslid dynasty from the late ninth century until 1198, when Duke Ottokar I won a hereditary royal title. Beginning as a dependency of Great Moravia, it became a strong regional power and, from 1002, a member state of the [Holy Roman Empire](/place/holy-roman-empire), while keeping much of its own identity. It is the direct ancestor of the Kingdom of Bohemia and of the modern [Czech Republic](/country/czech-republic).

## Before the duchy

Slavic peoples settled the basin enclosed by the Bohemian Forest, the Ore Mountains, the Sudetes and the Bohemian-Moravian Highlands around the year 550. The name Bohemia, probably from the Celtic Boii, first appears in Frankish sources of the ninth century. In 805 [Charlemagne](/person/charlemagne) invaded; the Czechs avoided open battle and forced him to withdraw, but after a renewed campaign the following year the local tribes submitted to the [Carolingian Empire](/place/carolingian-empire).

## Great Moravia and independence

As Frankish power faded in the mid-ninth century, Bohemia came under the influence of Great Moravia. The Přemyslid prince Bořivoj, baptised under Moravian influence, moved his seat to Prague Castle around 880. After Great Moravia weakened under Magyar attacks, his son Spytihněv in 895 swore allegiance instead to the East Frankish king Arnulf at Regensburg, and Bohemia began to develop as a separate principality.

## Wenceslaus and Boleslaus

Duke Wenceslaus, who ruled from 921, submitted to the German king Henry I in 929 and was later murdered by his brother Boleslaus, who took power in 935. Wenceslaus came to be venerated as Bohemia's patron saint. Boleslaus I conquered Moravia and Silesia and pushed as far as Kraków, but after a long conflict he accepted the overlordship of [Otto I](/person/otto-i) in 950. Bohemian troops fought beside the Germans against the Magyars at the [Battle of Lechfeld](/event/battle-of-lechfeld) in 955. In 973 a bishopric was founded at Prague, subject to the archbishop of Mainz. In 995 the Přemyslids defeated their rivals, the Slavník family, and united the Czech lands under one ruling house.

## Within the empire

In 1002 Duke Vladivoj received Bohemia as a fief from the German king Henry II, making it part of the empire. Poland's Bolesław I briefly seized the country soon afterwards, but was driven out in 1004. Bretislaus I regained Moravia, which thereafter was usually held by a junior member of the ducal family, and in 1039 raided Poland as far as Gniezno, though the German king Henry forced him in 1041 to give up all his gains except Moravia. Two dukes won personal royal titles as rewards for supporting the emperors: Vratislaus II in 1085 and Vladislaus II in 1158. Neither title passed to their heirs.

## Economy and society

The duchy profited in the tenth and eleventh centuries from Prague's slave trade, which sent captives taken from pagan Slav peoples as far as Muslim Spain. Mining of silver and tin in the Ore Mountains began in the early twelfth century. Christianity spread through missions from Moravia and from the Bavarian bishops of Regensburg and Passau.

## Becoming a kingdom

During the German civil war between Philip of Swabia and Otto IV, Duke Ottokar I backed Philip and was rewarded in 1198 with a royal crown, this time hereditary. In 1212 the emperor [Frederick II](/person/frederick-ii-holy-roman-emperor) confirmed the title for Ottokar and his descendants in the Golden Bull of Sicily, which also freed Bohemia from most imperial obligations. The duchy had become the Kingdom of Bohemia, which the Přemyslids ruled until their male line died out in 1306.
