Sweden emerged as a kingdom in the early Middle Ages from the union of two peoples, the Svear around Lake Mälaren and the Götar further south. For most of its history it was a sparsely populated northern country, but in the seventeenth century it became one of the great powers of Europe.

## Viking Sweden

During the Viking Age, Swedish Vikings, known as Varangians, travelled east across the Baltic and along the rivers of Russia to Constantinople and the Caspian Sea, trading furs and slaves for silver; hoards of Arab silver coins have been found in Sweden, especially on Gotland. Runestones across Sweden record journeys and deaths abroad. The kingdom was gradually united, and Christianity spread from the tenth century; the pagan temple at Uppsala was described by the chronicler Adam of Bremen around 1070.

## The medieval kingdom

Swedish kings and nobles expanded into Finland from the twelfth century in crusades against the Finns. Birger Jarl, regent in the mid-thirteenth century, is traditionally credited with founding Stockholm. Saint Birgitta of Sweden, a fourteenth-century mystic, founded the Bridgettine order. The Hanseatic League, a federation of German trading cities, dominated Swedish trade, and German merchants lived in Stockholm and Visby. The Kalmar Union with Denmark and Norway from 1397 was resented by many Swedes.

## Gustav Vasa

Gustav Vasa led a rebellion after the Stockholm Bloodbath of 1520 and was elected king in 1523, founding the Vasa dynasty and an independent, centralised state. He confiscated church property, made Sweden Lutheran and made the monarchy hereditary. The Bible was translated into Swedish in 1541.

## The age of greatness

Sweden's period as a great power, stormaktstiden, lasted from 1611 to 1721. Gustavus Adolphus reformed the army with mobile field artillery and disciplined infantry, and intervened in the Thirty Years' War, winning at Breitenfeld in 1631. His daughter Queen Christina, who invited Descartes to her court, abdicated in 1654, converted to Catholicism and moved to Rome. Charles X Gustav marched his army across the frozen sea to Copenhagen in 1658, forcing Denmark to cede Skåne. The warship Vasa sank on its maiden voyage in Stockholm harbour in 1628; raised in 1961, it is now a museum.

## Charles XII

Charles XII, king from 1697, won stunning victories in the Great Northern War, defeating Russia at Narva in 1700. But he invaded Russia and was crushed at Poltava in 1709, fled to the Ottoman Empire, and was killed in Norway in 1718. Sweden lost its Baltic provinces to Russia by the Treaty of Nystad in 1721.

## The Age of Liberty and Gustav III

After 1719, the Riksdag, the parliament, held power, in the Age of Liberty, with parties called the Hats and Caps. Science flourished: Carl Linnaeus created the system for naming species, and Anders Celsius devised his temperature scale. Gustav III restored royal power in 1772, supported the arts, founding the Swedish Academy, and was assassinated at a masked ball in 1792.

## Modern Sweden

After losing Finland in 1809, Sweden turned to peace. In the nineteenth century, about a million Swedes emigrated to America, fleeing poverty. Industrialisation, popular movements and the Social Democratic Party transformed Sweden, which became a parliamentary democracy with universal suffrage by 1921. Under long Social Democratic governments from 1932, Sweden built a comprehensive welfare state. Swedes such as Alfred Nobel, who founded the Nobel Prizes, and companies like Volvo and IKEA became known worldwide.

## On our maps

Our maps show Sweden on most maps from 1000 to 2010. Its largest outline, about 920,000 square kilometres on the 1700 map, includes Finland, Estonia and parts of Latvia and Russia, which together make up more than half of it. Riga and Tallinn are Swedish on our maps through the seventeenth century, Helsinki until 1815, and the site of Saint Petersburg until 1783 on the basemap, although Russia founded the city there in 1703.
