The First French Empire was Napoleon Bonaparte's state. It lasted from 1804, when Napoleon crowned himself emperor, until his defeat in 1814, with a brief return in 1815 known as the Hundred Days. At its height it dominated continental Europe from Spain to Poland.

## From general to emperor

Napoleon, a Corsican artillery officer, rose to fame in the wars of the French Revolution and took power in a coup in 1799 as First Consul. In December 1804 he crowned himself emperor in Notre-Dame in Paris, in the presence of the pope. The Napoleonic Code, issued that year, set out civil law based on equality before the law and property rights, and became the model for legal systems across Europe and beyond.

## The imperial court

Napoleon created a new imperial nobility, granting titles to his marshals, ministers and supporters, and founded the Legion of Honour in 1802 to reward service. His court at the Tuileries and Saint-Cloud revived royal ceremony. In 1810 he divorced Joséphine, who had not given him children, and married Marie-Louise of Austria, daughter of the Austrian emperor, and in 1811 their son was born, given the title King of Rome.

## Government

The empire was highly centralised. Prefects governed each department, appointed by and answerable to Paris. The Napoleonic Code, issued in 1804, unified French civil law, guaranteeing equality before the law, freedom of religion and property rights, while reinforcing the authority of husbands and fathers. Napoleon created the lycées, secondary schools, and the University of France. The Bank of France, founded in 1800, stabilised the currency. Censorship and police surveillance, under Joseph Fouché, controlled opposition.

## Religion

Napoleon's Concordat with Pope Pius VII in 1801 restored the Catholic Church in France while keeping it under state control. Protestant and Jewish communities were also organised by the state; in 1806–1807 Napoleon convened a Grand Sanhedrin of Jewish leaders. Conflict with the pope later led Napoleon to annex the Papal States and imprison Pius VII.

## The Grand Empire

At its height in 1811, the French Empire included 130 departments, from Hamburg and Lübeck in the north to Rome in the south and the Illyrian Provinces on the Adriatic. Beyond it lay client states ruled by Napoleon's relatives and allies: the Kingdom of Italy, Naples, Spain, Westphalia, the Confederation of the Rhine, the Swiss Confederation and the Duchy of Warsaw. Napoleonic rule abolished feudalism and introduced the civil code in many of these lands, with lasting effects.

## War and its costs

Napoleon's wars required constant conscription, and around a million French soldiers died. The Continental System, which banned trade with Britain from 1806, damaged European economies and encouraged smuggling. In Spain, a long guerrilla war tied down hundreds of thousands of French troops; the painter Goya depicted its horrors. The invasion of Russia in 1812 destroyed the Grande Armée.

## Fall and the Hundred Days

After Leipzig in 1813, the allies invaded France, and Paris fell in March 1814. Napoleon abdicated and was exiled to Elba, while the Bourbon Louis XVIII took the throne. In March 1815 Napoleon landed in France, and troops sent to stop him joined him. During the Hundred Days, he issued a more liberal constitution, but was defeated at Waterloo on 18 June 1815 and exiled to Saint Helena.

## Legacy

Napoleon's empire spread the ideas of the French Revolution, the civil code and modern administration across Europe, and its collapse led to the Congress of Vienna. His nephew became Emperor Napoleon III, founding the Second Empire from 1852 to 1870. Napoleon's tomb lies in Les Invalides in Paris.
