The Göktürks, or Celestial Turks, built the first great empire to call itself Turkic. Starting in the 550s, from the Altai mountains, they created a khaganate that stretched across the Eurasian steppe from Manchuria to the Black Sea within a generation. They also left the oldest known texts in a Turkic language.

## The Ashina clan

The ruling clan of the Göktürks, the Ashina, had a legend that their ancestor was a boy raised by a she-wolf, and the wolf's head appeared on their banners. The Turks lived in the Altai mountains and worked iron for the Rouran. "Kök" in Old Turkic means "blue" or "celestial", and the name Göktürk may mean "Celestial Turks", referring to the sky god Tengri, the supreme deity of the steppe.

## The First Turkic Khaganate

The Turks were originally ironworkers subject to the Rouran, the ruling nomads of Mongolia. In 552 their leader, Bumin, overthrew the Rouran and took the title khagan. His son Muqan and his brother Istemi, who ruled in the west, expanded rapidly, defeating the Hephthalites in Central Asia together with Sasanian Persia. The Turks controlled the Silk Road and sent embassies to Constantinople to arrange the silk trade, bypassing Persia. Chinese states paid them huge tributes in silk.

## The Western Turks

Istemi, Bumin's brother, ruled the western part of the empire as yabgu. Allied with the Sasanian king Khosrow I, he destroyed the Hephthalites around 560 and divided their lands. He then turned against Persia and sent an embassy, led by the Sogdian Maniakh, to Constantinople in 568, seeking an alliance with Byzantium and a way to sell silk directly. A Byzantine envoy, Zemarchus, visited the khagan's court in the Altai. The Western Turks dominated Central Asia, including Sogdiana, and later fought both Persia and Byzantium.

## The split

Around 581–583, the empire divided into the Eastern Khaganate, in Mongolia, and the Western Khaganate, in Central Asia, after rivalries within the Ashina. The Sui dynasty encouraged the division. The Eastern Turks raided China and supported rebels during the Sui collapse.

## Under the Tang

In 630, the Tang emperor Taizong defeated the Eastern Turks, capturing the khagan Illig. Many Turks served in the Tang army, and the Turkic tribes gave Taizong the title "Heavenly Khagan". The Western Turks were defeated by Tang armies in the 650s. For about fifty years, the Turks were under Chinese rule.

## The Second Turkic Khaganate

In 682, Ilterish Khagan led a revolt and re-established the khaganate in Mongolia, with the help of his adviser Tonyukuk. His brother Kapaghan Khagan expanded it. Bilge Khagan, who ruled from 716 to 734, and his brother Kul Tegin, the army commander, brought it to its height, with Tonyukuk as a wise adviser.

## The Orkhon inscriptions

In the Orkhon valley in Mongolia, monuments to Kul Tegin, who died in 731, and Bilge Khagan, who died in 734, carry inscriptions in Old Turkic runic script and Chinese. They were rediscovered in 1889 and deciphered by the Danish linguist Vilhelm Thomsen in 1893. In them, Bilge Khagan speaks to his people, recounting their history, warning that Chinese silks and sweet words had made the Turks forget their ways, and praising the deeds of his brother. Tonyukuk's own inscription tells his story. They are the oldest known Turkic texts.

## The end and legacy

After Bilge Khagan was poisoned in 734, the khaganate fell into conflict. In 744 the Uyghurs, Basmyls and Karluks overthrew it. The name "Türk" spread to many peoples, and the Turkic languages spread across Central Asia and Anatolia. Modern Turkey and the Central Asian Turkic peoples honour the Göktürks as ancestors.

## On our maps

Our maps show the Göktürks on the 600 and 700 maps. Their largest outline, about 3.7 million square kilometres on the 700 map, is split between today's China and Mongolia, with parts in Russia and Kazakhstan. Ulaanbaatar's site lies inside it. Before them, the region belongs to the Rouran; by 800, to the Uyghurs.
