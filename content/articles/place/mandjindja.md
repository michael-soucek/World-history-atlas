In the sandhill country south of the Warburton Range, deep in Western Australia's interior, lies the traditional land of the Mandjindja, also written Mantjintjarra or Manytjilytjarra. They belong to the Western Desert cultural bloc, whose members speak dialects of one language across a huge area of the arid interior. In recent decades they have pursued recognition of their land rights together with the Ngalia.

## Country

Almost everything on record about Mandjindja country comes from estimates by Norman Tindale, whose figures for Western Desert peoples are not regarded as accurate. He put their land at about 21,000 square miles (roughly 54,000 square kilometres), beginning at a place called Papakula, marked on maps as Babbagoola Rockhole. To the west it reached Lakes Gillen and Throssell; Amy Rocks and the Saunders Range marked the south; and in the east it ran to a place called Lenga:na, perhaps east of the Sydney Yeo Chasm.

## Language and names

The Mandjindja speak Mantjintjarra, one dialect of the Western Desert language, a chain of closely related and mutually intelligible varieties that also includes [Ngaanyatjarra](/place/ngaanyatjarra) and [Pitjantjatjara](/place/pitjantjatjara). Their neighbours had their own names for them. To Ngaanyatjarra speakers they and the Wenamba were the Kalgoneidjara, while Pitjantjatjara speakers used Nanggarangku, meaning "hostile men", for the Mandjindja and the Ngalia.

## Movement to Kalgoorlie

The Mandjindja living today in Kalgoorlie may be descendants of Manyjilyjarra people driven out of the Gibson and Great Sandy deserts by a prolonged drought in 1906 and later, from the 1920s, made their way to Kalgoorlie and other towns. Kalgoorlie is one of several towns on the desert fringe where Western Desert people now live, alongside others such as Laverton and Meekatharra.

## Native title

The Mandjindja and Ngalia have sought legal recognition of their rights through the Federal Court. An earlier claim, lodged in 1996, was dismissed. In March 2009 the joint Mantjintjarra Ngalia claim passed the registration test under the Native Title Act. The claim area runs west from Lake Wells to Lakes Darlot and Miranda, and takes in country from Cosmo Newberry and Leonora to Lake Barlee and north as far as Wiluna.
