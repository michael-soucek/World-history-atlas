The modern Socialist Republic of Vietnam was formed in 1976, when North and South Vietnam were reunited after three decades of war. But Vietnam's history as a distinct country is much older, shaped above all by its relationship with China to the north.

## Ancient Vietnam

The Dong Son culture of the Red River valley, from around 1000 BCE, is famous for its large bronze drums. Vietnamese legends tell of the Hùng kings, who ruled the kingdom of Văn Lang. In 111 BCE the Han dynasty conquered the region, beginning a millennium of Chinese rule. The Trưng sisters led a revolt in 40 CE that briefly expelled the Chinese, and Lady Triệu led another in 248.

## Independent dynasties

After independence in 938, Vietnamese dynasties built a state modelled partly on China, with Confucian examinations and Buddhism. The Lý dynasty founded the Temple of Literature in 1070, Vietnam's first university. The Trần dynasty defeated three Mongol invasions in the thirteenth century. After the Ming occupation of 1407–1427, Lê Lợi founded the Lê dynasty; legend says he received a magic sword from a golden turtle in Hoàn Kiếm Lake in Hanoi and returned it after victory.

## The southward advance

Over centuries, Vietnamese settlers and armies moved south along the coast, a process called Nam tiến, absorbing the Cham kingdom of Champa and, in the seventeenth and eighteenth centuries, the Mekong delta, previously Khmer. From the sixteenth to eighteenth centuries, Vietnam was divided between the Trịnh lords in the north and the Nguyễn lords in the south. The Tây Sơn rebellion of 1771 overthrew both.

## The Nguyễn dynasty and French rule

Nguyễn Ánh, with French help, defeated the Tây Sơn and became Emperor Gia Long in 1802, ruling from Huế, where he built a citadel. France conquered Vietnam between 1858 and 1885, dividing it into Cochinchina, a colony, and the protectorates of Annam and Tonkin. The French built railways and plantations but suppressed resistance, and Vietnamese nationalists, such as Phan Bội Châu and Phan Châu Trinh, sought independence.

## Revolution and war

Hồ Chí Minh founded the Indochinese Communist Party in 1930 and the Viet Minh in 1941. After Japan's surrender, the Viet Minh took power in the August Revolution and declared independence on 2 September 1945. The famine of 1944–1945 had killed about a million people in the north. The First Indochina War against France ended with the French defeat at Điện Biên Phủ in 1954, and the country was divided. The Vietnam War followed, with massive American bombing and the deaths of millions, ending with the fall of Saigon in 1975.

## After reunification

Vietnam was reunified as the Socialist Republic of Vietnam in 1976. Hundreds of thousands of people, the "boat people", fled by sea. Vietnam invaded Cambodia in 1978, overthrowing the Khmer Rouge, and fought a border war with China in 1979. Economic hardship led to the Đổi Mới reforms of 1986, which introduced a market economy under Communist Party rule. Vietnam restored relations with the United States in 1995 and joined the World Trade Organization in 2007. Today it is one of Asia's fastest-growing economies, a major manufacturer and exporter, and its population exceeds 100 million.
