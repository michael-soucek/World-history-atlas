Around the town of Boulia in western Queensland lies the country of the Pitapita, whose name is also written Pitta Pitta. Their language is the best recorded of its local group, and in 2012 the Federal Court confirmed their native title across much of the Boulia district.

## Country

The exact limits of Pitapita land are uncertain. The earliest detailed account, published by Walter Roth in 1897 after a period at Boulia, describes many small groups and local bands in a way later scholars found hard to map. Norman Tindale concluded that the Pitapita held about 2,700 square miles (roughly 7,000 square kilometres) in what is now the Shire of Boulia, stretching from Fort William southwards past Boulia for about fifty miles. Their land adjoined that of the Wanggamala people. Pitapita initiation rites traditionally included circumcision and subincision.

## Language

Pitapita belongs to the Karnic group within the Pama–Nyungan family and was the best-described member of an eastern dialect group that also included Rangwa, Kunkalanya, Ngulupulu and Ringa-Ringa, and it is closely related to Wangka-Yutjurru further west. The Pitapita also used an elaborate sign language alongside speech. The word pituri, for a stimulant leaf chewed by Aboriginal people, has been claimed to come from Pitapita, though Roth recorded it as the neighbouring Yurlayurlanya term, noting that the Pitapita called the plant tarembola.

In 1979 the linguist Barry Blake reported only three remaining speakers, at Boulia and Mount Isa. The language is now thought to have no fluent speakers, but Roth's grammar and Blake's later work preserve a detailed record of it.

## Contact and survival

The opening of the Boulia district to white settlement drove many groups off their land. Roth wrote that privation, disease, alcohol and guns had destroyed whole communities, and estimated that no more than about 200 Aboriginal people remained in the entire district by the time of his stay. Pitapita families nevertheless kept their identity and connection to country. In 2012 a Federal Court determination recognised their native title rights over about 30,000 square kilometres around Boulia.
