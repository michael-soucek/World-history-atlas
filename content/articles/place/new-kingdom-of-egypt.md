The New Kingdom, from about 1550 to 1070 BCE, was the height of ancient Egypt's power. Its pharaohs built an empire reaching from Nubia to Syria, and its rulers include the best-known names in Egyptian history: Hatshepsut, Akhenaten, Tutankhamun and Ramesses II.

## The Hyksos and the Theban revival

During the Second Intermediate Period, the Hyksos ruled the north from Avaris, introducing the horse and chariot, the composite bow and new bronze weapons. The Theban kings of the Seventeenth Dynasty began a war of liberation; King Seqenenre Tao's mummy shows terrible head wounds from battle. His son Kamose continued the war, and his brother Ahmose I took Avaris and drove out the Hyksos around 1550 BCE.

## The Egyptian empire

The New Kingdom pharaohs built an empire. Thutmose I campaigned to the Euphrates and to the fourth cataract in Nubia. Thutmose III, after Hatshepsut's death, led seventeen campaigns into the Levant, winning at Megiddo around 1457 BCE, and made Egypt the dominant power in the region. Egypt ruled the Levantine city-states through local princes, whose sons were educated at the Egyptian court. Nubia was ruled by a viceroy, the "King's Son of Kush", and its gold made Egypt rich.

## Thebes and Amun

Thebes, the dynasty's home, became the religious capital, and its god Amun, combined with the sun god as Amun-Ra, became the king of the gods. The temples of Karnak and Luxor were greatly enlarged, with obelisks, pylons and the hypostyle hall. Kings were buried in the Valley of the Kings, in rock-cut tombs decorated with texts guiding the king through the underworld, such as the Amduat, while their memorial temples stood on the west bank. Workers who built the tombs lived at the village of Deir el-Medina, whose records reveal their daily lives.

## Amarna

Akhenaten, who ruled from around 1353 to 1336 BCE, promoted the worship of the Aten, the sun disc, above all other gods, closed traditional temples and founded a new capital at Amarna. Amarna art showed the royal family, including Queen Nefertiti, in a new, intimate style. The Amarna letters, cuneiform tablets found there, record diplomatic correspondence with Babylon, Mitanni, the Hittites and Levantine princes. After his death, the old religion was restored under Tutankhamun, and Akhenaten was later treated as a heretic.

## Tutankhamun

Tutankhamun became king as a boy and died around nineteen. His tomb, found almost intact by Howard Carter in 1922, contained over 5,000 objects, including the gold mask and coffins, chariots, furniture and jewellery, giving the world a vivid picture of royal burial.

## The Ramesside period

The Nineteenth and Twentieth Dynasties, the Ramesside period, were founded by the general Ramesses I. Seti I and Ramesses II fought the Hittites, and Ramesses II built Abu Simbel and Pi-Ramesses. Merneptah's stele of around 1208 BCE contains the earliest known mention of Israel. Ramesses III defeated the Sea Peoples.

## Ramesses II

The Nineteenth Dynasty's Ramesses II ruled for about 66 years in the thirteenth century BCE. He fought the Hittites at Kadesh around 1274 BCE and later signed one of the earliest known peace treaties with them. He built on a vast scale, at Abu Simbel, at Karnak and at his new capital, Pi-Ramesses, in the delta.

## Decline

In the Twentieth Dynasty, Egypt lost its empire in the Levant, faced economic difficulties and inflation, and the workers of Deir el-Medina went on strike, the first recorded strike in history, around 1157 BCE, when their rations were not paid. Royal tombs were robbed. The high priests of Amun at Thebes became powerful, and after Ramesses XI, Egypt divided.
