The Union of Soviet Socialist Republics was the world's first communist state, founded in 1922 and dissolved in 1991. It was the largest country on earth, a federation of fifteen republics by the end, and for over four decades one of two superpowers that shaped the world.

## Revolution and civil war

In October 1917 the Bolsheviks, led by Vladimir Lenin, seized power in Russia. They took Russia out of the First World War with the Treaty of Brest-Litovsk in 1918, giving up large territories, and fought a civil war against the anti-Bolshevik Whites, nationalist movements and foreign interventions. The Red Army, organised by Leon Trotsky, won by 1921. The Cheka, the secret police, carried out the Red Terror. In December 1922 Russia, Ukraine, Belarus and the Transcaucasian republic formed the Soviet Union. Lenin's New Economic Policy allowed some private trade.

## Stalin's revolution

After Lenin's death in 1924, Joseph Stalin defeated his rivals. From 1928 he launched five-year plans of rapid industrialisation, building steel works, dams and cities such as Magnitogorsk. He forced peasants into collective farms, deporting or killing millions labelled kulaks, wealthier peasants. The resulting famine of 1932–1933 killed millions, especially in Ukraine, where it is called the Holodomor, and Kazakhstan. In the Great Terror of 1936–1938, about 750,000 people were executed and over a million sent to the Gulag labour camps, including much of the Communist Party's leadership and the army's officers. Whole peoples, such as the Chechens, Crimean Tatars and Volga Germans, were later deported.

## The Great Patriotic War

In August 1939 the Soviet Union signed a non-aggression pact with Nazi Germany, dividing eastern Europe. It occupied eastern Poland, the Baltic states and Bessarabia, and fought Finland in the Winter War. In 1940, the NKVD murdered about 22,000 Polish officers and others at Katyn and other sites. On 22 June 1941 Germany invaded. The Soviets suffered catastrophic early losses, and Leningrad was besieged for nearly 900 days, during which about a million civilians died. The Red Army held Moscow in 1941, won at Stalingrad in 1942–1943 and Kursk in 1943, and took Berlin in 1945. About 27 million Soviet citizens died.

## Superpower

After 1945 the Soviet Union imposed communist governments on eastern Europe and faced the United States in the Cold War. It tested its first atomic bomb in 1949. After Stalin's death in 1953, Nikita Khrushchev denounced his crimes in a secret speech in 1956, released many Gulag prisoners and introduced the "Thaw", but crushed the Hungarian uprising in 1956. The Soviet space programme launched Sputnik, the first satellite, in 1957, and Yuri Gagarin, the first human in space, in 1961. Khrushchev's placement of missiles in Cuba led to the Cuban Missile Crisis of 1962.

## Stagnation

Under Leonid Brezhnev, from 1964 to 1982, the Soviet Union reached military parity with the United States but its economy stagnated. It crushed the Prague Spring in Czechoslovakia in 1968 and invaded Afghanistan in 1979, beginning a costly ten-year war. Dissidents, such as Andrei Sakharov and Aleksandr Solzhenitsyn, were persecuted.

## Gorbachev and collapse

Mikhail Gorbachev, who became leader in 1985, introduced glasnost, openness, and perestroika, restructuring. The Chernobyl nuclear disaster of 1986 exposed government secrecy. Gorbachev withdrew from Afghanistan, signed arms control treaties and allowed eastern Europe to throw off communism in 1989. Nationalist movements grew in the republics. After a failed coup by hardliners in August 1991, the republics declared independence, and on 26 December 1991 the Soviet Union was dissolved.
