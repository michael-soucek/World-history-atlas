The United Kingdom was formed in stages. England and Wales had been joined since the sixteenth century. The Acts of Union of 1707 united England and Scotland as the Kingdom of Great Britain, and a further act in 1801 added Ireland, creating the United Kingdom of Great Britain and Ireland. When most of Ireland became independent in 1922, the state took its present name, the United Kingdom of Great Britain and Northern Ireland.

## The Act of Union with Ireland

After the Irish rebellion of 1798, the Act of Union of 1800 abolished the Irish Parliament and created the United Kingdom of Great Britain and Ireland from 1 January 1801. Catholic emancipation, allowing Catholics to sit in Parliament, came only in 1829. The Great Famine of 1845–1852, caused by potato blight and worsened by government policy, killed about a million people in Ireland and drove another million to emigrate.

## The workshop of the world

In the nineteenth century, Britain was the world's leading industrial power. Railways spread from 1830, steamships connected the empire, and cities such as Manchester, Birmingham and Glasgow grew rapidly. The Great Exhibition of 1851 in London's Crystal Palace showcased British industry. Britain adopted free trade, repealing the Corn Laws in 1846. Social reformers fought child labour, unhealthy housing and poverty, and the novels of Charles Dickens highlighted the conditions of the poor.

## Reform and democracy

The Reform Act of 1832 extended the vote to more middle-class men, and acts of 1867 and 1884 extended it to many working men. The Chartists campaigned for universal male suffrage. The Labour Party was founded in 1900. The suffragettes, led by Emmeline Pankhurst, fought for votes for women, which were granted to some women in 1918 and to all women on equal terms in 1928. The Parliament Act of 1911 limited the power of the House of Lords.

## Ireland

Irish demands for home rule grew, and in 1914 a Home Rule Act was passed but suspended for the war. After the Easter Rising of 1916 in Dublin and the Irish War of Independence from 1919 to 1921, the Anglo-Irish Treaty created the Irish Free State in 1922, while six counties of Northern Ireland remained in the United Kingdom. The state became the United Kingdom of Great Britain and Northern Ireland in 1927.

## The world wars

In the First World War, about 880,000 British soldiers died. In the Second, Britain fought from 1939, and in 1940 faced the Blitz, the German bombing of London and other cities. Under Churchill, Britain fought on until the Allied victory. The war cost Britain much of its wealth and left it in debt to the United States.

## The welfare state

The Labour government of Clement Attlee, elected in 1945, created the National Health Service in 1948, offering free healthcare, expanded social security and nationalised coal, railways and other industries. Britain decolonised most of its empire in the following decades, and immigration from the Caribbean, South Asia and Africa, beginning with the Windrush generation in 1948, transformed British society.

## Modern Britain

Margaret Thatcher, prime minister from 1979 to 1990, privatised industries, reduced the power of trade unions and won the Falklands War of 1982. Northern Ireland's Troubles, a conflict between republicans and unionists that killed about 3,500 people from the late 1960s, largely ended with the Good Friday Agreement of 1998. Scotland held an independence referendum in 2014, which voted to stay. Britain left the European Union on 31 January 2020. Queen Elizabeth II reigned from 1952 to 2022, the longest reign in British history.

## On our maps

Our maps use this name from 1945 to 2010. Its largest outline, about 10 million square kilometres on the 1945 map, is mostly British territory in Africa, such as Sudan, Nigeria, Kenya and the Rhodesias, which the basemap counts under the United Kingdom at that date. The UK itself makes up only about 2 percent of it. Cairo, Khartoum and Jerusalem are under British control on our 1945 map, as are Hamburg and Cologne in the British occupation zone of Germany.
