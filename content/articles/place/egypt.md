Egypt has one of the longest histories of any country, from the pharaohs to the present day. This page covers modern Egypt, which took shape in the nineteenth and twentieth centuries; for earlier periods, see pages such as the [Ptolemaic Kingdom](/place/ptolemaic-kingdom) and the [Mamluk Sultanate](/place/mamluk-sultanate).

## Napoleon in Egypt

In 1798 Napoleon invaded Egypt, defeating the Mamluks at the Battle of the Pyramids. The British admiral Nelson destroyed the French fleet at the Nile, and the French army was eventually defeated in 1801. Napoleon brought scholars who produced the Description of Egypt, a vast study, and found the Rosetta Stone, which Champollion used to decipher hieroglyphs in 1822. The expedition began Europe's fascination with ancient Egypt, and the modern science of Egyptology.

## Muhammad Ali's dynasty

Muhammad Ali, governor from 1805 to 1848, massacred the Mamluk leaders in the Cairo Citadel in 1811 and built a modern state: a conscript army, factories, schools, a printing press and the cultivation of long-staple cotton for export. He conquered Sudan in 1820–1822 and, through his son Ibrahim, Syria. His grandson Ismail, khedive from 1863 to 1879, built railways, telegraphs, opera houses and new quarters of Cairo, aiming to make Egypt "part of Europe". The Suez Canal, built by the French engineer Ferdinand de Lesseps, opened in 1869.

## The British occupation

Ismail's spending led to bankruptcy, and he sold Egypt's shares in the canal to Britain in 1875. European control of Egypt's finances provoked a nationalist revolt led by Colonel Ahmed Urabi, and in 1882 Britain invaded and occupied Egypt. The British consul-general, Lord Cromer, effectively ruled for decades. In 1919 a nationwide revolution demanded independence, and in 1922 Britain recognised Egypt as a kingdom, though it kept troops and influence.

## Monarchy and revolution

The monarchy, under King Fuad and his son Farouk, coexisted with parliamentary politics dominated by the Wafd party and British influence. Defeat in the 1948 war against Israel discredited the government. On 23 July 1952, the Free Officers, led by Gamal Abdel Nasser and Muhammad Naguib, overthrew Farouk. Egypt became a republic in 1953.

## Nasser

Nasser became president in 1956. He carried out land reform, nationalised industries and built the Aswan High Dam with Soviet help, completed in 1970, which ended the annual Nile flood and generated electricity. His nationalisation of the Suez Canal in 1956 led to the Suez Crisis, which made him a hero of Arab nationalism. Egypt and Syria formed the United Arab Republic from 1958 to 1961. Defeat in the Six-Day War of 1967 was a heavy blow.

## Sadat and Mubarak

Anwar Sadat launched the October War of 1973, crossing the Suez Canal. He opened Egypt's economy, expelled Soviet advisers and allied with the United States. In 1977 he travelled to Jerusalem, and the Camp David Accords of 1978 led to the peace treaty of 1979, for which he shared the Nobel Peace Prize with Menachem Begin. Islamist militants assassinated him in 1981. Hosni Mubarak ruled until mass protests in Tahrir Square forced him out in 2011.

## Recent years

Mohamed Morsi of the Muslim Brotherhood was elected president in 2012 and overthrown by the military in 2013. Abdel Fattah el-Sisi became president in 2014. Egypt, with over 100 million people, remains the most populous Arab country.
