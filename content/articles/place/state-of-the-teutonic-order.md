The Teutonic Order was a German military religious order founded at Acre, in the Holy Land, during the Crusades. In the thirteenth century it turned its crusading to the Baltic, conquering the pagan Prussians and Livonians and creating a state ruled by monk-knights along the south-eastern shore of the Baltic Sea.

## Founding

The order began as a field hospital founded by German merchants from Bremen and Lübeck during the siege of Acre in 1190, and became a military order in 1198, the Order of Brothers of the German House of Saint Mary in Jerusalem. Its members took vows of poverty, chastity and obedience, wore white mantles with a black cross and fought as knights. It received lands across the Holy Roman Empire and, for a time, in Hungary's Transylvania, where it was expelled in 1225.

## The Prussian crusade

In 1226 Konrad, Duke of Masovia, a Polish prince, invited the order to help fight the pagan Prussians, a Baltic people, who raided his lands. Emperor Frederick II granted the order rights over the conquered lands in the Golden Bull of Rimini. From 1230 the knights, joined by crusaders from across Europe, conquered Prussia over about fifty years, building castles, such as Thorn, now Toruń, Kulm and Königsberg, founded in 1255 and named after the Bohemian king Ottokar II. The Prussians rebelled several times and were largely killed, converted or assimilated; their language died out by the seventeenth century.

## Livonia

In 1237 the order absorbed the Livonian Brothers of the Sword, a crusading order that had conquered much of today's Latvia and Estonia, which became its Livonian branch, with its own master. In 1242, at the Battle on the Ice on Lake Peipus, Alexander Nevsky of Novgorod defeated the order's Livonian knights, halting its expansion east.

## Marienburg

In 1309 the grand master moved his headquarters from Venice to Marienburg, today's Malbork, in Prussia. The castle was expanded into the largest brick castle in the world. The same year, the order seized Danzig and Pomerelia from Poland, massacring many inhabitants, which caused lasting conflict with Poland.

## A prosperous state

The order's state was well organised, divided into districts run by commanders, komturs. German settlers founded towns and villages under German law, and cities such as Danzig, Elbing, Thorn and Königsberg were members of the Hanseatic League, trading grain, timber, amber and furs. The order controlled the amber trade. Knights from across Europe, such as the future Henry IV of England, came to join "reises", campaigns against pagan Lithuania.

## Grunwald

In 1386 Grand Duke Jogaila of Lithuania married Queen Jadwiga of Poland and accepted Christianity, removing the order's crusading justification. On 15 July 1410, at Grunwald, or Tannenberg, a Polish-Lithuanian army under Władysław II Jagiełło and Vytautas crushed the order's army, killing the grand master Ulrich von Jungingen and many knights, one of the largest battles of medieval Europe. The order survived, holding Marienburg, but had to pay huge indemnities.

## Decline

The order's towns and nobles, resenting its taxes and rule, formed the Prussian Confederation and in 1454 rebelled, asking the king of Poland for protection. In the Thirteen Years' War, from 1454 to 1466, the order lost western Prussia, including Danzig and Marienburg, to Poland, and became a Polish vassal in eastern Prussia, moving its capital to Königsberg.

## Secularisation

In 1525 the grand master Albrecht of Brandenburg-Ansbach, influenced by Martin Luther, converted to Lutheranism and turned the order's Prussian lands into the secular Duchy of Prussia, a hereditary fief of Poland. In 1618 it passed to the Hohenzollerns of Brandenburg, forming the basis of the later Kingdom of Prussia. The Livonian Order was dissolved in 1561 during the Livonian War. The Teutonic Order survived in the Holy Roman Empire and still exists today as a Catholic religious order, based in Vienna.
