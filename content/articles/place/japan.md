Japan's history as a unified state begins with the Yamato court in the fifth to seventh centuries, centred on the plains around Nara and Osaka. Its rulers claimed descent from the sun goddess Amaterasu, and the imperial line they founded is the oldest continuous hereditary monarchy in the world.

## Early Japan

People have lived in Japan for over 30,000 years. The Jōmon culture, from around 14,000 to 300 BCE, made some of the world's oldest pottery. The Yayoi period brought rice farming and metalworking from the Asian mainland. Chinese records describe Himiko, a queen of Yamatai, in the third century CE. The Kofun period saw huge keyhole-shaped burial mounds, such as that attributed to Emperor Nintoku near Osaka.

## Nara and Heian

In 710 Japan built its first permanent capital at Nara, modelled on Tang Chang'an, and the great Buddha of Tōdai-ji was dedicated in 752. The earliest Japanese histories, the Kojiki and Nihon Shoki, were compiled in 712 and 720. In 794 the capital moved to Heian-kyō, Kyoto, which remained the imperial capital until 1868. Heian court culture produced the kana scripts, The Tale of Genji and The Pillow Book.

## Samurai and shoguns

From 1185, military governments, shogunates, held real power. The Kamakura shogunate repelled the Mongols in 1274 and 1281. The Ashikaga shogunate presided over Zen culture, the tea ceremony and Noh theatre, but lost control, leading to the Sengoku civil wars. The Tokugawa shogunate, from 1603, brought peace and isolation. During the Edo period, Japan's cities grew, literacy spread, and arts such as kabuki and ukiyo-e flourished.

## Meiji and empire

After Perry's arrival in 1853, the Meiji Restoration of 1868 transformed Japan into a modern state. Japan won wars against China in 1894–1895 and Russia in 1904–1905, annexed Korea in 1910, and joined the Allies in the First World War. In the 1920s, under "Taishō democracy", parties governed, and men gained the vote in 1925. In the 1930s, militarists took control. Japan seized Manchuria in 1931 and invaded China in 1937.

## The Second World War

Japan attacked Pearl Harbor and Southeast Asia in December 1941. Its forces committed many atrocities, including the Nanjing Massacre, the forced labour of prisoners and the "comfort women" system. American bombing devastated Japanese cities, and the firebombing of Tokyo in March 1945 killed about 100,000 people. The atomic bombs on Hiroshima and Nagasaki in August 1945 killed over 200,000 by the end of the year. Emperor Hirohito announced Japan's surrender on 15 August 1945.

## Postwar Japan

The American occupation, led by General Douglas MacArthur, introduced a new constitution in 1947 that made the emperor a symbol, gave women the vote, and renounced war. Japan's economy grew rapidly, and by 1968 it was the world's second-largest economy, famous for cars, electronics and the Shinkansen bullet train, opened in 1964. After the asset bubble burst in 1990, Japan faced decades of slow growth. The earthquake and tsunami of 2011 killed about 20,000 people and caused the Fukushima nuclear disaster. Japan's population is ageing and shrinking.

## On our maps

Our maps label Japan at several dates, including 800, around 1500, from 1783 to 1815 and from 1960 to 2010; at others the basemap uses different names, such as Yamato or the Japanese Empire. Its largest outline, about 360,000 square kilometres on the 1800 map, matches the main islands. Tokyo and Kyoto are inside it on every map where it appears.
