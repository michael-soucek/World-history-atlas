The Comanche, who call themselves Nʉmʉnʉʉ, "the people", were the dominant power of the southern Great Plains of North America for much of the eighteenth and nineteenth centuries. Their territory, which the Spanish called Comanchería, stretched across parts of today's Texas, New Mexico, Oklahoma, Kansas and Colorado.

## From the mountains to the plains

The Comanche were once part of the Eastern Shoshone, living in the Great Basin and the Rocky Mountains of today's Wyoming. Around the end of the seventeenth century, after acquiring horses descended from those brought by the Spanish, they moved south-east onto the southern plains, perhaps drawn by bison herds and trade with Spanish New Mexico. Their language is a Numic language, closely related to Shoshone. They first appear in Spanish records in 1706.

## Horse culture

The Comanche became some of the finest horsemen in the world, famous for riding skills such as firing arrows from under the neck of a galloping horse. They kept huge horse herds, sometimes thousands per band, and bred and traded horses across the plains. Horses allowed them to hunt bison on horseback, and bison provided food, hides for tipis and clothing, tools and trade goods.

## Society

The Comanche were organised in independent bands, such as the Penateka, Kotsoteka, Yamparika, Nokoni and Quahadi, each led by chiefs chosen for their wisdom and success, with war leaders for raids. Councils made decisions by consensus. They valued individual bravery, and men earned status through warfare and raiding. Captives taken in raids, including Mexicans, Americans and other Native people, were often adopted into Comanche families.

## An empire of the plains

By the mid-eighteenth century, the Comanche had driven the Apache from much of the southern plains and controlled a vast trade network. They traded horses, mules, bison robes and captives with New Mexico, French Louisiana, and other Indigenous peoples. They raided Spanish settlements in Texas and New Mexico, and later deep into northern Mexico. In 1758 they and their allies destroyed the Spanish mission of San Sabá, in Texas. The historian Pekka Hämäläinen has called their power the "Comanche Empire", though others debate the term.

## Peace with Spain

In 1779 the Spanish governor of New Mexico, Juan Bautista de Anza, defeated the Comanche chief Cuerno Verde. In 1786 Anza and the Comanche leader Ecueracapa made a peace that lasted for decades, with trade fairs and joint campaigns against the Apache.

## Wars with Texas and the United States

After Mexican independence, Comanche raids into northern Mexico intensified in the 1830s and 1840s. Texas, independent from 1836, fought the Comanche; in 1840, Texan officials killed Comanche chiefs who had come for peace talks at the Council House in San Antonio. In 1849, cholera, brought by travellers to California, killed many Comanche. The Medicine Lodge Treaty of 1867 assigned them a reservation in Indian Territory, but many bands remained free.

## The end of the buffalo

In the 1870s, American hunters slaughtered millions of bison for their hides, destroying the Comanche way of life. After the Red River War of 1874–1875, including the attack on the hunters' post at Adobe Walls in 1874, the last free band, the Quahadi, led by Quanah Parker, son of a Comanche chief and a captured white woman, Cynthia Ann Parker, surrendered in 1875. Quanah became a leader on the reservation and a successful rancher.

## Today

The Comanche Nation, headquartered in Lawton, Oklahoma, has over 17,000 enrolled members. In the Second World War, Comanche code talkers served in the US Army in Europe, transmitting messages in their language.

## On our maps

Our maps show the Comanche on every map from 1500 to 1715, with an outline of up to about 330,000 square kilometres in today's United States. On the earliest maps the name stands for the region they later dominated, since the Comanche only moved onto the southern plains around 1700. San Antonio's site lies inside it on the 1500 map. On the 1783 map the region is shown as part of Spanish Louisiana.
