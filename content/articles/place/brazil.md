Brazil is the largest country in South America and the only one in the Americas whose language is Portuguese. It was a Portuguese colony for over three centuries, became independent in 1822 as an empire, and has been a republic since 1889.

## Indigenous Brazil

Before the Portuguese arrived, Brazil was home to millions of Indigenous people, including the Tupi along the coast and many peoples in the Amazon and interior. Archaeology has revealed large settlements in the Amazon, with earthworks and fertile "dark earth" soils created by people. European diseases, enslavement and violence devastated Indigenous populations after 1500. Today, about 1.7 million Indigenous people live in Brazil, belonging to over 300 peoples.

## Colony

Portuguese navigators reached the coast in 1500. The colony took its name from brazilwood, a tree used for red dye, but its economy was built on sugar, grown on the north-eastern coast by enslaved Africans. Brazil received more enslaved Africans than any other country, around four to five million. Gold discoveries in Minas Gerais in the 1690s drew settlers inland, and the capital moved from Salvador to Rio de Janeiro in 1763.

## Colonial Brazil

The Portuguese colony's economy depended on sugar, gold and diamonds, and later coffee, all relying on enslaved Africans. African cultures deeply shaped Brazil, especially in Bahia, through religion, such as Candomblé, music, food and the martial art capoeira. Enslaved people formed quilombos, communities of escapees, such as Palmares. In 1789 a conspiracy in Minas Gerais, the Inconfidência Mineira, led by Tiradentes, aimed at independence; Tiradentes was executed in 1792.

## The Empire

When the Portuguese court fled to Rio de Janeiro in 1808, Brazil became the centre of the Portuguese empire, opened its ports to foreign trade and gained institutions such as a national library and a bank. Pedro I declared independence in 1822. His son Pedro II, who reigned from 1831, with a regency until 1840, presided over a stable constitutional monarchy, modernisation and the abolition of slavery in 1888 by the Golden Law, signed by Princess Isabel.

## The Old Republic

The republic proclaimed in 1889 was dominated by the coffee planters of São Paulo and the cattle ranchers of Minas Gerais, in an arrangement called "coffee with milk" politics. Millions of immigrants arrived from Italy, Portugal, Spain, Germany and Japan, especially to work on coffee plantations. In 1897 the government crushed the religious community of Canudos in the north-east, killing thousands.

## Vargas

Getúlio Vargas took power in a revolution in 1930. From 1937 he ruled as a dictator in the Estado Novo, "New State", centralising government, creating labour laws and building industry, such as the national steel company. Brazil sent troops to fight in Italy in the Second World War. Vargas was deposed in 1945, returned as elected president in 1951 and shot himself in 1954, during a political crisis.

## Brasília and the military regime

President Juscelino Kubitschek built the new capital, Brasília, designed by the architect Oscar Niemeyer and the planner Lúcio Costa, inaugurated in 1960. In 1964 the military overthrew President João Goulart. The military regime lasted until 1985, presiding over rapid growth, the "Brazilian miracle", but also torture, censorship and debt.

## Modern Brazil

Democracy returned in 1985, and a new constitution was adopted in 1988. The Real Plan of 1994 ended hyperinflation. Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva, a former metalworker, was president from 2003 to 2010 and again from 2023, reducing poverty through social programmes. Brazil is famous for football, winning the World Cup five times, music such as samba and bossa nova, and Carnival. Deforestation of the Amazon, the world's largest rainforest, is a major global concern.

## On our maps

Our maps label the country Brazil from 1900 to 2010, with an outline of about 8.4 million square kilometres, almost exactly the modern country. Rio de Janeiro and Salvador are Brazilian on these maps. On the 1878 and 1880 maps the basemap shows the Empire of Brazil, and earlier maps show it as Portuguese.
