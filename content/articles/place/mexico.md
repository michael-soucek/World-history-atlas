Mexico became independent from Spain in 1821, after an eleven-year war that began in 1810 with a call to arms by the parish priest Miguel Hidalgo in the town of Dolores, still celebrated each year on 16 September. Before that, for three centuries, it had been the Viceroyalty of New Spain, the richest part of Spain's American empire, built on the ruins of the Aztec Empire and other indigenous states.

## Ancient Mexico

Mexico was home to some of the great civilisations of the Americas: the Olmecs on the Gulf Coast, Teotihuacan in the central highlands, the Maya in the south-east, the Zapotecs of Monte Albán in Oaxaca, the Toltecs and the Aztecs. Maize, beans, squash, chillies, tomatoes and chocolate were domesticated in Mexico. The Spanish conquest of 1519–1521 began three centuries of colonial rule as New Spain.

## Independence and early troubles

After Hidalgo's call to arms in 1810 and a long war, Mexico won independence in 1821 under Agustín de Iturbide, who briefly made himself emperor. The general Antonio López de Santa Anna dominated politics for decades, serving as president eleven times. Texas broke away in 1836 after the Battle of San Jacinto. The war with the United States, from 1846 to 1848, ended with the Treaty of Guadalupe Hidalgo, which gave the United States California, Nevada, Utah and parts of other states. The Gadsden Purchase of 1853 sold more land.

## La Reforma and the French intervention

Liberals, led by Benito Juárez, a Zapotec lawyer from Oaxaca, passed reforms reducing the power of the Church and the army, and the Constitution of 1857, leading to the War of Reform with conservatives. In 1862, France invaded, using unpaid debts as a pretext; a Mexican victory at Puebla on 5 May 1862 is celebrated as Cinco de Mayo. France installed Maximilian of Habsburg as emperor in 1864, but after French troops withdrew, Juárez's forces captured and executed him in 1867.

## The Porfiriato

Porfirio Díaz ruled from 1876 to 1911, with a short break. His regime brought railways, mining, oil and foreign investment, under the motto "order and progress". But land was concentrated in large estates, haciendas, and peasants, especially Indigenous communities, lost their lands. Opposition was repressed.

## The revolution

In 1910 Francisco Madero called for revolt against Díaz, who fled in 1911. Madero was murdered in 1913, and years of war followed among revolutionary leaders: Emiliano Zapata in the south, demanding "land and liberty", Pancho Villa in the north, Venustiano Carranza and Álvaro Obregón. The Constitution of 1917 promised land reform, workers' rights and national ownership of resources. Zapata, Carranza, Villa and Obregón were all assassinated.

## Modern Mexico

The Institutional Revolutionary Party, PRI, ruled from 1929 to 2000. Lázaro Cárdenas, president from 1934 to 1940, distributed land and nationalised oil in 1938. Mexico grew rapidly in the "Mexican miracle" of the mid-twentieth century. In 1968, troops killed students at Tlatelolco. The 1994 NAFTA agreement, the Zapatista uprising in Chiapas and the election of an opposition president, Vicente Fox, in 2000 transformed politics. Violence linked to drug cartels has killed hundreds of thousands since 2006. In 2024 Claudia Sheinbaum became Mexico's first woman president.

## Culture

Mexican culture blends Indigenous and Spanish traditions, seen in the Day of the Dead, cuisine recognised by UNESCO, and the murals of Diego Rivera, José Clemente Orozco and David Alfaro Siqueiros. Frida Kahlo's paintings, the writings of Octavio Paz and Carlos Fuentes, and Mexican cinema are internationally famous.
