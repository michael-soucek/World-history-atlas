The Dutch Republic was born out of a revolt. In the 1560s the provinces of the Low Countries, ruled by the Spanish king Philip II, rose against high taxes and the persecution of Protestants. The northern provinces united in 1579 and declared independence in 1581, and from 1588 governed themselves as a republic, the United Provinces. Spain did not recognise its independence until 1648.

## The Eighty Years' War

The revolt of the Netherlands against Spain began in the 1560s, sparked by religious persecution of Protestants, heavy taxes and the threat to local privileges. After an outbreak of iconoclasm in 1566, Philip II sent the Duke of Alba with an army, whose Council of Troubles executed over a thousand people. William of Orange, known as William the Silent, led the revolt. The rebels, called Sea Beggars, captured Brill in 1572. Spain recaptured the southern provinces, now Belgium, while the northern provinces held out. William was assassinated in 1584. The war ended with the Peace of Münster in 1648.

## A federal republic

The Republic was a federation of seven provinces: Holland, Zeeland, Utrecht, Gelderland, Overijssel, Friesland and Groningen, plus territories governed directly. Each province sent delegates to the States General in The Hague. Holland, the richest province, provided most of the taxes, and its chief official, the Grand Pensionary, was often the most powerful politician. The princes of Orange served as stadtholders and military commanders, and there was frequent tension between the Orangist and republican factions.

## Trade and finance

The Dutch dominated European shipping in the seventeenth century, with the fluyt, an efficient cargo ship. They controlled trade in Baltic grain and timber, North Sea herring, and spices and textiles from Asia. Amsterdam became Europe's financial capital, with the Amsterdam Exchange Bank, founded in 1609, and a stock exchange where shares of the East India Company were traded. In the "tulip mania" of the 1630s, prices of tulip bulbs reached extraordinary heights before collapsing in 1637.

## The VOC

The Dutch East India Company, the VOC, founded in 1602, had the right to wage war, make treaties and govern territories. It took the Moluccas from the Portuguese, founded Batavia in 1619 and conquered Ceylon and Malacca. In 1621 it conquered the Banda Islands, killing or enslaving most of the inhabitants, to control the nutmeg trade. It held the only European trading post in Japan, at Dejima. The VOC also founded Cape Town in 1652. It was involved in the slave trade in the Indian Ocean.

## Science and tolerance

The Republic was a centre of science and learning. Antonie van Leeuwenhoek discovered micro-organisms with his microscopes; Christiaan Huygens developed the pendulum clock and a wave theory of light; and the University of Leiden attracted students from across Europe. The philosophers René Descartes and John Locke lived there, and Baruch Spinoza was born in Amsterdam. Printers published books banned elsewhere.

## The Golden Age of painting

Dutch painters produced works for a broad market of merchants and middle-class buyers. Rembrandt van Rijn painted portraits, biblical scenes and The Night Watch; Johannes Vermeer painted quiet interiors, such as Girl with a Pearl Earring; and Frans Hals, Jan Steen and Jacob van Ruisdael painted portraits, scenes of everyday life and landscapes.

## Wars and decline

The Republic fought three wars with England over trade in the seventeenth century, and in 1672, the "disaster year", it was invaded by France, England and others. William III of Orange led resistance, and in 1688 invaded England, becoming its king. In the eighteenth century, the Republic declined relative to Britain and France. In 1795 French troops and Dutch Patriots ended the Republic.

## On our maps

Our maps draw the Republic from 1600 to 1783. Its largest outline, about 440,000 square kilometres on the 1700 map, lies mostly in Brazil, because the basemap groups the Dutch colony of Suriname and other overseas posts under the same name. Amsterdam is Dutch on our maps throughout; New York's site until 1715, and Cape Town and Colombo in the eighteenth century.
