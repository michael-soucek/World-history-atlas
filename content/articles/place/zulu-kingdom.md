The Zulu Kingdom was a monarchy in south-eastern Africa, in what is now KwaZulu-Natal in South Africa. In less than a decade in the 1810s and 1820s it grew from a small chiefdom into the dominant power of the region, and in 1879 it inflicted one of the heaviest defeats ever suffered by a British army in a colonial war.

## The Nguni chiefdoms

Before the Zulu Kingdom, the region between the Drakensberg mountains and the Indian Ocean was home to many Nguni-speaking chiefdoms, herding cattle and growing grain. Cattle were central to wealth, marriage and status. In the late eighteenth century, competition for land, perhaps worsened by drought, and trade in ivory through Delagoa Bay, now Maputo, led to the rise of larger states: the Mthethwa under Dingiswayo, the Ndwandwe under Zwide, and the Ngwane, later the Swazi.

## Shaka's state

Shaka reorganised the Zulu into a centralised military kingdom. Young men served in regiments, amabutho, organised by age, quartered in royal homesteads, and could not marry until the king allowed it. The regiments fought with the short stabbing spear and large shields, and used the "horns of the buffalo" formation, encircling the enemy. Shaka defeated Zwide's Ndwandwe in 1818–1819 and absorbed many smaller chiefdoms. The capital was at kwaBulawayo.

## The Mfecane

The wars of this period drove many groups to flee and form new states. Mzilikazi led the Ndebele north, eventually founding a kingdom in today's Zimbabwe. Soshangane founded the Gaza kingdom in Mozambique, and Sobhuza and Moshoeshoe built the Swazi and Basotho kingdoms. Historians once blamed the upheavals mostly on the Zulu, but now point to many causes, including colonial slave raiding and drought.

## Dingane and the Boers

Dingane killed Shaka in 1828 and became king. In 1838 the Voortrekker leader Piet Retief came to negotiate land, and Dingane had him and his party killed. The Boers defeated Dingane's army at Blood River on 16 December 1838, a battle commemorated by Afrikaners. Dingane's brother Mpande allied with the Boers, overthrew Dingane in 1840, and ruled for over thirty years, a period of relative peace. The British annexed Natal, south of the Thukela river, in 1843.

## Cetshwayo and the Anglo-Zulu War

Cetshwayo, king from 1872, rebuilt the army. In December 1878, the British high commissioner Sir Bartle Frere, seeking to bring South Africa into a confederation, demanded that the Zulu disband their army. When Cetshwayo refused, British forces invaded in January 1879. At Isandlwana, on 22 January, the Zulu army killed over 1,300 British and allied soldiers, one of the worst defeats of the British Army in a colonial war. The defence of Rorke's Drift the same night became a British legend. Reinforced, the British defeated the Zulu at Ulundi on 4 July 1879 and burned the royal homestead.

## Division and annexation

Britain divided Zululand into thirteen chiefdoms, which led to civil war. Cetshwayo was restored to part of his kingdom in 1883 but was defeated by a rival, Zibhebhu, and died in 1884. His son Dinuzulu, with Boer help, defeated Zibhebhu. Britain annexed Zululand in 1887 and exiled Dinuzulu. A rebellion against a poll tax, the Bambatha Rebellion, was crushed in 1906.

## Today

The Zulu are South Africa's largest ethnic group, with over 12 million people. The Zulu king has a ceremonial role, and the annual Reed Dance and other ceremonies preserve Zulu traditions.
