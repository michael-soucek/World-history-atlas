The Song dynasty ruled China from 960 to 1279. It was never as militarily powerful as the Han or Tang, and lost the north of China halfway through its history, but it was one of the most inventive and prosperous periods in Chinese history.

## The founding

Zhao Kuangyin, commander of the palace guard of the Later Zhou, was proclaimed emperor by his troops in 960, becoming Emperor Taizu. According to a famous story, he then invited his generals to a banquet and persuaded them to retire peacefully with generous pensions, ending the era of military strongmen. He and his brother, Emperor Taizong, conquered the southern kingdoms and reunited most of China by 979, but failed to take the Sixteen Prefectures from the Liao.

## Scholar-officials

The Song expanded the examination system enormously, with tens of thousands of candidates, and printing made books widely available. The scholar-official class dominated government. Reformers and conservatives clashed: in the 1070s the chancellor Wang Anshi introduced the New Policies, including loans to farmers and changes to taxes and the army, which the historian Sima Guang and others opposed. Factional struggles between the two sides lasted for decades.

## Economy

The Song economy grew rapidly. New strains of fast-ripening rice from Champa allowed two harvests a year in the south. The population rose to over 100 million. Cities such as Kaifeng and Hangzhou had hundreds of thousands of people, with markets open at night, restaurants and entertainment quarters. The famous scroll painting Along the River During the Qingming Festival shows the bustle of Kaifeng. Coal and iron production increased greatly. The state issued paper money, the jiaozi, from the early eleventh century.

## Technology

The Song saw important inventions or developments: movable type, invented by Bi Sheng in the 1040s, using clay characters; the magnetic compass for navigation; gunpowder weapons, such as fire lances and bombs; and the astronomical clock tower built by Su Song in Kaifeng in 1088. The scholar Shen Kuo wrote about magnetism, geology and astronomy.

## Arts and thought

Song landscape painting, by artists such as Fan Kuan and Guo Xi, is one of the great achievements of Chinese art. Emperor Huizong was a gifted painter and calligrapher. Song porcelain, such as celadon and Jun ware, was prized. Ci poetry flourished with poets such as Su Shi and the woman poet Li Qingzhao. Neo-Confucian philosophers, culminating in Zhu Xi, created a synthesis of Confucian ethics and metaphysics.

## The loss of the north

The Song allied with the Jurchens against the Liao, but the Jurchens, having founded the Jin dynasty, turned on the Song and besieged Kaifeng in 1126–1127, capturing the emperors Huizong and Qinzong. The general Yue Fei fought to recover the north, but was executed in 1142 on the orders of the chancellor Qin Hui, who made peace with the Jin; Yue Fei is still honoured as a patriot.

## The Southern Song

From Hangzhou, the Southern Song developed maritime trade, with ports such as Quanzhou trading with Southeast Asia, India and the Middle East. A navy defended the Yangtze. The Mongols attacked from the 1230s; the siege of Xiangyang and Fancheng lasted from 1268 to 1273. In 1279, at the sea battle of Yamen, the last Song loyalists were defeated, and the official Lu Xiufu jumped into the sea with the boy emperor in his arms.

## On our maps

Our maps show the Song on the 1000, 1100 and 1200 maps, entirely within today's China. Its largest outline, about 3 million square kilometres, is on the 1200 map, although by then the north had been lost to the Jin; the basemap draws the earlier Song more tightly. Shanghai's and Nanjing's sites, Guangzhou and Hong Kong are Song on our maps throughout. Before the Song our maps show the Tang; on the 1279 map the region belongs to the Mongol Great Khanate.
