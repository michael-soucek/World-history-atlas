Iran, known in the West as Persia until 1935, is one of the world's oldest continuous civilisations. Its long history includes some of the great empires of antiquity, the Achaemenids, Parthians and Sasanians, before the Arab conquest of the seventh century brought Islam. This page focuses on Iran since the eighteenth century.

## Ancient and medieval Iran

Iran has been home to some of history's great empires: the Elamites, the Medes, the Achaemenid Persians of Cyrus and Darius, the Parthians and the Sasanians. After the Arab conquest in the seventh century, Iran gradually became Muslim, but kept its language and culture. New Persian literature flourished from the tenth century, with Ferdowsi's Shahnameh, and later the poets Omar Khayyam, Rumi, Saadi and Hafez. Iran was ruled by many dynasties, including the Seljuks, the Mongol Ilkhans and the Timurids, before the Safavids made it a Shia state in 1501.

## After the Safavids

After the Safavid collapse, Nader Shah drove out the Afghans and conquered as far as Delhi in 1739, but was assassinated in 1747. Karim Khan Zand ruled much of Iran from Shiraz from 1751 to 1779, as regent rather than shah. The Qajars, a Turkmen tribe, took power under Agha Mohammad Khan, who was crowned in 1796.

## The Qajar era

The Qajars lost the Caucasus to Russia in the treaties of Golestan in 1813 and Turkmenchay in 1828, and Herat to Afghanistan with British pressure. They granted foreign concessions; a tobacco monopoly given to a British company in 1890 led to a nationwide boycott, supported by Shia clerics, that forced its cancellation. The Constitutional Revolution of 1905–1911 produced a constitution and parliament, the Majlis, but Russia and Britain intervened. Oil was discovered in 1908, and the Anglo-Persian Oil Company, later BP, controlled it.

## Reza Shah

Reza Khan, an officer of the Persian Cossack Brigade, took power in a coup in 1921 and was crowned shah in 1925. He built railways, roads, a modern army, schools and the University of Tehran, required Western dress and banned women's veils in 1936. He changed the country's official name in international use to Iran in 1935. After he showed sympathy for Germany, Britain and the Soviet Union invaded in 1941 and forced him to abdicate.

## Mosaddegh and the shah

Prime Minister Mohammad Mosaddegh nationalised the oil industry in 1951, provoking a British boycott. In 1953 he was overthrown in a coup organised by the American CIA and British intelligence, and the shah, Mohammad Reza Pahlavi, returned with greater power. His White Revolution of the 1960s included land reform and women's suffrage, and oil revenues brought rapid growth, but the secret police, SAVAK, repressed opposition, and inequality and Westernisation angered many.

## The Islamic Revolution

Ayatollah Ruhollah Khomeini, exiled in 1964, led opposition from Iraq and France. Mass protests in 1978 brought millions into the streets, and the shah left in January 1979. Khomeini returned in February, and a referendum created the Islamic Republic, with the doctrine of velayat-e faqih, the rule of the jurist. The new regime imposed Islamic law, required women to wear the hijab and executed thousands of opponents.

## War and after

Iraq's invasion in 1980 began an eight-year war that killed hundreds of thousands. Khomeini died in 1989 and was succeeded as Supreme Leader by Ali Khamenei. Reformist president Mohammad Khatami, elected in 1997, sought greater freedoms. Disputes over Iran's nuclear programme led to sanctions. Protests in 2009, 2019 and 2022, the last after the death of Mahsa Amini in police custody, were met with force.
