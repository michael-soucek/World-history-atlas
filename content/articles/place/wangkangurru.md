Much of the Simpson Desert, in the far north of South Australia, is Wangkangurru country. They also call themselves Nharla, and today they and the neighbouring Yarluyandi hold native title, recognised in 2014, over a large part of the desert.

## Country

Norman Tindale estimated Wangkangurru country at about 14,000 square miles (roughly 36,000 square kilometres). It ran north from Stevenson Creek to Mount Dare, took in the lower Finke River and Macumba Creek, and covered the southern Simpson Desert, reaching south-east to the Warburton River and Kallakoopah Creek, and included Atna Hill and Blood Creek. Early outsiders recorded the name in many garbled forms; Tindale noted that one expedition member in 1895 seemed unable to hear some of its sounds, writing "Wonkongnuru".

## Language

Wangkangurru is a Pama–Nyungan language, usually treated as a dialect of [Arabana](/place/arabana-people) and grouped with the Karnic languages. Their self-name Nharla, used for their language subgroup, comes from the Arabana word meaning "man". Neighbouring peoples had their own names for them, including Partama and Wingkungira.

## Knowledge and law

The Wangkangurru word minparu describes a man of high degree, a "clever man" or medicine man. One such man, Mick McLean (Irinyili, 1888–1977), was a source of knowledge highly valued by ethnographers such as the linguist Luise Hercus. He knew the song cycles and lore spanning a huge area of northern South Australia, from the Simpson Desert and Dalhousie south to Port Augusta and from Indulkana east to Innamincka. Besides English, he spoke five Aboriginal languages: he was a master of Wangkangurru, Arabana and southern [Arrernte](/place/arrernte-people), and also competent in Diyari and Antakarinya. His range shows how widely songs, law and languages were shared across the region.

## Native title

The Wangkangurru today form a combined group with the Yarluyandi, and a joint Aboriginal corporation speaks for both. Their native title over a broad sweep of the desert was recognised in 2014.
