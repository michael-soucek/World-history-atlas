Hungary was founded by the Magyars, a people speaking a Finno-Ugric language, who crossed the Carpathians and settled in the Carpathian basin in the late ninth century. Their language, unrelated to those of their Slavic and Germanic neighbours, is still the country's defining feature.

## The Magyars

The Magyars were a confederation of tribes who spoke a Finno-Ugric language related to Finnish and Estonian, originally from the region east of the Ural mountains. They moved west across the steppe, and, led by Árpád, crossed the Carpathians into the Carpathian basin around 895–896, an event Hungarians call the "conquest of the homeland". For decades they raided across Europe, reaching Spain, Italy and France, until their defeat at the Lechfeld in 955.

## Saint Stephen

Árpád's descendant Géza began Christianisation, and his son Stephen was crowned king on Christmas Day 1000 or 1 January 1001, with a crown sent by Pope Sylvester II, according to tradition. Stephen founded bishoprics, organised the kingdom into counties and suppressed pagan revolts. He was canonised in 1083. The Holy Crown of Hungary, associated with him, became the symbol of the kingdom.

## The medieval kingdom

The Árpád dynasty ruled until 1301. Hungary gained Croatia in 1102. The Golden Bull of 1222, issued by Andrew II, limited royal power and protected the nobility's rights. The Mongol invasion of 1241–1242 devastated the country, killing perhaps a quarter of the population; King Béla IV rebuilt it with stone castles and invited settlers. The Anjou kings, Charles Robert and Louis the Great, made Hungary a major power. John Hunyadi defeated the Ottomans at Belgrade in 1456, and the noon bells rung across Christendom are said to commemorate the victory.

## Matthias Corvinus

Hunyadi's son Matthias Corvinus, king from 1458 to 1490, built a strong state with a professional army, the Black Army, conquered Vienna in 1485, and created a Renaissance court at Buda, with Italian artists and humanists. His library, the Bibliotheca Corviniana, was one of the largest in Europe.

## Three parts

After Mohács in 1526, Hungary was divided: the Habsburgs ruled Royal Hungary in the north and west, the Ottomans ruled the centre for about 150 years, and Transylvania became a semi-independent principality, where Protestantism flourished and religious tolerance was proclaimed at Torda in 1568. After the Habsburgs reconquered Hungary by 1699, Francis II Rákóczi led a war of independence from 1703 to 1711.

## Reform and revolution

In the early nineteenth century, the reform era, led by István Széchenyi, who founded the Hungarian Academy of Sciences and built the Chain Bridge in Budapest, and Lajos Kossuth, sought modernisation and national rights. The revolution of 15 March 1848, inspired by the poet Sándor Petőfi, led to a war of independence against Austria, crushed with Russian help in 1849. The Compromise of 1867 created Austria-Hungary, and Budapest grew into a great city.

## The twentieth century

After 1918, the Treaty of Trianon in 1920 reduced Hungary's territory by about two-thirds and left millions of Hungarians outside its borders. The regent Miklós Horthy ruled from 1920 to 1944. Hungary allied with Nazi Germany and regained some lands, but suffered terrible losses; in 1944, after the German occupation, Hungarian Jews were deported to Auschwitz. Under communist rule after 1945, the revolution of 1956 was crushed by Soviet troops, and its leader Imre Nagy was executed. Hungary became a democracy in 1989–1990. Its language, cuisine, music, from Liszt to Bartók, and thermal baths are distinctive parts of its culture.
