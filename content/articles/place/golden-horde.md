The Golden Horde was the westernmost part of the Mongol Empire, the khanate that ruled the steppes from the Danube to Siberia for more than two centuries. Its people called it the Ulus of Jochi, after Genghis Khan's eldest son, whose descendants ruled it, or simply the Great State. The name "Golden Horde" was given to it later, by Russian writers.

## Batu and the Rus'

Jochi's son Batu led the great western campaign of 1236–1242, which conquered the Volga Bulgars, the Kipchak nomads of the steppe and the Rus' principalities, sacking Ryazan, Vladimir and Kyiv. Batu then settled on the lower Volga and built his capital, Sarai. The Rus' princes were left in place but had to travel to Sarai to receive a patent to rule and to pay tribute, collected at first by Mongol officials and later by the princes themselves. Moscow's princes rose to power partly as the Horde's trusted tax collectors.

## The khans and Sarai

The Golden Horde was ruled by descendants of Jochi. Its capital, Sarai on the lower Volga, and later New Sarai, built under Özbeg or his successors, were large cities with palaces, mosques, workshops and markets, attracting merchants from Italy, Egypt, Central Asia and Russia. The Moroccan traveller Ibn Battuta, who visited in the 1330s, described Sarai as a beautiful, crowded city. Most of the population remained nomadic herders, moving between summer and winter pastures.

## The "Tatar yoke"

For Russia, the period of Horde rule is known as the "Tatar yoke". The Rus' princes had to travel to Sarai, and sometimes to Karakorum, to receive patents, yarlyks, to rule. Some who displeased the khans were executed there, such as Mikhail of Chernigov in 1246 and Mikhail of Tver in 1318. The Horde conducted censuses for tax and conscription, and Rus' cities that revolted, such as Tver in 1327, were crushed. The Orthodox Church, however, was exempted from taxes and flourished.

## Islam and trade

Berke Khan, who ruled from 1257 to 1266, was the first khan to convert to Islam and allied with the Mamluks of Egypt against his cousin Hülegü of the Ilkhanate. Under Özbeg, Islam became the state religion. The Horde controlled the northern Silk Road and the Black Sea trade, and the Genoese and Venetians had trading colonies at Kaffa and Tana. The Horde supplied enslaved Kipchak boys who became Mamluk soldiers in Egypt.

## The Black Death

In 1346, according to one account, the Horde's army besieging Kaffa catapulted the bodies of plague victims into the city. Whether or not this happened, plague spread from the region to the Mediterranean in the following year. The Black Death also devastated the Horde itself, weakening its cities and trade.

## The great troubles

After the murder of Khan Berdibek in 1359, the Horde fell into two decades of civil war, the "Great Troubles", with over twenty khans. The powerful general Mamai controlled the west, and his defeat by Dmitry of Moscow at Kulikovo in 1380 showed the Horde's weakness. Tokhtamysh, backed by Timur, reunited the Horde and burned Moscow in 1382, but then turned against Timur, who defeated him and sacked Sarai and other cities in 1395.

## Successor khanates

In the fifteenth century the Horde broke apart into the khanates of Kazan, Crimea, Astrakhan, Sibir and Kasimov, the Nogai Horde, and the remnant Great Horde. Moscow, once the Horde's tributary, gradually absorbed them: Kazan in 1552, Astrakhan in 1556, and Crimea finally in 1783. The Tatars of the Volga and Crimea are descendants of the Horde's peoples.
