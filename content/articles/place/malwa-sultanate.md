The Malwa Sultanate was a Muslim kingdom of central India that ruled the Malwa plateau, roughly today's western Madhya Pradesh and south-eastern Rajasthan, from about 1401 until the Mughal conquest of 1562. It was founded by Dilawar Khan Ghuri, an Afghan governor of the [Delhi Sultanate](/place/delhi-sultanate), as Delhi's authority disintegrated. Ruled mostly by Afghan dynasties from the hilltop fortress-city of Mandu, it fought constantly with its neighbours and was a notable centre of manuscript painting.

## Founding

Dilawar Khan stopped sending tribute to Delhi after 1392, and the invasion of northern India by [Timur](/person/tamerlane) and his sack of Delhi left provincial governors free to go their own way. Dilawar Khan is generally said to have assumed royal status in 1401, founding what is called the Ghurid dynasty. His first capital was Dhar, but the court soon moved to Mandu, which was renamed Shadiabad, "the city of joy".

## The Ghurid sultans

Dilawar Khan's son Alp Khan took the throne as Hoshang Shah. Accusing him of having killed his father, Muzaffar Shah I of the [Gujarat Sultanate](/place/gujarat-sultanate) invaded in 1407, captured him and installed a governor at Dhar, but Hoshang was restored the following year. Wars with Gujarat and with the [Bahmani Sultanate](/place/bahmani-kingdom) of the Deccan recurred for decades.

## The Khalji dynasty

In 1436 Mahmud Khan, a commander related both to Dilawar Khan's family and to the earlier Khalji sultans of Delhi, deposed Hoshang Shah's grandson and declared himself sultan as Mahmud Shah I. The Khalji dynasty he founded held Malwa until 1531. His son Ghiyas-ud-Din was followed by Nasir-ud-Din, who took the throne in October 1510 after a struggle with his brother.

## Decline and Gujarati rule

The sultanate weakened in the early sixteenth century. In 1519, after the Battle of Gagron, much of Malwa fell under the control of Rana Sanga of Mewar, who placed his vassal Medini Rai in charge of part of it. The last Khalji ruler, Mahmud Shah II, became dependent on Gujarat, and in May 1531 Bahadur Shah of Gujarat took Mandu and annexed the kingdom. The Mughal emperor Humayun held it briefly in 1535 and 1536, and a former Khalji officer, Qadir Shah, regained part of the country in 1537.

## Baz Bahadur and the Mughal conquest

In 1542 [Sher Shah Suri](/person/sher-shah-suri) conquered Malwa and appointed Shujaat Khan as governor. His son Baz Bahadur declared himself independent in 1555. In 1561 [Akbar](/person/akbar) sent an army under Adham Khan and Pir Muhammad Khan, which defeated Baz Bahadur at Sarangpur on 29 March. Baz Bahadur briefly won back his kingdom with help from Khandesh and Berar after Pir Muhammad's death, but in 1562 a second Mughal army drove him out. Malwa became a province of the [Mughal Empire](/place/mughal-empire), governed from Ujjain.

## Art and culture

Mandu was an important centre of book painting. An illustrated Jain text, the Kalpa Sutra, was produced there in 1439 under Mahmud Shah I. The best known work is the Nimat Nama, a book of recipes and cookery whose pictures show Sultan Ghiyas-ud-Din. Other illustrated manuscripts include a Bustan painted by Haji Mahmud in 1502 and a dictionary of rare words. Persian was the language of government.
