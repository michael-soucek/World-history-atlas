The Republic of Turkey was proclaimed on 29 October 1923, from the remains of the Ottoman Empire. Its founder, Mustafa Kemal, later given the name Atatürk, "father of the Turks", led the national movement that rejected the peace terms imposed by the Allies after the First World War.

## Anatolia before Turkey

Anatolia, the Asian part of modern Turkey, has been home to many civilisations: the Neolithic settlement of Çatalhöyük, the Hittites, the Phrygians of King Midas, the Lydians of Croesus, Greek cities such as Troy, Ephesus and Miletus, the Romans and the Byzantines. Turkish peoples arrived after the Seljuk victory at Manzikert in 1071. The Seljuk Sultanate of Rum and then the Ottoman Empire ruled Anatolia for centuries.

## The end of the empire

The Ottoman Empire fought on Germany's side in the First World War. During the war, the government carried out the Armenian genocide, in which around a million or more Armenians were killed in massacres and death marches, as well as killings of Assyrians and Greeks. After defeat in 1918, the Allies occupied Istanbul, and the Treaty of Sèvres of 1920 planned to divide Anatolia, giving territory to Greece, Armenia and others.

## The War of Independence

Mustafa Kemal, a successful general at Gallipoli, landed at Samsun on 19 May 1919 and organised a nationalist movement. The Grand National Assembly in Ankara, opened in 1920, led the struggle. Turkish forces defeated the Armenians in the east and the French in the south, and in the Greco-Turkish War of 1919–1922, drove the Greek army from western Anatolia; the Greek and Armenian quarters of Smyrna, now İzmir, burned in 1922. The Treaty of Lausanne in 1923 recognised Turkey's borders.

## Kemalism

Atatürk's reforms aimed to create a modern, secular, Western-oriented nation-state. The six principles of Kemalism were republicanism, nationalism, populism, secularism, statism and revolutionism. The fez was banned in 1925 and replaced with Western hats; Sufi lodges were closed; the Gregorian calendar and metric system were adopted; and the Latin alphabet replaced the Arabic script in 1928, followed by a mass literacy campaign. Women gained the vote in 1934, and eighteen women were elected to parliament in 1935. The Kurdish rebellions of 1925 and 1937–1938 were crushed.

## Democracy and coups

After Atatürk's death in 1938, İsmet İnönü kept Turkey neutral in the Second World War until 1945. Multi-party elections in 1950 brought Adnan Menderes's Democrat Party to power. The army, as guardian of Kemalism, overthrew Menderes in 1960 and had him hanged, and intervened again in 1971, 1980 and 1997. Turkey joined NATO in 1952 and applied to join the European Community in 1987.

## Erdoğan

Recep Tayyip Erdoğan's Justice and Development Party, with Islamist roots, won the election of 2002. As prime minister and, from 2014, president, Erdoğan oversaw economic growth and reduced the military's political role. After a failed coup attempt in July 2016, his government arrested tens of thousands of people. A referendum in 2017 created a powerful executive presidency.

## Culture

Turkey has a rich cultural heritage, from Ottoman architecture to Turkish cuisine and the novels of Orhan Pamuk, winner of the Nobel Prize in Literature in 2006. Istanbul, spanning Europe and Asia, is one of the world's largest cities.
