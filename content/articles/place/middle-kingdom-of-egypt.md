The Middle Kingdom was Egypt's second great age of unity and prosperity, from about 2040 BCE to around 1780 BCE. Egyptians of later ages looked back on it as a classical era, especially for its literature.

## The First Intermediate Period

After the Old Kingdom's long-reigning Pepi II died, around 2180 BCE, central authority collapsed. Low Nile floods may have caused famine. Egypt split into rival powers: kings at Herakleopolis in the north, and a family of nomarchs at Thebes in the south. Provincial governors, nomarchs, ruled their regions almost independently. Later literature, such as the Admonitions of Ipuwer, describes a world turned upside down, though this may be a literary picture.

## Mentuhotep II

The Theban king Mentuhotep II, of the Eleventh Dynasty, defeated Herakleopolis around 2040 BCE and reunited the country. He built a unique memorial temple at Deir el-Bahari, west of Thebes, with terraces and colonnades, later imitated by Hatshepsut. A tomb near his temple contained the bodies of about sixty soldiers killed in battle.

## The Twelfth Dynasty

Amenemhat I, probably Mentuhotep IV's vizier, founded the Twelfth Dynasty around 1985 BCE. He moved the capital to Itjtawy, near the Faiyum, and introduced co-regencies, in which the king's son ruled alongside him. He was assassinated in a palace conspiracy, described in the Instruction of Amenemhat. His son Senusret I built widely, including the White Chapel at Karnak. Senusret III campaigned in Nubia, built the fortresses at the second cataract and reduced the power of the nomarchs. Amenemhat III undertook large irrigation works in the Faiyum and built a pyramid at Hawara with a huge temple later called the Labyrinth.

## Nubia

The Middle Kingdom conquered Lower Nubia, south of the first cataract, to control gold mines and trade with the south. A chain of mud-brick forts, such as Buhen and Semna, with walls, towers and ditches, guarded the river at the second cataract. Inscriptions forbade Nubians from passing north except for trade. Beyond lay the powerful Nubian kingdom of Kerma.

## Society and religion

The Middle Kingdom saw the growth of a middle class of officials and craftsmen. Coffin Texts, spells painted inside coffins, made the afterlife available to wealthier people, not just kings. Osiris, god of the dead, became central, and Abydos, his cult centre, attracted pilgrims, who set up memorial stelae. Model figures of servants, boats and workshops were placed in tombs, such as the famous models of Meketre.

## Literature and art

The Middle Kingdom is the classical period of Egyptian literature. The Tale of Sinuhe, about an official who flees to the Levant and returns home in old age, the Story of the Shipwrecked Sailor, and wisdom texts such as the Instruction of Ptahhotep were copied for centuries. Royal portrait sculpture, especially of Senusret III and Amenemhat III, showed careworn, realistic faces. Fine jewellery from princesses' tombs at Dahshur and Lahun is among the best of ancient Egypt.

## The end

The Thirteenth Dynasty had many short-lived kings, though the administration continued. Asiatic immigrants settled in the eastern delta, at Avaris, and around 1650 BCE, the Hyksos, "rulers of foreign lands", took control of northern Egypt, beginning the Second Intermediate Period.

## On our maps

Our maps show the Middle Kingdom on the 2000 BCE map, with an outline of about 320,000 square kilometres along the Nile from the Mediterranean into northern Sudan. The same outline appears for Egypt on earlier maps, as the basemap draws ancient Egypt broadly; on the 1500 BCE map it becomes the [New Kingdom](/place/new-kingdom-of-egypt).
