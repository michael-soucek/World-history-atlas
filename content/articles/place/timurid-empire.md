The Timurid Empire was founded by Timur, known in Europe as Tamerlane, a Turkic-speaking warlord from the Barlas tribe near Samarkand. From 1370 until his death in 1405 he conquered an empire across Central Asia, Iran and the Middle East in some of the most destructive campaigns in history. His descendants ruled a smaller state for another century and presided over a flowering of Persian art and learning.

## Rise to power

Timur rose in Transoxiana during the chaotic decline of the Chagatai Khanate, initially as a follower of the Moghul khan, then in alliance with Emir Husayn of Balkh, whose sister he married. In 1370 he turned on Husayn, took Balkh and was proclaimed ruler at a kurultai, an assembly. He made Samarkand his capital and spent the next decade securing Central Asia.

## Campaigns

From 1380 Timur invaded Iran, destroying cities that resisted, such as Isfahan, where, according to contemporaries, towers were built from the skulls of tens of thousands of people in 1387. He fought Tokhtamysh, his former protégé, crushing him in 1391 and 1395 and sacking Sarai. In 1398 he invaded India and sacked Delhi, massacring its inhabitants. In 1400–1401 he took Aleppo, Damascus and Baghdad. In 1402 he defeated the Ottoman sultan Bayezid I at Ankara, temporarily stopping Ottoman expansion. Timur's conquests were not followed by stable administration in many regions.

## Samarkand

Timur filled Samarkand with monuments built by artisans taken from conquered cities: the Bibi-Khanym Mosque, one of the largest of its time; the Gur-e Amir mausoleum, where he is buried; and the Shah-i Zinda necropolis. His hometown, Shahrisabz, received the Ak-Saray palace. The Castilian ambassador Ruy González de Clavijo, who visited in 1404, described gardens, feasts and Timur himself, then old and nearly blind.

## Shah Rukh and Ulugh Beg

After Timur's death, his son Shah Rukh, ruling from Herat from 1409 to 1447, reunited most of the empire. His wife, Gawhar Shad, built a great mosque in Mashhad and a complex in Herat. Shah Rukh's son Ulugh Beg governed Samarkand and was a great astronomer: his observatory, with a huge meridian arc, produced a star catalogue of over a thousand stars, the most accurate since antiquity. He built a madrasa on the Registan square. Ulugh Beg was murdered in 1449, on the orders of his own son.

## The Timurid renaissance

In the late fifteenth century, Sultan Husayn Bayqara's court in Herat was a centre of Persian and Turkic culture. The poet and statesman Ali-Shir Navai wrote in Chagatai Turkic, raising it to a literary language; the poet Jami wrote in Persian; and the painter Behzad created miniatures of great refinement. Timurid architecture and art influenced the Safavids, the Ottomans and the Mughals.

## Fall

The empire was divided among Timur's descendants, who often fought each other. In the west, the Qara Qoyunlu and Aq Qoyunlu Turkmen took Iran and Iraq. In the east, the Uzbek leader Muhammad Shaybani took Samarkand in 1500–1501 and Herat in 1507. Babur, a Timurid prince, failed to hold Samarkand and founded the Mughal Empire in India instead.

## On our maps

Our maps draw the empire on the 1400, 1492 and 1500 maps. It is largest on the first at about 5.1 million square kilometres, almost a third of it in today's Iran, with the rest spread from Iraq and Turkey to Kazakhstan, Afghanistan and Pakistan. Samarkand, Tashkent, Kabul, Isfahan, Baghdad and Baku are Timurid on our 1400 map. Before it, Iran is part of the Ilkhanate; by 1530 it belongs to the Safavids.
