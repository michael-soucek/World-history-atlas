Ethiopia is one of the oldest states in the world and, apart from a five-year Italian occupation, the only African country never colonised by a European power. Its highland Christian kingdom traces its roots to ancient Aksum, and its church to the fourth century.

## The Zagwe dynasty and Lalibela

After the decline of Aksum, the Zagwe dynasty ruled the highlands from around the twelfth century. King Lalibela, in the late twelfth and early thirteenth centuries, ordered the carving of eleven churches from solid volcanic rock at his capital, Roha, now called Lalibela, as a "New Jerusalem". The rock-hewn churches, such as the cross-shaped Bete Giyorgis, are a World Heritage Site and still a place of pilgrimage.

## The Solomonic kingdom

In 1270 a dynasty claiming descent from King Solomon and the Queen of Sheba took power in the highlands. According to the Kebra Nagast, a fourteenth-century chronicle, Menelik I, son of Solomon and Sheba, brought the Ark of the Covenant to Ethiopia. The Solomonic emperors ruled from moving royal camps for centuries and fought Muslim sultanates to the east.

## Church and kingdom

The Ethiopian Orthodox Tewahedo Church, one of the oldest Christian churches, has its own traditions, such as the use of the ancient Ge'ez language in worship, fasting for over two hundred days a year, and the tabot, a replica of the Ark of the Covenant, in every church. Monasteries on islands in Lake Tana and in the mountains preserved manuscripts and icons. Emperor Zara Yaqob in the fifteenth century strengthened the church and royal power. Ethiopia also had Muslim and Jewish communities, the Beta Israel.

## Wars with Adal

In the 1520s and 1530s, the Adal Sultanate, led by Imam Ahmad ibn Ibrahim, known as Gragn, "the left-handed", invaded the Christian highlands with firearms obtained from the Ottomans, burning churches and conquering much of the kingdom. In 1541 a Portuguese force under Cristóvão da Gama arrived to help the emperor; Gragn was killed in 1543. The Oromo people then migrated into much of the south and centre in the sixteenth century, changing the region.

## Gondar

Emperor Fasilides founded Gondar as a permanent capital in 1636, and he and his successors built castles there, combining Ethiopian, Portuguese and Indian styles. Jesuit missionaries had converted Emperor Susenyos to Catholicism, causing civil war; Fasilides expelled the Jesuits. From 1769 to 1855, the Zemene Mesafint, the "Era of the Princes", saw the emperors powerless and regional lords fighting each other.

## Tewodros and Yohannes

Tewodros II, who took power in 1855, reunited the country and tried to modernise it. When he imprisoned British envoys, a British expedition stormed his fortress at Magdala in 1868, and he shot himself. Yohannes IV defeated Egyptian invasions in 1875–1876 and was killed fighting the Mahdists of Sudan in 1889.

## Menelik II and Adwa

Menelik II expanded the empire south, east and west, doubling its size and incorporating many peoples, and founded Addis Ababa in 1886. When Italy claimed a protectorate based on a disputed treaty, Menelik's army defeated the Italians at Adwa on 1 March 1896. Adwa became a symbol of African resistance to colonialism.

## Haile Selassie

Ras Tafari Makonnen became regent in 1916 and emperor in 1930 as Haile Selassie I. He introduced a written constitution in 1931 and modern schools. When Fascist Italy invaded in 1935, using poison gas, he appealed to the League of Nations in a famous speech in Geneva. He returned in 1941. He became a leading figure of African independence and helped found the Organisation of African Unity, headquartered in Addis Ababa, in 1963. In Jamaica, the Rastafari movement regards him as divine.

## Since 1974

The Derg's Marxist regime, led by Mengistu Haile Mariam, ruled through terror and fought rebellions in Eritrea and Tigray, while famine in 1983–1985 killed hundreds of thousands. Since 1991, Ethiopia has had a federal system based on ethnic regions. A war in Tigray from 2020 to 2022 killed many thousands. Ethiopia is Africa's second most populous country.
