The Sui dynasty reunified China in 589 after nearly three centuries of division, and ruled it until 618. Like the Qin, eight centuries earlier, it was short-lived, overreached and collapsed, but it laid the foundations of the Tang dynasty, one of China's greatest.

## The Northern and Southern Dynasties

After the fall of the Western Jin in 316, China was divided for nearly three centuries. In the north, states ruled by non-Chinese peoples, such as the Xianbei Northern Wei, governed alongside Chinese elites; in the south, a series of Chinese dynasties ruled from Jiankang, now Nanjing. Buddhism spread widely in both, and the Northern Wei created the great Buddhist cave temples of Yungang and Longmen.

## Emperor Wen

Yang Jian, a general from an aristocratic family of mixed Chinese and Xianbei ancestry, was the father-in-law of the Northern Zhou emperor. In 581 he seized the throne, killing many members of the Zhou royal family, and became Emperor Wen of Sui. In 589 his armies crossed the Yangtze and conquered the Chen dynasty, uniting China. Emperor Wen built a new capital, Daxing, on the site of the later Tang Chang'an.

## Reforms

Emperor Wen reorganised the central government into Three Departments and Six Ministries, a system used for centuries. He simplified local government, reducing the number of administrative units, and issued a new law code, the Kaihuang Code, which reduced punishments. He continued the equal-field system of land distribution and reformed taxes. He held census counts that found millions of unregistered households. He introduced examinations for selecting some officials, a step towards the later civil-service system. He was a devout Buddhist and built many temples and pagodas.

## Emperor Yang

Emperor Yang, who came to the throne in 604, possibly after murdering his father, has been portrayed by later historians as a tyrant. He built a lavish eastern capital at Luoyang, rebuilt the Great Wall and completed the Grand Canal, linking Hangzhou in the south with the Yellow River and Zhuojun, near Beijing, in the north, more than 2,000 kilometres. Millions of labourers worked on these projects, many dying. He travelled the canal in a fleet of luxurious barges.

## The Korean campaigns

Emperor Yang launched three campaigns against Goguryeo, in 612, 613 and 614. The first, with over a million soldiers, according to records, ended in disaster when the Goguryeo general Eulji Mundeok defeated a Sui army at the Salsu river. The other campaigns also failed, while rebellions spread in China.

## The fall

Peasants burdened by forced labour, conscription and floods rose in revolt from 611. Generals and aristocrats rebelled too. Emperor Yang retreated to Jiangdu, now Yangzhou, and was strangled by his officers in 618. The Duke of Tang, Li Yuan, took Chang'an and founded the Tang dynasty.

## Legacy

The Sui is often compared with the Qin: a short-lived dynasty that unified China and laid the foundations for a great successor. The Grand Canal, its institutions and the reunited empire made the Tang golden age possible.
