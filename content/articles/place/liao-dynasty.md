The Liao dynasty was founded by the Khitans, a nomadic people of what is now Inner Mongolia and Manchuria. From 916 to 1125 it ruled a large empire across north-east Asia, including a strip of northern China around modern Beijing. The Khitans were so well known along the trade routes that their name became, via Central Asian languages, the word for China in Russian and other languages, Kitai, and the old English "Cathay".

## The Khitans

The Khitans, a people speaking a language related to Mongolian, lived along the Liao river, herding horses, cattle and sheep, hunting and fishing. Chinese sources mention them from the fourth century. They were organised in eight tribes, whose leaders were chosen in turn. They were sometimes subject to the Turks, Uyghurs and Tang China.

## Abaoji

In 907, as the Tang dynasty collapsed, Yelü Abaoji became leader of the Khitans and refused to give up power after his term. In 916 he proclaimed himself emperor, Taizu, in the Chinese style, founding a hereditary dynasty. He created a Khitan script, conquered neighbouring peoples and, in 926, destroyed the kingdom of Balhae, in Manchuria and northern Korea, dying on the way back. His widow, Empress Shulü Ping, a powerful figure, reportedly cut off her own hand to place in his tomb rather than follow him in death.

## The Sixteen Prefectures

In 938 the Khitans helped Shi Jingtang found the Later Jin dynasty in northern China, receiving in return the Sixteen Prefectures, the region around Beijing and Datong, south of the Great Wall. In 947 the Khitans invaded northern China and took Kaifeng, and the dynasty took the Chinese name Liao. The Sixteen Prefectures, with their Chinese population, gave the Liao a strategic position and wealth.

## A dual state

The Liao developed a dual administration. The Northern Administration governed the Khitans and other tribal peoples according to their customs, and the Southern Administration governed the Chinese population using Chinese institutions, including examinations. The emperors kept a nomadic court, moving between seasonal camps, nabo. The Liao had five capitals, including the Supreme Capital, Shangjing, in Inner Mongolia, and the Southern Capital, Nanjing, today's Beijing.

## Peace with the Song

The Song dynasty, which reunified most of China from 960, tried to take the Sixteen Prefectures in 979 and 986 but failed. In 1004 the Liao invaded deep into Song territory, and in 1005 the two empires signed the Treaty of Chanyuan: the Song agreed to pay annual gifts of 100,000 taels of silver and 200,000 bolts of silk, and the two emperors called each other brothers. The peace lasted for over a century, with trade and diplomatic exchanges.

## Culture

The Liao were Buddhists, and built many temples and pagodas, such as the Yingxian wooden pagoda, built in 1056, the oldest and tallest surviving wooden pagoda in China, and the Dule temple near Tianjin. Khitan tombs contain murals of daily life, hunting and herding, and gold and silver objects, including masks for the dead. The Khitan large and small scripts are still only partly deciphered.

## The fall

In the early twelfth century, the Jurchens, a subject people in Manchuria, rebelled under Wanyan Aguda, who founded the Jin dynasty in 1115. The Song allied with the Jurchens against the Liao, and in 1125 the last Liao emperor, Tianzuo, was captured. A Khitan prince, Yelü Dashi, fled west with followers and founded the Western Liao, or Qara Khitai, in Central Asia, which lasted until the Mongol conquest in 1218.
