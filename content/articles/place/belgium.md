Belgium became independent in 1830, after a revolution against the United Kingdom of the Netherlands, into which it had been placed by the Congress of Vienna. Before that its provinces had been ruled in turn by Burgundy, Spain, Austria and France, and had been the battlefield of Europe for centuries.

## Before Belgium

The name comes from the Belgae, Celtic tribes conquered by Julius Caesar, who called them the bravest of the Gauls. In the Middle Ages, Flanders and Brabant grew rich from the cloth trade, and cities such as Bruges, Ghent, Antwerp and Leuven flourished. The Flemish painters Jan van Eyck, Rogier van der Weyden and later Pieter Bruegel and Peter Paul Rubens are among the greatest in European art. In 1302, at the Battle of the Golden Spurs, Flemish townsmen defeated French knights.

## Under foreign rulers

The region passed to the dukes of Burgundy in the fifteenth century, then to the Habsburgs. After the Dutch revolt, the southern provinces remained under Spain as the Spanish Netherlands and became a battlefield in Europe's wars, earning the name "the cockpit of Europe". They passed to Austria in 1714, were annexed by France in 1795, and joined the United Kingdom of the Netherlands in 1815.

## The revolution of 1830

On 25 August 1830, a performance in Brussels of the opera La Muette de Portici, with its patriotic duet, inspired riots. Dutch troops were driven out, and a provisional government declared independence. The National Congress drafted a liberal constitution, and chose Leopold of Saxe-Coburg as king. The great powers recognised Belgium and guaranteed its neutrality in the Treaty of London of 1839.

## Industrial power

Belgium was the first country in continental Europe to industrialise, with coal mines and iron works in Wallonia, especially around Liège and Charleroi, and textile mills in Ghent. The first railway on the continent opened between Brussels and Mechelen in 1835. Antwerp became a major port. Conditions for workers were harsh, leading to strikes and the founding of the Belgian Workers' Party in 1885.

## Leopold II and the Congo

Leopold II, king from 1865 to 1909, acquired the Congo Free State as his personal property in 1885. His regime forced Congolese people to collect rubber, using hostage-taking, violence and mutilation, causing millions of deaths. The scandal, exposed by Edmund Morel, Roger Casement and others, led Belgium to annex the colony in 1908. Leopold used the profits to build monuments in Brussels and Ostend.

## The world wars

In August 1914, Germany invaded neutral Belgium, killing thousands of civilians and burning Leuven's university library. King Albert I led the army from a small unoccupied area behind the Yser river, and the Ypres salient saw some of the war's worst fighting. Germany invaded again in 1940, and King Leopold III surrendered, causing controversy that led to his abdication in 1951. The Battle of the Bulge was fought in the Ardennes in 1944–1945.

## Language and federalism

Belgium has three official languages: Dutch, French and German. For much of the nineteenth century, French dominated government and society, and the Flemish movement fought for equal rights for Dutch. Tensions grew as Flanders became richer than Wallonia after the decline of heavy industry. Constitutional reforms from 1970 to 2011 created a federal state with Flemish, Walloon and Brussels regions and language communities. Brussels is the main seat of the European Union and the headquarters of NATO.
