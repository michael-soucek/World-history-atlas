The Mongol Empire was the largest land empire in history. It began in 1206, when an assembly of the steppe peoples of Mongolia proclaimed a leader named Temüjin as Genghis Khan. Within seventy years his family ruled from Korea to Hungary, and from Siberia to the Persian Gulf.

## Genghis Khan

Temüjin grew up in poverty after his father was poisoned, and built his power through alliances, loyalty and victories over rival clans. As khan he reorganised the Mongols into military units of tens, hundreds and thousands that cut across old tribal lines, gave them a written law code and adopted a script for the Mongolian language. His armies of mounted archers conquered the Western Xia and the north of the Jin state in China, then destroyed the Khwarazmian Empire in Central Asia after it killed his envoys.

## The Mongol army

The Mongol army was made up almost entirely of mounted archers, each man with several horses so that he could change mounts and cover long distances fast. Soldiers carried powerful composite bows and lived largely off their animals' milk and meat. Units of ten, a hundred, a thousand and ten thousand were commanded by officers chosen for loyalty and skill. Discipline was strict, and the army used feigned retreats, encirclement and careful scouting. The Mongols quickly adopted siege techniques and engineers from China and the Muslim world, using catapults, gunpowder bombs and diverted rivers against fortified cities.

## Terror and submission

Cities that surrendered were often spared, though required to pay tribute and supply troops; cities that resisted were frequently destroyed and their populations killed, except for craftsmen and others who were useful. The destruction of cities such as Merv, Nishapur and Baghdad was so complete that historians debate the population losses, which may have reached millions in Central Asia, Iran and northern China. Terror was used deliberately to encourage others to surrender.

## Expansion

Genghis died in 1227, but his sons and grandsons kept expanding. They took northern China, invaded the Rus' principalities and sacked Kyiv in 1240, and swept into Poland and Hungary in 1241 before turning back. In 1258 Hülegü sacked Baghdad and ended the Abbasid Caliphate. Kublai Khan completed the conquest of Song China in 1279. Along the way the Mongols killed on an enormous scale, but they also protected trade along the Silk Road, and merchants, missionaries and travellers such as Marco Polo crossed Eurasia in their wake.

## Ögedei and Möngke

Genghis's third son, Ögedei, became Great Khan in 1229. He completed the conquest of the Jin dynasty in 1234, built a capital at Karakorum in Mongolia and sent the great western campaign under Batu. His death in 1241 led to a succession crisis; his son Güyük reigned briefly. In 1251 Möngke, son of Tolui, became Great Khan after a purge of rivals. He sent his brother Hülegü to conquer the Middle East and his brother Kublai to fight the Song, and died in 1259 while campaigning in China.

## Ruling the empire

The Mongols ruled through local officials, often Muslims, Chinese and Uyghurs, and used a census to assess taxes and conscript soldiers. A relay system of postal stations, the yam, allowed messengers and officials to cross the empire quickly, with passes called paiza. The khans were generally tolerant of religions, exempting clergy of all faiths from taxes, and debates among Buddhists, Muslims, Christians and Daoists were held at court. The Franciscan friar William of Rubruck visited Möngke's court at Karakorum in 1254 and described one such debate.

## The Pax Mongolica

By uniting much of Eurasia under one dynasty, the Mongols made it easier for merchants, missionaries and diplomats to travel between China, Persia and Europe. Technologies and ideas moved along the routes: Chinese medicine and printing, Persian astronomy and Islamic science. The Black Death, which spread west in the 1340s, may also have travelled along these routes.

## Division

The empire began to split after Möngke's death, when Kublai and his brother Ariq Böke fought for the throne. The Golden Horde, the Chagatai Khanate and the Ilkhanate became independent in practice, and sometimes fought each other. The Ilkhanate and the Golden Horde clashed over the Caucasus, and the Golden Horde allied with the Mamluks against the Ilkhans.

## Legacy

The Mongol Empire reshaped Eurasia. It united China under one rule, contributed to the rise of Moscow, ended the Abbasid caliphate, and spread contacts across the continent. Genghis Khan's descendants ruled states in Central Asia, Crimea and India, through the Mughals, for centuries. In Mongolia today, Genghis is honoured as a national hero.
