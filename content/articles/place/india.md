The Republic of India became independent from Britain at midnight on 14–15 August 1947. It is the world's most populous country and its largest democracy, and its civilisation is one of the oldest on earth, going back to the cities of the Indus valley in the third millennium BCE.

## Ancient roots

India's history as a civilisation reaches back to the Indus Valley cities of the third millennium BCE. The Vedic period, from around 1500 BCE, produced the Vedas and the foundations of Hinduism. The Maurya and Gupta empires united much of the subcontinent in ancient times, and the Delhi Sultanate and the Mughal Empire did so in the medieval and early modern periods. Buddhism, Jainism and Sikhism all began in India.

## British rule

The British East India Company took control of Bengal after 1757 and most of India by the mid-nineteenth century. After the uprising of 1857, the British crown ruled directly. The Raj built railways, universities and a civil service, but India's economy served British interests, and famines killed millions. Indian reformers, such as Raja Ram Mohan Roy in the early nineteenth century, campaigned against practices such as sati, the burning of widows.

## The freedom struggle

The Indian National Congress, founded in 1885, at first sought reforms. Leaders such as Bal Gangadhar Tilak demanded self-rule. After the Amritsar massacre of 1919, Mahatma Gandhi led mass campaigns of non-violent non-cooperation and civil disobedience. Jawaharlal Nehru, Sardar Vallabhbhai Patel, Maulana Azad and Sarojini Naidu were among the movement's leaders. Subhas Chandra Bose formed the Indian National Army, fighting alongside Japan in the Second World War. The Muslim League under Muhammad Ali Jinnah demanded a separate Muslim state.

## Independence and partition

The movement for independence from British rule grew from the founding of the Indian National Congress in 1885. Under Mahatma Gandhi, from the 1920s, it used mass non-violent civil disobedience, such as the Salt March of 1930. Independence came with partition: British India was divided into India and Pakistan, a Muslim-majority state. The partition was accompanied by violence in which hundreds of thousands, possibly far more, were killed, and some 15 million people were displaced. Gandhi was assassinated in January 1948.

## Independence and the princely states

India became independent on 15 August 1947, as Pakistan was created. Around 560 princely states had to join India or Pakistan; Patel and his secretary V. P. Menon persuaded most to join India, while Hyderabad was taken by force in 1948. Kashmir's accession led to war with Pakistan. Gandhi was assassinated in January 1948.

## The constitution

The constitution, drafted by a committee chaired by B. R. Ambedkar, a jurist from the Dalit community, came into force on 26 January 1950, now Republic Day. It created a federal parliamentary democracy, guaranteed fundamental rights, abolished untouchability and provided reserved places for disadvantaged castes and tribes. India's first general election in 1951–1952 was the largest democratic exercise the world had seen.

## Nehru to Indira Gandhi

Nehru, prime minister from 1947 to 1964, pursued planned economic development, built dams, steel plants and institutes of technology, and led the Non-Aligned Movement. States were reorganised along linguistic lines. India lost a border war with China in 1962. Indira Gandhi, prime minister from 1966 to 1977 and 1980 to 1984, nationalised banks, led India to victory over Pakistan in 1971, creating Bangladesh, and declared the Emergency in 1975. She ordered the army into the Golden Temple in Amritsar in 1984 and was assassinated by her Sikh bodyguards, which was followed by massacres of Sikhs.

## Liberalisation and the present

Economic reforms in 1991, led by finance minister Manmohan Singh, opened the economy, and India grew rapidly, especially in information technology and services. Coalition governments became common in the 1990s and 2000s. The Bharatiya Janata Party, a Hindu nationalist party, led governments from 1998 to 2004 and, under Narendra Modi, from 2014. India has become the world's most populous country and one of its largest economies, with a space programme that reached the Moon and Mars.
