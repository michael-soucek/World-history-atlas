Austria-Hungary was the last form of the Habsburg Monarchy. It was created by the Compromise of 1867, which turned the Austrian Empire into a partnership of two states, Austria and Hungary, each with its own parliament and government, sharing a monarch, an army and a foreign policy. The emperor, Franz Joseph, was emperor of Austria and king of Hungary.

## The Compromise of 1867

After Austria's defeat by Prussia in 1866, Emperor Franz Joseph reached an agreement with Hungarian leaders, such as Ferenc Deák and Gyula Andrássy, that created a dual monarchy. Austria, officially "the kingdoms and lands represented in the Imperial Council", and the Kingdom of Hungary each had its own parliament, government and laws. Joint ministries handled foreign affairs, the army and the finances that paid for them. The economic arrangements were renegotiated every ten years, a source of constant tension. Franz Joseph was crowned king of Hungary in Budapest in 1867.

## Nationalities

The monarchy's population, about 52 million in 1914, included Germans, Hungarians, Czechs, Poles, Ukrainians, Romanians, Croats, Slovaks, Serbs, Slovenes and Italians. In the Austrian half, the constitution of 1867 recognised equal rights for all nationalities, and languages were used in schools and administration, though disputes, such as over the use of Czech in Bohemia, were bitter. In Hungary, Magyar became the dominant language, and other nationalities faced Magyarisation. Croatia had its own autonomy under the Croatian-Hungarian Settlement of 1868.

## Franz Joseph

Franz Joseph reigned from 1848 to 1916, 68 years. He was a conscientious, conservative monarch who suffered great personal tragedies: his brother Maximilian, emperor of Mexico, was executed in 1867; his son Crown Prince Rudolf died in an apparent murder-suicide at Mayerling in 1889; and his wife, Empress Elisabeth, known as Sisi, was assassinated by an anarchist in Geneva in 1898.

## Culture and science

Vienna and Budapest flourished around 1900. In Vienna, Sigmund Freud developed psychoanalysis, Gustav Klimt and the Secession artists created new styles, Gustav Mahler composed symphonies, and the architect Otto Wagner designed modern buildings. Budapest built the Hungarian Parliament and the first underground railway in continental Europe, opened in 1896. Prague was home to Franz Kafka. The monarchy's universities produced scientists such as the physicist Ludwig Boltzmann and the Czech-born geneticist Gregor Mendel.

## The economy

The monarchy industrialised, especially in Bohemia, Austria and around Budapest, with steel, machinery, glass, sugar and beer. Hungary was a major producer of grain. A large free trade area, a common currency, the crown, and railways connected the regions. But much of Galicia and Transylvania remained poor, and millions emigrated, especially to the United States.

## Foreign policy and the Balkans

Austria-Hungary allied with Germany in 1879. It occupied Bosnia and Herzegovina in 1878 and annexed it in 1908, angering Serbia and Russia. After the heir, Archduke Franz Ferdinand, was assassinated in Sarajevo on 28 June 1914 by Gavrilo Princip, a member of a Bosnian Serb nationalist group, Austria-Hungary declared war on Serbia, beginning the First World War.

## Collapse

The monarchy's army suffered huge losses against Russia and Italy. Franz Joseph died in 1916, and his successor Charles I tried to make a secret peace. In October 1918, Charles offered to turn the Austrian half into a federation, but it was too late. Czechoslovakia, the State of Slovenes, Croats and Serbs, Poland and others declared independence, Hungary ended the union, and Charles withdrew from government in November 1918.
