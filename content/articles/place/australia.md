Australia has been home to Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander peoples for at least 50,000 to 65,000 years, making their cultures among the oldest continuous cultures in the world. The modern country was formed in 1901, when six British colonies federated as the Commonwealth of Australia.

## The First Peoples

Before European arrival, hundreds of Aboriginal nations, speaking around 250 languages, lived across the continent, with complex systems of law, kinship and spiritual belief tied to the land, often called the Dreaming. They managed the land with fire, built fish traps and eel farms, and travelled along trade and ceremonial routes, songlines, that crossed the continent. The Torres Strait Islanders, with their own Melanesian culture, lived on the islands between Australia and New Guinea.

## European exploration

Dutch navigators charted parts of the western and northern coasts in the seventeenth century, calling the land New Holland; Abel Tasman reached Tasmania in 1642. In 1770 James Cook sailed along the east coast in the Endeavour, landing at Botany Bay, and claimed it for Britain. The naturalist Joseph Banks recommended it as a site for a colony.

## The convict colony

Britain, having lost its American colonies, needed a place to send convicts. In January 1788 the First Fleet, eleven ships carrying about 750 convicts and their guards under Captain Arthur Phillip, arrived at Sydney Cove. The colony of New South Wales struggled with hunger at first. Between 1788 and 1868, around 160,000 convicts were transported to Australia. New colonies were founded: Van Diemen's Land, later Tasmania, Western Australia, South Australia, founded by free settlers in 1836, Victoria and Queensland.

## Frontier violence

Colonisation devastated Aboriginal societies. Smallpox and other diseases killed many, and settlers took land for sheep and cattle. Frontier conflicts and massacres, such as Myall Creek in 1838, killed many thousands of Aboriginal people. In Tasmania, the "Black War" of the 1820s and the removal of survivors to Flinders Island almost destroyed the Aboriginal population. The legal doctrine of terra nullius treated the land as belonging to no one.

## Gold and growth

Gold was discovered in New South Wales and Victoria in 1851, starting rushes that brought hundreds of thousands of migrants, including many from China. Melbourne grew into one of the richest cities in the world. In 1854, gold miners at Ballarat rebelled against licence fees at the Eureka Stockade, a symbol of Australian democracy. Wool, wheat and minerals became major exports. The colonies gained self-government in the 1850s and introduced the secret ballot, sometimes called the "Australian ballot".

## Federation and the twentieth century

The colonies federated on 1 January 1901, with a constitution combining British parliamentary government and American-style federalism. Canberra was built as the capital. The White Australia policy restricted non-European immigration until the 1970s. Australian soldiers fought at Gallipoli in 1915, remembered on Anzac Day, 25 April, and about 60,000 died in the First World War. In the Second World War, Japan bombed Darwin in 1942, and Australia turned to the United States for protection.

## Modern Australia

After 1945, mass immigration from Britain and Europe, and later Asia, transformed the population. The 1967 referendum allowed Aboriginal people to be counted in the census. The Stolen Generations, Aboriginal children removed from their families from around 1910 to the 1970s, received a national apology in 2008. The Mabo decision of 1992 overturned terra nullius and recognised native title. A 2023 referendum on an Indigenous Voice to Parliament was rejected. Australia is a prosperous democracy, a major exporter of minerals and a close ally of the United States.
