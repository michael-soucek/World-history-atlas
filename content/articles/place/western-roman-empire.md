"Western Roman Empire" is a modern name. Romans at the time thought of a single empire ruled by two emperors, one in the west and one in the east. The arrangement became permanent after Theodosius I died in 395 and left the two halves to his young sons, Honorius in the west and Arcadius in the east. Within eighty years the western half had disappeared.

## Emperors in name

The western emperors of the fifth century were often children or figureheads. Honorius, who ruled from 395 to 423, spent his reign behind the marshes of Ravenna while generals fought the empire's wars. Real power lay with commanders known as magister militum: Stilicho, until his execution in 408; Constantius III; Aetius; and later the Germanic general Ricimer, who made and unmade several emperors between 456 and 472.

## The barbarians within

Rome had long recruited Germanic soldiers, and in the fifth century whole peoples settled inside the empire as allies, foederati, under their own kings, in return for military service. The Visigoths were settled in Aquitaine in 418, and the Burgundians along the Rhône. These arrangements kept the peace for a time, but the kings increasingly acted as independent rulers, taxing and governing Roman provincials themselves.

## Alaric and the sack of Rome

The Visigothic leader Alaric, who had served Rome, demanded land and pay for his people. After Stilicho's fall, Alaric besieged Rome three times and in August 410 sacked it for three days. The event shocked the Roman world; in North Africa, Augustine of Hippo began his great work The City of God partly in response, arguing that Christians should not place their hopes in earthly cities.

## The loss of Africa and Britain

Roman troops withdrew from Britain around 410, leaving the Britons to defend themselves against Picts, Scots and later Angles and Saxons. In 429 the Vandals, led by King Gaiseric, crossed from Spain to Africa, and in 439 they took Carthage. Africa was the western empire's richest province and supplied Rome's grain; its loss was a financial disaster. The Vandal fleet then raided across the Mediterranean, and in 455 sacked Rome for two weeks.

## Aetius and Attila

Flavius Aetius, who had spent time as a hostage among the Huns, dominated the west from the 430s, using Hunnic troops to control the Burgundians and others. When Attila invaded Gaul in 451, Aetius led a coalition of Romans, Visigoths, Franks and others that stopped him at the Catalaunian Plains. In 454, the emperor Valentinian III, jealous of Aetius, killed him with his own hands; Valentinian was assassinated the next year by Aetius's followers.

## The final years

After 455, the west had nine emperors in twenty-one years. A great combined expedition of east and west against the Vandals failed in 468. In 475 the general Orestes made his son Romulus emperor; the next year the German soldiers in Italy, led by Odoacer, revolted, killed Orestes and deposed Romulus, who was pensioned off to a villa near Naples. Julius Nepos, a rival emperor in Dalmatia, claimed the title until his death in 480.

## After Rome

Italy under Odoacer, and then under the Ostrogothic king Theodoric from 493, kept the senate, Roman law and Roman administration. In Gaul, the Franks under Clovis replaced Roman authority and Visigothic power. Roman landowners, bishops and towns survived in the new kingdoms, and Latin evolved into the Romance languages. Historians debate whether the period should be seen as a sudden fall or a gradual transformation.
