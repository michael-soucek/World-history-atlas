The Seljuks were a family of Oghuz Turks from the steppes east of the Caspian who, in the eleventh century, conquered Iran, Iraq and much of the Middle East. Their empire, often called the Great Seljuk Empire, marked the start of centuries of Turkic rule in the Islamic heartlands and opened Anatolia to Turkish settlement.

## The Oghuz Turks

The Seljuks were a clan of the Oghuz, Turkic nomads of the steppes north of the Aral Sea and the Syr Darya. Seljuk, the founder, served the Oghuz yabgu, then broke away and converted to Islam with his followers, settling near Jand around 985. His grandsons Tughril and Chaghri led their followers into Khurasan in the 1030s, where they clashed with the Ghaznavids.

## Tughril and the caliph

After Dandanaqan in 1040, the Seljuks took Khurasan and western Iran, making Isfahan and Ray their capitals. In 1055 Tughril entered Baghdad at the invitation of the Abbasid caliph al-Qaim, ending the domination of the Shia Buyids. The caliph gave him the title "Sultan of East and West". Tughril married the caliph's daughter. The Seljuks presented themselves as defenders of Sunni Islam against the Fatimids and Shia.

## Alp Arslan and Malik Shah

Alp Arslan, who ruled from 1063 to 1072, took Armenia, Georgia and Aleppo. In August 1071, at Manzikert, he defeated and captured the Byzantine emperor Romanos IV Diogenes; he treated him with respect and released him. After the battle, Turkish groups moved into Anatolia. Alp Arslan was killed in 1072 by a captive. Malik Shah, his son, ruled from 1072 to 1092, the empire's height, from Central Asia to Syria and Anatolia.

## Nizam al-Mulk

The Persian vizier Nizam al-Mulk ran the empire for thirty years. He used the iqta system, granting soldiers the tax revenue from land, and founded madrasas, colleges called Nizamiyya, in Baghdad, Nishapur and other cities to train Sunni scholars and officials. The theologian al-Ghazali taught at the Baghdad Nizamiyya. Nizam al-Mulk wrote the Siyasatnama, the Book of Government, advice for rulers.

## Science and culture

Under Malik Shah, the astronomer and poet Omar Khayyam led a team that created the Jalali calendar, introduced in 1079, more accurate than the Gregorian calendar adopted in Europe five centuries later. Khayyam is famous in the West for his Rubaiyat, quatrains of poetry, translated by Edward FitzGerald. Seljuk architecture developed the four-iwan mosque plan, as at the Great Mosque of Isfahan, and caravanserais along trade routes.

## The Assassins

The Nizari Ismailis, led by Hasan-i Sabbah, seized the mountain fortress of Alamut in northern Iran in 1090 and used targeted killings against Seljuk leaders. Nizam al-Mulk was murdered in 1092, probably by an Assassin, and Malik Shah died weeks later.

## Fragmentation

After 1092, the empire split among Malik Shah's sons and relatives. Separate Seljuk states arose in Iraq, Kerman, Syria and Anatolia, where the Sultanate of Rum lasted until 1308. The Crusaders took Antioch and Jerusalem during this disunity. Sultan Sanjar ruled Khurasan until he was defeated by the Qara Khitai at Qatwan in 1141 and captured by Oghuz tribes in 1153. The Khwarazm Shah Tekish killed the last Seljuk sultan of Iran, Tughril III, in 1194.

## On our maps

Our maps show the Seljuk Empire on the 1100 map, after its first break-up, with an outline of about 2.1 million square kilometres. Nearly two-thirds of that is in today's Iran, with most of the rest in Iraq and Syria. Baghdad, Damascus, Tehran's site and Isfahan lie inside it. Before it, the region belongs to the Buyids; by 1200, to the Khwarazmian Empire.
