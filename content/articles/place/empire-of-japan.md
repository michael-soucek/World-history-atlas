The Empire of Japan is the name for the Japanese state from the Meiji Restoration of 1868 to the adoption of the postwar constitution in 1947. In eighty years, Japan turned itself from an isolated feudal country into an industrial and military power, built an empire across East Asia and the Pacific, and was defeated in the Second World War.

## The Meiji Restoration

In 1868 samurai from the domains of Satsuma and Chōshū overthrew the Tokugawa shogunate and restored power to the young Emperor Meiji. The new leaders, such as Ōkubo Toshimichi, Kido Takayoshi, Itō Hirobumi and Yamagata Aritomo, adopted the slogan "rich country, strong army". They abolished the feudal domains in 1871, ended the samurai class, introduced conscription in 1873 and universal education, and sent the Iwakura Mission to study Western countries. Railways, factories, banks and a modern navy were built. The Satsuma Rebellion of 1877, led by Saigō Takamori, was the last samurai revolt.

## The Meiji Constitution

The constitution of 1889, modelled partly on Prussia's, created a parliament, the Imperial Diet, with an elected lower house, but gave great power to the emperor, considered sacred, and to the military, which answered directly to him. State Shinto promoted loyalty to the emperor, and the Imperial Rescript on Education of 1890 taught loyalty and filial piety.

## Wars and colonies

Japan defeated China in 1894–1895, gaining Taiwan, and defeated Russia in 1904–1905, gaining southern Sakhalin, the Liaodong peninsula and influence in Manchuria; the naval victory at Tsushima in 1905 astonished the world. Japan made Korea a protectorate in 1905 and annexed it in 1910, ruling it harshly. The unequal treaties with Western powers were revised by 1911. In the First World War, Japan joined the Allies, took German possessions in China and the Pacific, and issued the Twenty-One Demands to China in 1915.

## Taishō democracy

In the 1910s and 1920s, political parties gained influence, and universal male suffrage was introduced in 1925, alongside the Peace Preservation Law, which suppressed communists. The Great Kantō earthquake of 1923 destroyed much of Tokyo and Yokohama and killed over 100,000 people; in its aftermath, mobs and police murdered thousands of Koreans falsely accused of crimes.

## Militarism

The Great Depression hurt Japan's economy, and nationalists and young officers attacked party politicians; Prime Minister Inukai Tsuyoshi was assassinated in 1932. In 1931 the army staged the Mukden Incident and seized Manchuria, creating the puppet state of Manchukuo. Japan left the League of Nations in 1933. A coup attempt by young officers in February 1936 failed, but the military gained more control.

## War in China and the Pacific

In 1937 full-scale war began with China. Japanese troops took Shanghai and Nanjing, where they carried out the Nanjing Massacre, killing tens of thousands, and possibly many more, civilians and prisoners. Unit 731 conducted biological experiments on humans. Japan allied with Germany and Italy in 1940. On 7 December 1941, Japan attacked Pearl Harbor and invaded the Philippines, Malaya, Singapore, the Dutch East Indies and Burma, proclaiming a "Greater East Asia Co-Prosperity Sphere". Occupied peoples suffered forced labour, massacres and the "comfort women" system of sexual slavery.

## Defeat

From the Battle of Midway in 1942, the tide turned. American forces advanced across the Pacific, and bombing destroyed Japanese cities. After the atomic bombings of Hiroshima on 6 August and Nagasaki on 9 August 1945, and the Soviet declaration of war, Emperor Hirohito announced Japan's surrender on 15 August. Under American occupation, the 1947 constitution made the emperor a symbol, gave sovereignty to the people and renounced war, ending the Empire of Japan.
