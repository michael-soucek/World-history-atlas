The Umayyad Caliphate was the first Islamic dynasty, ruling from Damascus between 661 and 750. Under the Umayyads the Arab conquests reached their furthest extent, from Spain and Morocco in the west to Central Asia and the Indus valley in the east, making it one of the largest states the world had yet seen.

## Muawiya's rule

Muawiya governed through tribal alliances, diplomacy and patience, describing his approach as not using the sword where the whip would do, or the whip where a word would do. He relied on the Syrian Arab tribes and the Byzantine-trained bureaucracy of Syria, and built a navy that raided the Aegean and besieged Constantinople. He named his son Yazid as heir, introducing hereditary succession, which many Muslims opposed.

## Karbala and the second civil war

When Yazid became caliph in 680, Husayn, the grandson of the Prophet and son of Ali, refused to recognise him and set out for Kufa with his family and a small band of followers. At Karbala, in Iraq, they were surrounded by an Umayyad army and, on 10 Muharram, killed. The martyrdom of Husayn became the central event of Shia Islam, commemorated each year at Ashura. In the civil war that followed, Abd Allah ibn al-Zubayr declared himself caliph in Mecca, and controlled much of the caliphate until Abd al-Malik's general al-Hajjaj took Mecca in 692.

## The Dome of the Rock

Abd al-Malik completed the Dome of the Rock in Jerusalem in 691–692, on the Temple Mount, the oldest surviving major Islamic monument. Its mosaics and inscriptions, which proclaim the unity of God and address Christians directly about the nature of Jesus, announced the new faith's place in the holy city. His son al-Walid I built the Great Mosque of Damascus on the site of a church, with spectacular mosaics of landscapes and buildings, and rebuilt the Prophet's Mosque in Medina.

## A state of Arabs

Under the Umayyads, the ruling class was Arab. Arab soldiers lived in garrison cities and received stipends, while the conquered populations paid taxes. Many people converted to Islam, but converts, called mawali, often had to attach themselves to Arab tribes as clients and did not receive equal treatment, sometimes still paying taxes meant for non-Muslims. The caliph Umar II (717–720) tried to address these grievances, but his reforms did not last.

## Desert palaces

The Umayyad elite built palaces and bathhouses in the deserts of Syria and Jordan, such as Qusayr Amra, whose walls are painted with hunting scenes, bathing women, and rulers of the world, and Khirbat al-Mafjar near Jericho, with its famous mosaic of a lion attacking a gazelle. These show a court culture drawing on Byzantine and Persian traditions.

## Expansion and its limits

Under al-Walid I, armies took Transoxiana under Qutayba ibn Muslim, Sind under Muhammad ibn al-Qasim, and Spain under Tariq ibn Ziyad, whose name lives on in Gibraltar, Jabal Tariq. But the failed siege of Constantinople in 717–718, the defeat by the Khazars in the Caucasus, the battle near Tours in 732, and a great Berber revolt in North Africa from 740 showed the limits of expansion and strained the treasury.

## The fall

Discontent spread among non-Arab Muslims, Shia supporters of the Prophet's family, Kharijites and rival Arab tribal factions. In Khurasan, the Abbasid movement, led by Abu Muslim, raised black banners in 747. In 750 the Abbasid army defeated the last Umayyad caliph, Marwan II, at the Battle of the Zab, and Marwan was killed in Egypt. The Abbasids invited Umayyad family members to a banquet and massacred them. Abd al-Rahman I escaped and founded the Emirate of Córdoba in 756.
