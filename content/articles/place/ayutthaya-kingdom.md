The Ayutthaya Kingdom was the main Thai state for four centuries, from 1351 to 1767, and is regarded as the forerunner of modern Thailand. Its capital, the island city of Ayutthaya on the Chao Phraya river, north of today's Bangkok, was one of the great trading cities of Asia, and European visitors compared it to Venice and Paris.

## Founding

Thai-speaking peoples had moved south from today's southern China and northern Vietnam over several centuries, founding states such as Sukhothai in the thirteenth century. Ayutthaya was founded in 1351 by King Ramathibodi I, also known as U Thong, on an island at the junction of three rivers. He named it after Ayodhya, the city of the hero Rama in the Ramayana. The city controlled the fertile rice lands of the Chao Phraya basin and access to the sea.

## Expansion

Ayutthaya absorbed Sukhothai in the fifteenth century and fought the Khmer Empire, capturing Angkor in 1431, after which the Khmer court moved south. Ayutthaya's kings adopted Khmer court rituals, ideas of divine kingship and the use of Khmer and Sanskrit in royal language, while following Theravada Buddhism. Ayutthaya fought the northern Thai kingdom of Lan Na and extended its influence over the Malay peninsula.

## Trailok's reforms

King Trailok, who ruled from 1448 to 1488, reorganised government into ministries for civil and military affairs, the capital, the palace, finance and land. He created the sakdina system, which assigned every person a numerical rank, from 5 for slaves to tens of thousands for princes, measured in notional rice land. Most commoners, phrai, owed labour service to the state or to nobles for months each year. Law codes based on Indian traditions governed society.

## A cosmopolitan trading city

Ayutthaya was a hub of trade between China, Japan, India, Persia, the Malay world and later Europe. Royal monopolies controlled trade in goods such as deerskins, sappanwood and rice. Foreign communities lived in their own quarters: Chinese, Japanese, Malays, Persians, Portuguese, Dutch, English and French. The Japanese adventurer Yamada Nagamasa commanded the royal guard in the early seventeenth century. The Greek adventurer Constantine Phaulkon became chief minister under King Narai.

## Wars with Burma

The Burmese Toungoo kings invaded several times. In 1563 Bayinnaung took Ayutthaya's white elephants, and in 1569 he captured the city and made it a vassal. Prince Naresuan, who had been a hostage in Burma, declared independence in 1584, and in 1593, according to tradition, killed the Burmese crown prince in single combat on elephants at Nong Sarai. Naresuan is honoured as a national hero in Thailand.

## Narai and the French

King Narai, who ruled from 1656 to 1688, opened Ayutthaya to the West. He exchanged embassies with Louis XIV of France, and French Jesuits and soldiers came to the kingdom. Fearing French influence and Phaulkon's power, nobles staged a revolution when Narai fell ill in 1688; Phaulkon was executed, and the French were expelled. Ayutthaya continued to trade, but became more cautious about Europeans.

## Culture

Ayutthaya's temples, such as Wat Phra Si Sanphet, with its three bell-shaped stupas, Wat Mahathat and Wat Chaiwatthanaram, show the kingdom's wealth. Literature, music and dance flourished at court, and the classical Thai dance drama, khon, developed.

## The fall

In 1765, the Burmese Konbaung dynasty invaded with two armies. After a siege of over a year, Ayutthaya fell in April 1767. The city was burned, temples were destroyed and tens of thousands of people were taken to Burma. Taksin, a general of Chinese and Thai descent, gathered forces, drove out the Burmese within months and founded a new capital at Thonburi. His successor Rama I founded the Chakri dynasty at Bangkok in 1782. Ayutthaya's ruins are now a World Heritage Site.

## On our maps

Our maps show Ayutthaya from 1400 to 1715. Its largest outline, about 870,000 square kilometres on the 1530 map, includes much of today's Thailand, with parts of Laos, Malaysia, Cambodia and Myanmar, reflecting the areas that owed it tribute. Bangkok's site lies inside it throughout.
