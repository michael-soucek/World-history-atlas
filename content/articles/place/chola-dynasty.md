The Cholas were one of the three ancient royal houses of the Tamil country in southern India, alongside the Cheras and Pandyas. They are mentioned in an inscription of the Maurya emperor Ashoka in the third century BCE. Their greatest age came between the ninth and thirteenth centuries CE, when the Chola Empire dominated southern India and became a sea power whose fleets reached Southeast Asia.

## The early Cholas

The early Cholas ruled the fertile delta of the Kaveri river, with their port at Puhar, or Kaveripattinam, and their capital at Uraiyur. Tamil Sangam poetry, from the first centuries CE, praises Chola kings, especially Karikala, credited with building an embankment on the Kaveri, the Grand Anicut, one of the oldest water-regulation structures still in use. After this period, the Cholas faded for several centuries, as the Pallavas and Pandyas dominated the south.

## The revival

Around 850, Vijayalaya Chola, a vassal of the Pallavas, captured Thanjavur from a local ruler. His successors defeated the Pallavas and Pandyas and expanded the kingdom. Parantaka I, in the early tenth century, took Madurai, but was defeated by the Rashtrakutas of the Deccan.

## Rajaraja I

Rajaraja I, who ruled from 985 to 1014, built the Chola Empire. He conquered the Chera kingdom in Kerala, the Pandyas, the northern half of Sri Lanka, where he sacked the old capital Anuradhapura and made Polonnaruwa his base, and the Maldives. He fought the Western Chalukyas in the Deccan. He organised a detailed land survey, reorganised the administration and promoted local self-government through village assemblies. He built the Brihadisvara temple at Thanjavur, completed around 1010, with its tower about 66 metres high, topped by a huge capstone, and decorated with inscriptions listing donations and temple staff, including hundreds of dancers.

## Rajendra I

Rajendra I, who ruled from 1014 to 1044, completed the conquest of Sri Lanka and led an army north to the Ganges, defeating the Pala king Mahipala. To celebrate, he took the title Gangaikonda, "the one who took the Ganges", and founded a new capital, Gangaikonda Cholapuram, with a great temple. In 1025 he sent a naval expedition across the Bay of Bengal to attack Srivijaya, the maritime kingdom of Sumatra, sacking its ports, including Kedah on the Malay peninsula.

## Government and society

The Chola state was divided into provinces, mandalams, districts and villages. Village assemblies, such as the sabha of Brahmin villages and the ur of other villages, managed local affairs, land, tanks and temples. Inscriptions at Uttaramerur describe how members of the village committees were chosen by lot from eligible candidates. Temples were major landowners, employers and centres of learning, music and dance.

## Trade

The Cholas promoted trade with Southeast Asia, China and the Arab world. Merchant guilds, such as the Ainnurruvar, "the five hundred", traded across the Indian Ocean. Chola embassies reached the Song court in China.

## Art and religion

The Cholas were devoted to Shiva, though they also supported Vishnu worship and, at times, Buddhism. Chola bronzes, cast by the lost-wax method, especially images of Shiva as Nataraja, the cosmic dancer within a ring of fire, are among the finest sculptures in Indian art. The Tamil devotional poems of the Nayanars were collected in this period.

## Decline

After the eleventh century, the Cholas were united by marriage with the Eastern Chalukyas, and Kulottunga I, of mixed descent, took the throne in 1070. The Cholas lost Sri Lanka, faced the rise of the Hoysalas and Pandyas, and by the thirteenth century were weakened. The Pandyas became the dominant power in the south, and the last Chola king, Rajendra III, disappears from records around 1279.

## On our maps

Our maps show the Cholas on several maps from 200 to 1300. Their largest outline, about 320,000 square kilometres on the 1000 map, is four-fifths in today's India and the rest in Sri Lanka. Chennai's site lies inside it on most maps, and Colombo's from 1000 to 1200. By 1400 the region belongs to the [Vijayanagara Empire](/place/vijayanagara-empire).
