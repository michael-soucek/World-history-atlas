The Tsardom of Russia was the Russian state from 1547, when Ivan IV was crowned tsar, until 1721, when Peter the Great proclaimed the Russian Empire. In those 174 years, Russia expanded from the forests around Moscow to the Pacific, becoming the largest country in the world.

## Ivan the Terrible

Ivan IV was crowned tsar of all Russia in January 1547, at sixteen, the first ruler formally crowned with the title, from the Latin Caesar. In his early reign, with a group of advisers, he issued a new law code in 1550, reformed local government, created a standing infantry armed with firearms, the streltsy, and called the first zemsky sobor, an assembly of the land. He conquered the Tatar khanates of Kazan in 1552, celebrated with St Basil's Cathedral in Moscow, and Astrakhan in 1556, giving Russia the whole Volga and access to the Caspian. Trade with England began in 1553.

## The oprichnina

After the death of his first wife Anastasia in 1560, Ivan became increasingly paranoid and cruel. In 1565 he created the oprichnina, a personal domain governed by his own black-clad guard, the oprichniki, who terrorised the nobility and population. In 1570 they massacred much of Novgorod's population. The long Livonian War, from 1558 to 1583, for access to the Baltic, ended in defeat, and in 1571 the Crimean Tatars burned Moscow. In 1581 Ivan killed his son and heir in a fit of rage.

## The Time of Troubles

After Ivan's son Fyodor died in 1598, the Rurik dynasty ended. Boris Godunov became tsar, but a terrible famine in 1601–1603 killed perhaps a third of the population. Pretenders claiming to be Dmitry, Ivan's son who had died as a child, appeared; the first, False Dmitry I, with Polish support, took Moscow in 1605 and was killed in 1606. Rebellions, civil war and invasions by Poland-Lithuania and Sweden followed; Polish forces occupied Moscow from 1610. In 1612, a national militia organised by the merchant Kuzma Minin and Prince Dmitry Pozharsky drove the Poles out.

## The Romanovs

In 1613 a zemsky sobor elected the sixteen-year-old Michael Romanov as tsar, founding the Romanov dynasty, which ruled until 1917. His son Alexis, from 1645 to 1676, issued the Law Code of 1649, the Sobornoye Ulozheniye, which bound peasants permanently to the land, making serfdom hereditary. Patriarch Nikon's reforms of church rituals, in the 1650s, to align them with Greek practice, caused a schism; the Old Believers, who rejected the reforms, were persecuted, and the archpriest Avvakum was burned in 1682.

## Expansion

Russian expansion across Siberia, driven by the fur trade and carried out by Cossacks and traders, began with Yermak's campaign against the Khanate of Sibir in the 1580s and reached the Pacific at Okhotsk in 1639. Yakutsk was founded in 1632. Clashes with Qing China on the Amur river ended with the Treaty of Nerchinsk in 1689. In 1654 Bohdan Khmelnytsky's Cossacks in Ukraine accepted the tsar's protection at Pereiaslav, leading to war with Poland; Russia gained Kyiv and left-bank Ukraine in 1667. Cossack revolts, such as that of Stenka Razin in 1670–1671, were crushed.

## Peter the Great

Peter I, who became tsar in 1682, travelled to western Europe in 1697–1698 and returned determined to modernise Russia. He built a navy, reorganised the army, forced nobles to shave their beards and wear Western dress, and created a Senate and colleges. He fought Sweden in the Great Northern War, losing at Narva in 1700 but winning at Poltava in 1709, and founded Saint Petersburg in 1703, making it the capital in 1712. After the Treaty of Nystad in 1721, which gave Russia the Baltic provinces, Peter took the title of emperor, beginning the Russian Empire.
