Haiti was the first country in the Americas after the United States to win independence, and the only one founded by a successful revolt of enslaved people. It declared independence on 1 January 1804, after thirteen years of revolution and war against France.

## Taíno and Spanish Hispaniola

The island of Hispaniola, which the Taíno called Ayiti or Quisqueya, was where Columbus founded the first European settlement in the Americas, La Navidad, in 1492. Under Spanish rule, the Taíno population collapsed from disease, forced labour and violence; within a few decades, few remained. The Spanish focused on the eastern part of the island, and the west became a base for French pirates and buccaneers.

## Saint-Domingue

France acquired the western third of the island in 1697. By the 1780s, Saint-Domingue produced about 40 percent of Europe's sugar and over half its coffee. Its plantation economy depended on about 500,000 enslaved Africans, many of whom died within a few years of arrival from overwork, disease and brutal punishments. The colony also had about 40,000 white colonists and 30,000 free people of colour, some of whom were wealthy plantation owners but faced discrimination.

## The revolution

In August 1791, inspired in part by the French Revolution, enslaved people in the north rose in a massive revolt. Over the next decade a former enslaved coachman, Toussaint Louverture, became the leading figure of the colony. France abolished slavery in 1794, and Toussaint fought for the Republic against Spanish and British invaders, then made himself governor of the whole island. In 1802 Napoleon sent a large army to restore French control and, it was feared, slavery. Toussaint was captured and died in a French prison, but his general Jean-Jacques Dessalines led the final war, and yellow fever destroyed much of the French army. Dessalines proclaimed Haiti's independence, using the island's Taíno name.

## Independent Haiti

Dessalines declared independence on 1 January 1804 and made himself emperor. He ordered the killing of most of the remaining white French population in 1804. He was assassinated in 1806, and Haiti split into a kingdom in the north under Henri Christophe, who built the Citadelle Laferrière and the Sans-Souci palace, and a republic in the south under Alexandre Pétion. Pétion helped Simón Bolívar with arms and refuge on the condition that he free slaves. Jean-Pierre Boyer reunited Haiti and occupied the eastern part of the island from 1822 to 1844.

## The indemnity

In 1825, under threat of a French fleet, Haiti agreed to pay France 150 million francs, later reduced to 90 million, to compensate former slave owners, in return for recognition. Haiti borrowed from French banks to pay, and the debt and interest burdened its economy until the mid-twentieth century.

## The twentieth century

The United States occupied Haiti from 1915 to 1934, controlling its finances and forcing labour on road building. François Duvalier, "Papa Doc", elected in 1957, ruled as a dictator, using the Tonton Macoute militia; his son, "Baby Doc", ruled until 1986. Jean-Bertrand Aristide, a priest elected in 1990, was overthrown in 1991, restored in 1994 and ousted again in 2004.

## Recent years

The earthquake of 12 January 2010 killed over 200,000 people and destroyed much of Port-au-Prince. A cholera epidemic followed, brought by UN peacekeepers. President Jovenel Moïse was assassinated in 2021, and armed gangs have since controlled much of the capital. Haitian culture, with its Kreyòl language, Vodou religion, painting and music, remains vibrant.
