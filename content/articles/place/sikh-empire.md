The Sikh Empire was a state in the Punjab, in north-western India and what is now Pakistan, founded by Maharaja Ranjit Singh in 1799 and annexed by the British in 1849. For half a century, it was the last major independent power in the Indian subcontinent, and its army was one of the strongest in Asia.

## The Sikh gurus

Sikhism was founded by Guru Nanak, born in 1469 in the Punjab, who taught devotion to one God, equality of all people regardless of caste or religion, and honest work. Nine gurus followed him. The fifth, Guru Arjan, compiled the Adi Granth, the sacred scripture, and completed the Harmandir Sahib in Amritsar; he was executed by the Mughals in 1606. The ninth guru, Tegh Bahadur, was executed in Delhi in 1675. The tenth guru, Gobind Singh, founded the Khalsa, a brotherhood of initiated Sikhs, in 1699, with its five symbols, including uncut hair, and declared that after him the scripture, the Guru Granth Sahib, would be the guru.

## The misls

After Gobind Singh's death in 1708, Banda Singh Bahadur led a Sikh uprising against the Mughals, until he was captured and executed in 1716. As Mughal power weakened in the eighteenth century and the Afghan ruler Ahmad Shah Durrani invaded the Punjab repeatedly, destroying the Harmandir Sahib in 1762, Sikh military bands, called misls, fought back and took control of much of the Punjab. There were twelve major misls, each led by a sardar, and they met twice a year at Amritsar.

## Ranjit Singh

Ranjit Singh, born in 1780, was the leader of the Sukerchakia misl. He lost an eye to smallpox as a child. In 1799 he took Lahore and in 1801 was proclaimed Maharaja of the Punjab. Over the following decades, he united the misls, by conquest and diplomacy, and conquered Multan in 1818, Kashmir in 1819 and Peshawar in 1834, taking territory from the Afghans. In 1809 he signed the Treaty of Amritsar with the British East India Company, which set the Sutlej river as the border, and he kept peace with the British for the rest of his life.

## The Khalsa army

Ranjit Singh built a modern army, the Khalsa Army, trained by European officers, many of them veterans of Napoleon's armies, such as the Frenchmen Jean-François Allard and Jean-Baptiste Ventura, and the Italian Paolo Avitabile, who governed Peshawar. It had strong artillery and disciplined infantry, and numbered tens of thousands.

## Government and court

The empire was religiously diverse, with a majority of Muslims, as well as Hindus and Sikhs, and Ranjit Singh employed officials of all faiths, such as his Muslim foreign minister, Fakir Azizuddin. He covered the Harmandir Sahib with gold, giving it the name the Golden Temple. He acquired the Koh-i-Noor diamond from the Afghan ruler Shah Shuja in 1813. His court at Lahore was famous for its splendour.

## Collapse

After Ranjit Singh's death in 1839, the empire fell into chaos. His sons and successors, Kharak Singh, Nau Nihal Singh and Sher Singh, died or were killed within a few years, along with ministers. The army became a political force. In 1843 the five-year-old Duleep Singh became maharaja, with his mother, Maharani Jind Kaur, as regent.

## The Anglo-Sikh wars

In the First Anglo-Sikh War of 1845–1846, the Sikhs were defeated in hard-fought battles at Mudki, Ferozeshah and Sobraon. The British took the Jullundur Doab and sold Kashmir to the Dogra ruler Gulab Singh. After revolts, the Second Anglo-Sikh War of 1848–1849 ended in defeat at Gujrat, and the British annexed the Punjab in 1849. Duleep Singh was sent to Britain, where he converted to Christianity, and the Koh-i-Noor was given to Queen Victoria.

## On our maps

Our maps show the Sikh Empire on the 1800 and 1815 maps. Its largest outline, about 530,000 square kilometres on the 1800 map, is three-fifths in today's Pakistan, with most of the rest in India and a part in the Ladakh region claimed by China. Lahore lies inside it on both maps. On the 1878 map, the Punjab is part of the [British Raj](/place/british-raj).
