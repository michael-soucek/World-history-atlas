The Asante Empire, also spelled Ashanti, was an Akan state in the forest of what is now Ghana, which dominated the region from around 1700 until British conquest at the end of the nineteenth century. It was rich in gold, highly organised and militarily formidable, and fought several wars against Britain. The Asante kingdom survives today within Ghana.

## The Akan forest

The Akan peoples of the forest zone of today's Ghana and Ivory Coast grew yams, plantains and cocoyams, and mined and panned gold. From the fifteenth century they traded gold northwards to the Mande traders of the Sahel and, after the Portuguese arrived on the coast in 1471, to Europeans at forts such as Elmina. Several Akan states competed for power, and Denkyira dominated the Asante clans in the seventeenth century.

## Osei Tutu and the Golden Stool

In the late seventeenth century, from about the 1670s by some reckonings, Osei Tutu, ruler of Kumasi, began uniting the Asante clans into a confederacy. With his priest and adviser Okomfo Anokye, he created a powerful symbol: according to tradition, Anokye called down from the sky a stool covered in gold, which landed on Osei Tutu's knees. The Golden Stool, Sika Dwa Kofi, was said to hold the sunsum, the soul, of the Asante nation. It has never been sat on and is kept in secret. The Asante defeated Denkyira at Feyiase in 1701. Osei Tutu became the first Asantehene, king of Asante, and the empire is usually dated from this victory. He died in battle against the Akyem around 1717.

## Building an empire

Under Opoku Ware I, from about 1720 to 1750, Asante conquered neighbouring states, including Bono, Gonja and Dagomba to the north, and Akyem and Akwamu to the south and east, creating an empire of three to five million people. Conquered states paid tribute in gold, slaves and goods. In the late eighteenth century Osei Kwadwo and Osei Bonsu reformed government, appointing officials by merit rather than birth.

## Government

The Asantehene ruled with the Asanteman council of chiefs and the Queen Mother, the Asantehemaa, who played a key role in choosing the king. Kumasi, the capital, had broad streets, large houses decorated with relief patterns, and a palace. A network of great roads radiated from Kumasi to the provinces, with checkpoints and messengers. A treasury managed gold dust, weighed with brass weights cast in the shapes of animals, people and proverbs, now prized by collectors. Muslim scholars from the north served as scribes and advisers.

## Trade and the slave trade

Asante traded kola nuts north to the Hausa cities and gold and slaves south to the coast. In the eighteenth century, Asante was a major supplier of captives to European slave traders on the Gold Coast, mostly prisoners of war. After Britain abolished the slave trade in 1807, Asante's economy shifted towards kola, gold and other goods.

## Culture

Asante is famous for kente cloth, woven in narrow strips of bright patterns, once reserved for royalty, and for adinkra symbols printed on cloth, each with a meaning or proverb. The Akwasidae festival, held every six weeks, honours the ancestors. Gold regalia, swords and stools expressed royal power.

## Wars with Britain

From the early nineteenth century, Asante clashed with the British over the coast and the coastal Fante. In 1824, at Nsamankow, Asante forces defeated a British army and killed Governor Sir Charles MacCarthy. Further wars followed. In 1874 a British expedition under Sir Garnet Wolseley burned Kumasi. In 1896 the British occupied Kumasi without a fight and exiled King Prempeh I to the Seychelles.

## The War of the Golden Stool

In 1900 the British governor, Sir Frederick Hodgson, demanded the Golden Stool so that he could sit on it. Yaa Asantewaa, the Queen Mother of Ejisu, rallied the Asante, reportedly asking whether the men would fight or whether the women must. The revolt besieged the British fort in Kumasi for months before it was crushed. Yaa Asantewaa was exiled to the Seychelles, where she died in 1921. Asante was annexed to the Gold Coast colony in 1902, but the Golden Stool was never surrendered. Prempeh returned in 1924, and in 1935 the Asante kingdom was restored as a constitutional monarchy within the colony, and later within independent Ghana.
