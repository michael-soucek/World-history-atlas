The Spanish Empire was the first global empire, the one Europeans described as "the empire on which the sun never sets". It began with the voyage of Christopher Columbus in 1492, sponsored by the Catholic Monarchs, Isabella of Castile and Ferdinand of Aragon, and at its height ruled most of South and Central America, much of North America, the Philippines, and large parts of Italy and the Low Countries.

## Castile and the Americas

The Spanish Empire in the Americas was formally a possession of the Crown of Castile. Columbus's voyages from 1492 were followed by conquests: Hispaniola, Cuba and Puerto Rico in the Caribbean, then the Aztec Empire in 1519–1521 and the Inca Empire in 1532–1533. Expeditions explored as far as Florida, the Mississippi, the Grand Canyon and the Amazon. The Spanish founded cities on a grid plan around a central square with a cathedral and town hall, such as Mexico City, Lima, Bogotá and Buenos Aires.

## Governing the empire

The empire was governed by the Council of the Indies in Spain, and in the Americas by viceroys, of New Spain from 1535 and Peru from 1542, later joined by New Granada and the Río de la Plata. Courts called audiencias handled justice and administration. Trade with the Americas was controlled by the House of Trade in Seville, and later Cádiz, and annual fleets carried silver to Spain and goods to the colonies. The Manila galleons linked Mexico with the Philippines, conquered from 1565, and China.

## Indigenous peoples

The conquest devastated Indigenous populations, mainly through epidemics of smallpox, measles and other diseases, but also through war, forced labour and the encomienda system. The Dominican friar Bartolomé de las Casas denounced abuses, and the New Laws of 1542 tried to limit forced labour. Indigenous communities kept their own leaders and lands in many places, and Indigenous languages such as Nahuatl and Quechua were used by missionaries. Many Indigenous people converted to Christianity, often blending it with older traditions.

## Silver

Silver from Potosí, in today's Bolivia, discovered in 1545, and from Zacatecas and Guanajuato in Mexico, made the Spanish peso a global currency. Much of the silver flowed through Spain to pay for wars and debts, and to Asia, especially China. Inflation in Europe has been linked to the influx of American silver.

## Europe and the Habsburgs

Under Charles V and Philip II, Spain was Europe's leading power. Spanish armies, the tercios, were feared across Europe, and Spain fought France, the Ottomans and Protestant powers. Philip II annexed Portugal and its empire in 1580. But the Dutch revolt from 1568, the defeat of the Armada in 1588, and the costs of war led to repeated bankruptcies. In the seventeenth century Spain declined, losing Portugal in 1640 and the Dutch Republic's independence in 1648.

## The Golden Age

The sixteenth and seventeenth centuries were Spain's cultural Golden Age. Miguel de Cervantes wrote Don Quixote, published in 1605 and 1615. Lope de Vega and Calderón de la Barca wrote plays; El Greco, Velázquez, Zurbarán and Murillo painted; and Teresa of Ávila and John of the Cross wrote mystical works.

## The Bourbons and independence

After the War of the Spanish Succession, the Bourbon dynasty reformed the empire in the eighteenth century, increasing control and revenue. The colonies' criollo elites grew resentful. Napoleon's invasion of Spain in 1808 led to the wars of independence, and by 1826 Spain had lost all its mainland American colonies. Cuba, Puerto Rico and the Philippines were lost in 1898, and the last African territories were given up in the twentieth century.

## On our maps

Our maps use the name from 1530 to 1880. Its largest outline, on the 1815 map at about 3 million square kilometres, is mostly in Argentina, because the basemap draws Spain's American viceroyalties under separate labels at most dates. The figure is therefore far smaller than the empire's real extent. The cities listed as Spanish on our maps include Madrid, Seville, Havana and Mexico City, and Los Angeles' and San Antonio's sites from 1783.
