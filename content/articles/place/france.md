France as a modern nation-state is the heir of the medieval Kingdom of France, the Revolution of 1789 and the republics and empires that followed. This page covers France from 1815 on; for earlier periods, see the [Kingdom of France](/place/kingdom-of-france) and the [French First Republic](/place/french-first-republic).

## The Restoration and the July Monarchy

After 1815, the Bourbon kings Louis XVIII and Charles X ruled under a constitutional charter. When Charles X tried to restrict the press and voting, the July Revolution of 1830 overthrew him, and his cousin Louis-Philippe of Orléans became "king of the French". The July Monarchy favoured the wealthy middle classes, and its slogan was attributed to its minister François Guizot: "Enrich yourselves". The conquest of Algeria began in 1830.

## 1848 and the Second Empire

The revolution of February 1848 created the Second Republic, which introduced universal male suffrage and abolished slavery in the colonies. Louis-Napoleon Bonaparte was elected president, then seized power in a coup in 1851 and became Emperor Napoleon III in 1852. Under the Second Empire, Baron Haussmann rebuilt Paris with wide boulevards and parks, railways spread, and France intervened in Mexico and Italy. The Franco-Prussian War of 1870 ended with Napoleon III's capture at Sedan.

## The Paris Commune

In 1871, after the siege of Paris, a revolutionary government, the Paris Commune, took control of the city. In the "Bloody Week" of May 1871, the government's troops retook Paris, killing thousands of Communards.

## The Third Republic

The Third Republic became France's longest-lasting regime since the Revolution. It introduced free, compulsory, secular primary education under Jules Ferry in the 1880s, and separated church and state in 1905. The Dreyfus affair, from 1894, in which a Jewish army officer was falsely convicted of treason, divided France; the writer Émile Zola's open letter "J'accuse" defended him, and he was exonerated in 1906. Paris was a world capital of art, from the Impressionists to Picasso.

## Colonial empire

In the nineteenth century France built the world's second-largest colonial empire, starting with the invasion of Algeria in 1830, which it made part of France itself. It took most of north-west and equatorial Africa, Madagascar and Indochina, and after the First World War received League of Nations mandates over Syria and Lebanon.

## The world wars

France suffered enormous losses in the First World War, including at Verdun in 1916. After the war, it struggled with debt and political instability. In 1940 Germany invaded and defeated France. Marshal Pétain's Vichy regime collaborated with Germany and deported about 75,000 Jews to their deaths. Charles de Gaulle led the Free French, and the Resistance fought the occupation. France was liberated in 1944.

## The Fourth and Fifth Republics

The Fourth Republic, from 1946, gave women the vote, rebuilt the economy and helped found the European Coal and Steel Community, but was weakened by unstable governments and colonial wars. Defeat at Điện Biên Phủ in 1954 ended French rule in Indochina, and the Algerian War from 1954 to 1962 brought the country close to civil war. De Gaulle returned in 1958 and founded the Fifth Republic, with a strong presidency. Algeria became independent in 1962.

## Modern France

The student and worker protests of May 1968 shook French society. France developed nuclear power and weapons, high-speed trains and a strong welfare state. It remains a founding member of the European Union, a nuclear power and a permanent member of the UN Security Council, and the most visited country in the world.
