The Rashidun Caliphate is the name Sunni Muslims give to the rule of the first four caliphs after the death of the Prophet Muhammad in 632: Abu Bakr, Umar, Uthman and Ali. "Rashidun" means "rightly guided". In under thirty years, the community Muhammad had built in Arabia became an empire stretching from Libya to Iran.

## Abu Bakr and the Ridda wars

Abu Bakr, Muhammad's close friend and father-in-law, was chosen as caliph, khalifa, meaning "successor", at a meeting in Medina in 632. Several Arabian tribes refused to pay alms to Medina or followed other prophets, such as Musaylima. In the Ridda wars, "wars of apostasy", Muslim armies led by commanders such as Khalid ibn al-Walid defeated them by 633, uniting Arabia under Medina's rule for the first time. Abu Bakr also began the collection of the Quran in written form.

## Umar

Umar ibn al-Khattab, caliph from 634 to 644, was famous for his austerity and sense of justice. He took the title Commander of the Faithful. Under him, the conquests expanded with remarkable speed. He set up the diwan, a register that paid stipends to Muslim soldiers and their families from the revenues of the conquered lands, and founded garrison cities where Arab troops lived apart from conquered populations. He visited Jerusalem after its surrender in 637 or 638, and tradition credits him with a covenant guaranteeing the safety of its Christians. Umar was stabbed to death in 644 by a Persian slave, Abu Lu'lu'a.

## The conquests

The Arab armies were relatively small but highly mobile, and led by experienced commanders such as Khalid ibn al-Walid, Amr ibn al-As and Sa'd ibn Abi Waqqas. They fought two empires exhausted by decades of war against each other, with populations often alienated by heavy taxes and religious disputes. Conquered peoples were generally allowed to keep their religions in return for paying a tax, the jizya, and many local officials stayed in place. Damascus fell in 634–635, Jerusalem and Ctesiphon by 637–638, and Alexandria in 641–642.

## Uthman

Uthman ibn Affan, caliph from 644 to 656, was a wealthy member of the Umayyad clan. Under him the conquests continued into Iran, Armenia and North Africa, and a navy was built that defeated the Byzantines at the Battle of the Masts in 654. He ordered the compilation of a standard text of the Quran and the burning of variant copies. But he was accused of favouring his own relatives in appointments, and rebels from Egypt and Iraq besieged his house in Medina and killed him in 656.

## Ali and the first civil war

Ali ibn Abi Talib, Muhammad's cousin and son-in-law, became caliph in Medina but faced immediate opposition. Muhammad's widow Aisha, with Talha and Zubayr, rebelled, and was defeated at the Battle of the Camel in 656. Muawiya, governor of Syria and Uthman's relative, demanded punishment of Uthman's killers. At Siffin in 657, the two armies fought, then agreed to arbitration. Some of Ali's followers, who rejected arbitration, broke away as the Kharijites, and one of them assassinated Ali in Kufa in 661.

## Legacy

Sunni Muslims regard the four caliphs as the rightly guided ones and their era as a model of Islamic government. Shia Muslims believe that Ali should have been Muhammad's immediate successor, and honour him as the first imam. The conquests of these thirty years transformed the map of the Middle East and laid the foundations for the Islamic world.
