Saudi Arabia, which occupies most of the Arabian peninsula, was founded in 1932 by Abdulaziz ibn Saud. It is named after his family, the House of Saud, and is the guardian of Islam's two holiest cities, Mecca and Medina.

## Arabia before the Saudis

The Arabian peninsula was the birthplace of Islam, and Mecca and Medina became its holiest cities. After the early caliphs moved their capitals to Damascus and Baghdad, most of Arabia became politically marginal, though the pilgrimage to Mecca, the hajj, drew Muslims from around the world. The Hejaz, with the holy cities, was ruled by the sharifs of Mecca under the Mamluks and Ottomans. Central Arabia, the Najd, was divided among tribes and small oasis towns.

## The Wahhabi movement

Muhammad ibn Abd al-Wahhab, born in the Najd in 1703, preached a strict monotheism, opposing practices he considered idolatrous, such as the veneration of saints and tombs. His alliance with Muhammad ibn Saud in 1744 combined religious reform with political expansion. His teachings, known to outsiders as Wahhabism and to followers as Salafism, remain the official religious doctrine of Saudi Arabia.

## The three Saudi states

The first Saudi state, based at Diriyah, conquered much of Arabia and took Mecca and Medina, but was destroyed by Egyptian forces in 1818. The second, from 1824 to 1891, was based at Riyadh. In 1902 Abdulaziz ibn Saud retook Riyadh. He used the Ikhwan, religious warriors settled in agricultural colonies, to conquer the Najd and the Hejaz. When the Ikhwan rebelled against his authority, he defeated them at Sabilla in 1929.

## Oil

In 1933 Abdulaziz granted an oil concession to the Standard Oil Company of California. In 1938 oil was found in commercial quantities at Dammam Well No. 7. The Arabian American Oil Company, Aramco, developed vast fields, including Ghawar, the world's largest. In 1945 Abdulaziz met President Franklin Roosevelt aboard a US warship, beginning a close relationship. Oil revenues transformed the kingdom, funding cities, roads, schools and hospitals. Saudi Arabia led OPEC's oil embargo in 1973.

## The kingdom since Abdulaziz

Abdulaziz, who died in 1953, had dozens of sons, and the throne passed among them: Saud, Faisal, Khalid, Fahd, Abdullah and Salman. King Faisal introduced education for girls and television in the 1960s and was assassinated in 1975. In 1979 Islamist militants seized the Grand Mosque in Mecca; after the siege ended, the government gave clerics more influence. Saudi Arabia supported the Afghan mujahideen in the 1980s. In 1990–1991 it hosted US forces against Iraq after the invasion of Kuwait. Fifteen of the nineteen hijackers of 11 September 2001 were Saudi citizens.

## Mohammed bin Salman

Since 2017, Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman has led Vision 2030, a programme to diversify the economy away from oil. Women were allowed to drive in 2018, cinemas reopened and tourism was promoted. At the same time, critics and activists were imprisoned, and the journalist Jamal Khashoggi was murdered in the Saudi consulate in Istanbul in 2018. Saudi Arabia led a military intervention in Yemen's civil war from 2015.
