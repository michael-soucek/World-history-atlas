The British Empire was the largest empire in history. At its height, around 1920, it covered about a quarter of the world's land and ruled about a quarter of its people, from Canada and the Caribbean to Africa, India, Southeast Asia and Australia.

## The first British Empire

English colonisation began with Jamestown, Virginia, in 1607, followed by Plymouth in 1620, Massachusetts and the Caribbean islands of Barbados and Jamaica. Scotland's attempt at a colony in Panama, the Darien scheme, failed in the 1690s. The sugar islands of the Caribbean, worked by enslaved Africans, were among the most profitable colonies. Britain gained Gibraltar, Newfoundland and Nova Scotia in 1713, and Canada and Florida in 1763.

## The East India Company

The East India Company, founded in 1600, began as a trading company with posts at Surat, Madras, Bombay and Calcutta. After Robert Clive's victory at Plassey in 1757, it gained control of Bengal and its revenues. Over the next century, through wars with the Marathas, Mysore, the Sikhs and others, it came to rule most of India, with its own army of Indian soldiers, sepoys. After the uprising of 1857, the crown took over.

## Slavery and abolition

Britain was the leading slave-trading nation of the eighteenth century, carrying about three million Africans across the Atlantic. Ports such as Liverpool and Bristol grew rich on the trade. Campaigners such as Thomas Clarkson, William Wilberforce and formerly enslaved writers such as Olaudah Equiano pressed for abolition, and resistance by enslaved people, including revolts in Jamaica, put pressure on the system. Britain abolished the slave trade in 1807 and slavery in most of its colonies in 1833, paying compensation to slave owners, not to the formerly enslaved.

## Settler colonies

Britain's settler colonies, Canada, Australia, New Zealand and South Africa, became self-governing dominions. Their growth involved the dispossession of Indigenous peoples, including wars against the Māori in New Zealand and violence against Aboriginal Australians. Millions of British and Irish emigrants settled in these lands and in the United States.

## The Victorian empire

In the nineteenth century, Britain's navy dominated the oceans, and its industry and finance led the world. Britain took Singapore in 1819, Hong Kong after the Opium War in 1842, and, in the Scramble for Africa, Egypt, Sudan, Nigeria, Kenya, Rhodesia and other territories. The Suez Canal, opened in 1869, which Britain controlled from 1875, became a vital route to India. Supporters saw the empire as spreading trade, law and Christianity; its victims experienced conquest, famines and repression.

## The empire at its height

After the First World War, Britain received League of Nations mandates over Palestine, Iraq, Transjordan and former German colonies in Africa, bringing the empire to its greatest extent. But nationalism was growing: Ireland won independence in 1922, Egypt nominal independence the same year, and the Indian independence movement grew under Gandhi.

## Decolonisation

After the Second World War, Britain could not maintain its empire. India and Pakistan became independent in 1947, Ceylon and Burma in 1948, and Britain left Palestine in 1948. The Suez crisis of 1956 showed Britain's weakened position. Ghana became independent in 1957, and most African and Caribbean colonies followed in the 1960s. Conflicts accompanied some transitions, such as in Malaya and Kenya. Today, most former colonies belong to the Commonwealth of Nations, and the British monarch is head of state of some, such as Canada and Australia.

## On our maps

Our maps show the British Empire from 1715 to 1938. Its largest outline, about 7.9 million square kilometres on the 1900 map, lies almost entirely in Australia, because the basemap gives most British territories their own labels, such as British India or Canada. The real empire was several times larger. Cities listed as British on our maps include Delhi, Mumbai and Kolkata from 1878, Hong Kong from 1878, and Jerusalem and Baghdad between the world wars.
