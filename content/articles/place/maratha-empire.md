The Maratha Empire was the last great Indian power before British rule. It began as a small kingdom in the western Deccan in the 1670s and, a century later, controlled much of the subcontinent, including the Mughal emperor himself.

## The Deccan and the Marathas

The Marathas were Marathi-speaking people of the western Deccan, including farmers, warriors and landowning families who had served the Deccan sultanates as soldiers and officials. The region's rugged landscape of the Western Ghats, with hundreds of hill forts, suited guerrilla warfare. The Bhakti saints of Maharashtra, such as Tukaram and Ramdas, had created a strong regional religious culture.

## Shivaji's rise

Shivaji Bhonsle, born in 1630 at the fort of Shivneri, was the son of Shahaji, a commander in the service of the Bijapur Sultanate. From the 1640s he captured forts from Bijapur. In 1659 he killed the Bijapur general Afzal Khan at a meeting at Pratapgad, wearing tiger claws hidden in his hand; he claimed Afzal Khan attacked first. He raided the Mughal port of Surat in 1664. In 1666, invited to Aurangzeb's court at Agra, he was placed under house arrest but escaped, according to legend hidden in a fruit basket.

## The Chhatrapati

In 1674 Shivaji was crowned Chhatrapati at Raigad, with elaborate Vedic rituals. He created a council of eight ministers, the Ashta Pradhan, and a system of revenue collection, collecting chauth and sardeshmukhi, shares of revenue, from neighbouring territories. He built a navy to protect the Konkan coast. He is remembered in Maharashtra as a national hero and a model ruler. He died in 1680.

## The war with Aurangzeb

Aurangzeb moved to the Deccan in 1681 and spent the rest of his life there fighting the Marathas. In 1689 he captured and executed Shivaji's son Sambhaji. But Maratha bands, led by Rajaram and, after his death, by his widow Tarabai, continued fighting across the Deccan. Aurangzeb died in 1707 without defeating them.

## The Peshwas

Under Shahu, Shivaji's grandson, the chief minister, the Peshwa Balaji Vishwanath, gained power, and the office became hereditary. Baji Rao I, Peshwa from 1720 to 1740, was a brilliant cavalry commander who led campaigns into central and northern India, reaching Delhi in 1737. Maratha chiefs established their own territories: the Gaekwads in Baroda, the Holkars in Indore, the Scindias in Gwalior and the Bhonsles in Nagpur. Pune became a great capital.

## Panipat

In 1761, the Maratha army met the Afghan ruler Ahmad Shah Durrani at Panipat, north of Delhi. The Marathas were cut off from supplies and starved before the battle, and on 14 January 1761 they were crushed, with tens of thousands killed, including the Peshwa's son. Under Peshwa Madhavrao I and the statesman Nana Fadnavis, the Marathas recovered, and Mahadaji Scindia took control of Delhi in 1771.

## A confederacy

By then the empire was really a confederacy: the Peshwa in Pune and several powerful Maratha houses, such as the Scindias of Gwalior, the Holkars of Indore, the Gaekwads of Baroda and the Bhonsles of Nagpur, who often acted independently.

## Wars with the British

The First Anglo-Maratha War, from 1775 to 1782, ended in a stalemate. In the Second, from 1803 to 1805, the British under Arthur Wellesley, later Duke of Wellington, won at Assaye, and the British took Delhi. In the Third, in 1817–1818, the British defeated the Peshwa Baji Rao II and annexed his lands. Some Maratha states, such as Gwalior, Indore and Baroda, survived as princely states under British rule.
