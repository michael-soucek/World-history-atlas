Shah Jahan was the fifth Mughal emperor, ruling from 1628 to 1658. His reign marked the height of Mughal architecture and splendour, with the building of the Taj Mahal, the Red Fort and the city of Shahjahanabad in Delhi. He ended his life imprisoned by his son Aurangzeb.

## Prince Khurram

Shah Jahan was born Khurram on 5 January 1592 in Lahore, the third son of Jahangir and a Rajput princess. His grandfather Akbar was fond of him and supervised his upbringing. He became a successful general, leading campaigns against Mewar, which submitted in 1615, and in the Deccan, and Jahangir gave him the title Shah Jahan, "king of the world". In 1612 he married Arjumand Banu Begum, niece of the empress Nur Jahan, who became known as Mumtaz Mahal, "chosen one of the palace".

## Seizing the throne

In the last years of Jahangir's reign, Nur Jahan, who held great power, favoured another prince, and Shah Jahan rebelled in 1622 without success. When Jahangir died in 1627, Shah Jahan, with the help of Mumtaz Mahal's father, Asaf Khan, took the throne, and had his brother, his nephews and other rivals killed.

## Wars

Shah Jahan expanded the empire in the Deccan, annexing Ahmadnagar in 1636 and forcing the sultanates of Bijapur and Golconda to pay tribute. His attempts to recover the Mughal homeland in Central Asia, Balkh and Badakhshan, in 1646–1647, failed at great cost. He lost Kandahar to the Safavids in 1649 and failed to retake it in three sieges.

## The Taj Mahal

In 1631 Mumtaz Mahal died while giving birth to their fourteenth child, in Burhanpur. Shah Jahan was grief-stricken. He ordered the construction of a mausoleum on the bank of the Yamuna river at Agra, the Taj Mahal. Built over about twenty years by thousands of workers and craftsmen, under the architect Ustad Ahmad Lahauri, it is made of white marble inlaid with semi-precious stones, pietra dura, with calligraphy of Quranic verses, set in a garden with water channels representing paradise. It is considered the finest example of Mughal architecture and is a World Heritage Site.

## Building

Shah Jahan was the greatest builder of the Mughal dynasty. He rebuilt parts of the Agra Fort in white marble, and in 1639 began a new capital at Delhi, Shahjahanabad, with the Red Fort, which contains the Diwan-i-Am, the hall of public audience, and the Diwan-i-Khas, the hall of private audience, inscribed with the verse "If there is paradise on earth, it is this". He built the Jama Masjid, one of the largest mosques in India. He commissioned the Peacock Throne, decorated with gold and jewels, which was taken to Persia by Nadir Shah in 1739. These projects were very expensive, and taxes on peasants were heavy.

## War of succession

In 1657 Shah Jahan fell ill, and his four sons fought for the throne. His favourite, the eldest, Dara Shikoh, a scholar who sought common ground between Hinduism and Islam, was defeated by Aurangzeb, a more orthodox Muslim, who executed him in 1659. Aurangzeb imprisoned Shah Jahan in the Agra Fort in 1658. According to tradition, he spent his last years gazing at the Taj Mahal from the fort, cared for by his daughter Jahanara. He died on 22 January 1666 and was buried beside Mumtaz Mahal in the Taj Mahal.
