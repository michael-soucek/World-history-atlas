Sun Yat-sen was a Chinese revolutionary and political leader who played a key role in the overthrow of the Qing dynasty in 1911 and served as the first provisional president of the Republic of China. Founder of the Nationalist Party, the Kuomintang, he is honoured as the "Father of the Nation" in Taiwan and the "forerunner of the revolution" in the People's Republic of China.

## Early life

Sun was born on 12 November 1866 in Cuiheng, a village in Guangdong province, into a farming family. At thirteen, he went to Hawaii, where his elder brother had emigrated, and studied at missionary schools, where he learned English and became interested in Christianity; he was later baptised. He studied medicine in Guangzhou and Hong Kong, graduating in 1892, and practised briefly as a doctor in Macau.

## Revolutionary

Convinced that the Qing dynasty, weak and corrupt, could not save China, Sun founded the Revive China Society in Honolulu in 1894. In 1895 he organised an uprising in Guangzhou, which failed, and fled abroad, with a price on his head. In 1896 he was kidnapped by agents of the Qing legation in London and held captive; his former teacher, James Cantlie, alerted the press and the British government, and he was released. The incident made him famous. He spent years travelling in Japan, Southeast Asia, the United States and Europe, raising money from overseas Chinese communities.

## The Tongmenghui

In 1905 in Tokyo, Sun united several revolutionary groups into the Tongmenghui, the Revolutionary Alliance. He developed his ideology, the Three Principles of the People: nationalism, ending Manchu rule and foreign domination; democracy, a constitutional government; and the people's livelihood, often translated as socialism, including land reform. The alliance launched several unsuccessful uprisings.

## The Xinhai Revolution

On 10 October 1911, an uprising in Wuchang, in which Sun was not directly involved, sparked the revolution. Provinces declared independence. Sun, who was in the United States, returned and on 1 January 1912 was inaugurated in Nanjing as provisional president of the Republic of China. To secure the abdication of the last emperor, Puyi, and avoid civil war, he gave the presidency to Yuan Shikai, the most powerful general, in February 1912.

## Opposition

Sun founded the Kuomintang, which won elections in 1912–1913. But Yuan's government had the party's leader, Song Jiaoren, assassinated, and suppressed the "Second Revolution". Sun fled to Japan. In 1915 he married Soong Ching-ling, sister-in-law of Chiang Kai-shek. After Yuan's death, China fell into the Warlord Era. Sun set up governments in Guangzhou, depending on southern warlords.

## Soviet help

Disappointed by Western powers, Sun accepted help from the Soviet Union in 1923. He reorganised the Kuomintang on the Leninist model, allied with the Chinese Communist Party and founded the Whampoa Military Academy, with Chiang Kai-shek as commandant, to train an army to unify China.

## Death

Sun travelled to Beijing for talks with the northern government and died of cancer on 12 March 1925. It was long called liver cancer, but the autopsy found it had begun in the gallbladder and spread to the liver. His mausoleum is in Nanjing. Both the Nationalists and Communists claim his legacy.
