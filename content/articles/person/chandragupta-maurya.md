Chandragupta Maurya founded the Maurya Empire, the first state to unite most of the Indian subcontinent. Ruling from about 321 to 297 BCE from his capital at Pataliputra, he overthrew the Nanda dynasty of Magadha, took over the lands Alexander the Great had conquered in the north-west and built a centralised empire.

## Obscure origins

Very little is certain about Chandragupta's background. Buddhist and Jain sources connect him to the Moriya clan, Kshatriyas, while some Brahmanical texts suggest he was of low birth. Greek writers call him Sandrokottos. According to many traditions, he was guided by a Brahmin teacher and minister, Chanakya, also called Kautilya, who had been humiliated by the Nanda king and found Chandragupta as a young man.

## Overthrowing the Nandas

The Nanda dynasty ruled Magadha, the most powerful kingdom of the Ganges plain, and was said to be rich but unpopular. Alexander the Great's invasion of the Punjab in 326–325 BCE, and his army's refusal to go further, left the north-west unsettled. Chandragupta and Chanakya raised an army and, after initial failures, overthrew the last Nanda king, Dhana Nanda, around 321 BCE, taking Pataliputra.

## Seleucus

After Alexander's death, his general Seleucus took over his eastern lands and, around 305 BCE, crossed the Indus to recover the Indian provinces. Seleucus and Chandragupta made a treaty: Seleucus ceded Arachosia, Gedrosia, and parts of Paropamisadae, today's southern Afghanistan and Baluchistan, and Chandragupta gave him 500 war elephants, which helped Seleucus win at Ipsus in 301 BCE. The treaty included a marriage alliance.

## Megasthenes

Seleucus sent an ambassador, Megasthenes, to Chandragupta's court. His book, the Indica, survives only in quotations by later writers, but describes Pataliputra as a huge city surrounded by a wooden wall with 570 towers and 64 gates, governed by committees. He describes Indian society as divided into seven classes and claims, inaccurately, that there was no slavery. He describes the king as guarded by armed women and changing his bedroom each night out of fear of assassination.

## The Arthashastra

The Arthashastra, a treatise on statecraft, economics and law, is attributed to Chanakya, though in its current form it was probably compiled over several centuries. It describes a highly organised state with officials for taxation, trade, mines, forests and agriculture, a network of spies and pragmatic, sometimes ruthless, advice to kings. Historians debate how far it reflects Maurya government.

## The empire

Chandragupta's empire stretched from Afghanistan to Bengal and south into the Deccan. It had a large army, said by Greek sources to number 600,000 infantry, 30,000 cavalry and 9,000 elephants, numbers that are probably exaggerated. It was governed through provinces with royal princes as governors. An inscription from the second century CE at Junagadh records that his governor in Gujarat built the Sudarshana lake, a reservoir.

## Renunciation

According to Jain tradition, Chandragupta abdicated in favour of his son Bindusara around 297 BCE, became a Jain monk under the teacher Bhadrabahu, and travelled to Shravanabelagola, in Karnataka, where he fasted to death, a Jain ritual called sallekhana. A hill there, Chandragiri, is named after him. His grandson Ashoka became the most famous Maurya ruler.
