Victoria was queen of the United Kingdom from 1837 until her death in 1901, and from 1876 Empress of India. Her reign of sixty-three years, the Victorian era, saw Britain become the world's leading industrial and imperial power, and saw the monarchy become a constitutional and symbolic institution.

## Princess

Alexandrina Victoria was born on 24 May 1819 at Kensington Palace, the only child of Edward, Duke of Kent, a son of George III, and Princess Victoria of Saxe-Coburg-Saalfeld. Her father died when she was eight months old. She was raised by her mother under a strict system, the "Kensington System", devised by her mother's adviser John Conroy, which kept her isolated. She became heir to her uncle, William IV.

## Queen

William IV died on 20 June 1837, and Victoria became queen at eighteen. Her first prime minister, Lord Melbourne, became her mentor. In 1840 she married her cousin, Prince Albert of Saxe-Coburg and Gotha. They had nine children, who married into royal families across Europe, earning Victoria the title "grandmother of Europe". Albert became her closest adviser, promoting science, industry and education, and organising the Great Exhibition of 1851 in the Crystal Palace in London.

## Widow of Windsor

Albert died of typhoid fever on 14 December 1861, at forty-two. Victoria was devastated and went into deep mourning, wearing black for the rest of her life. She withdrew from public life for years, living mainly at Windsor, Osborne House on the Isle of Wight and Balmoral in Scotland, which made her unpopular and encouraged republican sentiment. Her friendship with her Scottish servant John Brown caused gossip.

## Politics

Victoria's reign saw Britain become more democratic, with the Reform Acts of 1867 and 1884 extending the vote. The monarch's political power declined, though Victoria held strong opinions, influenced ministers and had favourites: she admired the Conservative Benjamin Disraeli, who flattered her and made her Empress of India in 1876, and disliked the Liberal William Gladstone, who she complained addressed her like a public meeting.

## Empire

Under Victoria, the British Empire expanded to cover about a quarter of the world's land. After the Indian Rebellion of 1857, the British crown took over the government of India from the East India Company. Britain fought the Crimean War and the Opium Wars, colonised much of Africa and fought the Boer War, which began in 1899. Victoria encouraged the creation of the Victoria Cross for bravery.

## The Victorian age

The era saw rapid industrialisation, railways, telegraphs, the growth of cities, scientific progress, such as Darwin's theory of evolution, and great writers, such as Charles Dickens and the Brontës. It also saw great poverty, the Irish Famine of the 1840s, and social reform. The word "Victorian" came to suggest strict morality and respectability.

## Jubilees and death

Victoria's Golden Jubilee in 1887 and Diamond Jubilee in 1897 were celebrated across the empire, and she regained popularity. She survived several assassination attempts. She died on 22 January 1901 at Osborne House, and was succeeded by her son, Edward VII. Her descendants include monarchs of Britain, Spain, Norway and Sweden.
