Albert Einstein was a German-born physicist whose theories of special and general relativity transformed our understanding of space, time, gravity and energy. He also made fundamental contributions to quantum theory, received the Nobel Prize in Physics in 1921 and became the most famous scientist of the twentieth century.

## Early life

Einstein was born on 14 March 1879 in Ulm, in Germany, into a secular Jewish family, and grew up in Munich, where his father and uncle ran an electrical company. As a child he was fascinated by a compass. He disliked the rigid discipline of school, left without graduating and joined his family in Italy. He completed school in Aarau, Switzerland, and studied physics and mathematics at the Federal Polytechnic in Zurich, graduating in 1900. He gave up his German citizenship and became Swiss in 1901.

## The patent office

Unable to find an academic job, Einstein worked from 1902 as a technical examiner at the Swiss patent office in Bern, evaluating inventions. In 1903 he married Mileva Marić, a fellow physics student; they had two sons, and a daughter born before their marriage whose fate is unknown. In his spare time, he did physics.

## The miracle year

In 1905, at the age of twenty-six, Einstein published four papers that changed physics. The first explained the photoelectric effect by proposing that light consists of quanta, packets of energy, now called photons; this work earned him the Nobel Prize. The second explained Brownian motion, the random movement of particles in a liquid, as evidence of atoms. The third introduced the special theory of relativity, which states that the laws of physics are the same for all observers moving at constant speed and that the speed of light is constant, implying that time and space are relative: moving clocks run slow and moving objects contract. The fourth showed that mass and energy are equivalent, expressed in the equation E = mc².

## General relativity

Einstein became a professor in Zurich, Prague and, in 1914, Berlin. In 1915 he completed the general theory of relativity, which describes gravity not as a force but as the curvature of space-time caused by mass and energy. It explained an anomaly in the orbit of Mercury and predicted that light would bend near the sun. In 1919 the British astronomer Arthur Eddington's measurements during a solar eclipse confirmed the prediction, making Einstein world-famous overnight. General relativity also predicts black holes, the expansion of the universe and gravitational waves, first detected in 2015.

## Quantum debates

Although his work helped found quantum mechanics, Einstein was uncomfortable with its probabilistic interpretation, remarking that God "does not play dice". He debated Niels Bohr for years and, in 1935, with Boris Podolsky and Nathan Rosen, published a paper arguing that quantum mechanics was incomplete, which led to the study of quantum entanglement. He spent his later years searching unsuccessfully for a unified field theory.

## Exile and the bomb

Einstein divorced Mileva in 1919 and married his cousin Elsa. A pacifist and supporter of Zionism, he was attacked by German nationalists and antisemites. When Hitler came to power in 1933, Einstein was abroad; he never returned to Germany and settled at the Institute for Advanced Study in Princeton, becoming an American citizen in 1940. In 1939 he signed a letter, drafted by the physicist Leo Szilard, warning President Roosevelt that Germany might build an atomic bomb, which contributed to the Manhattan Project, though he did not work on it. After the war, he campaigned for nuclear disarmament and, in 1952, declined an offer to become president of Israel.

## Death

Einstein died in Princeton on 18 April 1955. His name has become a byword for genius.
