Isabella I was queen of Castile from 1474 until her death in 1504. With her husband, Ferdinand II of Aragon, she united the crowns that formed the basis of modern Spain, completed the conquest of Granada, sponsored Christopher Columbus's voyage to the Americas and expelled Spain's Jews. The pope gave the couple the title "the Catholic Monarchs".

## A contested succession

Isabella was born on 22 April 1451 at Madrigal de las Altas Torres, the daughter of John II of Castile and his second wife, Isabella of Portugal. Her half-brother Henry IV became king in 1454. Castile was troubled by noble factions. In 1468, after the death of her brother Alfonso, whom rebellious nobles had proclaimed king, Henry recognised Isabella as his heir in the Treaty of the Bulls of Guisando. In 1469, against Henry's wishes, she secretly married Ferdinand, heir to the crown of Aragon, in Valladolid. Henry then named his daughter Joanna, called la Beltraneja by her enemies, who doubted her paternity, as heir.

## War of succession

When Henry died in December 1474, Isabella proclaimed herself queen. Joanna's supporters, backed by Portugal, whose king, Afonso V, married Joanna, went to war. Isabella and Ferdinand won at Toro in 1476, and the Treaty of Alcáçovas in 1479 confirmed Isabella as queen. That year Ferdinand became king of Aragon. Each kept their own kingdom, but they ruled together, with the motto "Tanto monta, monta tanto", "they amount to the same".

## Restoring order

Isabella strengthened royal authority, reduced the power of the nobles, reorganised the royal council and created the Santa Hermandad, a police force, to fight crime. She reformed the church, with her confessor Francisco Jiménez de Cisneros.

## The Inquisition

In 1478 the pope authorised the monarchs to establish the Spanish Inquisition, controlled by the crown, to investigate conversos, Jews who had converted to Christianity, suspected of secretly practising Judaism. Under Tomás de Torquemada, the Inquisition tried thousands of people, and hundreds were burned.

## Granada

From 1482 the monarchs waged a ten-year war against the Nasrid emirate of Granada, the last Muslim state in Iberia. Isabella organised supplies and field hospitals. On 2 January 1492, Granada surrendered, and the last emir, Muhammad XII, Boabdil, handed over the Alhambra. The treaty promised Muslims freedom of religion, but from 1499 they were pressured to convert, and in 1502 Muslims in Castile were ordered to convert or leave.

## 1492

On 31 March 1492, the monarchs issued the Alhambra Decree, ordering all Jews to convert or leave their kingdoms by the end of July. Tens of thousands left, many to Portugal, North Africa and the Ottoman Empire. That April, Isabella agreed to sponsor the voyage of Christopher Columbus, who reached the Caribbean in October. She took an interest in the treatment of the Indigenous people, whom she declared her subjects and not to be enslaved, though abuses continued.

## Family and death

The monarchs married their children to the royal families of Portugal, the Habsburgs and England; their daughter Catherine married Henry VIII. Isabella died on 26 November 1504 at Medina del Campo and was buried in Granada. Her daughter Joanna inherited Castile.
