Charlemagne, or Charles the Great, was king of the Franks from 768, king of the Lombards from 774 and, from 800, emperor in the West. He united most of western and central Europe under his rule for the first time since the Roman Empire, and his court led a revival of learning that shaped medieval European culture.

## The Carolingians

Charlemagne was born around 748 (some scholars say 742 or 747), the son of Pepin the Short, who had overthrown the last Merovingian king in 751 with the pope's blessing, founding the Carolingian dynasty. His grandfather, Charles Martel, had defeated an Umayyad army near Tours in 732. When Pepin died in 768, the kingdom was divided between Charles and his brother Carloman, who died in 771, leaving Charles sole king.

## Conquests

Charlemagne campaigned almost every year. In 774, answering the pope's call, he conquered the Lombard kingdom in Italy and took its crown. In a series of brutal wars lasting over thirty years, from 772 to 804, he conquered the pagan Saxons in northern Germany, destroying their sacred pillar, the Irminsul, and forcing conversion; at Verden in 782 he reportedly had 4,500 Saxons executed. He annexed Bavaria in 788 and destroyed the Avar khaganate on the Danube in the 790s, seizing its treasure. In Spain, his campaign of 778 failed, and the rearguard of his army was destroyed by Basques at Roncesvalles, an event later turned into the Song of Roland. He later created the Spanish March, around Barcelona.

## Emperor

On Christmas Day 800, in St Peter's Basilica in Rome, Pope Leo III crowned Charlemagne emperor of the Romans. Charlemagne had restored Leo after he was attacked by enemies in Rome. His biographer Einhard claimed that Charlemagne did not know about the coronation in advance, but this is doubted. The title challenged the Byzantine emperors, who considered themselves the only Roman emperors, and in 812 Byzantium recognised him as emperor, though not "of the Romans".

## Government

Charlemagne's empire was governed through about 300 counties, run by counts, and checked by royal envoys, missi dominici, who travelled in pairs, a churchman and a layman, inspecting and reporting. He issued laws, capitularies, on government, religion and the economy, reformed the currency, introducing the silver penny, and standardised weights and measures. He travelled constantly but from the 790s settled in Aachen, where he built a palace and a chapel that survives in Aachen Cathedral.

## The Carolingian Renaissance

Charlemagne, who could read but struggled to write, gathered scholars at his court, such as the Anglo-Saxon Alcuin of York, the Lombard Paul the Deacon and the Visigoth Theodulf. He ordered monasteries and cathedrals to open schools and copy texts. Scribes developed a clear handwriting, Carolingian minuscule, the ancestor of our lowercase letters. Many classical Latin texts survive only because they were copied in this period.

## Character

Einhard describes Charlemagne as tall and strong, fond of hunting and swimming, speaking Latin and understanding Greek, and keeping a writing tablet under his pillow to practise. He married several times and had many children. He exchanged embassies with the caliph Harun al-Rashid, who sent him an elephant named Abul-Abbas.

## Death and legacy

Charlemagne died in Aachen on 28 January 814 and was succeeded by his son Louis the Pious. Under Louis's sons, the empire was divided by the Treaty of Verdun in 843, into kingdoms that foreshadowed France and Germany. Both nations claim Charlemagne, and he has been called "the father of Europe".
