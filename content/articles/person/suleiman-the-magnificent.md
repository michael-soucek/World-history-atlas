Suleiman I was the tenth and longest-reigning sultan of the Ottoman Empire, ruling from 1520 to 1566. Known in Europe as "the Magnificent" and in Turkey as Kanuni, "the Lawgiver", he led the empire to the height of its power, conquering Hungary and much of the Middle East, and presided over a golden age of Ottoman culture.

## Prince

Suleiman was born on 6 November 1494 in Trabzon, on the Black Sea, the only son of Selim I, who doubled the size of the empire by conquering the Mamluk Sultanate of Egypt and Syria in 1516–1517. As a young man, Suleiman served as governor of several provinces, including Manisa. When Selim died in 1520, Suleiman became sultan at twenty-five, without the succession struggles that usually accompanied a new sultan.

## Conquests in Europe

In 1521 Suleiman took Belgrade, the key to Hungary, and in 1522 the island of Rhodes, after a long siege, from the Knights Hospitaller, who were allowed to leave. On 29 August 1526, at Mohács, the Ottoman army crushed the Hungarian army, killing King Louis II. Most of Hungary came under Ottoman control. In 1529 Suleiman besieged Vienna, the Habsburg capital, but autumn rains, supply problems and stubborn resistance forced him to withdraw. Central Hungary, with Buda, became an Ottoman province in 1541. France allied with the Ottomans against the Habsburgs.

## The east and the sea

Suleiman fought the Safavid Empire of Persia, taking Baghdad in 1534 and much of Iraq. The Peace of Amasya, in 1555, set the border. At sea, the Ottoman fleet, under the admiral Hayreddin Barbarossa, defeated a Christian alliance at Preveza in 1538, giving the Ottomans control of the eastern Mediterranean. Ottoman fleets also operated in the Red Sea and Indian Ocean. In 1565 the Ottomans failed to take Malta from the Knights Hospitaller.

## The lawgiver

Suleiman issued laws, kanun, on taxation, land, criminal law and administration, which, along with Islamic law, sharia, governed the empire. His chief jurist, Ebussuud Efendi, harmonised the two. He reformed the education system and protected his non-Muslim subjects.

## Golden age

Under Suleiman, Ottoman art, architecture and literature flourished. The architect Mimar Sinan, the chief imperial architect, built hundreds of buildings, including the Süleymaniye Mosque in Istanbul, completed in 1557, a complex with schools, a hospital, a library and baths. Suleiman himself wrote poetry under the pen name Muhibbi. Court painters produced illuminated manuscripts, and Iznik ceramics reached their height.

## Hürrem

Suleiman married Hürrem Sultan, known in Europe as Roxelana, a former slave from Ruthenia, in today's Ukraine, who had been captured by Tatars. Their marriage broke with tradition, and she became an influential political figure, corresponding with foreign rulers. His grand vizier and close friend Ibrahim Pasha was executed in 1536, probably because of his growing power.

## Succession and death

In 1553 Suleiman had his eldest son, Mustafa, a popular prince, executed, believing he was plotting against him; many blamed Hürrem and the vizier Rüstem Pasha. Another son, Bayezid, rebelled and was executed in 1561. Suleiman died on 6 September 1566 during the siege of Szigetvár in Hungary. His death was kept secret until his son Selim II reached the army. He is buried at the Süleymaniye Mosque.
