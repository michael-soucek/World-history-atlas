Vladimir Lenin was a Russian revolutionary who led the Bolshevik Party to power in the October Revolution of 1917 and founded the Soviet state, the world's first communist state. He headed the Soviet government until his death in 1924, and his ideas, Leninism, shaped communist movements across the twentieth century.

## Early life

Vladimir Ilyich Ulyanov was born on 22 April 1870 in Simbirsk, on the Volga, the son of a school inspector who had been ennobled. In 1887 his elder brother, Alexander, was hanged for taking part in a plot to assassinate Tsar Alexander III. The same year, Vladimir was expelled from Kazan University for taking part in student protests. He studied law and became a Marxist.

## Revolutionary

In St Petersburg, Lenin joined Marxist circles. In 1895 he was arrested and later exiled to Siberia for three years, where he married the revolutionary Nadezhda Krupskaya. He then lived in exile in western Europe, writing and editing the newspaper Iskra, "the spark". He adopted the pseudonym Lenin around 1901. In What Is to Be Done?, in 1902, he argued for a party of professional revolutionaries, a vanguard, to lead the working class. In 1903 the Russian Social Democratic Labour Party split into the Bolsheviks, "majority", led by Lenin, and the Mensheviks, "minority".

## War and revolution

Lenin opposed the First World War as an imperialist war and hoped it would turn into a civil war of workers against their governments. In Imperialism, the Highest Stage of Capitalism, in 1916, he argued that capitalism had developed into imperialism. In February 1917, the tsar was overthrown. The German government, hoping to destabilise Russia, allowed Lenin to travel from Switzerland through Germany in a sealed train. Arriving in Petrograd in April, he called in his April Theses for "all power to the soviets", the workers' and soldiers' councils, and opposed the Provisional Government. After a failed uprising in July, he hid in Finland.

## October

On 25 October 1917, by the old calendar, 7 November by the new, the Bolsheviks, led by Lenin and Leon Trotsky, seized power in Petrograd in a largely bloodless coup, storming the Winter Palace. The Congress of Soviets approved a new government, the Council of People's Commissars, chaired by Lenin. He issued decrees on peace and on land, giving land to the peasants. When the elected Constituent Assembly met in January 1918, with a non-Bolshevik majority, the Bolsheviks dissolved it.

## Civil war

In March 1918, Lenin signed the Treaty of Brest-Litovsk with Germany, giving up huge territories, to take Russia out of the war. Civil war followed between the Reds and the Whites. Lenin's government created the Cheka, the secret police, and launched the Red Terror after he was shot and wounded by Fanny Kaplan in August 1918. The tsar and his family were executed. Under "war communism", grain was requisitioned from peasants; with the war, it contributed to a famine in 1921–1922 that killed millions.

## The New Economic Policy

After the Bolsheviks won the civil war, a revolt by sailors at Kronstadt in 1921 led Lenin to introduce the New Economic Policy, allowing small private businesses and trade. In December 1922 the Soviet Union was founded.

## Death

Lenin suffered several strokes from 1922. In his "testament", he warned that Stalin should be removed as general secretary. He died on 21 January 1924, at Gorki, near Moscow. His body was embalmed and placed in a mausoleum on Red Square, where it remains. Petrograd was renamed Leningrad.
