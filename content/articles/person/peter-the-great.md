Peter I, known as Peter the Great, ruled Russia from 1682 to 1725. Determined to modernise his country on the European model, he reformed the army, government, church and society, built a navy, founded Saint Petersburg as his new capital and, after defeating Sweden, proclaimed Russia an empire.

## Childhood

Peter was born on 9 June 1672 in Moscow, the son of Tsar Alexis and his second wife, Natalia Naryshkina. After his father's death, the families of Alexis's two wives fought for power. In 1682, after his half-brother Fyodor died, Peter was proclaimed tsar, but the streltsy, the musketeer regiments, revolted, killing members of his mother's family before his eyes. Peter became co-tsar with his half-brother Ivan V, under the regency of his half-sister Sophia. He grew up outside Moscow, playing with "toy regiments" of boys, which became the elite guards regiments, learning sailing and spending time in the German Quarter, where foreigners lived. In 1689 he overthrew Sophia.

## The Grand Embassy

In 1696 Peter took the Ottoman fortress of Azov, with a newly built fleet. In 1697–1698 he travelled through Europe with a large delegation, the Grand Embassy, partly incognito, under the name Peter Mikhailov. He worked as a shipwright in the Netherlands and England, visited factories, museums and the Royal Mint, and recruited hundreds of experts. He returned early to crush another revolt of the streltsy, executing over a thousand.

## Reforms

Peter forced nobles to shave their beards, or pay a tax, and wear Western clothes. He introduced the Julian calendar from 1700, simplified the Cyrillic alphabet and founded schools of mathematics and navigation, and the Academy of Sciences. He replaced the old council of boyars with a Senate and organised government into colleges, ministries. In 1721 he abolished the patriarchate of the Orthodox Church and replaced it with the Holy Synod, under state control. The Table of Ranks, in 1722, made nobility depend on service to the state. He introduced a poll tax on all male peasants and conscripted them into a large standing army. Peter's reforms were costly and coercive, and the burden of serfdom grew.

## War with Sweden

In 1700 Peter began the Great Northern War against Sweden, the dominant power of the Baltic. Russia was defeated at Narva in 1700 by the young Charles XII, but Peter rebuilt his army and, on 8 July 1709, crushed the Swedes at Poltava, in Ukraine. The Treaty of Nystad in 1721 gave Russia Livonia, Estonia, Ingria and part of Karelia, making it a Baltic power. Russia was proclaimed an empire, and Peter emperor.

## Saint Petersburg

In 1703, on land taken from Sweden at the mouth of the Neva, Peter founded Saint Petersburg, his "window on Europe". Built on marshes by tens of thousands of forced labourers, many of whom died, it became the capital in 1712.

## Family and death

Peter's son Alexei opposed his reforms and fled abroad; he was brought back, tried for treason and died under torture in 1718. Peter's second wife, a Lithuanian peasant woman, became Empress Catherine I. Peter died on 8 February 1725, after an illness, without naming an heir.
