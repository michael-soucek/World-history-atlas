Wu Zetian was the only woman in Chinese history to rule as emperor in her own right. Rising from a concubine of the Tang emperor Taizong to empress, she dominated the government for decades and in 690 founded her own Zhou dynasty, ruling until 705.

## Concubine

Wu Zhao was born in 624 into a wealthy family; her father, Wu Shiyue, a timber merchant, became a high official under the Tang dynasty's founder. Unusually, she was educated in literature, history and music. Around the age of fourteen, she became a concubine of the second Tang emperor, Taizong. When Taizong died in 649, his concubines who had not borne children were sent to a Buddhist convent.

## Empress

Taizong's son, the Gaozong emperor, had met Wu Zhao while his father was alive, and brought her back to the palace as his concubine. She bore him several children. In 655 Gaozong deposed his wife, Empress Wang, and made Wu empress. Her enemies later accused her of killing her own infant daughter to blame Empress Wang, a story many historians doubt. Empress Wang and another consort were killed. Wu removed opponents at court, such as the powerful chancellor Zhangsun Wuji.

## Power behind the throne

From 660 Gaozong, who suffered strokes, increasingly let Wu handle government. She sat behind a screen at court audiences, and the two were called the "two sages". Under their rule, Tang armies conquered the Korean kingdoms of Baekje and Goguryeo, in alliance with Silla, and extended Chinese power in Central Asia.

## Empress dowager

After Gaozong died in 683, Wu became empress dowager. Her son Zhongzong became emperor, but she deposed him within weeks, when he tried to promote his wife's family, and replaced him with another son, Ruizong, who had no power. In 684 she crushed a rebellion. She used informers and secret police, encouraging people to denounce others through copper boxes, and many officials and members of the Tang royal family were killed.

## Emperor

In 690 Wu took the title of emperor, huangdi, and proclaimed the Zhou dynasty, interrupting the Tang. She used Buddhism to legitimise her rule; a text, the Great Cloud Sutra, was interpreted to prophesy a female ruler who was an incarnation of the future Buddha Maitreya. She sponsored Buddhist temples and the great Buddha carvings of the Longmen Grottoes. She moved the capital to Luoyang. She expanded the civil-service examinations, recruiting talented officials from outside the aristocracy, and promoted agriculture. She created new characters for writing, including one for her own name.

## Fall

In her old age, Wu relied on two brothers, the Zhang brothers, as favourites, who became powerful and resented. In 705, aged about eighty, she was forced to abdicate in a coup by officials who killed the Zhang brothers. Zhongzong was restored, and the Tang dynasty resumed. She died later that year and was buried with Gaozong at the Qianling Mausoleum, near Xi'an, beside an uninscribed stele.

## Legacy

Traditional Confucian historians condemned Wu as a ruthless usurper, emphasising her cruelty; modern historians recognise her as an able ruler, while noting her violence.
