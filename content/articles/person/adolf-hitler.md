Adolf Hitler was the dictator of Germany from 1933 to 1945. As leader of the Nazi Party, he established a totalitarian regime, started the Second World War in Europe by invading Poland, and directed the Holocaust, the murder of six million Jews and millions of other people.

## Early life

Hitler was born on 20 April 1889 in Braunau am Inn, Austria, the son of a customs official. He left school without qualifications and moved to Vienna in 1907, hoping to become an artist, but was twice rejected by the Academy of Fine Arts. He lived poorly, selling paintings, and absorbed the antisemitic and German nationalist ideas common in Vienna's politics. In 1913 he moved to Munich.

## The First World War

Hitler volunteered for the Bavarian army in 1914 and served as a dispatch runner on the Western Front, being wounded and awarded the Iron Cross. He was in hospital, temporarily blinded by a gas attack, when Germany surrendered in November 1918. Like many nationalists, he blamed the defeat on Jews and Marxists, claiming that the army had been "stabbed in the back".

## The Nazi Party

In 1919, working for the army in Munich, Hitler joined the small German Workers' Party, which became the National Socialist German Workers' Party, the Nazis, in 1920. His speaking ability made him its leader. In November 1923 he attempted a coup in Munich, the Beer Hall Putsch, which failed. While imprisoned, he wrote Mein Kampf, setting out his ideas: racial antisemitism, hatred of communism, and the conquest of "living space" in eastern Europe.

## Rise to power

The Great Depression brought mass unemployment, and Nazi support rose from 2.6 percent of the vote in 1928 to 37 percent in July 1932, making them the largest party. On 30 January 1933 President Paul von Hindenburg appointed Hitler chancellor, as conservatives believed they could control him. After the Reichstag fire in February, civil liberties were suspended, and in March the Enabling Act gave Hitler power to make laws without parliament. Other parties and trade unions were banned, and in the Night of the Long Knives in 1934, Hitler had rivals killed, including the leaders of the SA. After Hindenburg's death in August 1934, he combined the offices of chancellor and president as Führer.

## The Nazi state

The regime persecuted political opponents, Jews, Roma, gay men and others, and sent many to concentration camps. The Nuremberg Laws of 1935 deprived Jews of citizenship. In the Kristallnacht pogrom of November 1938, synagogues were burned and Jewish businesses destroyed. Rearmament and public works reduced unemployment. Hitler remilitarised the Rhineland in 1936, annexed Austria in 1938 and took the Sudetenland after the Munich Agreement, and the rest of Czechoslovakia in March 1939.

## War and genocide

After signing a pact with the Soviet Union, Hitler invaded Poland on 1 September 1939, and Britain and France declared war. Germany conquered much of Europe by 1941, including France in 1940. In June 1941 Hitler invaded the Soviet Union, and in December declared war on the United States. In occupied eastern Europe, mobile killing units shot over a million Jews, and from 1942 the regime systematically murdered Jews in extermination camps, such as Auschwitz-Birkenau and Treblinka. Millions of Soviet prisoners of war, Poles, Roma, disabled people and others were also killed.

## Defeat and death

After defeats at Stalingrad and in North Africa in 1942–1943, Germany retreated on all fronts. In July 1944, a group of officers tried to kill Hitler with a bomb, but he survived, and thousands were executed. As Soviet troops fought their way into Berlin, Hitler killed himself in his bunker on 30 April 1945, with Eva Braun, whom he had married the day before. Germany surrendered on 8 May 1945. The war had killed some 70 to 85 million people.
