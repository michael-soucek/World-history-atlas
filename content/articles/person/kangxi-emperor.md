The Kangxi emperor was the fourth emperor of the Qing dynasty and the longest-reigning emperor in Chinese history, ruling from 1661 to 1722. He consolidated Manchu rule over China, crushed a major rebellion, annexed Taiwan, fixed the border with Russia and was a patron of learning, laying the foundations of the Qing dynasty's golden age.

## A child emperor

Xuanye, as he was named, was born on 4 May 1654 in Beijing, the third son of the Shunzhi emperor, the first Qing emperor to rule from Beijing. He survived smallpox as a child, which may have helped him be chosen as heir, as he was immune. When his father died in 1661, he became emperor at seven, with four regents. One of them, Oboi, became dominant. In 1669, aged fifteen, the emperor had Oboi arrested, reportedly with the help of young wrestlers, and took power himself.

## The Three Feudatories

Southern China was controlled by three Chinese generals who had helped the Manchus conquer China and had been rewarded with huge fiefs: Wu Sangui in Yunnan, Shang Kexi in Guangdong and Geng Jingzhong in Fujian. When Kangxi decided to abolish their fiefs in 1673, they rebelled. The Revolt of the Three Feudatories lasted eight years and threatened the dynasty, but by 1681 Qing forces had suppressed it.

## Taiwan

Taiwan was held by the Zheng family, Ming loyalists, since Zheng Chenggong, known as Koxinga, had expelled the Dutch in 1662. In 1683 the Qing admiral Shi Lang defeated the Zheng fleet, and Taiwan was incorporated into the Qing empire for the first time.

## Russia and the steppe

Russian Cossacks had built forts on the Amur river. After Qing forces besieged Albazin, the Treaty of Nerchinsk was signed in 1689, with Jesuit interpreters, setting the border between the two empires; it was China's first treaty with a European power. Kangxi then campaigned personally against Galdan, the ruler of the Dzungar Mongols, defeating him in 1696; the Khalkha Mongols submitted to the Qing. In 1720 Qing forces entered Tibet.

## Government

Kangxi worked hard, reading memorials and meeting officials, and made six inspection tours of southern China, inspecting flood-control works on the Yellow River and the Grand Canal. In 1712 he froze the land tax at its current level, promising no future increases. He won over Chinese scholars by holding special examinations and employing them in projects.

## Scholarship

Kangxi sponsored the Kangxi Dictionary, completed in 1716, with over 47,000 characters, which remained the standard dictionary for two centuries, and the vast encyclopaedia Gujin Tushu Jicheng. He was interested in Western science and learned mathematics, astronomy and medicine from Jesuit missionaries, such as Ferdinand Verbiest, who directed the imperial observatory. In 1692 he issued an edict of toleration for Christianity, but the Chinese Rites controversy, in which the pope banned Chinese Christians from practising Confucian rites, led him to restrict missionaries.

## Succession

Kangxi had many sons. His heir, Yinreng, was deposed twice for misconduct, in 1708 and 1712, and the princes fought for the succession. When Kangxi died on 20 December 1722, his fourth son, Yinzhen, became the Yongzheng emperor, amid rumours that he had altered the will. Kangxi's grandson, the Qianlong emperor, would preside over the height of Qing power.
