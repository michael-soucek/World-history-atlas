Augustus was the founder of the Roman Empire and its first emperor, ruling from 27 BCE until his death in 14 CE. Rising to power in the civil wars that followed Julius Caesar's assassination, he ended a century of civil conflict and created a system of government that lasted for centuries, beginning the Pax Romana.

## Octavian

He was born Gaius Octavius in Rome on 23 September 63 BCE, into a wealthy family from Velitrae. His mother, Atia, was the niece of Julius Caesar, who took an interest in the boy. When Caesar was assassinated on 15 March 44 BCE, his will adopted the eighteen-year-old Octavius as his son and heir. Taking the name Gaius Julius Caesar, he is known to historians as Octavian. He raised an army from Caesar's veterans.

## The triumvirate

Octavian at first allied with the Senate against Mark Antony, Caesar's former lieutenant, but in 43 BCE joined Antony and Marcus Lepidus in the Second Triumvirate, given legal power to rule. They ordered proscriptions, lists of enemies to be killed and their property seized; the orator Cicero was among the victims. In 42 BCE Octavian and Antony defeated Caesar's assassins, Brutus and Cassius, at Philippi. The triumvirs divided the Roman world: Octavian took the West, Antony the East. Octavian's friend Agrippa, a gifted general, defeated Sextus Pompey, son of Pompey, at sea in 36 BCE, and Lepidus was pushed aside.

## Actium

Antony allied with Cleopatra, queen of Egypt, and lived with her in Alexandria. Octavian portrayed Antony as a traitor under a foreign queen's influence. In 31 BCE Agrippa defeated their fleet at Actium, in western Greece. In 30 BCE Octavian took Alexandria, and Antony and Cleopatra killed themselves. Egypt became a Roman province, governed by the emperor personally.

## The principate

In 27 BCE Octavian announced that he was restoring the Republic, returning power to the Senate and people. The Senate gave him the name Augustus, "revered", and control of key provinces with most of the legions. He presented himself as princeps, "first citizen", and kept the republican institutions, but held real power through his control of the army, his tribunician power and his wealth. In 2 BCE he was named "father of the country".

## Rebuilding Rome

Augustus claimed that he found Rome a city of brick and left it a city of marble. He and Agrippa built temples, the Forum of Augustus, aqueducts and the first Pantheon, and he organised the city into districts with fire brigades and police. He promoted traditional religion and morality, passing laws to encourage marriage and children and punish adultery; he exiled his own daughter Julia for adultery. The poets Virgil, who wrote the Aeneid, Horace and Ovid, whom Augustus exiled, flourished, supported by his friend Maecenas.

## The empire

Augustus reorganised the army into a permanent professional force of about 28 legions, created the Praetorian Guard and set up a treasury to pay veterans. His generals expanded the empire, conquering northern Spain, the Alpine regions and the Danube lands. An attempt to conquer Germany ended in disaster in 9 CE, when three legions under Varus were destroyed in the Teutoburg Forest.

## Succession

Augustus had no son, and his chosen heirs, his nephew Marcellus, his friend Agrippa and his grandsons Gaius and Lucius, all died before him. He finally adopted his stepson Tiberius. Augustus died at Nola on 19 August 14 CE; the month of August is named after him. His own account of his achievements, the Res Gestae, was inscribed on bronze in Rome.
