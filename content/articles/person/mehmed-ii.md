Mehmed II, known as Mehmed the Conqueror, was the seventh sultan of the Ottoman Empire, who conquered Constantinople in 1453, ending the Byzantine Empire. During his reigns, from 1444 to 1446 and from 1451 to 1481, he transformed the Ottoman state into an empire spanning the Balkans and Anatolia, with Constantinople as its capital.

## Early life

Mehmed was born on 30 March 1432 in Edirne, the Ottoman capital, the son of Sultan Murad II. He was educated in Islamic learning, and he learned several languages. In 1444 Murad abdicated in favour of the twelve-year-old Mehmed, but when a crusading army threatened, Murad returned to lead the army, winning the Battle of Varna in 1444, and resumed the throne in 1446 after a janissary revolt. When Murad died in 1451, Mehmed became sultan again, at nineteen.

## The conquest of Constantinople

Mehmed was determined to take Constantinople, the Byzantine capital, protected by its great land walls. In 1452 he built the fortress of Rumeli Hisarı on the Bosporus, to cut off help from the Black Sea. He hired the Hungarian engineer Orban to cast huge bronze cannons. In April 1453 he besieged the city with a large army, perhaps 80,000 men, against about 7,000 defenders under Emperor Constantine XI. When a chain blocked his fleet from the Golden Horn, he had ships dragged overland on greased logs. On 29 May 1453, after a fifty-three-day siege, Ottoman troops broke into the city. Constantine XI died fighting. Mehmed entered the Hagia Sophia and converted it into a mosque.

## A new capital

Mehmed made Constantinople, which the Ottomans also called Istanbul, his capital. He repopulated it, bringing Muslims, Christians and Jews from across the empire, and appointed Gennadios Scholarios as Greek Orthodox patriarch, giving him authority over the Orthodox community. He built the Topkapı Palace, the Fatih Mosque complex, and the Grand Bazaar. He took the title Kayser-i Rum, "Caesar of Rome".

## Further conquests

Mehmed conquered Serbia in 1459, the Despotate of the Morea in 1460, the Empire of Trebizond in 1461, Bosnia in 1463 and much of Albania, after the death of the resistance leader Skanderbeg. He fought a long war with Venice, from 1463 to 1479. In Wallachia, he fought the prince Vlad III, the Impaler, in 1462. He failed to take Belgrade in 1456, where he was wounded, and the island of Rhodes in 1480. In 1480 his forces landed at Otranto in southern Italy.

## Ruler and patron

Mehmed centralised power, issued a law code, the Kanunname, which notoriously allowed a sultan to kill his brothers to prevent civil war, and appointed grand viziers from the devshirme, men recruited as boys from Christian families, rather than from the old Turkish aristocracy. He was interested in Western art and science, had a library with Greek and Latin works, and invited the Venetian painter Gentile Bellini, who painted his portrait, now in London.

## Death

Mehmed died on 3 May 1481, possibly poisoned, while on campaign in Anatolia. His son Bayezid II succeeded him. In Turkey he is honoured as Fatih, "the Conqueror".
