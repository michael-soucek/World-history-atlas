Mohandas Karamchand Gandhi, called Mahatma, "great soul", was the leader of the Indian independence movement against British rule. He developed a method of non-violent mass resistance, satyagraha, which inspired civil rights and freedom movements around the world. He was assassinated in 1948, months after India became independent.

## Early life

Gandhi was born on 2 October 1869 in Porbandar, a coastal town in Gujarat, into a Hindu merchant-caste family; his father was chief minister of the princely state. His devout mother, a follower of Vaishnavism, influenced by Jainism, shaped his values of non-violence and fasting. At thirteen, he was married to Kasturba Makhanji. In 1888 he went to London to study law, and was called to the bar in 1891.

## South Africa

In 1893 Gandhi went to South Africa to work for an Indian firm. Thrown off a first-class train compartment at Pietermaritzburg because he was not white, he resolved to fight racial discrimination. He stayed for twenty-one years, organising the Indian community. In 1906, against a law requiring Indians to register, he developed satyagraha, "truth-force", non-violent resistance in which protesters willingly accepted punishment. He was jailed several times. He founded communal farms and simplified his life. His views on Black Africans at that time have been criticised.

## Return to India

Gandhi returned to India in 1915. He led campaigns for peasants in Champaran and Kheda and mill workers in Ahmedabad. After the Amritsar massacre of 1919, when British troops killed hundreds of unarmed protesters, he launched the non-cooperation movement in 1920, calling for a boycott of British goods, schools, courts and titles. He promoted khadi, homespun cloth, spinning daily on a spinning wheel, which became a symbol of the movement. When protesters killed policemen at Chauri Chaura in 1922, he called off the campaign. He was imprisoned in 1922–1924.

## The Salt March

In 1930 Gandhi led the Salt March, walking 390 kilometres from his ashram at Sabarmati to Dandi, on the coast, where he made salt from seawater, defying the British salt tax. Millions joined in civil disobedience, and tens of thousands were jailed. He attended the Round Table Conference in London in 1931.

## Social reform

Gandhi campaigned against untouchability, calling the Dalits harijans, "children of God", and for Hindu-Muslim unity. He fasted repeatedly as a form of protest. In 1932 he fasted against separate electorates for Dalits, leading to an agreement with the Dalit leader B. R. Ambedkar, who disagreed with him on many issues.

## Quit India

In 1942, during the Second World War, Gandhi launched the Quit India movement, demanding immediate independence. He and the Congress leaders were imprisoned; Kasturba died in detention in 1944.

## Independence and death

India became independent on 15 August 1947, but was partitioned into India and Pakistan, amid massive violence between Hindus, Muslims and Sikhs. Gandhi, who opposed partition, walked through riot-torn areas and fasted to stop the violence in Calcutta and Delhi. On 30 January 1948, he was shot dead in Delhi by Nathuram Godse, a Hindu nationalist who believed Gandhi had favoured Muslims. His birthday is the International Day of Non-Violence.
