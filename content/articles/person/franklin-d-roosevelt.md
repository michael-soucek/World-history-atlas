Franklin Delano Roosevelt was the thirty-second president of the United States, serving from 1933 until his death in 1945, the only president elected four times. He led the country through the Great Depression, with the New Deal programmes, and through most of the Second World War.

## Early life

Roosevelt was born on 30 January 1882 at Hyde Park, New York, into a wealthy family. He was educated at Groton, Harvard and Columbia Law School. His fifth cousin, Theodore Roosevelt, was president from 1901 to 1909. In 1905 he married Eleanor Roosevelt, Theodore's niece, who became an important political figure in her own right.

## Politics and polio

Roosevelt, a Democrat, served in the New York State Senate and as assistant secretary of the navy from 1913 to 1920, during the First World War. In 1920 he was the Democratic candidate for vice-president, but lost. In August 1921, aged thirty-nine, he contracted a paralytic illness, diagnosed as polio, which left his legs paralysed. He used a wheelchair in private and braces to appear to walk in public, and the press rarely showed his disability. He founded a rehabilitation centre at Warm Springs, Georgia. He became governor of New York in 1929.

## The New Deal

When Roosevelt took office in March 1933, a quarter of the workforce was unemployed and the banking system was collapsing. In his inaugural address, he declared that "the only thing we have to fear is fear itself". He closed the banks for a "bank holiday" and, in his first hundred days, Congress passed a wave of laws. Programmes such as the Civilian Conservation Corps, the Public Works Administration and later the Works Progress Administration employed millions. The Tennessee Valley Authority built dams and brought electricity to a poor region. The Federal Deposit Insurance Corporation insured bank deposits, and the Securities and Exchange Commission regulated the stock market. In 1935 the Social Security Act created old-age pensions and unemployment insurance, and the Wagner Act protected workers' right to form unions. He spoke to the public by radio in "fireside chats".

## Opposition

The New Deal faced opposition from conservatives and business, and the Supreme Court struck down some laws. In 1937 Roosevelt's plan to add justices to the court, "court packing", failed. A recession in 1937–1938 slowed recovery. Historians still debate how much the New Deal ended the Depression; full recovery came with wartime spending.

## War

As war spread in Europe and Asia, Roosevelt, facing isolationist opinion, gradually increased aid to Britain, through the Lend-Lease Act of 1941, calling America the "arsenal of democracy". In August 1941 he and Winston Churchill issued the Atlantic Charter. After Japan attacked Pearl Harbor on 7 December 1941, "a date which will live in infamy", the United States entered the war. Roosevelt worked with Churchill and Stalin, meeting them at Tehran in 1943 and Yalta in February 1945. He approved the Manhattan Project. In 1942 he ordered the internment of about 120,000 Japanese Americans, a decision later recognised as a grave injustice.

## Death

Roosevelt won a fourth term in 1944. On 12 April 1945, he died of a cerebral haemorrhage at Warm Springs, weeks before Germany's surrender. He had planned the United Nations, founded that year. The Twenty-Second Amendment, ratified in 1951, limited presidents to two terms.
