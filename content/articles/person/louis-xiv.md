Louis XIV, known as the Sun King, was king of France from 1643 to 1715, a reign of seventy-two years, the longest of any European monarch. He made France the dominant power in Europe, concentrated power in the crown, built the palace of Versailles and presided over a golden age of French culture.

## The Fronde

Louis was born on 5 September 1638 at Saint-Germain-en-Laye, the son of Louis XIII and Anne of Austria, after twenty-three years of childless marriage. He became king at four, in 1643, with his mother as regent and Cardinal Mazarin as chief minister. From 1648 to 1653, the Fronde, a series of revolts by the Paris parlement and the nobles against the crown's taxes and power, forced the young king to flee Paris. This experience made him distrust Paris and the nobility.

## Personal rule

When Mazarin died in 1661, Louis, aged twenty-two, announced that he would rule without a chief minister. He worked hard and chose capable ministers from the middle classes, such as Jean-Baptiste Colbert, who reformed finances, built up industry and trade, founded manufactures and the navy, and promoted colonies, and the Marquis de Louvois, who built a large, well-organised army. Louis's motto was "Nec pluribus impar", "not unequal to many", and his emblem the sun. The phrase "L'État, c'est moi", "I am the state", is attributed to him, probably falsely, but sums up his absolutism.

## Versailles

Louis turned his father's hunting lodge at Versailles, near Paris, into a vast palace, with the Hall of Mirrors and huge gardens designed by André Le Nôtre. In 1682 he moved the court and government there. Thousands of nobles lived at Versailles, competing for the king's favour through an elaborate ceremonial, in which even his rising and going to bed were public rituals. This kept the nobility under his eye and away from their power bases in the provinces.

## Culture

Louis was a great patron of the arts. Under his reign, the playwrights Molière, Racine and Corneille, the composer Jean-Baptiste Lully and the painter Charles Le Brun flourished. He founded academies of science, painting and music, and the Comédie-Française. French became the language of European diplomacy and elite culture.

## Wars

Louis fought a series of wars to expand France's borders: the War of Devolution, the Dutch War, the Nine Years' War and the War of the Spanish Succession, from 1701 to 1714, which placed his grandson Philip V on the Spanish throne but left France exhausted and heavily in debt. France gained territories such as Franche-Comté, Alsace and parts of Flanders. Coalitions of European powers formed against him.

## Religion

In 1685 Louis revoked the Edict of Nantes, which had granted toleration to Protestants, the Huguenots. Protestant worship was banned, and about 200,000 Huguenots fled to England, the Netherlands, Prussia and elsewhere, taking their skills with them.

## Death

Louis outlived his son and grandson, and when he died on 1 September 1715, his five-year-old great-grandson became Louis XV. On his deathbed, he reportedly told him that he had loved war too much.
