Ivan IV, known as Ivan the Terrible, was grand prince of Moscow from 1533 and the first ruler crowned tsar of all Russia, reigning until 1584. His reign saw great conquests, reforms and the expansion of Russia to the Volga and Siberia, but also a reign of terror and a disastrous war.

## A violent childhood

Ivan was born on 25 August 1530, the son of Grand Prince Vasily III. His father died when Ivan was three, and his mother, Elena Glinskaya, who ruled as regent, died in 1538, perhaps poisoned. Powerful boyar families, the Shuiskys and Belskys, fought for power, and Ivan later recalled being neglected and humiliated. At thirteen, he had a leading boyar arrested and killed.

## Tsar

On 16 January 1547, aged sixteen, Ivan was crowned tsar, a title derived from Caesar, emphasising Moscow's claim to be the heir of Byzantium. Weeks later he married Anastasia Romanovna, from whose family the Romanov dynasty would come. In 1547 a great fire in Moscow led to riots.

## Reforms

In the 1550s, with a group of advisers, the Chosen Council, including the priest Sylvester and the official Alexei Adashev, Ivan carried out reforms. He called the first zemsky sobor, an assembly of the land, issued a new law code, the Sudebnik, in 1550, reformed local government, created a standing infantry of musketeers, the streltsy, and regulated church affairs. The first printing press in Moscow was set up in 1553.

## Kazan and Astrakhan

In 1552 Ivan's army besieged and took Kazan, capital of a Tatar khanate on the Volga, which had raided Russian lands. To celebrate, he built the Cathedral of St Basil on Red Square, with its colourful domes. In 1556 he annexed the Khanate of Astrakhan, giving Russia control of the whole Volga. Russia became a multi-ethnic empire. In 1553 English merchants reached the White Sea, and Ivan granted trading rights to the Muscovy Company.

## The oprichnina

Anastasia's death in 1560, which Ivan believed was poisoning by boyars, marked a turning point. He broke with his advisers, and his commander Andrei Kurbsky defected to Lithuania in 1564, writing letters accusing Ivan of tyranny, to which Ivan replied. In December 1564, Ivan left Moscow and threatened to abdicate, returning only when granted absolute power. He created the oprichnina, a separate territory under his direct rule, policed by the oprichniki, dressed in black, riding with dogs' heads and brooms on their saddles, who arrested, tortured and killed boyars and others. In 1570, suspecting treason, Ivan's forces massacred thousands in Novgorod. The metropolitan Philip, who criticised him, was murdered. The oprichnina was abolished in 1572.

## War and disaster

Ivan's Livonian War, from 1558 to 1583, to gain access to the Baltic, ended in defeat by Poland-Lithuania and Sweden. In 1571 the Crimean Tatars burned Moscow. Meanwhile, the Stroganov merchants sent the Cossack Yermak across the Urals, beginning the conquest of Siberia.

## The death of his son

In November 1581, in a fit of rage, Ivan struck his eldest son and heir, Ivan, with his staff, killing him, an event depicted in Ilya Repin's famous painting. Ivan died on 28 March 1584, while playing chess. His feeble son Fyodor succeeded him, and the dynasty ended in 1598. His Russian epithet, Grozny, means "formidable" or "awe-inspiring" rather than simply "terrible".
