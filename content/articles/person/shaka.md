Shaka was the founder of the Zulu Kingdom in south-eastern Africa, ruling from about 1816 to 1828. A military innovator, he transformed a small chiefdom into the dominant power of the region, but his reign was marked by violence, and its effects were felt across southern Africa.

## Childhood

Shaka was born around 1787, the son of Senzangakhona, chief of the small Zulu clan, and Nandi, a woman of the Langeni clan. His parents were not properly married according to custom, and Shaka and his mother were driven away. They lived among the Langeni and then the Mthethwa, where, according to traditions, he was mistreated as a boy, experiences that some biographers believe shaped his later harshness.

## The Mthethwa army

Shaka served as a warrior in the army of Dingiswayo, the powerful chief of the Mthethwa, who was building a confederation of peoples in the region. Shaka distinguished himself in battle. When Senzangakhona died around 1816, Shaka, with Dingiswayo's help, took over the Zulu chiefdom, killing his half-brother.

## Military reforms

Shaka reorganised the Zulu army. He is credited with introducing, or popularising, a short stabbing spear, the iklwa, used for close combat instead of throwing spears, and a large cowhide shield. He organised his warriors into age-regiments, amabutho, who lived in military homesteads, trained hard and were forbidden to marry until released by the king. According to tradition, he made his warriors go barefoot to increase their speed. He perfected a battle formation known as the "bull's horns": the chest attacked the enemy head-on while the horns encircled them from the sides, with reserves, the loins, behind. Historians debate how many of these innovations were Shaka's own.

## Expansion

After Dingiswayo was killed by Zwide, chief of the Ndwandwe, around 1818, Shaka absorbed the Mthethwa and fought the Ndwandwe, defeating them around 1819 and 1826. He conquered or incorporated many neighbouring chiefdoms, building a kingdom of perhaps a quarter of a million people, centred on his capital, kwaBulawayo, in today's KwaZulu-Natal.

## The Mfecane

The rise of the Zulu kingdom was part of a period of wars and migrations in southern Africa in the 1810s to 1830s, known as the Mfecane, "the crushing", or Difaqane. Groups fleeing or breaking away from the Zulu founded new states: Mzilikazi founded the Ndebele kingdom in today's Zimbabwe, Soshangane the Gaza kingdom in Mozambique, and Moshoeshoe built the Basotho nation in the mountains of Lesotho. Historians debate the causes and extent of the Mfecane, some emphasising drought, competition for land and trade, including the slave trade from Delagoa Bay, and European pressures, rather than Shaka alone.

## Contact with Europeans

In 1824 British traders from the Cape, such as Henry Francis Fynn, established a trading post at Port Natal, today's Durban, with Shaka's permission. Their accounts, which describe Shaka's cruelty, are an important but biased source.

## Death

When his mother Nandi died in 1827, Shaka's grief led to mass killings and harsh decrees. On 24 September 1828, he was assassinated by his half-brothers, Dingane and Mhlangana, and his servant, Mbopha. Dingane became king. Shaka remains a powerful symbol of Zulu identity.
