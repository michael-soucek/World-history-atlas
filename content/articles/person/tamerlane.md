Tamerlane, or Timur, was a Turco-Mongol conqueror who founded the Timurid Empire in Central Asia in the late fourteenth century. In a career of conquest lasting over thirty years, he defeated the Golden Horde, the Ottomans, the Delhi Sultanate and the Mamluks, sacking great cities and causing immense destruction, while making his capital, Samarkand, one of the most beautiful cities of the Islamic world.

## Early life

Timur was born in the 1320s near Kesh, today's Shahrisabz, in Uzbekistan (later Timurid histories give 8 April 1336, a date most scholars now think was invented), into the Barlas tribe, a Mongol tribe that had become Turkic-speaking and Muslim. The name Tamerlane comes from the Persian Timur-i Lang, "Timur the Lame", because of injuries to his right leg and arm from arrows, possibly received while stealing sheep as a young man. His skeleton, examined in 1941, confirmed the injuries.

## Rise to power

The Chagatai Khanate, the Mongol state in Central Asia, was in disarray. Timur rose as a leader of a band of warriors, allied and fought with other leaders, including his brother-in-law, Amir Husayn, whom he defeated and killed in 1370. He became ruler of Transoxiana. Since he was not descended from Genghis Khan, he did not take the title of khan, ruling through puppet khans and calling himself amir, and "royal son-in-law", by marrying a Genghisid princess.

## Conquests

Timur spent the rest of his life campaigning. He conquered Persia, Iraq and the Caucasus. He supported Tokhtamysh as khan of the Golden Horde, then defeated him in two campaigns, in 1391 and 1395, sacking the Horde's cities, Sarai and Astrakhan. In 1398 he invaded India and sacked Delhi, massacring its inhabitants and carrying off craftsmen and treasure. In 1400–1401 he defeated the Mamluks in Syria and sacked Aleppo and Damascus, and in 1401 sacked Baghdad, where he reportedly built towers of skulls. In 1402, at Ankara, he defeated the Ottoman sultan Bayezid I, who died in captivity, nearly destroying the Ottoman state. His campaigns killed perhaps millions of people.

## Samarkand

Timur brought craftsmen, artists and scholars from conquered cities to his capital, Samarkand, which he filled with magnificent buildings with turquoise domes and tiled facades, such as the Bibi-Khanym Mosque and his own mausoleum, the Gur-e Amir. The Spanish ambassador Ruy González de Clavijo, who visited in 1404, described the city and court.

## Ibn Khaldun

During the siege of Damascus in 1401, Timur met the great North African historian Ibn Khaldun, who was lowered from the city walls in a basket, and they discussed history; Ibn Khaldun left an account of the meeting.

## Death

In late 1404, Timur set out to invade Ming China, but died at Otrar on 17 February 1405. His body was buried in the Gur-e Amir. According to legend, an inscription warned that whoever opened the tomb would unleash an invader worse than him; the Soviet archaeologist Mikhail Gerasimov opened it in June 1941, days before Germany invaded the Soviet Union.

## The Timurids

Timur's empire was divided among his descendants. Under his son Shah Rukh and grandson Ulugh Beg, an astronomer who built an observatory in Samarkand, the Timurids presided over a cultural renaissance in Herat and Samarkand. His descendant Babur founded the Mughal Empire in India.
