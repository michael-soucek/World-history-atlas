Joseph Stalin was the leader of the Soviet Union from the mid-1920s until his death in 1953. He transformed the country into an industrial and military superpower through forced industrialisation and collectivisation, led it to victory in the Second World War, and ruled through terror, at the cost of millions of lives.

## Georgia

Stalin was born Ioseb Jughashvili on 18 December 1878 in Gori, in Georgia, then part of the Russian Empire, the son of a shoemaker and a washerwoman. He studied at a seminary in Tiflis but left in 1899 and became a Marxist revolutionary, organising strikes and robberies, such as a bank robbery in Tiflis in 1907, to fund the Bolsheviks. He was repeatedly arrested and exiled to Siberia. He took the name Stalin, "man of steel", around 1912.

## Revolution and rise

After the February Revolution of 1917, Stalin returned to Petrograd. He took part in the Bolshevik seizure of power and became people's commissar for nationalities. In 1922 he became general secretary of the Communist Party, a post that let him appoint officials and build a base of support. Lenin, before his death in 1924, warned in his "testament" that Stalin was too rude and should be removed, but the warning was suppressed. Stalin allied with and then defeated his rivals, Leon Trotsky, who was exiled and murdered in Mexico in 1940, Grigory Zinoviev, Lev Kamenev and Nikolai Bukharin.

## Revolution from above

From 1928, Stalin launched the five-year plans, building heavy industry at breakneck speed. Peasants were forced into collective farms, and those labelled kulaks were deported or killed. The resulting famine of 1932–1933 killed millions, especially in Ukraine, where it is called the Holodomor, and Kazakhstan, where about a third of Kazakhs died. A system of labour camps, the Gulag, held millions of prisoners.

## The Great Terror

In 1936–1938, Stalin's secret police, the NKVD, arrested over a million and a half people, of whom around 750,000 were executed. Show trials convicted old Bolsheviks of absurd crimes. Much of the army's officer corps was purged. A cult of personality presented Stalin as the wise leader of the people. Entire national groups were later deported.

## War

In August 1939 Stalin signed a non-aggression pact with Hitler, dividing eastern Europe, and occupied eastern Poland, the Baltic states and parts of Romania. He ignored warnings of a German attack, and when Germany invaded on 22 June 1941, the Soviet forces suffered catastrophic losses. Stalin remained in Moscow during the German advance in 1941. The Red Army, at enormous cost, defeated Germany at Stalingrad and Kursk and took Berlin in 1945. Around 27 million Soviet citizens died. Stalin met Churchill and Roosevelt at Tehran and Yalta, and Truman and Attlee at Potsdam.

## Post-war

After the war, Stalin imposed communist regimes on eastern Europe, leading to the Cold War. The Soviet Union tested its first atomic bomb in 1949. He launched new purges, and in his last years an alleged "Doctors' Plot", mainly against Jewish doctors, raised fears of a new terror.

## Death and legacy

Stalin died on 5 March 1953 after a stroke. In 1956 Khrushchev denounced his crimes, and his body was removed from Lenin's mausoleum in 1961. His legacy remains contested in Russia, where some admire him as the victor of the war.
