Nelson Mandela was a South African anti-apartheid leader who spent twenty-seven years in prison and became South Africa's first Black president, from 1994 to 1999. He led the transition from apartheid to a multiracial democracy and became a global symbol of reconciliation.

## Early life

Rolihlahla Mandela was born on 18 July 1918 in Mvezo, in the Eastern Cape, into the Thembu royal family of the Xhosa people. A teacher gave him the English name Nelson. After his father's death, he was raised by the Thembu regent. He studied at the University of Fort Hare, where he was expelled for taking part in a protest, then moved to Johannesburg to escape an arranged marriage, studied law and, in 1952, opened South Africa's first Black law firm with Oliver Tambo.

## The ANC

Mandela joined the African National Congress in 1943 and helped found its Youth League. After the National Party introduced apartheid in 1948, he became a leader of the Defiance Campaign of 1952, a campaign of non-violent civil disobedience. He was arrested in 1956 and charged with treason, with 155 others; after a trial lasting until 1961, all were acquitted.

## Armed struggle

After police killed 69 protesters at Sharpeville in 1960, the ANC was banned. In 1961 Mandela co-founded Umkhonto we Sizwe, "Spear of the Nation", the ANC's armed wing, which carried out sabotage against government targets. He went underground, travelled abroad for military training and was arrested in 1962.

## Rivonia and prison

In the Rivonia Trial of 1963–1964, Mandela and other leaders were charged with sabotage. In his speech from the dock, he said that he had cherished the ideal of a democratic and free society, an ideal for which he was prepared to die. He was sentenced to life imprisonment. He spent eighteen years on Robben Island, breaking rocks in a lime quarry, and later was held at Pollsmoor and Victor Verster prisons. He became the world's most famous prisoner, and "Free Nelson Mandela" became a rallying cry. He rejected offers of release conditional on renouncing violence.

## Release and negotiation

Under internal and international pressure, President F. W. de Klerk lifted the ban on the ANC, and Mandela was released on 11 February 1990. He led the ANC in negotiations to end apartheid, amid political violence. In 1993 Mandela and de Klerk shared the Nobel Peace Prize.

## President

In the first democratic elections, in April 1994, the ANC won, and Mandela was inaugurated as president on 10 May 1994. He promoted reconciliation between Black and white South Africans; at the 1995 Rugby World Cup, he wore the jersey of the Springboks, a team associated with white Afrikaners, when they won. He established the Truth and Reconciliation Commission, chaired by Desmond Tutu, to investigate human rights abuses. A new constitution was adopted in 1996. Critics point to his government's slow progress against poverty and its response to HIV/AIDS.

## Later years

Mandela served only one term, stepping down in 1999. He campaigned against HIV/AIDS, from which his son died, and founded charitable organisations. He was married three times, including to Winnie Madikizela-Mandela and, later, Graça Machel. He died on 5 December 2013, aged ninety-five. South Africans call him Madiba, his clan name.
