George Washington was the commander of the Continental Army during the American Revolutionary War and the first president of the United States, from 1789 to 1797. Called the "father of his country", he set many precedents for the new nation, including the peaceful transfer of power.

## Virginia planter

Washington was born on 22 February 1732 in Westmoreland County, Virginia, into a family of planters. His father died when he was eleven, and he had little formal education. He worked as a surveyor and, in 1752, inherited the estate of Mount Vernon after his half-brother Lawrence's death. In 1759 he married Martha Dandridge Custis, a wealthy widow, and raised her two children. Mount Vernon, like other Virginia plantations, depended on enslaved labour; at his death, over 300 enslaved people lived there.

## Frontier soldier

In 1753, as a young officer in the Virginia militia, Washington carried a message to the French in the Ohio country, demanding they leave. In 1754 he led an attack on a French party, killing its commander, which helped spark the French and Indian War. He was forced to surrender at Fort Necessity. In 1755 he served under the British general Edward Braddock, who was killed in an ambush, and Washington, though two horses were shot under him, organised the retreat. He commanded Virginia's forces until 1758. He then served in the Virginia House of Burgesses and opposed British taxation.

## Commander-in-chief

In 1775, after fighting began at Lexington and Concord, the Second Continental Congress appointed Washington commander-in-chief of the Continental Army. He forced the British to leave Boston in 1776, but was defeated at New York and retreated across New Jersey. On the night of 25 December 1776 he crossed the icy Delaware river and surprised the Hessian garrison at Trenton, then won at Princeton. After defeats at Brandywine and Germantown in 1777, his army endured a hard winter at Valley Forge, where the Prussian officer von Steuben trained it. With French help, Washington trapped the British army under Cornwallis at Yorktown in October 1781, effectively ending the war.

## Stepping down

In 1783, Washington defused a potential mutiny of officers at Newburgh and resigned his commission to Congress, returning to Mount Vernon. King George III reportedly said that if he did this, he would be the greatest man in the world. In 1787 he presided over the Constitutional Convention in Philadelphia.

## President

Washington was unanimously elected the first president, taking office in New York on 30 April 1789. He chose a cabinet including Alexander Hamilton as treasury secretary and Thomas Jefferson as secretary of state, whose rivalry led to the first political parties. He supported Hamilton's financial plans, put down the Whiskey Rebellion in 1794 and kept the country neutral in the wars between Britain and France. He was re-elected unanimously in 1792. He chose the site of the new capital on the Potomac, later named Washington.

## Farewell

Washington declined a third term, setting a precedent followed until 1940. His Farewell Address of 1796 warned against political factions and permanent foreign alliances. He died at Mount Vernon on 14 December 1799. In his will, he ordered that the people he enslaved be freed after Martha's death; she freed them in 1801. Dower slaves, owned by the Custis estate, remained enslaved.
