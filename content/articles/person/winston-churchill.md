Winston Churchill was a British statesman, soldier and writer who served as prime minister of the United Kingdom from 1940 to 1945, leading Britain through the Second World War, and again from 1951 to 1955. His speeches during the war became famous, and he won the Nobel Prize in Literature in 1953. His record, especially on empire, remains debated.

## Early life

Churchill was born on 30 November 1874 at Blenheim Palace, the son of Lord Randolph Churchill, a Conservative politician, and Jennie Jerome, an American heiress. He did poorly at school and entered the army through the military college at Sandhurst. He served in Cuba as an observer, in India, in Sudan, where he took part in the cavalry charge at Omdurman in 1898, and as a war correspondent in the Boer War, where he was captured and escaped, making him famous.

## Politician

Churchill entered Parliament as a Conservative in 1900, switched to the Liberals in 1904, and served in Liberal governments as president of the Board of Trade, home secretary and, from 1911, first lord of the Admiralty. In the First World War he championed the Gallipoli campaign of 1915, which ended in a disastrous defeat, and he resigned and served as an officer on the Western Front. He returned to government as minister of munitions and secretary of state for war and the colonies. He rejoined the Conservatives in 1924 and, as chancellor of the exchequer, returned Britain to the gold standard in 1925, a decision later widely criticised.

## The wilderness years

In the 1930s, out of office, Churchill opposed Indian self-government and warned of the danger of Nazi Germany and the need to rearm, opposing the appeasement policy of Neville Chamberlain, including the Munich Agreement of 1938.

## War leader

When war broke out in 1939, Churchill returned as first lord of the Admiralty. On 10 May 1940, as Germany invaded the Low Countries, he became prime minister of a coalition government. In speeches to Parliament and on the radio, he rallied the nation: "I have nothing to offer but blood, toil, tears and sweat"; "we shall fight on the beaches"; "this was their finest hour". After the evacuation of Dunkirk and the fall of France, Britain fought on alone, winning the Battle of Britain in the air. Churchill built an alliance with Franklin Roosevelt and, after June 1941, with Stalin, meeting them at Tehran, Yalta and other conferences.

## Controversies

Churchill's views on race and empire, his role in the Bengal famine of 1943, in which about three million people died, his decisions on the bombing of German cities and his earlier actions, such as sending troops during strikes, remain controversial.

## After the war

In July 1945, weeks after victory in Europe, the Labour Party under Clement Attlee won the general election. In opposition, Churchill warned in 1946 at Fulton, Missouri, that an "iron curtain" had descended across Europe. He returned as prime minister from 1951 to 1955. His six-volume history of the Second World War helped win the Nobel Prize.

## Death

Churchill died on 24 January 1965 and received a state funeral.
