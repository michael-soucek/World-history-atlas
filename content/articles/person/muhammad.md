Muhammad was an Arab religious, social and political leader who founded Islam. Born in Mecca around 570, he began receiving, according to Muslims, revelations from God through the angel Gabriel around 610, which were later collected as the Quran. By his death in 632, most of the Arabian Peninsula had united under Islam. Muslims revere him as the final prophet of God.

## Sources

The main sources for Muhammad's life are the Quran, which Muslims regard as God's word revealed to him, the hadith, reports of his words and actions, and biographies, sira, the earliest surviving of which, by Ibn Ishaq, edited by Ibn Hisham, was written over a century after his death. Historians discuss how to use these sources, but the main outline of his life is widely accepted.

## Mecca

Muhammad was born in Mecca, a trading town in western Arabia, around 570, into the Banu Hashim clan of the Quraysh tribe, which controlled the Kaaba, a shrine housing the idols of many gods, visited by pilgrims. His father, Abdullah, died before his birth and his mother, Amina, when he was six. He was raised by his grandfather and then his uncle, Abu Talib. He worked as a merchant and earned a reputation for honesty, being called al-Amin, "the trustworthy". At about twenty-five, he married Khadija, a wealthy widow who employed him; they had several children, including Fatima.

## Revelation

Muhammad often retreated to a cave on Mount Hira to meditate. According to Islamic tradition, in 610, at about forty, the angel Gabriel appeared to him and commanded him to "recite" in the name of God. Khadija was the first to believe in him, followed by his cousin Ali and his friend Abu Bakr. He preached that there is one God, Allah, that idol worship must end, that people would be judged after death, and that the rich must care for the poor. Most of the Quraysh opposed him, as his message threatened their religion and the pilgrimage trade, and his followers were persecuted; some fled to Christian Ethiopia.

## The Hijra

After Khadija and Abu Talib died around 619, Muhammad's position in Mecca grew dangerous. In 622 he and his followers migrated to Yathrib, an oasis town to the north, later called Medina, "the city of the Prophet", whose tribes invited him to arbitrate their disputes. This migration, the Hijra, marks the beginning of the Islamic calendar. In Medina he became the leader of a community, the umma, with a written agreement, the Constitution of Medina, among Muslims, Jews and other groups.

## Conflict with Mecca

War followed with the Meccans. The Muslims won at Badr in 624, lost at Uhud in 625, and resisted a Meccan siege of Medina in 627, the Battle of the Trench. Conflicts with Jewish tribes of Medina, accused of betraying the Muslims, led to their expulsion, and in the case of the Banu Qurayza, the execution of the men, an episode historians discuss. In 628 the Treaty of Hudaybiyya made a truce with Mecca. In 630 Muhammad entered Mecca with a large army almost without fighting, pardoned most of his opponents and cleared the Kaaba of idols.

## Death

In 632 Muhammad made a final pilgrimage to Mecca, where he gave a farewell sermon. He fell ill and died in Medina on 8 June 632, in the house of his wife Aisha, and was buried there. His succession caused disagreement: Abu Bakr became the first caliph, but some believed Ali should have succeeded him, which eventually led to the division between Sunni and Shia Muslims. Islam spread rapidly and today has nearly two billion followers.

## On our maps

Muhammad's lifetime falls between our 500 and 600 maps, when Arabia lay between the [Byzantine Empire](/place/byzantine-empire) and the [Sasanian Empire](/place/sasanian-empire). On the 700 map, the [Umayyad Caliphate](/place/umayyad-caliphate) shows how quickly Islam spread.
