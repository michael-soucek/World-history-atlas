Nebuchadnezzar II was king of the Neo-Babylonian Empire from 605 to 562 BCE, its longest-reigning and most powerful ruler. He conquered the Levant, destroyed Jerusalem and its Temple, deporting many Judeans to Babylon, and rebuilt Babylon into one of the greatest cities of the ancient world.

## The Chaldean dynasty

Nebuchadnezzar, in Akkadian Nabû-kudurri-uṣur, "O Nabu, protect my heir", was the son of Nabopolassar, who founded the Neo-Babylonian, or Chaldean, dynasty in 626 BCE by freeing Babylon from Assyrian rule. With the Medes, Nabopolassar destroyed the Assyrian Empire, taking Nineveh in 612 BCE. Nebuchadnezzar commanded the army as crown prince.

## Carchemish

In 605 BCE Nebuchadnezzar defeated the Egyptian army of Pharaoh Necho II and the remnants of the Assyrians at Carchemish, on the Euphrates, giving Babylon control of Syria and the Levant. Hearing of his father's death, he hurried back to Babylon to claim the throne.

## Jerusalem

The kingdom of Judah, caught between Egypt and Babylon, rebelled. In 597 BCE Nebuchadnezzar captured Jerusalem, deported King Jehoiachin and thousands of the elite to Babylon and installed Zedekiah as king; the Babylonian Chronicles, cuneiform tablets, record this event. When Zedekiah rebelled, encouraged by Egypt, Nebuchadnezzar besieged Jerusalem again, and in 587 or 586 BCE took the city, destroyed the Temple of Solomon, blinded Zedekiah after killing his sons, and deported much of the population to Babylon. The Babylonian exile shaped Judaism; Psalm 137 begins "By the rivers of Babylon, there we sat down and wept". Nebuchadnezzar also besieged Tyre for thirteen years.

## Rebuilding Babylon

Nebuchadnezzar devoted his wealth to rebuilding Babylon, which became perhaps the largest city in the world, covering about 900 hectares. He built double walls around the city, palaces, and the Ishtar Gate, decorated with glazed blue bricks showing dragons, mushhushshu, and bulls, now reconstructed in the Pergamon Museum in Berlin. The Processional Way, lined with lions in glazed brick, led to the temple of Marduk, the Esagila, and the great ziggurat Etemenanki, "house of the foundation of heaven and earth", about 90 metres high, often thought to have inspired the story of the Tower of Babel. Millions of bricks were stamped with his name.

## The Hanging Gardens

According to Greek and Roman writers, Nebuchadnezzar built the Hanging Gardens of Babylon, one of the Seven Wonders of the Ancient World, for his wife Amytis, a Median princess who missed the green mountains of her homeland. No Babylonian text mentions the gardens, and archaeologists have not found them; some scholars think they were in Nineveh, built by the Assyrian king Sennacherib, or are legendary.

## In the Bible

Nebuchadnezzar appears in the books of Kings, Jeremiah, Ezekiel and Daniel. In Daniel, he throws three young Judeans into a fiery furnace, from which they emerge unharmed, and goes mad, living like an animal for seven years. These stories were written much later.

## Death

Nebuchadnezzar died in 562 BCE. Babylon fell to Cyrus the Great of Persia in 539 BCE, just twenty-three years later.
