Moctezuma II was the ninth ruler, or huey tlatoani, of the Aztec capital Tenochtitlan, reigning from 1502 to 1520. Under him, the Aztec Empire reached its greatest extent, but his reign ended with the arrival of the Spanish under Hernán Cortés, who took him captive; he died in 1520 in disputed circumstances.

## A warrior and priest

Moctezuma Xocoyotzin, "the younger", was born around 1466, a son of the ruler Axayacatl and a great-grandson of Moctezuma I. He was trained as a warrior and a priest, and distinguished himself in war. In 1502 he was chosen as huey tlatoani, "great speaker", by a council of nobles, after the death of his uncle Ahuitzotl.

## Ruler

Moctezuma reorganised the government, replacing commoners in high posts with nobles and increasing court ceremony. Commoners could not look at his face, and nobles approached him barefoot. He continued wars of expansion, conquering territories in the south, to Oaxaca and the Pacific coast of Guatemala, but failed to conquer the neighbouring state of Tlaxcala, a long-time enemy, with which the Aztecs fought ritual "flower wars" to capture prisoners for sacrifice. Tenochtitlan, built on an island in Lake Texcoco, had perhaps 200,000 inhabitants, with the Templo Mayor, palaces, markets and causeways. Moctezuma's palace included a zoo, an aviary and gardens.

## Omens

According to accounts collected by Spanish friars after the conquest, a series of omens preceded the Spaniards' arrival: a comet, a temple fire, a woman weeping in the night. Historians question these accounts, which may have been constructed after the event.

## Cortés

In 1519 Hernán Cortés landed on the Gulf coast with about 500 Spaniards. Moctezuma sent ambassadors with gifts of gold, hoping to persuade them to leave, which only encouraged them. Cortés allied with the Totonacs and, after battles, with the Tlaxcalans, and marched to Tenochtitlan. Moctezuma welcomed him into the city on 8 November 1519, and lodged the Spaniards in his father's palace. A later story claimed that Moctezuma believed Cortés was the god Quetzalcoatl returning; many historians now think this idea was largely created after the conquest.

## Captive

A week after their arrival, the Spaniards took Moctezuma hostage in his own palace. He continued to rule in name, but under Spanish control. In May 1520, while Cortés was away fighting a Spanish force sent to arrest him, his lieutenant Pedro de Alvarado massacred Aztec nobles during the festival of Toxcatl in the Templo Mayor. The people rose against the Spaniards.

## Death

When Cortés returned, the Spaniards were besieged. According to Spanish accounts, Moctezuma was brought onto a roof to calm the crowd, but was struck by stones and died days later, at the end of June 1520. Aztec accounts say the Spaniards killed him. The Spaniards fled the city on the night of 30 June, La Noche Triste, losing many men. Moctezuma's brother Cuitláhuac became ruler but died of smallpox, and his successor, Cuauhtémoc, led the defence of Tenochtitlan, which fell in August 1521.

## Legacy

Spanish chroniclers and later writers often portrayed Moctezuma as indecisive. Modern historians emphasise the impossible situation he faced and his attempts at diplomacy. His descendants were recognised by the Spanish crown as nobles. The feathered headdress in Vienna known as "Moctezuma's headdress" probably did not belong to him.
