Elizabeth I was queen of England and Ireland from 1558 to 1603, the last monarch of the Tudor dynasty. Her reign of forty-four years, often called the Elizabethan age, saw the establishment of the Church of England, the defeat of the Spanish Armada, English voyages of exploration and a flowering of literature and theatre.

## A dangerous childhood

Elizabeth was born on 7 September 1533 at Greenwich, the daughter of Henry VIII and his second wife, Anne Boleyn. When she was two, her mother was executed on charges of adultery and treason, and Elizabeth was declared illegitimate. She was later restored to the line of succession. She received an excellent education, learning Latin, Greek, French and Italian. Under her half-brother Edward VI and her Catholic half-sister Mary I, her position was dangerous: after Wyatt's Rebellion in 1554, a Protestant uprising, Mary imprisoned her in the Tower of London.

## Queen

When Mary died on 17 November 1558, Elizabeth became queen at twenty-five. She chose able advisers, especially William Cecil, later Lord Burghley, who served her for forty years, and later Francis Walsingham, her secretary and spymaster.

## The religious settlement

In 1559 Parliament passed the Acts of Supremacy and Uniformity, making Elizabeth Supreme Governor of the Church of England and introducing a Protestant Book of Common Prayer, while keeping some traditional forms, such as bishops and vestments. Elizabeth sought outward conformity, saying she did not wish to make "windows into men's souls". But after the pope excommunicated her in 1570, and Catholic plots multiplied, Catholic priests were persecuted and many executed. Puritans, who wanted further reform, were also restrained.

## The Virgin Queen

Elizabeth never married, though many suitors, foreign princes and English nobles, sought her hand, and Parliament urged her to marry and produce an heir. She had a close relationship with Robert Dudley, Earl of Leicester. She used the possibility of marriage as a diplomatic tool and cultivated an image as the Virgin Queen, married to her kingdom.

## Mary, Queen of Scots

In 1568 her Catholic cousin Mary, Queen of Scots, fled to England after being forced to abdicate. As Elizabeth's heir and a Catholic, she became the focus of plots, such as the Ridolfi and Babington plots. Elizabeth kept her prisoner for nineteen years. After Walsingham exposed the Babington Plot, Mary was tried and executed in February 1587.

## The Armada

Elizabeth supported the Dutch revolt against Spain and allowed English privateers, such as Francis Drake, who sailed around the world in 1577–1580, to raid Spanish shipping. In 1588 Philip II of Spain sent the Armada, a fleet of about 130 ships, to escort an invasion army from the Netherlands. Elizabeth reportedly told her troops at Tilbury that she had "the heart and stomach of a king". English fireships broke up the Armada's formation off Gravelines, and storms wrecked many ships as they sailed home around Scotland and Ireland.

## The golden age

The Elizabethan age saw the plays of Christopher Marlowe and William Shakespeare, the poetry of Edmund Spenser, whose Faerie Queene celebrated Elizabeth, and the music of William Byrd. Walter Raleigh attempted to found a colony at Roanoke, in Virginia, named after the Virgin Queen. The East India Company was chartered in 1600.

## Later years

Elizabeth's last years were troubled by war in Ireland, where the Nine Years' War brutally suppressed rebellion, poor harvests and the rebellion of her favourite, the Earl of Essex, executed in 1601. She died on 24 March 1603 at Richmond Palace. Her cousin James VI of Scotland, Mary's son, succeeded her, uniting the crowns.
