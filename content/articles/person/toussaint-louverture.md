Toussaint Louverture was the leader of the Haitian Revolution, the only successful slave revolt in history to found an independent state. Born enslaved in the French colony of Saint-Domingue, he became a general, governor and the effective ruler of the colony, abolishing slavery, before being captured by Napoleon's forces and dying in a French prison in 1803.

## Saint-Domingue

Saint-Domingue, the western part of the island of Hispaniola, was the richest colony in the world in the eighteenth century, producing much of the world's sugar and coffee. About 500,000 enslaved Africans worked on its plantations in brutal conditions, alongside about 30,000 white colonists and a similar number of free people of colour.

## Early life

Toussaint was born around 1743 on the Bréda plantation near Cap-Français, enslaved. His father, according to tradition, was the son of an African king. He learned to read and write, gained knowledge of medicine and herbs, and worked as a coachman and livestock keeper. He was freed around 1776, and later owned a small coffee plantation and, briefly, slaves. He was a devout Catholic.

## The revolution

In August 1791, inspired partly by the French Revolution, enslaved people in the north of Saint-Domingue rose up, burning plantations and killing colonists. Toussaint joined the revolt, serving as a doctor and then an officer, and became a leader, training his troops in European-style warfare and guerrilla tactics. He took the name Louverture, "the opening", perhaps because of his ability to find openings in enemy lines. In 1793 he allied with Spain, which controlled the eastern part of the island, against France.

## Siding with France

In 1793 French commissioners in Saint-Domingue proclaimed the abolition of slavery, and in February 1794 the French National Convention abolished slavery in all French colonies. In May 1794 Toussaint switched sides to France, and defeated the Spanish and the British, who had invaded the colony, forcing the British to withdraw in 1798. In 1799–1800 he defeated his rival, the mixed-race general André Rigaud, in the south, in a bitter war. He became governor and in 1801 occupied the Spanish part of the island, abolishing slavery there.

## The constitution

In 1801 Toussaint issued a constitution for Saint-Domingue, declaring slavery abolished forever and making himself governor for life, while remaining nominally loyal to France. To restore the economy, he forced former slaves to return to work on plantations as paid labourers, under strict discipline, and invited white planters to return, which caused discontent.

## Napoleon

Napoleon, who intended to restore slavery in the French colonies, sent a large army under his brother-in-law, General Charles Leclerc, in 1802. After months of fighting, Toussaint agreed to a truce and retired to his plantation. In June 1802 he was invited to a meeting, arrested and deported to France, saying that in overthrowing him, the French had only cut down the trunk of the tree of liberty, which would grow back from its many deep roots. He was imprisoned at Fort de Joux, in the cold Jura mountains, where he died on 7 April 1803.

## Independence

When it became clear that France intended to restore slavery, Toussaint's lieutenants, Jean-Jacques Dessalines and Henri Christophe, renewed the war. Yellow fever devastated the French army, and Dessalines defeated it at Vertières in November 1803. On 1 January 1804, Dessalines declared the independence of Haiti.
