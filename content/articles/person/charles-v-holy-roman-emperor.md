Charles V ruled the largest European empire since Charlemagne. Through a series of inheritances, he became lord of the Netherlands, king of Spain with its American empire, ruler of the Habsburg lands in Austria, king of much of Italy and Holy Roman Emperor. His reign, from 1516 to 1556, was dominated by wars with France, the Ottoman threat and the Protestant Reformation.

## A remarkable inheritance

Charles was born in Ghent, in Flanders, on 24 February 1500, the son of Philip the Handsome, a Habsburg, and Joanna of Castile, the daughter of Ferdinand and Isabella. He grew up in the Netherlands, which he inherited from his father in 1506. In 1516, on the death of his grandfather Ferdinand, he became king of Castile and Aragon, with their possessions in Italy and the Americas, ruling jointly with his mother, who was declared mentally unfit and confined at Tordesillas. In 1519, on the death of his grandfather Maximilian I, he inherited the Habsburg lands in Austria and was elected Holy Roman Emperor, after spending huge sums, borrowed from the Fugger bankers, to bribe the electors.

## Spain and the Americas

Charles arrived in Spain in 1517 speaking little Spanish and surrounded by Flemish advisers. His demands for money and his departure to Germany provoked the Revolt of the Comuneros in Castile in 1520–1521, which was crushed. Over time he learned Spanish and Spain became the centre of his power. During his reign, Hernán Cortés conquered the Aztec Empire and Francisco Pizarro the Inca Empire, and Magellan's expedition sailed around the world. Silver from the Americas funded his wars. In 1542 he issued the New Laws to protect Indigenous people, after debates involving Bartolomé de las Casas.

## Luther

At the Diet of Worms in 1521, Charles confronted Martin Luther, who refused to recant his writings. Charles declared him an outlaw, but Luther was protected by the Elector of Saxony, and Lutheranism spread among German princes and cities. Charles, often at war elsewhere, could not suppress it. In 1546–1547 he defeated the Protestant Schmalkaldic League at Mühlberg, but could not impose religious unity. The Peace of Augsburg in 1555 allowed each prince to choose Lutheranism or Catholicism for his territory.

## Wars with France

Charles and Francis I of France fought repeatedly over Italy and Burgundy. In 1525 Charles's army captured Francis at the Battle of Pavia. In 1527 Charles's unpaid troops mutinied and sacked Rome, imprisoning Pope Clement VII. In 1530 the pope crowned Charles emperor at Bologna, the last papal coronation of an emperor.

## The Ottomans

Suleiman the Magnificent conquered Hungary after the Battle of Mohács in 1526 and besieged Vienna in 1529, which held, defended by Charles's brother Ferdinand. Charles captured Tunis in 1535 but failed at Algiers in 1541. France allied with the Ottomans against him.

## Abdication

Exhausted and suffering from gout, Charles abdicated in a series of ceremonies in 1555–1556, dividing his empire. His son Philip II received Spain, the Americas, Italy and the Netherlands, and his brother Ferdinand the Austrian lands and the imperial title. Charles retired to the monastery of Yuste, in Spain, where he died on 21 September 1558. His motto, Plus Ultra, "further beyond", remains on Spain's coat of arms.
