Confucius was a Chinese teacher, philosopher and political thinker who lived from 551 to 479 BCE. His teachings on morality, family relationships, ritual and good government, recorded by his followers in the Analects, became the foundation of Confucianism, which shaped Chinese civilisation and the cultures of Korea, Japan and Vietnam for over two thousand years.

## A time of disorder

Confucius, in Chinese Kong Fuzi or Kongzi, "Master Kong", was born in the state of Lu, in today's Shandong province, during the Spring and Autumn period, when the Zhou dynasty's kings had lost power and rival states fought each other. His family belonged to the lower aristocracy, the shi, but his father died when he was young, and he grew up in modest circumstances. He held minor posts, such as manager of granaries and of livestock, while studying the ancient rituals, music, poetry and history.

## Teacher

Confucius became a teacher, accepting students regardless of their family background or wealth, as long as they were eager to learn, which was unusual at the time. According to tradition, he had about three thousand students, of whom seventy-two mastered his teachings. Among his favourite disciples was Yan Hui, whose early death grieved him. He taught that anyone could become a junzi, a "gentleman" or "noble person", through study and moral cultivation, not by birth.

## Office and exile

According to tradition, Confucius served as minister of justice in Lu, but became disillusioned with the ruler, who neglected government. Around 497 BCE he left Lu and spent about fourteen years travelling with his disciples between states, such as Wei, Chen and Cai, seeking a ruler who would put his ideas into practice. He was sometimes in danger, and failed to find a post. Around 484 BCE he returned to Lu, where he taught and, according to tradition, edited the classic texts, such as the Book of Songs and the Spring and Autumn Annals, until his death in 479 BCE.

## Teachings

Confucius saw himself as a transmitter of the wisdom of the early Zhou kings, especially the Duke of Zhou, rather than an innovator. Central to his teaching is ren, humaneness or benevolence, and li, the rituals and proper conduct that express respect and maintain social order. He stressed filial piety, xiao, respect for parents and ancestors, and the proper relationships between ruler and subject, father and son, husband and wife, elder and younger brother, and friends. He taught a form of the golden rule: "Do not impose on others what you do not wish for yourself." He believed rulers should govern by moral example rather than punishment: if the ruler is virtuous, the people will follow, as grass bends in the wind. He emphasised "rectification of names", that people should act according to their roles.

## The Analects

The Analects, Lunyu, a collection of his sayings and conversations with his disciples, was compiled by his followers over several generations after his death. It begins with a famous line about the joy of learning and practising what one has learned.

## Influence

After his death, his followers, such as Mencius, who taught that human nature is good, and Xunzi, who thought it needed shaping, developed his ideas. Under the Han dynasty, Confucianism became the state ideology, and the Confucian classics became the basis of the civil-service examinations, which lasted until 1905. Temples to Confucius were built across China. Confucianism was attacked in the twentieth century, particularly during the Cultural Revolution, but has since been revived. His family home and temple in Qufu are a World Heritage Site.
