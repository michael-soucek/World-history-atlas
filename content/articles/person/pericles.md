Pericles was the leading statesman of Athens during its golden age in the mid-fifth century BCE. A general and orator, he guided Athenian democracy, built the Parthenon and other monuments on the Acropolis, and led Athens at the start of the Peloponnesian War against Sparta, dying of plague in 429 BCE.

## A noble family

Pericles was born around 495 BCE into a leading Athenian family. His father, Xanthippus, was a general who fought the Persians at Mycale in 479 BCE, and his mother, Agariste, was a member of the Alcmaeonid family and the niece of Cleisthenes, the founder of Athenian democracy. He studied with philosophers, such as Anaxagoras, who taught that the sun was a fiery stone. In 472 BCE he sponsored Aeschylus's play The Persians.

## Democratic leader

Pericles allied with the democratic leader Ephialtes, who in 462 BCE reduced the power of the Areopagus, a council of former magistrates, transferring its powers to the assembly, the council and the courts. After Ephialtes was murdered, Pericles became the leading figure in Athens. He introduced pay for jurors, enabling poorer citizens to take part in government. In 451 BCE he passed a law restricting citizenship to those with both Athenian parents. From 443 BCE he was elected strategos, general, every year until his death. The historian Thucydides wrote that Athens was in name a democracy, but in fact ruled by its first citizen.

## Empire

Athens led the Delian League, an alliance of Greek states formed against Persia, which became an Athenian empire. In 454 BCE the league's treasury was moved from Delos to Athens. Pericles used the tribute paid by the allies to fund Athenian buildings, which critics called a misuse. Athens founded colonies, cleruchies, and crushed revolts, such as that of Samos in 440 BCE.

## The Acropolis

Pericles sponsored a great building programme. The Parthenon, a temple of Athena, was built between 447 and 432 BCE, designed by the architects Ictinus and Callicrates, with sculptures supervised by Phidias, who also made the gold and ivory statue of Athena inside. The Propylaea, the monumental gateway, and the Odeon were also built. Athens attracted artists, philosophers and writers, such as Sophocles, Herodotus and Socrates.

## Aspasia

Pericles divorced his wife and lived with Aspasia, a woman from Miletus, known for her intelligence; Socrates is said to have discussed rhetoric with her. As a foreigner, she could not marry an Athenian. Comic poets attacked her, and their son was later granted citizenship by special decree.

## The Peloponnesian War

Tensions with Sparta and its allies led to war in 431 BCE. Pericles's strategy was to avoid land battles with Sparta's superior army, bring the population of Attica inside Athens's walls and use Athens's navy to raid the Peloponnese. In the winter of 431–430 BCE, he delivered the Funeral Oration, reported by Thucydides, praising Athenian democracy and calling Athens "the school of Hellas".

## Plague and death

In 430 BCE a plague broke out in overcrowded Athens, killing perhaps a quarter of the population. Pericles was briefly removed from office and fined, but re-elected. His two legitimate sons died, and he died of the plague in 429 BCE. Athens lost the war in 404 BCE.
