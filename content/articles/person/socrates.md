Socrates was an Athenian philosopher of the fifth century BCE, regarded as one of the founders of Western philosophy. He wrote nothing; he is known through the writings of his students, especially Plato and Xenophon. He spent his life questioning his fellow Athenians about how to live well, and was executed in 399 BCE.

## The Socratic problem

Because Socrates left no writings, scholars debate how much of what Plato attributes to him represents the historical Socrates rather than Plato's own ideas. Xenophon's portrait is more practical, and the comic playwright Aristophanes mocked him in The Clouds, in 423 BCE, as a sophist who taught young men to win arguments by trickery.

## Life

Socrates was born around 470 BCE in Athens, the son of Sophroniscus, a stonemason, and Phaenarete, a midwife. He may have worked as a stonemason. He served as a hoplite, a heavy infantryman, in the Peloponnesian War, at Potidaea, where he saved the life of the young Alcibiades, Delium and Amphipolis, showing great courage and endurance. He married Xanthippe, who was described as quarrelsome, and they had three sons. He was famously ugly, with a snub nose and bulging eyes, and walked barefoot. He lived modestly and did not accept payment for teaching, unlike the sophists.

## The wisest man

According to Plato's Apology, Socrates's friend Chaerephon asked the oracle of Delphi whether anyone was wiser than Socrates, and the oracle answered no. Socrates, who believed he knew nothing, set out to find someone wiser. He questioned politicians, poets and craftsmen, and found that they thought they knew things they did not. He concluded that he was wiser only because he knew that he did not know.

## The Socratic method

Socrates engaged people in conversation in the marketplace, asking questions about the nature of virtues, such as justice, courage and piety. By questioning their answers, he exposed contradictions, leading them to recognise their ignorance and search for better definitions. This method of questioning, elenchus, is called the Socratic method. He compared himself to a midwife, helping others give birth to ideas, and to a gadfly, stinging Athens into thinking. He taught that "the unexamined life is not worth living", that virtue is knowledge, that no one does wrong knowingly, and that it is better to suffer injustice than to commit it. He claimed to hear an inner voice, a daimonion, warning him against certain actions.

## Politics

Socrates was critical of democracy, especially of the rule of the ignorant. In 406 BCE, serving on the council, he opposed the illegal mass trial of generals. Under the Thirty Tyrants, in 404 BCE, he refused an order to arrest an innocent man. Some of his associates, such as Alcibiades and Critias, had harmed Athens.

## Trial and death

In 399 BCE, after the restoration of democracy, Socrates was charged with impiety, not believing in the city's gods and introducing new ones, and corrupting the youth. A jury of 501 citizens found him guilty. Asked to propose a penalty, he suggested free meals at public expense, as a benefactor of the city. He was sentenced to death. He refused his friends' offers to help him escape, saying he must obey the laws. He drank a cup of hemlock, poison, surrounded by his friends, as described in Plato's Phaedo.
