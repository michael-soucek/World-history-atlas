Hannibal Barca was a Carthaginian general who led an army, with war elephants, from Spain over the Alps into Italy in 218 BCE, during the Second Punic War. He defeated Roman armies in a series of great battles, including Cannae, and is considered one of the greatest military commanders in history, though Rome ultimately won the war.

## Son of Hamilcar

Hannibal was born in 247 BCE in Carthage, in today's Tunisia, the son of Hamilcar Barca, a general in the First Punic War, which Carthage lost to Rome in 241 BCE. According to the historian Polybius, when Hannibal was nine, his father made him swear at an altar never to be a friend of Rome. Hamilcar took him to Spain, where he built a Carthaginian empire, rich in silver mines. After his father's death and that of his brother-in-law Hasdrubal the Fair, Hannibal took command in Spain in 221 BCE, aged twenty-five.

## Crossing the Alps

In 219 BCE Hannibal besieged and took Saguntum, a Spanish city allied with Rome, which declared war. Instead of waiting for Rome to attack, Hannibal marched overland to invade Italy. In 218 BCE he crossed the Pyrenees and the Rhône, with about 40,000 infantry, cavalry and thirty-seven elephants, and crossed the Alps in autumn, facing snow, narrow paths and attacks by mountain tribes. His route is still debated. He arrived in northern Italy with perhaps 26,000 men and few elephants, but Gallic tribes joined him.

## Victories in Italy

Hannibal defeated the Romans at the Ticinus and the Trebia in 218 BCE. In 217 BCE he ambushed a Roman army at Lake Trasimene, in the mist, destroying it. Rome appointed Quintus Fabius Maximus as dictator, who avoided battle and harassed Hannibal, earning the name Cunctator, "the delayer". On 2 August 216 BCE, at Cannae, in Apulia, Hannibal faced a Roman army of perhaps 80,000 men. He placed his weaker troops in the centre, which bent back, while his African infantry and cavalry closed on the flanks and rear, encircling and destroying the Roman army. Perhaps 50,000 Romans died, one of the worst defeats in Roman history. The battle is still studied by military academies as the model of encirclement.

## Stalemate

After Cannae, Capua and many southern Italian cities and Syracuse joined Hannibal, and he allied with Philip V of Macedon. But Hannibal did not march on Rome, which kept fighting, and most of Rome's allies remained loyal. He lacked siege equipment and reinforcements. For over a decade, he stayed in southern Italy, while Rome retook Capua and Syracuse. In 207 BCE his brother Hasdrubal, bringing reinforcements, was defeated and killed at the Metaurus; his head was thrown into Hannibal's camp.

## Zama

Meanwhile, the Roman general Publius Cornelius Scipio conquered Spain and invaded Africa. Carthage recalled Hannibal in 203 BCE. At Zama, in 202 BCE, Scipio, later called Africanus, defeated Hannibal, and Carthage made peace on harsh terms.

## Later life

Hannibal led Carthage's government, reforming its finances, but Rome demanded his surrender, and he fled in 195 BCE to the Seleucid king Antiochus III, whom he advised in war against Rome, and then to Bithynia. When the Romans demanded his surrender there, he took poison, around 183 BCE.
