Charles Darwin was an English naturalist whose theory of evolution by natural selection, published in On the Origin of Species in 1859, explained how living things change over time and how all life is related. His work became the foundation of modern biology. (His voyage on HMS Beagle is covered in detail on his explorer page.)

## Early life

Darwin was born on 12 February 1809 in Shrewsbury, England, the same day as Abraham Lincoln. His father, Robert, was a wealthy doctor, and his grandfathers were the physician and poet Erasmus Darwin, who had speculated about evolution, and the potter Josiah Wedgwood. He studied medicine at Edinburgh but disliked it, especially surgery without anaesthetic, and then studied at Cambridge to become a clergyman. There he collected beetles and became a protégé of the botanist John Stevens Henslow.

## The Beagle

Through Henslow, Darwin was invited in 1831 to join HMS Beagle, under Captain Robert FitzRoy, on a survey voyage around the world, which lasted five years. In South America, he collected fossils of extinct giant mammals, experienced an earthquake in Chile and saw how the land had risen. In the Galápagos Islands in 1835 he collected mockingbirds, finches and tortoises that differed from island to island. He read Charles Lyell's Principles of Geology, which argued that the earth had been shaped by slow processes over vast time.

## Developing the theory

Back in England, Darwin became a respected naturalist and published his account of the voyage. In 1837 he began notebooks on the "transmutation of species", sketching a branching tree of life. In 1838, after reading Thomas Malthus's essay on population, he realised that in the struggle for existence, individuals with favourable variations would survive and reproduce more, so species would gradually change: natural selection. He developed the theory privately for twenty years, gathering evidence, studying pigeon breeding and barnacles, worried about the reaction.

## Down House

In 1839 Darwin married his cousin Emma Wedgwood, and in 1842 they moved to Down House in Kent, where he lived for the rest of his life. They had ten children; the death of his daughter Annie in 1851 deeply affected him and weakened his faith. He suffered from a chronic illness, of uncertain cause, for most of his life.

## The Origin of Species

In 1858 Darwin received an essay from Alfred Russel Wallace, a naturalist in the Malay Archipelago, describing the same theory. Their papers were presented together at the Linnean Society in July 1858. Darwin then rushed to finish On the Origin of Species, published on 24 November 1859. Its first printing sold out. The book provoked intense debate; scientists such as Thomas Henry Huxley defended it, and at a famous debate in Oxford in 1860 Huxley clashed with Bishop Samuel Wilberforce. Many scientists accepted evolution within a few decades, though natural selection as its main mechanism was widely accepted only in the twentieth century, when it was combined with genetics.

## Later work

In The Descent of Man, in 1871, Darwin applied his theory to human beings, arguing that humans shared ancestors with apes, and introduced sexual selection. He also wrote on the expression of emotions, orchids, climbing plants, carnivorous plants and earthworms.

## Death

Darwin died at Down House on 19 April 1882 and was buried in Westminster Abbey, near Isaac Newton.
