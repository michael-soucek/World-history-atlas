Mao Zedong was a Chinese communist revolutionary who led the Chinese Communist Party to victory in the civil war and founded the People's Republic of China in 1949. He ruled until his death in 1976. His policies unified China and transformed its society, but the Great Leap Forward and the Cultural Revolution caused tens of millions of deaths and immense suffering.

## Early life

Mao was born on 26 December 1893 in Shaoshan, in Hunan province, the son of a prosperous peasant. He clashed with his strict father and left home to study, attending a teachers' college in Changsha. He read widely and was influenced by Chinese nationalism and Western ideas. In 1918 he worked as a library assistant at Peking University, where he encountered Marxism. In 1921 he was one of the founding members of the Chinese Communist Party.

## Peasant revolution

During the alliance between the Communists and the Nationalists, Mao worked among peasants in Hunan and concluded that the revolution in China would be led by peasants, not industrial workers, as in orthodox Marxism. After the Nationalist leader Chiang Kai-shek turned on the Communists in 1927, Mao led a failed uprising and retreated to the mountains, building a guerrilla army and a soviet base in Jiangxi.

## The Long March

In 1934, encircled by Nationalist forces, the Communists broke out and began the Long March, a retreat of about 10,000 kilometres through western China, ending at Yan'an, in Shaanxi, in 1935. Of about 86,000 who set out, fewer than 10,000 arrived. During the march, Mao rose to the leadership of the party. In Yan'an, he developed his ideology, Mao Zedong Thought, and in 1942 launched a "rectification" campaign against dissent.

## War and victory

During the war with Japan, from 1937 to 1945, the Communists expanded their forces and territory. After Japan's defeat, civil war resumed, and the Communists, with peasant support, won. On 1 October 1949, Mao proclaimed the People's Republic of China in Beijing.

## The new China

Mao's government carried out land reform, in which landlords were killed, possibly a million or more people, and campaigns against counter-revolutionaries. China fought in the Korean War. In 1956 the Hundred Flowers campaign invited criticism; when it came, the Anti-Rightist campaign of 1957 persecuted hundreds of thousands of critics.

## The Great Leap Forward

In 1958 Mao launched the Great Leap Forward, aiming to rapidly overtake Britain's industrial output by organising peasants into huge communes and producing steel in backyard furnaces. Unrealistic targets, false reports of record harvests and grain requisitions led to a famine in 1959–1961 in which an estimated 15 to 45 million people died, one of the deadliest famines in history.

## The Cultural Revolution

In 1966, to restore his authority and revive revolutionary zeal, Mao launched the Cultural Revolution. Young Red Guards, carrying his Little Red Book, attacked teachers, officials and "class enemies", destroyed temples and cultural relics, and persecuted millions; hundreds of thousands to perhaps two million people died. Party leaders, such as Liu Shaoqi, were purged. Schools and universities closed. The chaos lasted until his death.

## Later years and legacy

In 1972 Mao welcomed US President Richard Nixon to Beijing. He died on 9 September 1976. His body lies in a mausoleum in Tiananmen Square. In 1981 the Communist Party judged that his achievements outweighed his mistakes, "seventy percent good and thirty percent bad", a verdict that remains official in China.
