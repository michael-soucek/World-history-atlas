Alexander the Great was king of Macedon from 336 BCE until his death in 323 BCE. In just over a decade, he conquered the Persian Empire and led his army as far as the Indus valley, creating one of the largest empires of the ancient world and spreading Greek culture across western Asia and Egypt.

## Prince of Macedon

Alexander was born in 356 BCE in Pella, the capital of Macedon, the son of King Philip II and Olympias, a princess of Epirus. Philip transformed Macedon from a weak kingdom on the northern edge of the Greek world into the dominant power in Greece, with a professional army built around the phalanx of pikemen armed with long sarissas and the heavy cavalry of the Companions. The philosopher Aristotle tutored Alexander for several years. According to Plutarch, as a boy Alexander tamed the horse Bucephalus, which carried him into battle for years. At eighteen, he commanded the Companion cavalry at the Battle of Chaeronea in 338 BCE, where Philip defeated Athens and Thebes.

## King

In 336 BCE Philip was assassinated, and Alexander, aged twenty, became king. He crushed revolts in the north and, when Thebes rebelled, destroyed the city in 335 BCE, selling its people into slavery. He then took up his father's plan to invade the Persian Empire, the largest state in the world, presented as revenge for the Persian invasions of Greece.

## Conquest of Persia

In 334 BCE Alexander crossed into Asia with about 40,000 men. He defeated a Persian army at the Granicus river and freed the Greek cities of Asia Minor. According to legend, at Gordium he cut the Gordian knot. In 333 BCE, at Issus, he defeated the Persian king Darius III himself, capturing his family. He besieged Tyre for seven months, building a causeway to the island city, and took Egypt, where he was welcomed as a liberator and crowned pharaoh, founding Alexandria in 331 BCE. At the oracle of Siwa he was greeted as the son of Zeus-Ammon. In 331 BCE he defeated Darius decisively at Gaugamela, near today's Mosul, and took Babylon, Susa and Persepolis, which he burned. Darius was killed by his own officials in 330 BCE.

## To the edge of the world

Alexander pressed on through Central Asia, fighting a hard guerrilla war in Bactria and Sogdiana, where he married Roxana, a Sogdian noblewoman. In 327 BCE he invaded India, defeating King Porus at the Hydaspes river in 326 BCE, a battle in which the Macedonians faced war elephants. At the Hyphasis river, his exhausted troops refused to go further. On the return journey, his army suffered heavily crossing the Gedrosian desert.

## Ruling an empire

Alexander adopted Persian court dress and ceremonies, appointed Persian governors and married Persian princesses, and in 324 BCE held a mass wedding at Susa for his officers and Persian noblewomen. Many Macedonians resented these policies. He became increasingly suspicious and violent, executing his general Parmenion and killing his friend Cleitus in a drunken quarrel. He founded many cities, several named Alexandria.

## Death

Alexander died in Babylon in June 323 BCE, aged thirty-two, after a fever following a banquet; illness, poison and other causes have been suggested. He left no adult heir, and his generals, the Diadochi, fought for decades over his empire, which split into kingdoms ruled by the Ptolemies, Seleucids and Antigonids.

## Legacy

Alexander's conquests began the Hellenistic age, in which Greek language and culture spread from Egypt to Afghanistan, mixing with local traditions. Legends about him, the Alexander Romance, spread across Europe and Asia, and he appears in Persian, Arabic and Ethiopian literature.
