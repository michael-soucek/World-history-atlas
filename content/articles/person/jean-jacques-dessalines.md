Born enslaved on a sugar plantation in the French colony of Saint-Domingue, Jean-Jacques Dessalines led the final campaign of the [Haitian Revolution](/event/haitian-revolution) and proclaimed Haiti's independence on 1 January 1804. He ruled the new state, from late 1804 as Emperor Jacques I, until his assassination in 1806. He is honoured in Haiti as a founding father, but he also ordered the 1804 massacre of the country's remaining white French inhabitants.

## Slavery and rebellion

Dessalines was born, according to the usual date, on 20 September 1758, on a plantation near Grande-Rivière-du-Nord in the north of the colony. Known at first as Jean-Jacques Duclos after his father's owner, he laboured in the cane fields and became a foreman. At about thirty he was sold to a free man of colour named Dessalines, whose surname he kept. When the great slave uprising broke out on the northern plain in 1791, he joined it, first serving with rebel bands allied to Spain in the eastern half of the island.

## Toussaint's lieutenant

In 1794, after revolutionary France abolished slavery, [Toussaint Louverture](/person/toussaint-louverture) went over to the French side, and Dessalines followed him. He fought against Spanish and British forces, became a brigadier general by 1799, and in 1801 put down a revolt in the north. He was known for a ruthless style of warfare that spared few prisoners and burned villages.

## War against Leclerc

[Napoleon](/person/napoleon) sent an expedition under General Charles Leclerc in 1802 to restore French control. In March Dessalines held the small fort of Crête-à-Pierrot with about 1,300 men against a far larger French force for some twenty days before breaking out into the mountains. He then briefly came to terms with Leclerc, and several historians hold him partly responsible for Toussaint's arrest and deportation to France in June 1802, where Toussaint died the following year. When it became clear in the autumn that France meant to bring back slavery, Dessalines and the mixed-race general Alexandre Pétion turned against the French again, and by November Dessalines led the combined rebel forces. Yellow fever ravaged the French army and killed Leclerc. On 18 November 1803 the rebels defeated Leclerc's successor Rochambeau at Vertières, near Cap-Français, and the last French troops capitulated soon afterwards.

## Independence and massacre

At Gonaïves on 1 January 1804, Dessalines declared the colony independent under the name Haiti, taken from the island's indigenous Taíno name, and became governor-general for life. Haiti was the first state in the Americas to abolish slavery permanently. Between February and April 1804 he ordered the killing of the remaining French colonists, estimated at 3,000 to 5,000 people; some historians link the massacre to fear of a French return and the restoration of slavery. Certain groups, such as Polish soldiers who had deserted the French, were spared and given citizenship. The 1805 constitution defined Haiti as a black nation and barred white colonists from owning land or property.

## Emperor Jacques I

Proclaimed emperor by his generals in September 1804, Dessalines was crowned at Cap-Français in October. In 1805 he invaded the Spanish-speaking east of the island but withdrew when a French squadron appeared. At home he forced former slaves to work on the plantations or serve in the army to keep exports flowing, a system many felt resembled their old bondage. Mixed-race landowners resented his plans to redistribute land, and senior officers resented his autocratic rule.

## Assassination and legacy

A revolt broke out in the south in 1806, and on 17 October Dessalines was killed at Larnage, north of Port-au-Prince, after a plot by members of his own government, among them Pétion. Accounts of the killing differ, but most agree that a crowd mutilated his body. Haiti then split between Pétion in the south and Henri Christophe in the north. Long a divisive figure, Dessalines was later embraced as a national hero; Haiti's national anthem, written in 1903, is named after him.
