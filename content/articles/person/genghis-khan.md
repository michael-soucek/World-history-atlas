Genghis Khan was the founder of the Mongol Empire, which became the largest contiguous land empire in history. Born Temüjin on the Mongolian steppe, he united the nomadic tribes in 1206 and led campaigns that conquered northern China, Central Asia and parts of Persia before his death in 1227.

## A hard childhood

Temüjin was born around 1162, near the Onon river, the son of Yesügei, a chief of the Borjigin clan. According to the Secret History of the Mongols, the main source for his life, written soon after his death, he was born clutching a blood clot, an omen of greatness. When he was about nine, his father was poisoned by Tatars, and the clan abandoned his mother, Hö'elün, and her children, who survived by foraging and fishing. Temüjin killed his half-brother Begter in a quarrel over food. He was later captured by the Tayichiud clan and escaped.

## Rise to power

Temüjin married Börte, to whom he had been betrothed as a child. When the Merkits kidnapped her, he rescued her with the help of Toghrul, khan of the Keraits, and his blood brother, Jamukha. Temüjin built a following by rewarding loyalty and talent rather than birth, and by incorporating defeated tribes. He defeated the Tatars, broke with Jamukha and Toghrul, and defeated the Keraits and Naimans. In 1206, at a great assembly, a kurultai, by the Onon river, he was proclaimed Genghis Khan, probably meaning "universal" or "firm ruler".

## Organising the Mongols

Genghis organised his people into military units of ten, a hundred, a thousand and ten thousand, mixing tribes to break old loyalties. He created an imperial guard, the keshig, adopted the Uyghur script for writing Mongolian, and issued laws, later called the Yassa. Mongol armies were made up of mounted archers, extremely mobile, disciplined and skilled in feigned retreats and encirclements. Each soldier had several horses.

## Conquests in China

Genghis first attacked the Tangut kingdom of Western Xia, which submitted in 1210. In 1211 he invaded the Jin dynasty in northern China. In 1215 the Mongols took the Jin capital, Zhongdu, today's Beijing. The Mongols learned siege warfare from Chinese and Muslim engineers.

## Khwarazm

In 1218, Mongol forces conquered the Qara Khitai in Central Asia. That year, a Mongol trade caravan was massacred at Otrar, in the Khwarazmian Empire, and the shah, Muhammad II, executed a Mongol envoy. Genghis invaded in 1219 with a large army. Over the next three years, the Mongols destroyed the cities of Bukhara, Samarkand, Urgench, Merv, Nishapur and Herat, killing large numbers of inhabitants; contemporary chronicles give figures in the hundreds of thousands, probably exaggerated, but the destruction was enormous. The shah fled and died on an island in the Caspian. His generals Jebe and Subutai rode around the Caspian, defeating Georgians, Kipchaks and Rus' princes at the Kalka river in 1223.

## Death

Genghis returned to Mongolia and in 1226 attacked the Western Xia again. He died in August 1227, during the campaign, probably from illness or a fall from his horse; the cause is uncertain. He was buried in a secret place, probably near the sacred mountain Burkhan Khaldun, which has never been found.

## Legacy

Genghis divided his empire among his sons and grandsons; his son Ögedei became Great Khan. His successors expanded the empire from Korea to Hungary. He is remembered for the destruction his conquests caused, and for creating a state that linked East and West, protecting trade on the Silk Road and allowing religious freedom. In Mongolia he is a national hero. Genetic studies suggest that a large number of men in Asia descend from him or his close relatives, though this is debated.
