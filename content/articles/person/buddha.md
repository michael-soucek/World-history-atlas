The Buddha, "the awakened one", was a spiritual teacher who lived in northern India and Nepal around the fifth century BCE and founded Buddhism. Born Siddhartha Gautama, a prince of the Shakya clan, he left his comfortable life to seek an end to suffering, and spent his life teaching the path he had found.

## Sources and dates

The Buddha's life is known from Buddhist texts written down centuries after his death, which mix historical memory with legend. Most scholars accept that he was a real person, but his dates are uncertain. Older traditions place his death in 544 or 483 BCE; many modern scholars favour around 400 BCE. He lived during a time of growing cities and kingdoms in the Ganges valley, when many wandering ascetics questioned Vedic religion.

## Prince Siddhartha

According to tradition, Siddhartha was born in the Lumbini grove, in today's Nepal, the son of Suddhodana, a ruler of the Shakyas, whose capital was Kapilavastu, and Queen Maya, who died a week after his birth. A sage predicted that he would become either a great king or a great religious teacher. His father raised him in luxury, sheltered from suffering. He married Yasodhara, and they had a son, Rahula.

## The four sights

According to the legend, at about twenty-nine, Siddhartha left the palace and saw an old man, a sick man, a corpse and a wandering ascetic. Shaken by the realisation that everyone ages, sickens and dies, he left his family at night, cut his hair and became a wandering ascetic. He studied meditation with teachers and practised extreme self-denial, nearly starving himself to death, before deciding that this was not the path.

## Awakening

He accepted food, regained his strength and sat in meditation under a fig tree, later called the Bodhi tree, at Bodh Gaya. According to tradition, after resisting the temptations of Mara, a demon, he attained enlightenment, at about thirty-five, understanding the nature of suffering and the way to end it.

## The teaching

The Buddha gave his first sermon at the Deer Park in Sarnath, near Varanasi, to five former companions. He taught the Four Noble Truths: life involves suffering, dukkha; suffering arises from craving; suffering can end; and the way to end it is the Noble Eightfold Path, of right view, intention, speech, action, livelihood, effort, mindfulness and concentration. He described this as a Middle Way between indulgence and harsh self-denial. He also taught that all things are impermanent, that there is no permanent self, and that actions, karma, shape rebirth. The end of craving leads to nirvana, liberation from the cycle of rebirth.

## The community

For about forty-five years, the Buddha travelled across the Ganges plain, teaching people of all classes, from kings, such as Bimbisara of Magadha, to merchants and outcasts. He founded the sangha, a community of monks and, according to tradition at the request of his aunt and foster mother Mahapajapati, nuns. His disciples included Ananda, his cousin and attendant, Sariputta and Moggallana. Followers kept the rainy season in shelters donated by patrons.

## Death

At about eighty, the Buddha died at Kushinagar, after a meal, entering final nirvana, parinirvana. His last words, according to tradition, urged his followers to strive diligently. His remains were cremated and divided among several groups, who built stupas over them.

## Legacy

After his death, his teachings were passed on orally and recited at councils, then written down, in Pali in Sri Lanka in the first century BCE. Buddhism spread across Asia, dividing into traditions such as Theravada and Mahayana. Today there are several hundred million Buddhists.
