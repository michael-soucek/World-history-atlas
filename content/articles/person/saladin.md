Saladin, in Arabic Salah ad-Din Yusuf ibn Ayyub, was the first sultan of Egypt and Syria and the founder of the Ayyubid dynasty. He united much of the Muslim Middle East and in 1187 recaptured Jerusalem from the Crusaders. Respected by Muslims and Christians alike for his chivalry, he fought Richard the Lionheart in the Third Crusade.

## A Kurdish family

Saladin was born around 1137 in Tikrit, in today's Iraq, into a Kurdish family. His father, Ayyub, and uncle, Shirkuh, served Zengi and his son Nur ad-Din, rulers of Mosul and Aleppo, who led the Muslim struggle against the Crusader states. Saladin grew up in Baalbek and Damascus and received a religious education.

## Egypt

Nur ad-Din sent Shirkuh, with Saladin, on three expeditions to Egypt, ruled by the weak Shia Fatimid caliphs, which the Crusaders also sought to control. In 1169 Shirkuh became vizier of Egypt and died soon after, and Saladin succeeded him. In 1171, on Nur ad-Din's orders, he ended the Fatimid caliphate, restoring Sunni Islam and recognising the Abbasid caliph in Baghdad. He built the Citadel of Cairo.

## Uniting Syria and Egypt

After Nur ad-Din's death in 1174, Saladin took Damascus and, over the next decade, through campaigns and diplomacy, extended his authority over Syria and northern Iraq, taking Aleppo in 1183. He was now ruler of a state that surrounded the Crusader kingdom of Jerusalem.

## Hattin and Jerusalem

In 1187, after the Crusader lord Raynald of Châtillon attacked Muslim caravans, Saladin invaded the Kingdom of Jerusalem. On 4 July 1187, at the Horns of Hattin, near Lake Tiberias, he defeated the Crusader army, which had marched through dry country without water. King Guy of Jerusalem was captured and treated well; Raynald was executed, by Saladin's own hand according to some sources. Saladin then took most of the kingdom's cities, and on 2 October 1187 Jerusalem surrendered after a siege. Unlike the Crusaders, who had massacred the inhabitants in 1099, Saladin allowed the Christians to leave on payment of a ransom, and freed many who could not pay. He restored the Dome of the Rock and the Al-Aqsa Mosque to Muslim worship.

## The Third Crusade

The fall of Jerusalem led to the Third Crusade, led by Richard I of England, Philip II of France and the emperor Frederick Barbarossa, who drowned on the way. The Crusaders took Acre in 1191 after a long siege; Richard executed about 2,700 Muslim prisoners when ransom negotiations broke down. Richard defeated Saladin at Arsuf in 1191 but did not attempt to besiege Jerusalem. The two leaders exchanged gifts; Saladin reportedly sent Richard fresh fruit and snow when he was ill and a horse when his was killed. In 1192 they made the Treaty of Jaffa: the Crusaders kept a coastal strip, and Christian pilgrims could visit Jerusalem.

## Death and legacy

Saladin died in Damascus on 4 March 1193. He had given away so much of his wealth that there was not enough money for his funeral. His tomb is beside the Umayyad Mosque. His empire was divided among his relatives. He became a hero in both Muslim and European tradition, admired for his generosity and honour.

## On our maps

Saladin's death falls between our 1100 and 1200 maps. On the 1100 map, Egypt belongs to the [Fatimid Caliphate](/place/fatimid-caliphate); by 1200, his family's Ayyubid sultanate holds Egypt and Syria, before it gives way to the [Mamluk Sultanate](/place/mamluk-sultanate).
