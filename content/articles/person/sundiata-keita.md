Sundiata Keita was the founder of the Mali Empire in West Africa, in the thirteenth century. His story, the Epic of Sundiata, has been passed down for centuries by griots, the hereditary oral historians and storytellers of the Mande peoples, and is one of the great epics of Africa.

## Sources

Little is known of Sundiata from written sources. The North African historian Ibn Khaldun, writing in the fourteenth century, mentions him as the founder of Mali's power. The rest of his story comes from oral tradition, the Epic of Sundiata, which exists in many versions and mixes history with legend. Historians place his reign around 1235 to 1255.

## The lion child

According to the epic, Sundiata was the son of Naré Maghann Konaté, king of a small Mande kingdom on the upper Niger, and Sogolon, a woman described as ugly and hunchbacked, whom a hunter had prophesied would bear a great king. Sundiata was born unable to walk, crawling on all fours until the age of seven, and was mocked by the king's first wife, Sassouma Bereté. One day, after his mother was humiliated, he stood up, using an iron rod that bent under his weight, and uprooted a baobab tree. His name is often interpreted as "lion prince".

## Exile

After his father's death, Sassouma's son, Dankaran Touman, became king, and Sundiata, his mother and siblings fled into exile, living in several kingdoms, including Mema, where the king welcomed him and he became a great warrior.

## Sumanguru

Meanwhile, Sumanguru Kanté, king of the Sosso, a powerful sorcerer-king, conquered the Mande lands. The Mande people sent for Sundiata to free them. Sundiata gathered allies among the Mande clans. According to the epic, Sumanguru was invincible thanks to his magic, but Sundiata's sister, Nana Triban, who had been married to Sumanguru, learned his secret: he could be harmed by an arrow tipped with the spur of a white rooster. At the Battle of Kirina, around 1235, Sundiata defeated the Sosso; struck by the arrow, Sumanguru fled and disappeared into a mountain.

## The Mali Empire

After his victory, Sundiata was proclaimed mansa, king of kings, at an assembly at Kurukan Fuga, where, according to tradition, the social organisation of the empire was established, including the roles of the clans and castes, such as griots and blacksmiths. This charter, the Manden Charter, was recognised by UNESCO as intangible cultural heritage. Sundiata established his capital at Niani, though its location is debated. Mali took control of the gold fields and trade routes that had belonged to Ghana, and became one of the largest empires in African history, later famous under Mansa Musa.

## Death and legacy

According to different traditions, Sundiata drowned in the Sankarani river or was killed by an arrow during a celebration, around 1255. The Epic of Sundiata is still performed by griots, accompanied by the kora, a harp-lute. It has been compared to the epics of Homer and inspired, among other works, The Lion King.
