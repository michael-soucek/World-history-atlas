Gaius Julius Caesar was a Roman general and statesman who conquered Gaul, won a civil war and made himself dictator of Rome. His assassination on the Ides of March in 44 BCE led to the end of the Roman Republic and the rise of the empire under his adopted heir, Augustus.

## Patrician

Caesar was born on 12 or 13 July 100 BCE in Rome, into an old but not wealthy patrician family, the Julii, who claimed descent from the goddess Venus. His aunt Julia was married to the general Marius, leader of the populares, politicians who appealed to the people. In his youth, he refused the dictator Sulla's order to divorce his wife, Cornelia, and went into hiding. He was captured by pirates, and after being ransomed, he hunted them down and crucified them, as he had promised.

## Politics

Caesar rose through the offices of the Roman Republic, spending lavishly on public games and building his popularity, while running up huge debts. He was elected pontifex maximus, chief priest, in 63 BCE. In 60 BCE he formed an informal alliance, the First Triumvirate, with Pompey, Rome's greatest general, and Crassus, its richest man. Elected consul in 59 BCE, he passed laws by force against the opposition of the Senate. Pompey married his daughter Julia.

## Gaul

From 58 BCE Caesar governed Gaul, and in eight years of campaigns he conquered the whole of Gaul, roughly today's France and Belgium, crossed the Rhine into Germany and invaded Britain twice, in 55 and 54 BCE. In 52 BCE the Gauls rose under Vercingetorix, whom Caesar besieged and captured at Alesia. The war made Caesar rich and gave him a loyal, experienced army, but killed or enslaved perhaps a million Gauls. His own account, the Commentaries on the Gallic War, is a classic of Latin prose.

## Civil war

Crassus died in 53 BCE, and Julia's death in 54 BCE weakened the bond with Pompey, who allied with Caesar's enemies in the Senate. Ordered to give up his army, Caesar crossed the Rubicon, the river border of Italy, with his troops in January 49 BCE, reportedly saying "the die is cast", starting a civil war. He defeated Pompey at Pharsalus in Greece in 48 BCE. Pompey fled to Egypt, where he was murdered. In Egypt, Caesar supported Cleopatra in her struggle for the throne and became her lover. He then defeated his opponents in Asia Minor, where he reported "veni, vidi, vici", "I came, I saw, I conquered", in Africa and Spain.

## Dictator

As dictator, Caesar pardoned many enemies, enlarged the Senate, founded colonies for veterans and the poor, granted citizenship to provincials and reformed the calendar, introducing the Julian calendar of 365 days with a leap year, the basis of the calendar used today. In early 44 BCE he was named dictator for life.

## The Ides of March

Many senators feared he would make himself king. A conspiracy of about sixty senators, led by Marcus Brutus and Gaius Cassius, stabbed him to death on 15 March 44 BCE at a meeting of the Senate in the Theatre of Pompey. His murder led to more civil wars, from which his adoptive son Octavian emerged as Augustus, the first emperor. His name became a title, Kaiser and Tsar.
