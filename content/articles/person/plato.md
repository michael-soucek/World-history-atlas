Plato was an Athenian philosopher of the fourth century BCE, a student of Socrates and teacher of Aristotle. He founded the Academy in Athens, one of the earliest institutions of higher learning in the Western world, and his dialogues, which explore justice, knowledge, beauty, love and the nature of reality, are among the foundational works of Western philosophy.

## Early life

Plato was born around 428 or 427 BCE in Athens, into a wealthy aristocratic family. His mother's relatives included Critias, a leader of the Thirty Tyrants, who briefly ruled Athens after its defeat by Sparta in 404 BCE. His real name may have been Aristocles; "Plato" may have been a nickname referring to his broad shoulders. As a young man, he may have intended a political career, but he became a follower of Socrates.

## The death of Socrates

In 399 BCE the restored Athenian democracy tried Socrates for impiety and corrupting the youth and condemned him to death. This event deeply affected Plato, who wrote about the trial and death in the Apology, Crito and Phaedo. He became disillusioned with Athenian politics and left Athens for a time, travelling, according to tradition, to Megara, Egypt and southern Italy, where he met Pythagorean philosophers, such as Archytas of Tarentum.

## Syracuse

Plato visited Syracuse, in Sicily, three times. On his first visit, around 388 BCE, he befriended Dion, the brother-in-law of the tyrant Dionysius I. Later, around 367 BCE, Dion invited Plato to educate the young Dionysius II as a philosopher-king, but the attempt failed, and Plato was held as a virtual prisoner. Plato's letters, whose authenticity is debated, describe these events.

## The Academy

Around 387 BCE Plato founded the Academy, in a grove sacred to the hero Akademos outside Athens. Students studied mathematics, astronomy, dialectic and philosophy. Aristotle studied there for twenty years. The Academy continued, in various forms, for centuries.

## The dialogues

Plato wrote about thirty-five dialogues, philosophical conversations, most featuring Socrates as the main speaker. All his known works survive. Early dialogues, such as the Euthyphro and Laches, show Socrates questioning people about the meaning of virtues, such as piety and courage, and exposing their ignorance. In later dialogues, Socrates expresses ideas that are probably Plato's own.

## The theory of Forms

Plato argued that the world we perceive through the senses is constantly changing and only a shadow of true reality, the world of Forms, perfect, eternal and unchanging ideas, such as Beauty, Justice and the Good, which can be grasped only by reason. In the Allegory of the Cave, in the Republic, he compares people to prisoners chained in a cave, seeing only shadows on the wall, and the philosopher to one who escapes into the sunlight and sees reality.

## The Republic

In the Republic, Plato describes an ideal state, ruled by philosopher-kings, trained in mathematics and philosophy, assisted by guardians, who live without private property or families, while producers work. Justice is when each class does its own work. He argued that poets should be censored. He also explored the immortality of the soul, in the Phaedo, love, in the Symposium, and the creation of the world, in the Timaeus, which includes the story of Atlantis. In his last work, the Laws, he described a more practical state.

## Death and influence

Plato died in Athens around 348 or 347 BCE. His ideas influenced Christian and Islamic theology, Neoplatonism and Renaissance thought. The philosopher Alfred North Whitehead wrote that the European philosophical tradition consists of a series of footnotes to Plato.
