Cleopatra VII was the last active ruler of the Ptolemaic kingdom of Egypt, reigning from 51 to 30 BCE. Intelligent, multilingual and politically skilled, she allied with two of Rome's most powerful men, Julius Caesar and Mark Antony, to protect her kingdom's independence. Her death marked the end of Hellenistic Egypt and its absorption into the Roman Empire.

## The Ptolemies

Cleopatra was born in 69 BCE, the daughter of Ptolemy XII Auletes. Her family, the Ptolemies, were Macedonian Greeks, descended from Ptolemy I, a general of Alexander the Great, who had ruled Egypt from Alexandria since 305 BCE. They often married their siblings, following Egyptian royal custom. According to Plutarch, Cleopatra was the first of her dynasty to learn Egyptian, and she spoke many other languages. By her time, Egypt was rich but dependent on Rome, and her father had paid huge bribes to Roman leaders to keep his throne.

## Queen

When her father died in 51 BCE, Cleopatra, aged about eighteen, became joint ruler with her younger brother, Ptolemy XIII, whom she was expected to marry. His advisers forced her out of Alexandria, and she raised an army in Syria.

## Caesar

In 48 BCE Julius Caesar arrived in Alexandria pursuing his rival Pompey, whom Ptolemy's advisers had already killed. According to Plutarch, Cleopatra had herself smuggled into the palace, rolled in a bedding sack, to meet Caesar. They became lovers. Caesar's forces, besieged in Alexandria in the Alexandrian War, defeated Ptolemy XIII, who drowned in the Nile, and Caesar restored Cleopatra to the throne with another brother, Ptolemy XIV. In 47 BCE she gave birth to a son, Ptolemy Caesar, called Caesarion. She lived in Rome as Caesar's guest, causing scandal, and left after his assassination in 44 BCE. Ptolemy XIV died soon after, perhaps poisoned, and she made Caesarion her co-ruler.

## Antony

In 41 BCE Mark Antony, who controlled Rome's eastern provinces, summoned Cleopatra to Tarsus. She arrived, according to Plutarch, on a gilded barge with purple sails, dressed as Aphrodite. They became lovers and allies, and she bore him twins, Alexander Helios and Cleopatra Selene, and later a son, Ptolemy Philadelphus. Antony, who married Octavian's sister Octavia for political reasons, returned to Cleopatra. In the Donations of Alexandria in 34 BCE, Antony gave Roman territories in the east to Cleopatra and her children and proclaimed Caesarion as Caesar's true heir, a direct challenge to Octavian.

## Actium and death

Octavian declared war on Cleopatra in 32 BCE. In September 31 BCE, at the naval battle of Actium, Octavian's fleet under Agrippa defeated Antony and Cleopatra, who escaped to Egypt. When Octavian's army reached Alexandria in August 30 BCE, Antony stabbed himself, mistakenly believing Cleopatra was dead, and died in her arms. Cleopatra killed herself, according to tradition by the bite of an asp, an Egyptian cobra, though the method is uncertain, rather than be paraded in Octavian's triumph in Rome. Caesarion was killed on Octavian's orders. Egypt became a Roman province.

## The ruler

Cleopatra was a capable ruler who managed Egypt's economy and grain supply during famine and presented herself as the goddess Isis. Coins suggest she was not exceptionally beautiful; Plutarch wrote that her charm lay in her conversation and presence.

## The legend

Roman writers, influenced by Octavian's propaganda, portrayed her as a dangerous foreign seductress. Her story inspired Shakespeare's Antony and Cleopatra, George Bernard Shaw's Caesar and Cleopatra and many films. Her ancestry has been debated, as the identity of her mother and grandmother are uncertain.

## On our maps

The [Ptolemaic Kingdom](/place/ptolemaic-kingdom) appears on our maps from 300 to 100 BCE. By the 1 BCE map, after her death, Egypt is part of the [Roman Empire](/place/roman-empire).
