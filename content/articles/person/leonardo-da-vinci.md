Leonardo da Vinci was an Italian painter, engineer, scientist and inventor of the High Renaissance. He painted some of the most famous works in the history of art, including the Mona Lisa and The Last Supper, and filled thousands of notebook pages with studies of anatomy, machines, water, flight and nature, which make him the model of the "Renaissance man".

## Early life

Leonardo was born on 15 April 1452 in or near the town of Vinci, in Tuscany, the illegitimate son of Ser Piero, a notary, and Caterina, a young peasant woman. Around the age of fourteen, he was apprenticed to the artist Andrea del Verrocchio in Florence, whose workshop produced paintings, sculptures and metalwork. In Verrocchio's Baptism of Christ, Leonardo painted one of the angels, so well, according to Vasari, that Verrocchio gave up painting.

## Milan

Around 1482 Leonardo moved to Milan, offering his services to Duke Ludovico Sforza in a letter that stressed his skills as a military engineer, mentioning painting only at the end. He stayed for seventeen years, designing festivals, working on canals and fortifications, and making a huge clay model for a bronze horse, which was never cast; the bronze was used for cannons. In the refectory of the convent of Santa Maria delle Grazie, he painted The Last Supper, between about 1495 and 1498, showing the moment when Jesus announces that one of his apostles will betray him. Because he experimented with tempera on dry plaster rather than fresco, the painting began to deteriorate in his lifetime.

## Florence and the Mona Lisa

After the French invaded Milan in 1499, Leonardo returned to Florence. He worked as a military engineer for Cesare Borgia, making maps. Around 1503 he began the portrait of Lisa Gherardini, wife of the merchant Francesco del Giocondo, the Mona Lisa, which he worked on for many years and never delivered. He used sfumato, soft transitions between colours and tones without lines, and an atmospheric landscape. He was commissioned to paint the Battle of Anghiari in the Palazzo Vecchio, competing with Michelangelo, but abandoned it.

## The notebooks

Leonardo's notebooks, written in mirror writing from right to left, contain over 7,000 surviving pages. He dissected about thirty human bodies and drew detailed studies of the skeleton, muscles, heart, foetus in the womb and brain. He designed flying machines, inspired by birds, a diving suit, an armoured vehicle, bridges and machines, most never built. He studied water, geology, botany and optics. His Vitruvian Man illustrates the proportions of the human body. He published nothing, and his notebooks were dispersed after his death, so his scientific work had little influence.

## Last years

Leonardo worked again in Milan, then in Rome, from 1513 to 1516, under the patronage of the pope's brother. In 1516 he accepted the invitation of King Francis I of France and moved to the manor of Clos Lucé, near the royal castle of Amboise, taking the Mona Lisa with him. He died there on 2 May 1519. According to legend, he died in the arms of the king.

## Legacy

Fewer than twenty paintings are generally attributed to Leonardo. The Mona Lisa, now in the Louvre, became the world's most famous painting, especially after it was stolen in 1911 and recovered in 1913. In 2017 the Salvator Mundi, attributed to him, though some experts doubt the attribution, sold for $450 million.
