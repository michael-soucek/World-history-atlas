The Meiji emperor, Mutsuhito, reigned over Japan from 1867 to 1912. His reign, the Meiji era, "enlightened rule", saw Japan transformed from an isolated feudal country into a modern industrial state and a world power, though the reforms were largely carried out by his ministers in his name.

## Accession

Mutsuhito was born on 3 November 1852 in Kyoto, the son of Emperor Kōmei. For centuries, emperors had been ceremonial figures while the Tokugawa shoguns ruled. In 1853 American warships under Commodore Perry arrived, forcing Japan to open its ports. Many samurai opposed the shogunate's concessions and rallied around the emperor, under the slogan "revere the emperor, expel the barbarians". Kōmei died in January 1867, and Mutsuhito, aged fourteen, became emperor.

## The restoration

In late 1867 the shogun Tokugawa Yoshinobu resigned. On 3 January 1868, samurai from the domains of Satsuma and Chōshū seized the palace and proclaimed the restoration of imperial rule. In the Boshin War of 1868–1869, they defeated forces loyal to the shogunate. In 1868 the emperor moved to Edo, renamed Tokyo, "eastern capital", and the shogun's castle became the imperial palace. The Charter Oath of April 1868 promised deliberative assemblies and that knowledge would be sought throughout the world.

## Reforms

The young emperor's government, led by men such as Ōkubo Toshimichi, Kido Takayoshi, Saigō Takamori and later Itō Hirobumi, carried out sweeping reforms. In 1871 the feudal domains were abolished and replaced by prefectures. The samurai class lost its privileges and stipends, and was forbidden to carry swords in 1876; a samurai rebellion in Satsuma, led by Saigō, was crushed in 1877. Conscription created a national army. A land tax, a national education system, railways, telegraphs, banks and modern industries were introduced, often with foreign advisers. The Iwakura Mission of 1871–1873 toured the United States and Europe to study Western institutions.

## The constitution

In 1889 the emperor granted the Meiji Constitution, modelled on Prussia's, which created a parliament, the Diet, with an elected lower house, though with a limited franchise, and declared the emperor "sacred and inviolable", head of the state and commander of the army and navy. The Imperial Rescript on Education of 1890 taught loyalty to the emperor and Confucian values. State Shinto promoted the emperor as a descendant of the sun goddess Amaterasu.

## The emperor's image

Mutsuhito, who had grown up secluded in Kyoto, became a public symbol of the new Japan. He travelled around the country, wore Western uniforms and was portrayed in photographs and prints. Historians debate how much he influenced policy; he generally followed his ministers, but his approval was essential, and he took an interest in military affairs.

## War and empire

Japan defeated China in the First Sino-Japanese War of 1894–1895, gaining Taiwan, and Russia in the Russo-Japanese War of 1904–1905, the first victory of an Asian power over a European one in modern times. Japan annexed Korea in 1910. The unequal treaties with Western powers were revised by 1911.

## Death

The emperor died on 30 July 1912. General Nogi Maresuke and his wife killed themselves on the day of his funeral to follow him in death. The Meiji Shrine in Tokyo was built in his and the empress's honour.
