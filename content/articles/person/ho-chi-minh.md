Ho Chi Minh was a Vietnamese revolutionary who led the struggle for Vietnam's independence from France, founded the Democratic Republic of Vietnam in 1945 and served as its president until his death in 1969. A communist and nationalist, he led North Vietnam during the war against the American-backed South.

## Early life

He was born Nguyễn Sinh Cung on 19 May 1890 in Nghệ An province, in central Vietnam, then part of French Indochina. His father was a Confucian scholar and minor official who was dismissed for nationalist sympathies. He attended a French-Vietnamese school in Huế.

## Years abroad

In 1911, using the name Văn Ba, he left Vietnam as a kitchen helper on a French ship and spent thirty years abroad. He worked in the United States, Britain, where he was a pastry chef, and France. In Paris, under the name Nguyễn Ái Quốc, "Nguyen the Patriot", he joined the French Socialist Party, and in 1919 tried to present a petition for Vietnamese rights to the leaders at the Versailles peace conference. In 1920 he became a founding member of the French Communist Party, persuaded by Lenin's writings on colonial liberation.

## Communist organiser

From 1923 Ho worked for the Communist International in Moscow, then in China, where he organised Vietnamese exiles, and in Siam. In 1930 in Hong Kong he united Vietnamese communist groups into the Communist Party of Vietnam, soon renamed the Indochinese Communist Party. He was arrested in Hong Kong in 1931 and spent time in the Soviet Union and China in the 1930s.

## The Viet Minh

In 1941 he returned to Vietnam, then under Japanese occupation, and founded the Viet Minh, the League for the Independence of Vietnam, a coalition led by communists. He adopted the name Ho Chi Minh, "he who enlightens". The Viet Minh fought the Japanese and received some help from the American OSS. After Japan's surrender, in the August Revolution, the Viet Minh took power, and on 2 September 1945, in Hanoi, Ho declared Vietnam's independence, quoting the American Declaration of Independence.

## War with France

France sought to restore its rule. Negotiations failed, and war broke out in December 1946. The Viet Minh, under General Võ Nguyên Giáp, waged a guerrilla war, supported from 1950 by communist China. In 1954 they defeated the French at Điện Biên Phủ. The Geneva Accords divided Vietnam temporarily at the 17th parallel, with Ho's government in the North, and called for elections in 1956, which never took place.

## North Vietnam

In the North, Ho's government carried out a land reform in 1953–1956 in which thousands of landlords and alleged enemies were executed; Ho later acknowledged errors. From the late 1950s, North Vietnam supported the insurgency in the South, and from 1965 the United States sent combat troops and bombed the North. Ho, whose health declined, became more a symbolic leader, with Lê Duẩn holding much real power. He promoted an image of a simple, modest "Uncle Ho".

## Death and legacy

Ho died on 2 September 1969, before the war ended. In 1975 North Vietnamese forces took Saigon, which was renamed Ho Chi Minh City. Against his wishes for cremation, his body was embalmed and placed in a mausoleum in Hanoi. He is revered in Vietnam as the father of the nation, while critics stress the repression of his government.
