Jesus of Nazareth was a Jewish preacher and teacher in Roman-ruled Galilee and Judea in the first century CE, who was crucified in Jerusalem around 30 CE. Christians believe he is the Son of God and the Messiah, who rose from the dead; Muslims revere him as a prophet; and historians study him as a figure of the past. This page describes what historians generally accept and what different traditions believe.

## Sources

The main sources for Jesus's life are the four Gospels of the New Testament, Mark, Matthew, Luke and John, written in Greek between about 65 and 100 CE, and the letters of Paul, written in the 50s. They are religious texts, written to proclaim faith in Jesus, and differ in details. Non-Christian sources, such as the Jewish historian Josephus, writing in the 90s, and the Roman historian Tacitus, writing around 116, briefly mention Jesus and his execution. Almost all historians agree that Jesus existed, was baptised by John the Baptist and was crucified on the orders of the Roman governor Pontius Pilate.

## Background

Jesus lived in a land under Roman rule, through client kings, such as Herod the Great and his son Herod Antipas, ruler of Galilee, and, in Judea, Roman governors. Jewish society included different groups, such as the Pharisees, Sadducees and Essenes, and many hoped for a messiah, an anointed king or deliverer, and for God to restore Israel.

## Early life

According to the Gospels of Matthew and Luke, Jesus was born in Bethlehem to Mary, a virgin, betrothed to Joseph, a carpenter, during the reign of Herod the Great, who died in 4 BCE, so historians place his birth around 6 to 4 BCE. He grew up in Nazareth, a village in Galilee, and his first language was Aramaic. Little is known of his life before his public ministry.

## Ministry

Around the age of thirty, Jesus was baptised by John the Baptist in the Jordan river. He gathered disciples, including twelve apostles, such as the fishermen Peter, Andrew, James and John, and travelled through Galilee, teaching in synagogues, villages and the countryside. He proclaimed the coming of the kingdom of God, taught in parables, such as the Good Samaritan and the Prodigal Son, and gave moral teachings, collected in the Sermon on the Mount: love your enemies, forgive, and care for the poor. He associated with outcasts, such as tax collectors and sinners. The Gospels report that he healed the sick, cast out demons and performed miracles; historians agree that he had a reputation as a healer and exorcist, while whether miracles occurred is a matter of faith.

## Death

Around 30 or 33 CE, Jesus went to Jerusalem for the Passover festival. According to the Gospels, he entered the city on a donkey, welcomed by crowds, and caused a disturbance in the Temple by overturning the tables of money-changers. He shared a last supper with his disciples and was betrayed by one of them, Judas Iscariot, arrested and brought before the high priest Caiaphas and then before Pilate, who sentenced him to death by crucifixion, a Roman punishment for rebels and slaves. The inscription on the cross reportedly called him "King of the Jews".

## The resurrection

The Gospels report that, on the third day after his death, his tomb was found empty and that he appeared to his followers. Christians believe that he rose from the dead, and this belief is central to Christian faith. Historians note that his followers sincerely believed they had seen him alive; explanations of this experience differ according to belief.

## Beliefs about Jesus

Christians believe that Jesus is the Son of God, divine and human, and that his death and resurrection bring salvation. Judaism does not accept him as the Messiah. In Islam, Jesus, Isa, is one of the greatest prophets, born of the Virgin Mary, but not divine, and according to the Quran he was not crucified but raised to God. Christianity, which grew from his followers, became the world's largest religion.
