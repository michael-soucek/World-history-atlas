Justinian I was emperor of the Eastern Roman, or Byzantine, Empire from 527 to 565. He reconquered much of the western Mediterranean, codified Roman law in a work that shaped European legal systems, and built the Hagia Sophia in Constantinople, one of the greatest buildings of the ancient world.

## From peasant to emperor

Justinian was born around 482 in Tauresium, a village near today's Skopje, into a Latin-speaking peasant family. His uncle Justin, a soldier who rose to command the palace guard, became emperor in 518, and Justinian, whom he had adopted and educated, became his closest adviser and, in 527, co-emperor and then sole emperor.

## Theodora

In 525 Justinian married Theodora, a former actress, whose profession had been legally barred from marriage with senators, until Justin changed the law. She became one of the most powerful empresses in Byzantine history. The historian Procopius, in his Secret History, wrote scandalous attacks on her, but she was intelligent and influential, supporting laws protecting women and favouring the Miaphysite Christians.

## The Nika riots

In January 532 the rival chariot-racing factions of the Hippodrome, the Blues and Greens, united against the government, burning much of Constantinople and proclaiming a new emperor. According to Procopius, Justinian was ready to flee, but Theodora persuaded him to stay, saying that royalty is a good burial shroud. His generals, Belisarius and Mundus, trapped the rioters in the Hippodrome and killed about 30,000 people.

## The reconquest

Justinian sought to restore the Roman Empire. In 533–534 Belisarius conquered the Vandal kingdom in North Africa with a small army. In 535 he invaded Ostrogothic Italy, taking Rome in 536 and Ravenna in 540. The Goths fought back, and the war lasted until 554, devastating Italy; the eunuch general Narses completed the conquest. Justinian also took southern Spain from the Visigoths. Wars with Persia in the east were costly, and Justinian paid tribute to the Persian king Khosrow I for peace.

## The law

In 528 Justinian appointed a commission, led by the jurist Tribonian, to collect and organise Roman law. It produced the Code, a collection of imperial laws, the Digest, extracts from the writings of Roman jurists, and the Institutes, a textbook for students, followed by the Novels, new laws. Together, called the Corpus Juris Civilis, they were rediscovered in Italy in the eleventh century and became the basis of civil law in much of Europe and beyond.

## Hagia Sophia

After the riots, Justinian rebuilt the Church of Hagia Sophia, "holy wisdom", designed by Anthemius of Tralles and Isidore of Miletus and completed in 537 in under six years. Its huge dome, over 30 metres wide, seems to float above a ring of windows. Justinian reportedly said, "Solomon, I have surpassed you." He also built churches, fortresses and the basilica of San Vitale in Ravenna, with its famous mosaics of Justinian and Theodora.

## Plague and later years

In 541–542 a pandemic of bubonic plague, the Plague of Justinian, struck the empire, killing a large part of the population of Constantinople; Justinian himself was infected but survived. Theodora died in 548. Justinian died on 14 November 565. Much of Italy was lost to the Lombards soon after his death.
