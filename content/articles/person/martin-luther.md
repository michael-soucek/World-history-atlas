Martin Luther was a German monk, priest and theology professor whose challenge to the Catholic Church in 1517 began the Protestant Reformation. His teaching that people are saved by faith alone, his rejection of papal authority and his translation of the Bible into German transformed European religion, politics and culture.

## Monk

Luther was born on 10 November 1483 in Eisleben, in Saxony, the son of a miner who became a mine owner and wanted his son to be a lawyer. In 1505, caught in a thunderstorm, he vowed to become a monk if Saint Anne saved him, and entered the Augustinian monastery in Erfurt. He was ordained a priest in 1507. He was tormented by doubts about whether he could ever be righteous before God, despite fasting, prayer and confession. In 1512 he became professor of theology at the University of Wittenberg.

## Faith alone

Studying the letters of Paul, especially Romans, Luther came to believe that salvation is a free gift of God's grace, received through faith, not earned by good works or religious practices. This idea, justification by faith alone, became the centre of his theology.

## The Ninety-Five Theses

In 1517 the Dominican friar Johann Tetzel was selling indulgences nearby, certificates that reduced punishment for sins in purgatory, to raise money for rebuilding St Peter's Basilica in Rome. On 31 October 1517, Luther sent his Ninety-Five Theses, criticising the sale of indulgences, to the Archbishop of Mainz, and, according to tradition, nailed them to the door of the Castle Church in Wittenberg. Printed copies spread across Germany within weeks.

## Break with Rome

In debates and writings, Luther went further, arguing that the Bible, not the pope or church councils, was the only authority in matters of faith, and that all baptised Christians were priests. In 1520 he wrote three major works, calling on German nobles to reform the church and criticising the sacraments. Pope Leo X threatened him with excommunication, and Luther burned the papal bull publicly. He was excommunicated in 1521.

## Worms and the Wartburg

In April 1521 Luther was summoned before Emperor Charles V at the Diet of Worms. Asked to recant, he refused, reportedly saying "Here I stand, I can do no other." The emperor declared him an outlaw. On his way home, Frederick the Wise, Elector of Saxony, had him "kidnapped" for his protection and hidden in the Wartburg castle. There, in eleven weeks, he translated the New Testament into German, published in 1522. His complete Bible, published in 1534, helped shape the modern German language.

## The Reformation spreads

Luther's ideas spread through printing, sermons and hymns, such as "A Mighty Fortress Is Our God". Many princes and cities adopted Lutheranism. In 1525 he married Katharina von Bora, a former nun; they had six children. When peasants rebelled in the Peasants' War of 1524–1525, partly inspired by Reformation ideas, Luther urged the princes to crush them. He disagreed with other reformers, such as Zwingli, over communion.

## Later years

In his later years, Luther wrote violently antisemitic tracts, such as On the Jews and Their Lies, in 1543, which were later used by the Nazis and have been repudiated by Lutheran churches. He died in Eisleben on 18 February 1546.
