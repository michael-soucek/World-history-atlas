Pachacuti was the ninth Sapa Inca, ruler of the Inca state, from 1438 to about 1471. He transformed the small kingdom of Cusco into the Inca Empire, Tawantinsuyu, which would become the largest state in the pre-Columbian Americas, rebuilt Cusco and is traditionally credited with building Machu Picchu.

## Cusco and the Chanka

Pachacuti, originally named Cusi Yupanqui, was a son of the Inca ruler Viracocha. In the early fifteenth century, Cusco was one of several small states in the southern Peruvian highlands. Around 1438, according to Inca traditions recorded by Spanish chroniclers, the powerful Chanka people attacked Cusco. Viracocha and his heir, Inca Urco, fled. Cusi Yupanqui stayed and defended the city. According to legend, the stones of the field rose up as warriors to help him, the pururauca. After defeating the Chanka, he took power from his father and took the name Pachacuti, meaning "he who overturns the world" or "earth-shaker".

## Building an empire

Pachacuti, with his brother Capac Yupanqui and later his son Topa Inca Yupanqui, conquered the peoples of the Andes, extending Inca rule north to today's Ecuador and south to the Lake Titicaca region. Conquered peoples were integrated through a mixture of force, diplomacy and gifts. Rebellious populations were moved and replaced by loyal settlers, mitmaq. The Incas called their empire Tawantinsuyu, "the four parts together", divided into four quarters meeting at Cusco.

## Government

Pachacuti is traditionally credited with creating much of the Inca administrative system. People owed labour, mit'a, to the state, working on farms, building projects, roads and in the army, rather than paying taxes in money. Officials kept records using khipus, knotted cords. Storehouses, qollqa, held food and goods. Quechua became the language of administration. The Inca road system, eventually over 30,000 kilometres, linked the empire, with relay runners, chasquis, carrying messages.

## Cusco rebuilt

Pachacuti rebuilt Cusco as an imperial capital, with streets laid out, according to some accounts, in the shape of a puma. He rebuilt the Coricancha, the temple of the sun, whose walls were later covered with gold, and began the fortress-temple of Sacsayhuamán, with its massive, precisely fitted stone walls. Inca stonemasons fitted blocks without mortar so closely that a knife blade cannot fit between them.

## Machu Picchu

Machu Picchu, on a mountain ridge above the Urubamba river, was probably built around 1450 as a royal estate for Pachacuti, a retreat for the ruler and his family. It was abandoned around the time of the Spanish conquest and remained largely unknown to the outside world until 1911, when Hiram Bingham publicised it. It is now a World Heritage Site.

## Religion

Pachacuti promoted the worship of Inti, the sun god, ancestor of the Inca rulers, and reorganised religious ceremonies and the calendar. Mummies of former rulers were preserved and treated as living, keeping their estates.

## Death

Pachacuti handed power to Topa Inca Yupanqui around 1471, and died soon after. His mummy was hidden after the Spanish conquest and found by the Spanish in 1559.
