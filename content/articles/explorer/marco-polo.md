Marco Polo was a Venetian merchant and traveller who journeyed through Asia along the Silk Road and lived in China for seventeen years, from about 1275 to 1292, at the court of the Mongol ruler Kublai Khan. His account of his travels, known as The Travels of Marco Polo or The Description of the World, introduced Europeans to the wealth and culture of China and Asia.

## A Venetian family

Marco Polo was born around 1254 in Venice, a wealthy trading republic. His father, Niccolò, and uncle, Maffeo, were merchants who traded in Constantinople and the Crimea. Around 1260 they travelled east into the Mongol lands and eventually reached the court of Kublai Khan, the Great Khan of the Mongols and ruler of China. Kublai, curious about Europe and Christianity, sent them back with a request to the pope for a hundred learned men and oil from the lamp of the Holy Sepulchre in Jerusalem.

## The journey

In 1271 Niccolò and Maffeo set out again, taking the seventeen-year-old Marco. They travelled through Armenia, Persia and Afghanistan, crossed the Pamir mountains and the deserts of Central Asia, through Kashgar and around the Taklamakan desert, and through the Gansu corridor. The journey took about three and a half years. They reached Kublai's summer palace at Shangdu, Xanadu, around 1275.

## In Kublai's service

According to his account, Marco became a favourite of Kublai, who sent him on missions across China and to Southeast Asia, perhaps including Yunnan and Burma, and he claimed to have governed the city of Yangzhou for three years, a claim historians doubt. He described the splendour of Kublai's court, his palaces in Dadu, today's Beijing, the use of paper money, the postal relay system, the burning of "black stones", coal, as fuel, the great Grand Canal and the city of Hangzhou, which he called Kinsay, the finest and richest city in the world. He described the Mongol customs and Kublai's hunting parties.

## The return

Around 1292 the Polos left China, escorting a Mongol princess, Kököchin, who was to marry the Mongol ruler of Persia, by sea, through Southeast Asia, Sumatra, Sri Lanka and India, to the Persian Gulf. They returned to Venice in 1295, after twenty-four years.

## The book

In 1298 Marco was captured in a naval battle between Venice and Genoa and imprisoned in Genoa. There he dictated his story to a fellow prisoner, Rustichello da Pisa, a writer of romances, who wrote it down in Old French. The book circulated in many manuscripts and translations, with differences between versions. It described lands Europeans had never heard of, such as Japan, Cipangu, which Marco had not visited. Christopher Columbus owned a copy and made notes in it.

## Did he go to China?

Some scholars have questioned whether Marco Polo really went to China, noting that he does not mention the Great Wall, tea, chopsticks or foot-binding, and that he is not mentioned in Chinese records. Most historians, however, accept that he did, as his book contains accurate details, such as the description of paper money, salt production and the Mongol administration, that could hardly have been obtained otherwise.

## Death

Marco Polo married, had three daughters and lived as a merchant in Venice until his death in 1324. According to one story, on his deathbed he said that he had not told half of what he had seen.
