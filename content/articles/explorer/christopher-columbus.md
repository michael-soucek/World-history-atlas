Christopher Columbus was a Genoese navigator who, sailing for the Spanish crown, crossed the Atlantic Ocean in 1492 and reached the Caribbean, beginning lasting contact between Europe and the Americas. He made four voyages between 1492 and 1504. His voyages opened the way for European colonisation of the Americas, with devastating consequences for the Indigenous peoples.

## Early life

Columbus, in Italian Cristoforo Colombo, was born in Genoa around 1451, the son of a wool weaver. He went to sea young and sailed to the eastern Mediterranean, and in 1476 survived a shipwreck off Portugal. He settled in Lisbon, the centre of Portuguese exploration, sailed to England, possibly Iceland, and West Africa, and married Filipa Moniz Perestrelo, from a Portuguese noble family in Madeira.

## The plan

Columbus proposed to reach Asia, the "Indies", by sailing west across the Atlantic. Educated Europeans knew the earth was round, but Columbus greatly underestimated its size and overestimated the size of Asia, calculating that Japan was about 4,000 kilometres west of the Canary Islands; it is about 19,000. The Portuguese king's experts rejected the plan. After years of lobbying, in April 1492 Queen Isabella and King Ferdinand of Spain, having just conquered Granada, agreed to sponsor him, granting him the titles of admiral and viceroy of any lands he found and a share of the profits.

## The first voyage

Columbus sailed from Palos on 3 August 1492 with three ships, the Santa María, the Niña and the Pinta, and about 90 men. After stopping in the Canary Islands, they sailed west for over a month. The crew grew anxious, and Columbus kept two logs, one showing shorter distances. On 12 October 1492, they sighted an island in the Bahamas, which the Taíno inhabitants called Guanahani and Columbus named San Salvador. He believed he had reached the Indies and called the people "Indians". He visited Cuba and Hispaniola, where the Santa María ran aground and he left 39 men at a settlement, La Navidad. He returned to Spain in March 1493, bringing Taíno captives, gold and parrots, and was received with honours.

## Later voyages

On his second voyage, in 1493, with 17 ships and over 1,000 men, Columbus found La Navidad destroyed and founded the colony of La Isabela on Hispaniola. The colonists forced the Taíno to search for gold, enslaving and killing many. On his third voyage, in 1498, he reached Trinidad and the mainland of South America, at the mouth of the Orinoco. Complaints about his harsh and incompetent government of Hispaniola led to his arrest in 1500, and he was sent back to Spain in chains. He was released, but lost his governorship. On his fourth voyage, from 1502 to 1504, he explored the coast of Central America and was stranded in Jamaica for a year.

## Death and legacy

Columbus died in Valladolid on 20 May 1506, still believing he had reached Asia. The continents were named after Amerigo Vespucci. Columbus's voyages began the Columbian Exchange and European colonisation. The Taíno population of Hispaniola collapsed from disease, violence and forced labour within decades. His reputation has been reassessed, and many places now commemorate Indigenous Peoples' Day instead of Columbus Day.
