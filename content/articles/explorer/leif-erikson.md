Leif Erikson was a Norse explorer from Iceland and Greenland who, around the year 1000, is believed to have been the first European to reach North America, nearly five hundred years before Christopher Columbus. According to the Icelandic sagas, he explored and named lands he called Helluland, Markland and Vinland, and built a camp where he spent the winter.

## The family of Erik the Red

Leif was born around 970 or 980, probably in Iceland, the son of Erik the Red, a Norwegian-born settler who had been outlawed from Norway and then from Iceland for killings. Around 982 Erik sailed west and explored a land that he named Greenland, according to the sagas to attract settlers. In 985 or 986 he led a fleet of settlers there, founding two settlements on the south-west coast, the Eastern Settlement and the Western Settlement. Leif grew up in Greenland at Brattahlíð, his father's farm.

## Christianity

According to the Saga of Erik the Red, Leif sailed to Norway around 999, where King Olaf Tryggvason converted him to Christianity and sent him back to Greenland to spread the faith. His mother, Thjodhild, became a Christian and built a church, though his father remained pagan.

## The sagas

Our knowledge of Leif's voyages comes from two Icelandic sagas, the Saga of the Greenlanders and the Saga of Erik the Red, written down in the thirteenth century, about two hundred years after the events. They differ in details. According to the Saga of the Greenlanders, a merchant, Bjarni Herjólfsson, blown off course around 986, had seen unknown lands to the west of Greenland but had not landed. Leif bought Bjarni's ship and sailed with about thirty-five men to find them. The Saga of Erik the Red says Leif found the land by accident on his way back from Norway.

## Helluland, Markland and Vinland

Leif first reached a land of flat stones and glaciers, which he named Helluland, "land of flat stones", probably Baffin Island. Next he found a wooded land with white beaches, Markland, "land of forests", probably Labrador. Then he reached a land where he and his men built houses and spent the winter. They found grapes, or berries, and wild wheat, and named it Vinland, "wine land". The days were longer in winter than in Greenland. In spring they returned to Greenland with a cargo of timber and grapes. Leif later became the chief of the Greenland settlement after his father's death.

## Later voyages

According to the sagas, Leif's brother Thorvald led an expedition to Vinland and was killed in a fight with Indigenous people, whom the Norse called Skrælings. Thorfinn Karlsefni attempted to found a settlement with about 160 settlers; his son Snorri was born there, the first known European child born in the Americas. Conflict with the Indigenous people led them to leave after a few years.

## L'Anse aux Meadows

In 1960 the Norwegian explorer Helge Ingstad and his wife, the archaeologist Anne Stine Ingstad, found the remains of a Norse settlement at L'Anse aux Meadows, at the northern tip of Newfoundland, in Canada. Excavations revealed eight buildings, iron working and Norse objects, such as a bronze cloak pin. In 2021, analysis of wood cut with metal tools, using evidence of a solar storm in 993, dated activity at the site precisely to 1021. It is a World Heritage Site. Whether it was Leif's camp or a base from which Vinland was explored is uncertain; the butternuts found there suggest voyages further south. In the United States, 9 October is Leif Erikson Day.
