Ferdinand Magellan was a Portuguese explorer who led the Spanish expedition of 1519–1522 that made the first circumnavigation of the earth. He discovered the strait at the southern tip of South America that bears his name and crossed the Pacific Ocean, but was killed in the Philippines in 1521. The voyage was completed by Juan Sebastián Elcano.

## Portuguese service

Magellan, in Portuguese Fernão de Magalhães, was born around 1480 in northern Portugal, into a noble family, and served as a page at the royal court. From 1505 he served in the Portuguese East Indies, fighting at Diu in 1509 and the conquest of Malacca in 1511. He was wounded in Morocco. After a quarrel with King Manuel I, who refused to raise his pension, he went to Spain in 1517.

## The plan

Magellan proposed to the young King Charles I of Spain, the future emperor Charles V, to reach the Spice Islands, the Moluccas, by sailing west, around or through the Americas. He argued that the islands lay in the Spanish half of the world, according to the Treaty of Tordesillas. Charles agreed.

## Departure

The expedition left Sanlúcar de Barrameda on 20 September 1519 with five ships, the Trinidad, San Antonio, Concepción, Victoria and Santiago, and about 270 men. It crossed the Atlantic to Brazil and sailed south along the coast of South America, searching for a passage. In Patagonia, at Port San Julián, during the winter, Magellan crushed a mutiny by Spanish captains, executing one and marooning another. The Santiago was wrecked.

## The strait

On 21 October 1520, the fleet entered a strait near the tip of South America. During the 38 days it took to navigate the narrow, winding channel, the San Antonio deserted and returned to Spain. The sailors saw fires on the land to the south, which they named Tierra del Fuego. On 28 November 1520, the three remaining ships entered the ocean, which Magellan called the Pacific, "peaceful", because it seemed calm.

## The Pacific

The Pacific was far larger than anyone had imagined. For over three months, the ships sailed without finding food, except at two uninhabited islands. The crew ate rat-infested biscuits, sawdust and leather, and many died of scurvy and starvation. In March 1521 they reached Guam, and then the Philippines.

## Death at Mactan

In the Philippines, Magellan befriended Rajah Humabon of Cebu, who accepted baptism along with many of his people. Magellan then attacked the island of Mactan, whose chief, Lapulapu, refused to submit. On 27 April 1521, Magellan was killed in battle on the beach. Lapulapu is honoured as a national hero in the Philippines.

## Completing the voyage

The survivors burned the Concepción, as there were too few men to sail three ships, and reached the Moluccas, where they loaded cloves. The Trinidad tried to return across the Pacific and was captured by the Portuguese. Juan Sebastián Elcano sailed the Victoria west across the Indian Ocean and around Africa, avoiding Portuguese ports, and reached Spain on 6 September 1522, with 18 survivors. The chronicler Antonio Pigafetta, one of them, wrote an account of the voyage. The circumnavigation proved the earth's vast size.

## On our maps

Magellan's voyage took place between our 1500 and 1530 maps, as the [Spanish Empire](/place/spanish-empire) and the [Portuguese Empire](/place/portuguese-empire) competed for routes to Asia.
