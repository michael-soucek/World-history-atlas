Zheng He was a Chinese admiral and diplomat who commanded seven great voyages of the Ming dynasty's "treasure fleet" across the Indian Ocean between 1405 and 1433, reaching Southeast Asia, India, the Persian Gulf, Arabia and East Africa. His fleets were among the largest in history before modern times.

## Captive and eunuch

Zheng He was born Ma He in 1371 in Yunnan, in south-western China, into a Muslim family; his father and grandfather had made the pilgrimage to Mecca, as their title, hajji, shows. In 1381, when Ming forces conquered Yunnan from the last Mongol loyalists, he was captured as a boy, castrated, and sent to serve in the household of Zhu Di, the Prince of Yan, a son of the Ming founder. He became a trusted officer and distinguished himself in the civil war that brought Zhu Di to the throne as the Yongle emperor in 1402. The emperor gave him the surname Zheng.

## The treasure fleet

The Yongle emperor ordered a great fleet to sail to the "Western Ocean", to display Chinese power and wealth, bring foreign states into the tribute system, encourage trade and perhaps search for his deposed nephew, the previous emperor, who was rumoured to have fled. The first fleet, which left in 1405, had over 60 large "treasure ships" and over 200 smaller vessels, carrying about 27,800 men, including soldiers, sailors, officials, doctors, interpreters and astrologers. According to Ming sources, the largest treasure ships were about 120 metres long, though many modern historians believe they were smaller.

## The voyages

On the first three voyages, from 1405 to 1411, the fleet visited Champa, in today's Vietnam, Java, Sumatra, Malacca, Sri Lanka and Calicut, in India. In Sri Lanka, Zheng He's troops defeated the king, Alakeshvara, and took him to China. On the fourth voyage, from 1413 to 1415, the fleet reached Hormuz, in the Persian Gulf. On the fifth, sixth and seventh voyages, from 1417 to 1433, detachments sailed to Aden, Mecca and the Swahili coast of East Africa, reaching Mogadishu, Brava and Malindi. Envoys from dozens of states came to China with tribute, and the fleet brought back exotic goods, such as gems, spices, medicines and animals, including a giraffe from Malindi, which was presented to the emperor as a qilin, a mythical creature and good omen. The fleet sometimes used force, against pirates in Palembang in 1407 and in Sri Lanka.

## Records

Zheng He set up inscriptions recording his voyages, such as a trilingual stele in Sri Lanka, in Chinese, Tamil and Persian, and stelae in China. Ma Huan, a Muslim interpreter, wrote an account of the lands visited.

## End of the voyages

After the Yongle emperor's death in 1424, the voyages were suspended. The Xuande emperor ordered a seventh voyage in 1431. Zheng He probably died in 1433 during or soon after that voyage, possibly at Calicut. The Ming court then ended the voyages, because of their cost, the influence of Confucian officials who opposed eunuchs and foreign ventures, and the need to defend the northern frontier against the Mongols. Many records were later lost or destroyed. China turned away from the sea, decades before the Portuguese reached the Indian Ocean. Zheng He is today a national hero in China and is honoured by Chinese communities in Southeast Asia.
